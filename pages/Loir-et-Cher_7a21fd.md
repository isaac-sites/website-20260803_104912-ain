## Ternay: the department’s strongest case was later downgraded

The Ternay incident stands apart because it had several witnesses, prompt reporting and substantial documentation. At about 11.40 pm on 22 May 1981, four members of one family were travelling by car near Ternay. They saw a brightly illuminated phenomenon that appeared low in the sky near a small wood. The driver stopped, and the group watched it for roughly ten to fifteen minutes. Accounts described a dark, elongated or lens-shaped mass carrying strong white, blue-green and red-orange lights. Some witnesses estimated it at about eight metres long and several hundred metres away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The driver initially wondered whether it was an aircraft approaching the military aviation area around Tours, but rejected that interpretation because the light seemed stationary and unlike a conventional aeroplane. After it appeared to disappear behind trees, the witnesses drove towards the area and encountered what they described as thick mist or smoke. No sound, landing trace or measurable physical effect was established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The reporting process was unusually strong for a rural sighting. The principal witness telephoned the gendarmerie within minutes. Officers visited his home, returned with him the next morning for a three-hour reconstruction, searched for possible ground traces and interviewed all four witnesses separately over the following days. The case file included drawings, photographs and a location plan. Those features gave the report considerably more substance than a late recollection or unsupported newspaper anecdote.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

### Why the original interpretation seemed plausible

Several elements made the experience feel airborne:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the lights appeared above or beside a nearer clump of trees;
* the witnesses could not clearly see the horizon in darkness and overcast weather;
* the light seemed to change position as the car moved;
* branches may have intermittently obscured the source, producing apparent flashes;
* distance and size were estimated without a reliable reference object.

</div>

These are important weaknesses rather than accusations against the witnesses. A sincere observer can accurately report what an object looked like while being wrong about where it was. When the horizon is invisible, powerful lights on rising ground may appear suspended in the sky. Movement by the observer also creates parallax: a distant source seems to shift in relation to nearer trees or buildings. GEIPAN’s re-analysis regarded these effects as central to the Ternay sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

### The later forestry explanation

The case was once classified as unexplained. In 2018, however, GEIPAN re-examined it using geographical reconstruction, aerial photographs and its accumulated experience of similar reports. Investigators considered illuminated agricultural machinery and forestry equipment operating about two kilometres south of the witnesses. They favoured forestry work because aerial photographs showed that woodland in the relevant line of sight had been cut between May 1978 and July 1981.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The proposed reconstruction was that powerful work lights on higher ground appeared to be a low-flying object. The witnesses’ moving car made the lights shift against a nearer wood about 400 metres away, while trees around the machinery created the reported blinking and colour changes. When the group drove towards the nearer wood, they searched in the wrong place because the true source was much farther away and in a different direction. GEIPAN classified the case as B: probably explained, rather than proven beyond every doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

That distinction is fair. No machinery log or named operator was recovered to demonstrate that a particular vehicle was working at that exact time. The blue and green lights reported by three witnesses were also not considered typical of forestry equipment. Nevertheless, the revised explanation accounts for the apparent altitude, movement, flashing, disappearance and lack of physical traces more economically than an unknown aircraft. The later investigation therefore weakened the extraordinary interpretation substantially.

## Blois in 1965: a triangular object with a conventional match

A second historically interesting case occurred near Blois on 23 July 1965. Multiple people reportedly watched an immobile, metallic-looking triangular object reflecting sunlight at about 6 pm. Because the report was submitted long after the event, its evidential value is limited: memories can change, and contemporary meteorological or flight records may no longer be available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1965-07-02569?page=%2C528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BLOIS (41) 23.07.1965 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN classified the case as B and considered a tetrahedral stratospheric research balloon the probable explanation. Such balloons could present a bright, geometric appearance when sunlight caught their reflective surfaces, while their great distance made them seem stationary. The case is a useful reminder that “triangular” does not automatically imply a structured aircraft. Shape is often inferred from illuminated surfaces rather than observed as a complete solid outline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1965-07-02569?page=%2C528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BLOIS (41) 23.07.1965 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Blois report remains more interesting than a simple point of light because several witnesses apparently perceived the same unusual geometry. Even so, there is no recorded radar return, photograph, close-range detail, manoeuvre or interaction with aircraft. The official balloon interpretation fits the object’s brightness, immobility and daytime visibility without requiring an exotic cause.

## The 1990 wave: impressive testimony, but a national re-entry event

Loir-et-Cher also lay beneath one of France’s most famous mass-sighting episodes. At about 7 pm on 5 November 1990, witnesses across a very large part of the country reported formations of lights, silent triangular shapes and enormous dark objects moving broadly from west to east. Gendarmerie files include reports from several separated observers who agreed on the timing and general direction but did not see a supporting structure joining the lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B03697%20%281990307323%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Print 90307323-3697-90M.tif (23 pages)...</span></span></span>

Some local and specialist UFO accounts have argued that the Soviet rocket re-entry identified that evening cannot explain every individual observation. They point to reports of low altitude, turns, huge apparent dimensions or objects lingering after the main event. Those objections deserve to be recorded, but they do not overturn the central astronomical identification. GEIPAN’s national file classifies the main 5 November phenomenon as an atmospheric re-entry and retained that conclusion in an update published in January 2025.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=40%2C37&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

A fragmenting rocket stage can generate several bright bodies travelling in formation. At night, witnesses may connect those lights into the outline of one dark craft, particularly when no stars are visible between them. Distance is also easily underestimated: an object hundreds of kilometres away may seem to pass above a nearby road or town. Apparent silence is therefore unsurprising, while perspective can make parallel tracks look like turns or changes of direction.

This does not prove that every report filed in Loir-et-Cher that evening described the same object. A mass event can attract unrelated aircraft, stars and local lights into the reporting stream. The strongest conclusion is narrower: the timing, direction and nationwide distribution establish a major conventional stimulus, so individual claims require independent evidence before being separated from the re-entry. No Loir-et-Cher report from that night has produced physical evidence or verified radar data strong enough to do so.

## Aviation and military links are real, but easily overstated

Loir-et-Cher sits within an active aviation environment. Blois-Le Breuil aerodrome serves general aviation, while military flying from the wider Tours region has long been familiar in central France. Witnesses in both the Ternay and 1990 files compared what they saw with known aircraft or believed aeroplanes had reacted to the phenomenon. Such details often increase a report’s dramatic force, yet they are difficult to verify retrospectively.

The Ternay driver’s first thought was that the lights belonged to an aircraft connected with the Tours area. That was a sensible comparison, not evidence that military personnel tracked an unknown craft. The eventual explanation did not require an airborne object at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The 1990 gendarmerie material is more ambiguous. Officers reported seeing a passenger aircraft change course while lights crossed the sky, and one contact with an aviation test centre reportedly produced a reference to a demonstration flight. Yet the file did not establish that the airliner’s manoeuvre was caused by the lights; observers inferred the connection from simultaneous events. The same documents also emphasised that no solid body joining the lights was actually visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B03697%20%281990307323%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Print 90307323-3697-90M.tif (23 pages)...</span></span></span>

France’s investigative system can consult military air-operation records, civil aviation, meteorological expertise and local authorities when necessary. Gendarmes normally collect witness statements and sketches, while GEIPAN conducts the technical assessment. This institutional connection should not be confused with proof that the military regards a report as extraterrestrial. ## Why sincere witnesses can describe extraordinary craft

The Loir-et-Cher files repeatedly show that witness credibility and object identification are separate questions. Four family members can agree that they saw a bright object without correctly judging whether it was 400 metres or two kilometres away. Several people can describe a triangle because separate lights encourage the mind to complete an outline. A photograph can faithfully contain a dark shape even when that shape is a nearby insect blurred by motion.

Common mechanisms in the department’s records include:

**Loss of the horizon.** In darkness or low cloud, ground lights on elevated land may appear airborne.

**Parallax.** Movement by a car changes the apparent position of a distant light against nearer trees.

**Point lights becoming solid shapes.** People naturally connect three lights into a triangle or several lights into one large craft.

**Unfamiliar viewing angles.** An aircraft approaching head-on can appear stationary and may not reveal its wings.

**Small nearby objects in photographs.** Insects close to the lens can look like distant discs or elongated craft.

**Celebration lanterns.** Their warm colour, silence, slow drift and eventual fading regularly produce convincing UFO reports.

**Satellites and orbital structures.** The International Space Station can be extremely bright, while other satellites may move steadily without sound.

These explanations do not mean that witnesses are dishonest or foolish. GEIPAN explicitly treats testimony as a sincere account of an extraordinary personal experience and preserves anonymity because many witnesses fear ridicule. Its task is to identify the stimulus, not to dismiss the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## How strong is the case for an unresolved Loir-et-Cher phenomenon?

On the available public record, it is weak. Loir-et-Cher has several well-documented reports, but none currently stands as a robust official D case combining high strangeness with strong, independent evidence. The department’s most substantial incident has a plausible ground-based explanation; its 1965 triangular object matches a known balloon type; its contribution to the [1990 wave]({{ '1990-wave-5e855a/' | relative_url }}) sits within a confirmed national re-entry event; and recent reports are largely explained or too poorly documented to assess.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

There are still legitimate uncertainties. The exact forestry vehicle responsible at Ternay was never identified. The department’s press archive is fragmented, and some older local sightings may never have reached GEIPAN. A C classification can conceal an intriguing experience whose evidence was simply not preserved. None of those gaps, however, positively supports an extraordinary interpretation.

The fairest judgement is therefore that Loir-et-Cher’s UFO history is significant chiefly for the quality of its investigative lessons. It demonstrates why immediate witness interviews matter, why apparently close objects may be distant, why old unexplained files should remain open to reassessment and why “unidentified” describes the state of the evidence at a particular moment rather than the nature of the object itself.
