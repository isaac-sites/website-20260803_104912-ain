## Introduction

[Charente]({{ 'why-charentes-ufo-cases-remain/' | relative_url }})’s two publicly accessible cases classified **D — unidentified after investigation** are not its most dramatic reports, but they are its most carefully preserved unresolved ones. In Angoulême in 2009, a witness described a silent, matt-brown oval crossing the daytime sky. In Ruffec in 2022, another witness watched a white light repeat the same brief movement for about five minutes. Neither case has photographs, instrument readings, physical traces or independent witnesses. They remain open because investigators could not make the reported behaviour fit a known explanation with enough confidence, not because the evidence demonstrates an extraordinary craft. GEIPAN’s current Charente listing identifies these as the department’s two category D reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009ANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009. ## The silent brown oval over Angoulême

At about 13:15 on 15 August 2009, a person watching from the garden of their home in Angoulême reported seeing a matt-brown, egg-shaped or oval object travel in a straight line from east to west, towards Cognac. The witness heard no sound. GEIPAN’s summary does not record lights, exhaust, a trail, changes of direction or any interaction with the surroundings. No second witness came forward.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009ANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009. Résumé. Observation du passage silencieux E-O d&#x27;un objet ovoïde marron. Traject...</span></span></span>

The case is memorable because it was a daylight sighting of an apparently solid shape rather than a distant night-time light. Even so, almost every important physical property remained uncertain. With no known size or distance, an object that appeared to pass over roofs could not be reliably converted into a true altitude, speed or dimension. Silence is similarly ambiguous: it may indicate an unusual object, but it can also result when an ordinary aircraft is distant, when wind carries sound away or when the observation is too brief for noise to be noticed.

Investigators considered aircraft and helicopters, but the decisive weakness was timing. The gendarmerie statement reached GEIPAN too late for air-traffic checks to establish what had been operating over the area. The witness rejected an aircraft explanation, yet that personal judgement could not substitute for contemporary flight records. The delay did not prove that an aircraft was responsible; it removed one of the best ways of testing the possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009ANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009. Résumé. Observation du passage silencieux E-O d&#x27;un objet ovoïde marron. Traject...</span></span></span>

Several nearby-object explanations were also examined, including a rugby ball, a clay target, a flying disc and a model aircraft. GEIPAN judged that these did not fit the account, principally because the witness perceived the phenomenon as travelling clearly above the rooftops. That reasoning depends heavily on the accuracy of the witness’s depth and height judgement, however. A small, nearby object against an empty sky can be difficult to place because there are few visual cues from which to infer distance.

An atmospheric entry was another possibility. The date fell close to the annual Perseid meteor maximum, but the reported matt-brown oval and straight, apparently controlled-looking passage were not an obvious meteor description. More importantly, no corroborating reports were found to establish a wider fireball event. GEIPAN therefore had no consolidated explanation and classified the observation D, assigning a recorded strangeness value of 0.62 and describing its consistency as medium to strong.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009ANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009. Résumé. Observation du passage silencieux E-O d&#x27;un objet ovoïde marron. Traject...</span></span></span>

The official label should nevertheless be read alongside GEIPAN’s own 2011 description of the case among its then-published D1 reports. The agency explained that D1 cases were strange but comparatively limited in consistency, typically involving a single witness without photographic or video evidence. Angoulême’s unresolved status is therefore best understood as a credible-looking account that resisted the available checks, not as a heavily corroborated event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/nouveaux-cas-D1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">nouveaux cas D1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLe GEIPAN publie de nouveaux cas classés D114 Dec 2011 — Le GEIPAN publie de nouveaux cas classés D1. ## The repeating white light at Ruffec

The Ruffec report began at around 21:00 on 13 September 2022. A 40-year-old resident was sitting on steps outside the kitchen while speaking on the telephone and watching a sky containing stars, aircraft and the remnants of storm activity. The witness first took a white point of light for an aircraft. It moved across a short section of sky, disappeared, then reappeared at approximately its starting position and repeated the movement. The cycle lasted roughly six seconds and continued, with some variation, for about five minutes before the apparent track shifted slightly downwards and towards the west-south-west.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The repetition is what kept the case unusual. A single aircraft could explain a white moving point, but not an object apparently resetting to the same starting position every few seconds. The witness compared the effect to a light mounted on a rotating sphere: each turn would make the light visible briefly, then conceal it before the next pass. This was an analogy rather than evidence of a rotating object, but it captures the pattern the investigation had to explain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN reconstructed the viewing direction more carefully than the original report allowed. The witness initially described looking south-west, but comparison with photographs and later correspondence placed the phenomenon more nearly west, drifting towards the west-south-west. The investigation also established that the observation took place during nautical twilight, with no Moon present. Jupiter and Saturn were in other parts of the sky, while the bright star Arcturus was visible in the west. None matched the reported repetitive arc and disappearance pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Weather conditions complicated the interpretation. A storm cell and substantial cloud cover existed in the region, although the witness said the section of sky containing the light appeared clear. Investigators examined whether intermittent lightning might have illuminated ice crystals or cloud structures. They found that the repetition was too regular for normal lightning, while strong winds at altitude made a stable reflective formation difficult to sustain in one place for five minutes. The hypothesis was not mathematically impossible, but it lacked a workable physical match to the observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Aircraft were a stronger possibility. Flight-tracking reconstruction showed nearly a dozen airliners in the wider sky between about 20:55 and 21:10, mostly travelling from the south-west towards the north-east — broadly compatible with the direction first described. The light was also comparable in brightness and appearance to an aircraft lamp, and the witness had initially interpreted it that way. Against this, the number and rhythm of the repeated appearances appeared much greater than the number of aircraft actually crossing the relevant track. GEIPAN scored the aircraft hypothesis at 0.475: plausible in several respects, but not strong enough to account for the complete sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A moving searchlight, sometimes called a skytracker, offered another partial fit. Its beam could sweep in cycles, producing a repeating light on cloud while the beam itself remained invisible in clear air. Yet such a projector would normally create a diffuse patch rather than a sharp, star-like point, and investigators could not confirm suitable mid-level cloud at the required location. No particular event, venue or operator was identified. The possibility remained conceivable but unverified, receiving an estimated score of 0.425.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Investigators also tested reflections from Starlink satellites. The time after sunset and general direction offered some resemblance to reported satellite flares, but the geometry failed. The witness placed the light roughly 34 to 60 degrees above the horizon, whereas the relevant Starlink reflection effect would have appeared only a few degrees above it under those solar conditions. GEIPAN therefore rejected this as a satisfactory match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete801.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why unresolved does not mean extraordinary


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Angoulême and Ruffec remain open for different reasons. Angoulême presents a visually striking daytime shape, but the investigation was weakened by late reporting and the loss of timely aviation checks. Ruffec was reported promptly and received a much fuller reconstruction, yet its central feature — the repeated disappearance and return — could not be matched confidently to the recorded air traffic, satellite geometry, weather or projector hypothesis.

</div>

Their shared limitation is more important than their differences: each is a single-witness visual observation without an image of the phenomenon, radar confirmation, physical effects or an independently recorded second account. The available material may support the conclusion that the witnesses reported something genuinely puzzling. It cannot establish the objects’ true distance, dimensions, speed or nature.

GEIPAN’s classification system is designed to preserve precisely this kind of uncertainty. A D case has enough residual strangeness to resist the explanations tested, while a C case lacks enough reliable information for meaningful analysis. The D label therefore says that an investigation ended without an adequate identification. It does not rank a report as proof of non-human technology, nor does it guarantee that an overlooked ordinary explanation will never emerge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

For Charente’s UFO history, these are consequently the department’s best **documented unresolved reports**, rather than its strongest evidence of an extraordinary presence. Angoulême shows how delay can permanently close off verification. Ruffec shows that even extensive checking can leave a residue when a witness describes a precise but unrecorded pattern. Both cases stay open in the narrow, defensible sense: the published evidence does not identify what was seen, and it is not strong enough to tell us what the phenomena actually were.
