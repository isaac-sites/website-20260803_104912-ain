## What the official Gironde record actually contains

France is unusual in maintaining a public state archive of unidentified aerospace reports. GEIPAN, created within CNES in 1977, gathers witness statements submitted online or passed through bodies such as the gendarmerie and civil aviation authorities. Investigators compare testimony with astronomical data, weather, aircraft movements, satellite re-entries and other possible causes before publishing a classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[cnes.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

This system is important for understanding Gironde because a database entry is not an endorsement of an extraordinary object. It records a reported experience and the result of an assessment. GEIPAN says that, nationally, most sufficiently documented cases are explained through misidentification or perception error, while a much smaller proportion remain genuinely unexplained. A large additional group cannot be assessed because the available information is inadequate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aerospace Phenomena) sightings...</span></span></span>

Category C is easily misunderstood. It does not mean that investigators found an inexplicable craft. It means they could not reach a sound conclusion, often because the report arrived late, contained no independent witness, lacked an original photograph or gave insufficient direction, duration and angular information. Such cases remain unidentified in an everyday sense, but they are weak evidence because almost no hypothesis can be tested properly.

**Reports solved through careful reconstruction.** The Carcans case of 27 September 2011 shows the value of detailed checking. An amateur astronomer saw an unexpectedly bright, apparently stationary light low in the east and used a nearby tree as a reference point. GEIPAN’s investigation drew on the witness questionnaire, astronomical reconstruction and local geography rather than treating the witness’s experience as self-explanatory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete329.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete329</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete329</span></span></span>

This type of report is particularly instructive. Familiarity with the night sky can improve a witness’s description, but it does not make misidentification impossible. Bright planets near the horizon can appear unusually large, unstable or mobile because of atmospheric distortion and involuntary eye movements. The same principle applies to pilots, police officers and military personnel: professional experience can strengthen parts of a statement without turning visual perception into an infallible measuring instrument.

## Bordeaux aviation does not produce a clear radar mystery

Gironde contains one of south-west France’s major aviation centres. Bordeaux-Mérignac Airport, busy commercial routes, military activity and aircraft approaching or leaving the region all create opportunities for unusual lights to be seen under unfamiliar angles. That setting may sound promising to readers looking for radar-confirmed UFOs, but the accessible [official record]({{ 'official-record-634f2c/' | relative_url }}) does not reveal a landmark Gironde case supported by both strong radar data and a corresponding unknown visual target.

Instead, aviation often supplies the explanation. GEIPAN’s A63 case was attributed to an airliner, while the April 2010 Bordeaux aviation report was linked to Venus. These are useful reminders that an object apparently hovering or pacing a traveller may be a distant aircraft seen along its direction of travel, and that an exceptionally bright planet can remain visually compelling even to observers in an aeronautical setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=135&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">A63] de BORDEAUX (33) VERS BAYONNE (64) 07.10.2013 &#124; 07/10/2013 &#124; B &#124; Gironde &#124; Avion de ligne &#124; 18/11/2013 &#124; 2013-10-07...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: October 7, 2013</span></span></span>

Aviation also complicates distance estimates. At night, witnesses generally cannot determine the size, speed or altitude of a light unless it crosses a known reference or is supported by radar, photographs or multiple geometrically useful viewpoints. A slow nearby object and a fast distant one can create similar angular movement. Descriptions such as “low”, “huge” or “silent” are therefore important testimony, but not measurements.

This distinction matters when evaluating claims of military interest. The involvement of gendarmes or aviation authorities shows that a report entered an official channel; it does not show that defence systems detected an unknown vehicle. In Gironde, the strongest publicly accessible cases remain witness-led rather than instrument-led.

## The 1990 national wave reached Gironde but was not a local invasion

On 5 November 1990, more than a thousand people across France reported an enormous luminous formation moving across a clear evening sky. Bordeaux lay within the broad area from which reports were made, and the event became one of the most famous “UFO waves” in modern French history.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span></span></span>

The case initially seemed exceptional because witnesses in many regions described lights arranged in broad shapes, sometimes interpreted as triangles, trapezoids or a single vast craft. Reports also came from aviation personnel. Yet the central phenomenon was subsequently matched to the atmospheric re-entry of space hardware. GEIPAN later published a simulation showing how the trajectory and fragmentation could account for the large-scale sequence of observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span></span></span>

The Gironde relevance is less about a distinct local incident than about perception during a national event. As fragments crossed the sky, observers could group separate lights into the outline of one structured object. With no visible body connecting them, the mind supplied a coherent form. Different viewing angles, atmospheric conditions and fragmented debris then produced descriptions that sounded inconsistent with a single re-entry even though they arose from the same broad event.

Some witnesses and UFO researchers have argued that not every report from that evening fits the re-entry, particularly observations said to have occurred outside the calculated time or direction. That is possible: a large publicised event can gather unrelated sightings around it. But a report that does not fit the principal explanation must be evaluated independently. It cannot inherit strength merely from having occurred on the same evening. For Gironde, the 1990 wave therefore demonstrates both the value of a continent-scale explanation and the danger of treating every associated account as one unified mystery.

## Local flaps, media reports and false alarms

Gironde has experienced occasional bursts of interest rather than a clearly defined long-term flap comparable to France’s famous 1954 wave. Modern reports are often amplified through local news sites and social media before a technical explanation is available.

In February 2016, for example, a resident in southern Gironde reported seeing what appeared to be a low-flying aircraft disappear amid thick smoke near Sainte-Croix-du-Mont, between Langon and Cadillac. The Gironde gendarmerie publicised the call in a humorous tone, and a local news outlet repeated the story under a UFO-themed headline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.facebook.com/gendarmeriedelagironde/posts/%C3%A0-la-recherche-dun-ovni17-f%C3%A9vrier-18h20-appel-au-corg-33-dun-riverain-qui-signal/546044482228840/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: facebook.com">[Facebook]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">facebook.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">à la recherche dun ovni17 février 18h20 appel au corg 33 dun riverain qui signal</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">à la recherche dun ovni17 février 18h20 appel au corg 33 dun riverain qui signal</span></span></span>

This episode is revealing because the original concern was effectively an aviation emergency: an aircraft might have crashed. The “UFO” label entered through uncertainty and media framing rather than evidence of an exotic object. Cases of this kind should first be checked against aircraft movements, agricultural or industrial smoke, controlled burning, cloud banks and perspective effects.

Modern technology has added further recurring explanations. Drones can hover, change direction and display bright navigation lights. Balloon lanterns and Mylar balloons can drift in ways that appear purposeful. Camera processing can turn insects, birds, lens reflections or compression errors into apparently structured forms. GEIPAN’s Gironde files contain examples assigned to airliners, Venus, meteors, balloons and biological photographic artefacts, showing that there is no single dominant cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=24&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## How strong is the evidence for an unresolved Gironde case?

The public record supports three broad conclusions.

First, people in Gironde have reported genuinely puzzling experiences. There is no need to assume dishonesty or fantasy. The Gujan-Mestras witnesses, the Carcans amateur astronomer and travellers reporting lights from roads around Bordeaux may all have described what they sincerely believed they saw. Official investigation begins from that human fact rather than dismissing it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-06-00525" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGUJAN-MESTRAS (33) 19.06.1978Le 19 juin 1978 entre 23h et 0h45 plusieurs personnes entendent unbruit sourd et fort puis observent u...</span></span></span>

The most defensible judgement is therefore that Gironde has a rich history of unusual-sky reports but no publicly established case demonstrating an exotic craft. A few episodes remain incomplete or ambiguous at the level of published documentation. Most modern cases with adequate data have moved towards conventional explanations, while poorly recorded cases remain uncertain mainly because decisive information is missing.

## Reading a Gironde sighting critically

A useful assessment begins with the observation rather than the label attached to it. The strongest Gironde reports are those recorded quickly and precisely: exact time, direction, angle above the horizon, duration, weather, movement relative to landmarks and the presence of independent witnesses.

Several questions sharply improve the evaluation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Was the original material preserved?** An unedited photograph or video with metadata is far more valuable than a compressed social-media copy.
2. **Were witnesses independent?** Several people discussing a light before giving statements do not provide the same evidential strength as observers who reported it separately.
3. **Can direction and timing be reconstructed?** These allow comparisons with aircraft, planets, satellites, meteors and re-entering space debris.
4. **Was an environmental effect documented independently?** In Gujan-Mestras, the claimed street-light disturbance would be much stronger evidence if supported by timed maintenance or electricity-network records.
5. **Does “unidentified” mean unexplained or merely under-documented?** A GEIPAN category C case is not equivalent to a robust category D case. Missing information prevents both a conventional explanation and an extraordinary one.
6. **Did later investigation strengthen the claim?** In Gironde, later analysis has more often narrowed cases towards familiar causes than uncovered new physical evidence.

</div>

Seen this way, Gironde’s UFO history is not disappointing because many reports have ordinary explanations. Those explanations are part of the history. They show how geography, aviation, astronomy, technology, memory and media framing combine to create convincing mysteries—and why a small residue of uncertainty should be preserved without being inflated into proof.
