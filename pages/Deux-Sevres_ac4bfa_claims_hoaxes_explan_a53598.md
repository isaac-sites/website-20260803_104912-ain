## Introduction

Deux-Sèvres UFO stories have changed less because the sky became less mysterious than because investigations became more systematic. The department’s most dramatic old claim — a landed disc and a small humanoid near Bressuire in October 1954 — was repeated for decades in books and catalogues, but later testimony attributed the story to a joke invented by the witness. More recent reports have usually involved lights rather than occupants, and several have been matched to ordinary sources such as event projectors and floating lanterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954bressuiref.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 3 octobre 1954, Bressuire, Deux-Sèvres...</span></span></span>

That does not mean every Deux-Sèvres sighting has been solved. The red, silent object reported at [Sainte-Soline]({{ 'sainte-soline/' | relative_url }}) in 1980 remains officially unexplained. The broader historical pattern is nevertheless clear: later evidence weakened the department’s most spectacular encounter story, while improved checking of weather, lighting, movement and local events has explained many newer observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-08-01694" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINTE-SOLINE (79) 16.08.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The disputed Bressuire humanoid story

At dawn on 3 October 1954, Angelo Girardo, described in most accounts as a 55-year-old worker travelling to his job, supposedly encountered an object beside the road near Bressuire. The familiar version says that the craft was circular, about three metres wide and resting near the grain silos of an agricultural cooperative. A small figure wearing something like a diving suit stood beside it, moved towards Girardo and then returned to the object, which rose at extraordinary speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954bressuiref.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 3 octobre 1954, Bressuire, Deux-Sèvres...</span></span></span>

The story appeared during France’s intense autumn 1954 “flying saucer” wave, when newspapers were publishing reports of discs, landings and strange occupants from across the country. National papers including *Le Figaro*, *La Croix*, *France-Soir* and *Libération* were subsequently cited as early sources for the Bressuire account. Later writers such as Aimé Michel and Jacques Vallée reduced the story to a compact catalogue entry, helping it circulate far beyond Deux-Sèvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://journals.openedition.org/terrain/2973" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: journals.openedition.org">[openedition.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">journals.openedition.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open Edition JournalsEnquêtes sur les soucoupes volantesby P Lagrange · 1990 · Cited by 32 — Il publie en 1954 Les Soucoupes volantes vie...</span></span></span>

The apparent abundance of references can be misleading. Most later entries did not represent new witnesses or independent investigations; they repeated the same original claim. One database reportedly contained 18 versions of the Bressuire incident, with inconsistent dates, times and descriptions. Girardo was variously said to have travelled by bicycle or motor scooter, while the figure appeared in some versions with a helmet and in others bare-headed. Even the date drifted between 27 September, 2 October, 3 October and 13 October.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954bressuiref.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 3 octobre 1954, Bressuire, Deux-Sèvres...</span></span></span>

This is a useful warning about historical UFO catalogues. Repetition can make a story look multiply confirmed when several entries actually descend from one newspaper report. Copying also introduces small alterations that later readers may mistake for additional detail rather than transmission errors.

### Why the hoax claim carries weight

The decisive challenge appeared years later. According to accounts citing investigator René Pacaut, Girardo’s widow said that her late husband had invented the encounter to fool or impress his friends and workmates. Later critical catalogues therefore labelled Bressuire a hoax rather than an unexplained landing. One reconstruction also says that the Deux-Sèvres edition of *La Nouvelle République du Centre-Ouest* discussed the joking nature of the story as early as 4 October 1954, although the archival article itself is not readily available in the public material examined here.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954bressuiref.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 3 octobre 1954, Bressuire, Deux-Sèvres...</span></span></span>

The hoax conclusion is persuasive but should still be described accurately. There is no surviving public confession from Girardo, and the key statement came through his widow or acquaintances after his death. It is therefore a reported admission, not a recorded first-person retraction. Even so, it is stronger negative evidence than the original case possesses positive evidence: the sighting had one witness, no photograph, no known physical trace, no independent observer and no documented official investigation.

Later reporting consequently weakened the claim rather than strengthening it. Nothing emerged to corroborate the alleged object or occupant, while the hoax testimony provided a straightforward reason why such a dramatic story had left no supporting evidence. Bressuire is best treated not as an unresolved close encounter, but as a probable fabrication preserved by repeated copying.

## A small cluster around 1980

By the late 1970s and early 1980s, French UFO reporting had entered a different phase. The state-backed investigation service established by the French space agency in 1977 collected witness statements and gendarmerie material, attempted conventional identifications and assigned classifications according to the quality and strangeness of each case. The surviving Deux-Sèvres files from this period are therefore more structured than the Bressuire narrative, although their evidence remains uneven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — S&#x27;il y a de fortes chances pour que ce phénomène puisse recevoir une explication rationnelle (satellites, lanterne...</span></span></span>

The official database lists reports at Beauvoir-sur-Niort and Saint-Maixent-l’École in 1978. The Beauvoir case received a category B classification, indicating that investigators considered a conventional explanation probable, while the Saint-Maixent report was placed in category C because reliable information was insufficient. The entries illustrate an important distinction: an unidentified report may be weak because too little was recorded, rather than because the observation resisted every plausible explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=32&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPANGeipan Groupe d’études et d’informations sur les phénomènes aérospatiaux non identifiés.... The most important local case came at Sainte-Soline on 16 August 1980. At approximately 3.30 am, a witness returning from a village dance reported a bright red object above a field. It was described as shaped like the cap of a mushroom, perhaps 15 metres across, silent and visible from an estimated distance of about 400 metres. The witness later thought it was above the village. Investigators recorded no marks on the ground or damage to vegetation, and GEIPAN continues to classify the case as category D — unexplained after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-08-01694" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINTE-SOLINE (79) 16.08.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Sainte-Soline differs sharply from Bressuire. It does not depend on a later chain of sensational retellings, and there is no reported confession that the witness invented it. Yet it remains evidentially limited. There was one known witness, no photograph, no radar return and no material trace. Estimates of size, distance and altitude were made at night, when a bright isolated light can appear nearer, larger or more mobile than it really is.

A second nocturnal report at Courlay followed in September 1980, contributing to the impression of a brief local concentration of sightings. The reports are sometimes treated as a Deux-Sèvres “flap”, but the term should not imply a coordinated or independently confirmed event. The surviving cases involved separate witnesses and different descriptions, with no established common source.

The 1980 period therefore occupies a middle position in the department’s history. It contains the strongest officially unresolved Deux-Sèvres report, but not a body of mutually supporting evidence for unusual craft. It is more accurately described as a small cluster of mixed-quality observations.

## Lanterns and projected light

Later Deux-Sèvres cases increasingly show how ordinary lights can acquire extraordinary apparent behaviour at night. GEIPAN’s database records an August 1997 Niort observation as category B and identifies the likely source as a “sky rose” — a powerful event or entertainment light projected upwards and reflected from cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=132&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIORT (79) --.08.1997 &#124; --/08/1997 &#124; B &#124; Deux-Sèvres &#124; Sky rose &#124; 17/06/2014 &#124; 1997-08-01... [N145] DE SAINT-PRIEST-LA-PLAINE (23) VERS...</span></span></span>

Such a projection can look like a luminous object moving inside or above the clouds. When several beams sweep in a pattern, witnesses may see circles, discs or patches that accelerate, reverse direction or disappear instantly. The apparent object has no visible connection to the ground because the beam itself may be difficult to see in clear air; only the illuminated patch on a low cloud layer is obvious. GEIPAN has used the same mechanism to explain other French cases in which moving white shapes appeared on overcast skies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-04-51431" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Niort identification matters because descriptions of extreme manoeuvring do not necessarily describe the movement of a solid object. A spotlight can cross a cloud base far faster than an aircraft, halt abruptly when the mechanism pauses and vanish when the beam moves into clear sky. The unusual motion is real as a visual effect, but the inferred craft is not.

A report made while travelling from Airvault towards Viennay on 6 September 2015 was classified as category B and attributed to a probable sky lantern. These small paper balloons carry a flame that heats the air inside them. From a distance they typically appear as orange or reddish points, drift silently with the wind and fade when the fuel burns out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=%2C51&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">R] de AIRVAULT (79) vers VIENNAY (79) 06.09.2015 &#124; 06/09/2015 &#124; B &#124; Deux-Sèvres &#124; Lanterne thai &#124; 22/06/2016 &#124; 2015-09-06...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2015</span></span></span>

Lanterns are particularly deceptive when witnesses cannot judge their distance. A nearby lantern may be interpreted as a large object far away; a group released together may look like a formation of aircraft. Turbulence can produce apparently irregular movement, while individual lights seem to vanish one after another as their burners expire. GEIPAN investigators compare the colour, duration and direction of travel with wind data and the timing of nearby celebrations before assigning this explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ladepeche.fr/2023/10/07/les-agents-du-geipan-les-detectives-des-ovnis-detaillent-trois-cas-de-phenomenes-celestes-fascinants-11501464.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ladepeche.fr">[ladepeche.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ladepeche.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Les agents du Geipan, les détectives des ovnis détaillent</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les agents du Geipan, les détectives des ovnis détaillent</span></span></span>

These modern identifications are not dismissals based merely on resemblance. Their strength comes from several details agreeing at once:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Colour:** warm orange or red light is characteristic of a flame-lit lantern.
* **Silence:** a drifting paper balloon has no engine.
* **Shared direction:** several lights normally move with the same prevailing wind.
* **Finite lifetime:** the light dims or disappears when the fuel is exhausted.
* **Event timing:** releases often coincide with weddings, festivals or private celebrations.
* **Cloud conditions:** moving discs or patches become plausible when low cloud can reflect a ground-based projector.

</div>

An explanation becomes weaker when these features do not fit. A light that travels repeatedly against the measured wind, remains visible far longer than a lantern’s burner should last or is observed in clear sky as a sharply defined structured object would require another hypothesis. The value of the modern approach lies in testing these details rather than assigning a familiar label automatically.

## How the stories changed

The contrast between Bressuire and the later cases is partly a contrast between two information systems. In 1954, a vivid newspaper story could quickly be copied into books and international catalogues. The retellings preserved the most dramatic elements — landed disc, humanoid, rapid departure — but rarely added independent evidence. Once catalogued, the story acquired an appearance of permanence even after investigators reported that it had been invented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954bressuiref.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 3 octobre 1954, Bressuire, Deux-Sèvres...</span></span></span>

Modern files are more likely to retain the observation date, location, classification and proposed phenomenon in a searchable official record. Investigators can compare witness accounts with weather conditions, astronomical positions, aviation data, satellite passages and public events. France’s space agency notes that most reported phenomena ultimately have rational candidates such as aircraft, balloons, lanterns or satellites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — S&#x27;il y a de fortes chances pour que ce phénomène puisse recevoir une explication rationnelle (satellites, lanterne...</span></span></span>

The change is not a simple journey from belief to disbelief. Witnesses in 2015 or 1997 could be entirely sincere and still mistake a lantern or projected light for something much stranger. Conversely, a case such as Sainte-Soline can remain unexplained without becoming evidence of an extraterrestrial craft. “Explained”, “unresolved” and “poorly documented” describe different evidential outcomes and should not be collapsed into a choice between hoax and alien visitation.

For Deux-Sèvres, the most defensible historical assessment is therefore:

* **Bressuire, 1954:** a dramatic but single-witness story, later substantially undermined by testimony that it was invented as a joke.
* **The late 1970s and 1980 period:** a small group of uneven cases, including the genuinely unresolved but weakly corroborated Sainte-Soline observation.
* **Niort, 1997:** a probable projected-light effect associated with event lighting.<span class="citation-chip-wrap"><a class="citation-chip" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=132&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIORT (79) --.08.1997 &#124; --/08/1997 &#124; B &#124; Deux-Sèvres &#124; Sky rose &#124; 17/06/2014 &#124; 1997-08-01... * **Airvault–Viennay, 2015:** a probable sky-lantern misidentification.

## The lasting lesson from Deux-Sèvres

The department’s UFO history is most revealing when the cases are compared rather than ranked by drama. Bressuire shows how a colourful story can survive through citation and repetition after its credibility has collapsed. Sainte-Soline shows the opposite problem: a sincere-looking report may remain unresolved simply because the available evidence is too narrow to reconstruct what happened. Niort and Airvault show how local lighting technology and celebratory objects can generate convincing aerial mysteries.

Later investigation has therefore weakened the idea that Deux-Sèvres experienced a succession of extraordinary craft or occupants. The surviving record instead contains a probable hoax, several ordinary misidentifications, reports too incomplete to assess and a small residual group that remains unexplained. That is less sensational than the humanoid story printed in 1954, but it is a clearer and more useful account of how UFO claims develop, circulate and change when new evidence is added.
