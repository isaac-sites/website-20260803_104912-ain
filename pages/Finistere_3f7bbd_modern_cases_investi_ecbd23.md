## Introduction

Two modern Finistère sightings show both the value and the limit of a serious UFO investigation. At La Forêt-Fouesnant in December 2018 and near Crozon in January 2020, GEIPAN investigators visited the locations, interviewed the witnesses in depth, reconstructed sight lines and tested explanations involving drones, aircraft, projected lights, windborne objects and local military activity. In neither case did they find an ordinary explanation that fitted all the reported details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFORET-FOUESNANT (LA) (29) 10.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That does not make either sighting evidence of an extraordinary craft. Both depended on a single witness, neither produced a photograph or video of the phenomenon, and important measurements such as distance and size were reconstructed after the event. Their real significance within Finistère’s UFO history is narrower but useful: they demonstrate how much investigators can eliminate while still being unable to establish what actually happened.

## The 2018 and 2020 witness accounts

### A luminous bar over La Forêt-Fouesnant

Before sunrise on 10 December 2018, a woman driving along the roughly 300-metre entrance to her rural home north of La Forêt-Fouesnant noticed a luminous form over a neighbouring field. She described a pale-yellow horizontal bar with an intense fluorescent-green circular light at its centre. According to her account, it moved slowly, turned towards her, remained apparently stationary for about 20 seconds and then changed direction before disappearing into mist. She opened the car windows but heard no sound from it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The complete sequence was not observed from one fixed position. The witness stopped her car three times while progressing along the drive, so her own movement complicated any estimate of the phenomenon’s course. During a later reconstruction, investigators timed the three phases at about 30 seconds each, giving a total discontinuous observation of roughly a minute and a half. They also measured the viewing directions and compared the apparent positions with fields, trees and the local horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The witness reported a very large apparent width when asked to reproduce it at arm’s length. Depending on an assumed distance, that estimate would imply an object ranging from several metres to hundreds of metres across. GEIPAN did not treat those calculated dimensions as dependable physical measurements. The witness acknowledged difficulty judging size, and investigators explicitly considered that she had overestimated the apparent width. This is an important restraint: the reconstruction made the report more precise, but it did not turn an impression of size into proof of a huge nearby object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The investigators considered her account coherent and information-rich. They found no evident prior fixation on UFOs and no reason to suspect deliberate fabrication. Even so, credibility is not the same as observational accuracy. A sincere witness can misjudge range, motion or scale in darkness, especially when the object has no known background against which to measure it. GEIPAN’s assessment therefore supported the honesty and internal consistency of the report, not the literal accuracy of every estimated dimension.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

### Three red rectangles near Crozon

The Crozon report occurred at about 7.48 am on 13 January 2020. A lone driver was travelling on an unlit rural road south of Crozon in heavy rain and strong gusting wind when he saw two bright red horizontal rectangles beyond or above a stand of pine trees. A third similar rectangle then appeared. He said the lights moved alongside his route, stopped near a junction and later departed southwards, first slowly and then with a rapid apparent acceleration towards the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

At their closest apparent position, the witness estimated that the three rectangles occupied about two-thirds of the width of his windscreen. He compared them to large, sharply outlined vehicle rear lights containing small moving points. No enclosing structure was visible. The engine was running, the windows were closed, rain was falling and wind was buffeting the vehicle, all of which reduced the evidential force of the reported silence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN began with a remote inquiry, followed by a cognitive interview, a site inspection and a visual reconstruction. A cognitive interview is designed to recover details by returning a witness mentally to the circumstances of an event without relying solely on leading questions. In this case, however, the original technical questionnaire was completed about five months after the sighting, and the field reconstruction took place roughly a year later. GEIPAN warned that such delays can alter or consolidate memories, even when the witness remains confident and coherent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

The Crozon account was nevertheless unusually detailed for a single-witness report. Investigators mapped the route, checked the terrain, modelled what could be visible from each position and examined the surrounding aviation, lighthouse and military environment. The result was a strong narrative record but no independent record of the lights themselves.

## Conventional explanations that were tested

### La Forêt-Fouesnant: the drone came closest

A drone was the leading conventional possibility at La Forêt-Fouesnant. A remotely piloted craft could, in principle, move slowly, hover and turn towards an observer. Its motor might also have been masked by the running car engine, so GEIPAN did not regard the absence of sound as decisive. Passing mist apparently obscuring the light also favoured some kind of physical object rather than a beam projected directly onto cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Several details weakened that hypothesis. The reported yellow-bar-and-green-centre appearance did not resemble ordinary drone navigation lighting. The very large apparent width was difficult to reconcile with a small recreational model, although that objection depends heavily on the unreliable size estimate. The flight would also have occurred before dawn on a Monday in a sparsely populated area, outside the ordinary rules then governing recreational night flying. Investigators noted a model-aircraft club about 11 kilometres away but found no evidence connecting it to the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN ultimately judged a drone the strongest hypothesis, but still well below the threshold at which it could be adopted as the probable explanation. That wording matters. It means “least unsatisfactory among those considered”, not “almost certainly a drone”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFORET-FOUESNANT (LA) (29) 10.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators also examined agricultural and entertainment lighting. They contacted the people farming the relevant fields, who checked their records and said neither they nor their machinery had been working there that morning. A powerful beam reflected from low cloud was geographically possible because cloud bases were reported at roughly 720 to 1,020 metres, but the nearest known nightclub was about eight kilometres away. More importantly, the witness described clean, stable edges and said wisps of mist passed in front of the phenomenon. A projected patch on cloud would normally be expected to look more diffuse and to remain associated with the cloud layer rather than appear locally hidden by nearer mist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A lantern or balloon carried by the wind was also considered. The initial direction was not wholly incompatible with the measured north-westerly wind, but the reported pause, pivot and subsequent change of direction were not. GEIPAN therefore regarded the full three-stage trajectory as a substantial objection to a passive windborne object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Astronomical causes were comparatively easy to reject. Venus was visible in another part of the sky, the Moon was below the horizon and the only relevant satellite passage was too faint and poorly matched to the reported form and motion. The sighting did not resemble a fixed star, planet, satellite or meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

### Crozon: military checks narrowed the field

Crozon presented a wider range of ordinary possibilities because the peninsula contains naval aviation facilities, maritime surveillance sites, lighthouses and restricted military areas. The nearby Lanvéoc-Poulmic naval air station was an obvious line of inquiry. GEIPAN consulted the base and was told that none of its aircraft was manoeuvring at the relevant time. The base also said it had not been conducting a drone exercise connected with the sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

That check materially weakens a local military-flight explanation, but it does not amount to a complete radar exclusion of every aircraft or drone in the region. Public cooperative flight-tracking records are incomplete for military traffic, and GEIPAN acknowledged that a drone from another base or a private operator could not be ruled out absolutely. Investigators judged it unlikely because three separate drones would have had to maintain a stable formation, appear unusually large and bright, and then reproduce the reported departure without recognisable navigation lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

Helicopters were tested for similar reasons. Lanvéoc-Poulmic operates rotary-wing aircraft, and several lights seen through rain could conceivably produce an unfamiliar pattern. Yet the witness’s large apparent angular size implied, on his own account, that the source was relatively close. Three nearby helicopters should have produced conspicuous engine and rotor noise, even allowing for the closed vehicle, rain and wind. Maintaining the reported spacing and rectangular appearance would also have been difficult. GEIPAN considered helicopters poorly compatible with the account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

The local geography made lighthouse or maritime lighting a serious alternative rather than an afterthought. Low clouds and rain can reflect or scatter bright beams, creating apparent shapes whose movement is partly caused by changing cloud. Investigators mapped nearby lighthouses and semaphore stations, inspected the area and examined their sectors and flash patterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

The active semaphore at Cap de la Chèvre did not emit the kind of powerful directional light required. The Toulinguet lighthouse had a red sector, but its orientation did not fit the observation. The red sector of the Morgat light was geographically more promising, yet its repeated occultations, diffuse beam and location did not reproduce three continuously visible, sharply bounded rectangles apparently moving together. GEIPAN also noted that the witness knew the area and would normally have been familiar with its lighthouse signals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

Wind-turbine obstruction lights across the Bay of Douarnenez were checked as another possible set of red points. Terrain modelling showed that some could theoretically be visible at low elevation, but they were about 18 kilometres away and did not fit the witness’s account of large rectangles moving from the trees to a much closer apparent position. Their fixed spacing and flashing behaviour were also a poor match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

Other proposals included a nightclub projector, an aircraft, internal reflections on the windscreen and vehicle lights or reflective panels on a lorry. Each could reproduce one part of the experience. Windscreen reflections might explain sharp red shapes that appeared to move with the car; a lorry could account for the resemblance to rear lamps; projected light could exploit the low cloud. None fitted the full reported sequence, particularly the first sighting through the trees, the apparently stationary formation at the junction and the departure southwards. GEIPAN rejected these hypotheses after comparing them with the reconstructed route and viewing geometry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

## Why both cases remained unresolved

Both investigations reached the same broad result by different routes: ordinary explanations were tested and weakened, but the remaining testimony was not strong enough to establish an extraordinary cause.

The most important limitation was the absence of independent observation. La Forêt-Fouesnant involved one driver in an isolated rural setting before dawn. Crozon likewise involved one person on a minor road in bad weather. No second witness supplied a separate direction, duration or description. Without two known viewpoints, investigators could not triangulate altitude, distance or speed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFORET-FOUESNANT (LA) (29) 10.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Neither witness photographed or recorded the phenomenon. The later site photographs and simulation material document where each person believed the lights had appeared; they do not depict the original event. Consequently, there is no image from which to test whether the shapes were physical outlines, glare, reflections, defocused lights or visual effects created by fog and rain.

Distance uncertainty affected almost every attempted explanation. A light that appears broad can be a small nearby source or a much larger distant one. Apparent hovering can arise when a distant object moves along the observer’s line of sight, while apparent acceleration can result from a turn, disappearance into cloud or a loss of visual reference. The investigators could reconstruct viewing angles, but not recover the missing range measurement.

Memory delay was particularly relevant at Crozon. The questionnaire arrived months after the event, and the most detailed reconstruction followed later still. That does not invalidate the testimony, but it lowers confidence in fine-grained features such as exact spacing, duration, apparent “ricochets” and the proportion of the windscreen occupied. GEIPAN explicitly retained this reservation in its conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

The La Forêt-Fouesnant account was reported and investigated more promptly, yet it also contained signs of normal perceptual uncertainty. The implied dimensions became implausibly large under many distance assumptions, and the witness admitted difficulty estimating scale. The investigators therefore used the size description as one factor among several rather than as hard evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s published case pages now place both sightings in its unresolved D grouping, while the underlying investigation reports used the more specific D1 description for cases that were strange and reasonably documented but lacked the exceptional corroboration expected of the strongest files. The La Forêt-Fouesnant page records an assessed strangeness of 0.70 and consistency of 0.68; Crozon is listed at 0.55 and 0.65 respectively. These are internal assessments of how unusual and information-rich the reports were, not probabilities that the objects were exotic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50686" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFORET-FOUESNANT (LA) (29) 10.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the investigations actually established

The two cases are strongest as studies in elimination rather than identification. At La Forêt-Fouesnant, investigators established that no known farm activity matched the event, astronomical sources were unsuitable, a passive balloon did not fit the reported turns and projected-light explanations struggled with the sharp outline and mist. A drone remained possible but insufficiently supported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R12.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At Crozon, investigators established that the nearby naval air station reported no relevant aircraft or drone exercise, that local lighthouse and semaphore systems did not reproduce the three red rectangles, and that helicopters, wind turbines and road vehicles each conflicted with significant parts of the reconstructed account. They did not establish that every civilian, military or optical source had been excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-12/CROZON%20%2829%29%2013.01.2020%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">CROZON (29) 13.01.2020 [E Cgei CR T,C,D S] compte rendu enquete</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModèle de document par défaut CNES version 2.0 Janvier 2010...</span></span></span>

Later reporting has not supplied the missing decisive evidence. No independent recording, additional witness or identified operator has emerged in the published files. The investigations therefore strengthened confidence that the witnesses described genuinely puzzling experiences, while weakening any claim that the reported sizes, distances and manoeuvres were objectively measured.

The appropriate verdict is unresolved but limited. These are not weak reports discarded for lack of detail, nor are they corroborated encounters supported by radar, photographs or several independent observers. They occupy the middle ground that makes Finistère’s modern UFO record instructive: detailed testimony survived substantial conventional checking, yet the evidential ceiling remained fixed by what was not captured at the time.

    });

    });

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
