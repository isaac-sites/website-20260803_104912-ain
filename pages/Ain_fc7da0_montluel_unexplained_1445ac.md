## Introduction

The Montluel sighting remains unexplained because investigators could reconstruct much of the witness’s viewing geometry, yet none of the conventional explanations tested matched the whole account. At about 3.10 am on 14 May 2022, a man on his balcony reported a large, dark rectangle moving silently across the southern sky. He said it followed a level path between two hills, briefly passed behind foreground landmarks and appeared to distort distant cloud around its edges. There was no photograph, second witness or confirmed radar return.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That combination makes Montluel an important case in Ain’s UFO history, but not proof of an extraordinary aircraft. Its value lies in the unusually detailed investigation of a short, single-witness observation. A site visit, terrain measurements, timing tests and checks of aviation, weather and perceptual explanations narrowed the possibilities without establishing what was actually seen. In March 2026, the French space agency’s investigation service, GEIPAN, classified the report as category D: unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the witness reported

The witness had been working at home during the night and went onto his balcony for a cigarette. From Montluel’s higher ground, the balcony overlooked the Rhône valley through a gap between hills. The view included the town’s church tower, nearby trees and, farther south, the general directions of Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport, the La Valbonne military camp and the Bugey nuclear site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

According to the initial technical questionnaire, completed three days later, the object appeared on his right and travelled from west to east. He described it as rectangular, anthracite grey and much darker than the moonlit night sky. It displayed no obvious navigation lights, emitted no visible trail and produced no sound. He initially estimated that it remained visible for about eight seconds and subtended roughly 20 by 40 millimetres when compared with an object held at arm’s length.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

During the later reconstruction, the account became more spatially specific. The witness said the rectangle first emerged near a tall fir tree, moved just above another tree and passed partly behind the upper section of the church spire. He then changed position along the balcony, briefly losing sight of it while putting down his cigarette. It continued behind further vegetation and finally disappeared behind the eastern hill without re-emerging. Investigators measured the apparent path between approximate azimuths of 153 and 101 degrees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Two reported details made the observation stranger than a simple dark silhouette. The witness described a faint border around the object which he struggled to characterise as light, and said distant clouds appeared deformed as it passed in front of them. His comparison with the visual distortion around a black hole expressed what the effect looked like to him; it was not evidence that any comparable physical process occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The weather did not immediately supply an explanation. Data from Lyon–Saint-Exupéry, around 15 kilometres away, indicated almost no wind, visibility of about 20 kilometres and generally clear conditions. The Moon was nearly full and low in the south-west, helping to brighten the sky behind a dark object. These conditions were compatible with seeing a silhouette, although they did not establish its distance or nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## How investigators reconstructed the flight

GEIPAN received the completed questionnaire on 17 May 2022. Radar information was requested in July that year, and an investigator visited the site on 1 July 2023. The final investigation report was dated February 2026, almost four years after the sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The reconstruction materially changed one important figure: duration. Although the witness’s first estimate was eight seconds, repeating his movements and timing the reconstructed passage produced a possible viewing time of roughly 20 to 28 seconds. This discrepancy matters because speed calculations are highly sensitive to duration. GEIPAN retained both ends of the range rather than treating either recollection as exact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Terrain offered a partial distance constraint that is absent from many night-sky reports. If the object really disappeared behind the final hill while maintaining the described straight course, it had to be beyond that ridge rather than close to the balcony. The investigation model placed its nearest distance at roughly one kilometre and its altitude at about 250 metres. These are conditional results: they follow from accepting the reported occlusion, trajectory and viewing angles, rather than from an independent measurement of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Investigators also tested the witness’s ability to judge apparent size. A comparison involving the full Moon suggested that he might overestimate angular size by approximately a factor of three. GEIPAN therefore calculated several possibilities rather than simply converting his original arm’s-length estimate into a fixed dimension. Under the minimum-distance model, the object’s estimated length ranged from about 15 metres after applying the correction to nearly 60 metres using the uncorrected initial estimate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Using a modelled travel distance of about one kilometre, the eight-second estimate implied roughly 450 kilometres per hour, whereas 28 seconds gave about 130 kilometres per hour. GEIPAN consequently described 15 metres and 130 kilometres per hour as minimum values within its chosen geometric scenario—not direct measurements of a confirmed craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding the case. Human observers cannot normally determine the distance, size and true speed of an unfamiliar object against the sky without a reliable reference. GEIPAN’s own methodology warns that witnesses may unknowingly infer distance first and then derive an apparently convincing speed from that assumption. Montluel is stronger than a wholly unconstrained light report because hills and foreground objects supplied references, but its numerical reconstruction still depends on the accuracy of one person’s memory and interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* Erreurs de perception issues de mécanismes instantanés (autocinétique) ou de constructions mentales (le témoin évalue une distance et e...</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary aircraft did not settle the case

The reconstructed dimensions and speed were not aerodynamically impossible. GEIPAN acknowledged that an object 15–60 metres long travelling at 130–450 kilometres per hour falls within the broad physical range of conventional aircraft. The problem was matching that possibility to the reported appearance: a dark, sharply rectangular form flying very low, silently and without visible lights. Investigators considered a large drone less plausible because the calculated minimum size was already substantial.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

No relevant aircraft appeared on the radar restitution supplied by the French air-defence organisation now known as CAPCODA. That absence weakened a straightforward aircraft identification, but it did not prove that nothing physical crossed the area. Radar coverage and display products do not guarantee detection of every low, small or poorly reflecting target, while the investigation report itself noted that the supplied maps contained false tracks and limited useful information. The safe conclusion is therefore that no supporting radar match was found, not that radar conclusively excluded every aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The nearby La Valbonne camp naturally encouraged speculation about military activity. GEIPAN found no persuasive link. The camp principally housed medical and artillery units rather than an aviation formation, and investigators could not identify equipment from its nearby firing area that would resemble the reported object or follow the reconstructed path. The witness’s suggestion of a camouflaged military aircraft remained speculation unsupported by a detected flight or a known matching platform.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other physical suggestions performed no better. A dirigible or balloon would struggle to account for the relatively rapid, level movement in almost calm conditions, while a low cloud or swarm of insects did not convincingly match the regular rectangular outline, apparent occlusion by landmarks and sustained directional travel. None was impossible in only one isolated respect; each failed to explain too many features at once.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Could the rectangle have been a visual afterimage?

The most developed sceptical explanation was negative retinal persistence: an afterimage produced after staring at a bright or contrasting shape. Before going outside, the witness had been working close to a large curved computer monitor in a darkened room, with rectangular windows displayed on the screen. A dark rectangular afterimage against the brighter night sky could therefore resemble the reported form. About 25–30 seconds elapsed between leaving the workstation and first noticing the object, a delay the investigator considered potentially compatible with a lingering visual effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This hypothesis also offered an economical explanation for why only one person saw the rectangle and why neither radar nor a camera recorded it. GEIPAN assigned it an investigative reliability score of 0.325—within the report’s “weak” range rather than dismissing it entirely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Several difficulties prevented it from becoming the preferred answer. The witness had followed the same screen-to-balcony routine for months without experiencing a comparable event. He did not notice the supposed afterimage while moving through the dark interior or lighting his cigarette. A retinal image should also move with the eyes, generally through gaze shifts rather than appearing to travel smoothly and independently across a fixed landscape. Most importantly, the witness said the rectangle passed behind the church spire, trees and the distant hill—depth relationships that an afterimage would not naturally reproduce.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The investigation could not fully test the idea. An experiment attempted during the site visit was compromised by different lighting conditions, and the witness did not complete requested repeat trials before the report closed. The claimed cloud distortion also remained difficult to fit, although that feature itself rested entirely on subjective visual testimony and could have resulted from contrast, memory or interpretation rather than an external physical effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A sleep-related experience was considered as well because the sighting occurred during the night. Investigators judged it unlikely: the witness was deliberately awake, had adopted a sustained nocturnal working pattern and reported no episode of falling asleep during his breaks. That assessment reduced the plausibility of a hypnagogic or hypnopompic image but could not independently verify his state of alertness at the exact moment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What remains uncertain

The case’s strongest feature is the witness’s cooperation. He reported promptly, supplied a reconstruction and allowed investigators to examine his home, work routine and viewing position. The terrain gave GEIPAN enough information to test a specific flight model, and the report openly documented contradictions such as the change from an eight-second recollection to a 20–28-second reconstruction. GEIPAN consequently rated the file’s consistency at 0.75 and its residual strangeness at 0.68.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-05-51350" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTLUEL (01) 14.05.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Its weaknesses are equally substantial. There was only one observer. The object itself was not photographed or filmed, although a picture of the scene was taken shortly afterwards. No independent witness, trace, radar track or other sensor confirmed its presence. The estimated length, altitude and speed depend on the object genuinely passing behind the hill and maintaining the path remembered during a reconstruction conducted 13 months later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Attempts at further corroboration did not close the gap. By the time the report was completed, investigators had received no response from Montluel’s municipality or from the organisation responsible for cameras at the Bugey nuclear site. The witness’s own internet search immediately after the event had found no matching reports. Later investigation therefore added analytical detail but no second source confirming that a large rectangular object had crossed the valley.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete817.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s category D decision should be read literally: the phenomenon remained unidentified with the evidence available. The classification system weighs both residual strangeness and the amount of usable information; it is not a declaration that an exotic craft existed. GEIPAN expressly distinguishes an unresolved observation from proof of any particular origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* La classification repose sur une évaluation quantitative et qualitative de deux paramètres: la consistance (C) de l’observation et l’é...</span></span></span>

For Ain, Montluel is notable because investigation strengthened the structure of the testimony without strengthening its independent verification. The reconstruction made a casual misidentification harder to specify, but it also exposed major uncertainty in duration and apparent size. The result is a genuinely unresolved report in the administrative sense: better documented than a vague light in the sky, weaker than a multi-witness or instrumented event, and still open to an unusual aircraft, a complex perceptual episode or another conventional cause that the available evidence could not recover.
