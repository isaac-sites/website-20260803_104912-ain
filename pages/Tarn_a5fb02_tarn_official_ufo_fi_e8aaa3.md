## Introduction

Official investigation changed the meaning of several Tarn UFO reports without necessarily challenging the honesty of the people involved. At Murat-sur-Vèbre, two witnesses described a large orange object with an unusual shape and apparent movement. A later review found that its direction matched the Moon to within roughly one or two degrees, leading GEIPAN, the French government’s unidentified-aerospace-phenomena unit, to classify the case as an identified lunar misperception. At Bertre, by contrast, investigators examined a dramatic light, alleged roof damage and collected samples, yet the surviving official record does not establish what caused the event. ## The Moon explanation at Murat-sur-Vèbre

In the early hours of 30 July 1978, a moped rider returning from a celebration near Castanet-le-Haut saw an immense orange glow while travelling through the Murat-sur-Vèbre area. A second person reportedly observed it from the same location. The sighting lasted about five minutes, and the witnesses described a complex object rather than an obvious astronomical body. Gendarmes questioned them on 1 August, but their local enquiries found neither additional witnesses nor traces on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00533" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMURAT-SUR-VEBRE (81) 30.07.1978La Lune rousse au levant ou au couchant est à l&#x27;origine de nombreuses méprises pour les témoins (voi...</span></span></span>

For years, a report such as this could retain much of its mystery because the original description sounded specific: an orange, structured form that appeared to sway and then disappear. The later GEIPAN review shifted attention away from the verbal image of an object and towards measurable circumstances. Investigators reconstructed the viewing direction and found that it corresponded to the Moon’s position to within about one or two degrees. The case is now classified **A**, meaning that GEIPAN regards the phenomenon as identified without significant ambiguity; the database names the cause as a reddish Moon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00533" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMURAT-SUR-VEBRE (81) 30.07.1978La Lune rousse au levant ou au couchant est à l&#x27;origine de nombreuses méprises pour les témoins (voi...</span></span></span>

That conclusion does not require the witnesses to have invented anything. A low Moon can look orange or red because its light passes through more of the atmosphere. Broken cloud may obscure its familiar circular outline while allowing irregular bright areas to remain visible. Those openings can seem to form domes, discs or other structured shapes. Moving cloud can also create an impression that the light itself is rocking, changing form or moving independently. GEIPAN specifically proposed this mechanism for the reported complex shape and apparent swaying at Murat-sur-Vèbre.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00533" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMURAT-SUR-VEBRE (81) 30.07.1978La Lune rousse au levant ou au couchant est à l&#x27;origine de nombreuses méprises pour les témoins (voi...</span></span></span>

The review also retained an important limitation. Weather records available nearly forty years later could not confirm whether the necessary cloud arrangement was present. The explanation therefore rests chiefly on the exceptionally close match between the reported direction and the Moon, combined with a well-known pattern of lunar misidentification. The missing cloud data weaken the reconstruction, but they do not outweigh the positional match in GEIPAN’s assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00533" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMURAT-SUR-VEBRE (81) 30.07.1978La Lune rousse au levant ou au couchant est à l&#x27;origine de nombreuses méprises pour les témoins (voi...</span></span></span>

Murat-sur-Vèbre is valuable within Tarn’s UFO history precisely because the original observation was not vague or obviously trivial. Two people saw something memorable, supplied a seemingly elaborate description and reported apparent motion. Yet once investigators tested the sightline rather than taking the perceived shape literally, an ordinary astronomical source became the strongest explanation. The case illustrates the difference between accurately reporting an experience and accurately identifying its physical cause.

## The unresolved roof-damage case at Bertre

The Bertre case, near Puylaurens, followed a very different investigative path. At about 4.30 am on 4 September 1989, a retired man was awakened by intense light passing through partly closed shutters. He went outside, saw a brightly illuminated area in a field below the house and then looked back towards the roof. There, he described a silent, stationary luminous form resembling a gas cylinder or bottle. It reportedly went dark after a short interval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete R2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete R2</span></span></span>

The report attracted unusual attention because it appeared to include physical effects. Gendarmes noted what they called signs of heated roof tiles. The witness also complained that the roof had developed serious leaks, requiring containers in the loft. Contemporary press accounts described displaced or discoloured tiles, localised loss of moss and a slight sag in part of the roof. Those reports made the incident seem more substantial than a light-only observation, although newspaper descriptions were not themselves laboratory findings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete R2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete R2</span></span></span>

SEPRA, then the CNES service responsible for such enquiries, conducted a site investigation on 12 September. It used a helicopter to inspect or photograph the roof and collected tiles and biological material, including moss. The investigator described the witness’s repeated account as broadly coherent and noted that he did not appear to be seeking a particular outcome. The report also recorded that national and regional press activity disrupted the enquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete R2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete R2</span></span></span>

The document’s conclusion was explicitly provisional. At that stage, SEPRA considered a helicopter, the Moon and a balloon unlikely explanations. It said that the remaining possibilities were deliberate fabrication or an unexplained phenomenon, and that analysis of the physical and biological samples would be needed to resolve the doubt. That wording is much narrower than later retellings suggesting that officials confirmed an unknown craft. Investigators had ruled out several initial ideas, not established a positive cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete R2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete R2</span></span></span>

The present GEIPAN entry classifies Bertre as **D**, describing an unexplained phenomenon of medium-to-strong consistency. The record was updated in June 2026; earlier listings had displayed the older D1 label. The current classification indicates that the available material has not produced a validated conventional explanation, but it does not identify the source of the light or prove a connection between the sighting and the roof’s condition.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/50191" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« LES TUILES » BERTRE (81) 04.09.1989 T1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The central evidential problem is that the publicly available file contains the provisional 1989 report, photographs, press material and witness records, but no clearly documented final laboratory result demonstrating that the tiles were altered by an external aerial phenomenon. The roof damage may be relevant, coincidental, pre-existing or inadequately characterised; the surviving published material does not settle the issue. An unexplained classification is therefore appropriate in the literal sense: investigators could not confidently reconstruct what happened.

Bertre should consequently be treated as unresolved rather than strong proof of something exotic. Its strengths are the prompt gendarmerie involvement, an on-site CNES investigation, collected samples and a witness account judged coherent. Its weaknesses are the single principal observer, uncertain weather information in the current database, no measured distance to the phenomenon, heavy media intrusion and the absence of a publicly demonstrated causal link between the light and the roof.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/50191" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« LES TUILES » BERTRE (81) 04.09.1989 T1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What official classifications really mean


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

The contrast between Murat-sur-Vèbre and Bertre becomes clearer once GEIPAN’s classification system is read as a statement about evidence, not a ranking of strangeness alone.

</div>

* **A** means that the observation has been explained without meaningful ambiguity. Murat-sur-Vèbre falls here because the reported direction closely matched the Moon and the visual effects were consistent with cloud-obscured lunar light.
* **B** means that an explanation is considered very probable, although not conclusively proved.
* **C** means that the report cannot be properly analysed because crucial information is missing, unreliable or contradictory.
* **D** means that no satisfactory explanation has been established despite evidence considered sufficiently substantial to examine. It does not mean that an extraordinary craft has been verified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN has repeatedly revised older files as methods, astronomical software and standards for judging witness consistency have improved. The organisation says that tighter definitions and deeper investigation have reduced the proportion of cases placed in the unexplained category. It also stresses that classifications other than a firmly identified A case may change if new records, comparisons or technical findings emerge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This matters because the same witness can be credible in one sense and mistaken in another. Investigators may find that a person is sincere, coherent and accurately recalls seeing an intense light, while rejecting the person’s interpretation of its size, distance or mechanical form. Human vision does not supply a direct measurement of an unfamiliar light against a dark or cloudy sky. With no reliable scale, a distant Moon, aircraft light or atmospheric source can be perceived as a nearby structured object.

Physical traces require a separate test. Damage found after a sighting is not automatically evidence that the observed phenomenon caused it. Investigators need a documented baseline, measurements, material analysis and a plausible mechanism connecting the two. Bertre attracted official interest because tiles and vegetation offered a potential route beyond testimony. Yet without a conclusive published analysis, the traces increase the case’s complexity rather than proving its interpretation.

## How the files changed Tarn’s UFO narrative

Official review weakened the extraordinary reading of Murat-sur-Vèbre. The case moved from a story about a large, strangely shaped orange object to a well-supported example of how the Moon can be misperceived under poor viewing conditions. Later investigation did not dismiss the witnesses; it separated their genuine visual experience from their inference that they had seen a nearby object.

Bertre was reframed differently. The official response elevated it above a casual anecdote by documenting the scene, interviewing the witness and taking samples. At the same time, the enquiry imposed limits that sensational press coverage often blurred. SEPRA’s own report was provisional, acknowledged fabrication as one possibility and made resolution dependent on later analysis. The present D classification preserves the absence of an answer, not a finding of alien or advanced technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R2.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete R2</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete R2</span></span></span>

The broader lesson for Tarn is that official files produce more than a simple division between “explained” and “unexplained”. They reveal three distinct outcomes: a credible conventional reconstruction, an evidentially adequate mystery and a report too weak to support firm conclusions. Murat-sur-Vèbre represents the first. Bertre represents the second, though with significant gaps. Many less detailed Tarn reports fall closer to the third.

That distinction is more useful than treating every surviving mystery as equally important. A case can remain unidentified because its evidence is genuinely puzzling, because decisive records have been lost, or because investigators lack enough information to test competing explanations. Official classification records the state of the enquiry. It does not certify the witness’s original interpretation, and it does not turn uncertainty into confirmation.
