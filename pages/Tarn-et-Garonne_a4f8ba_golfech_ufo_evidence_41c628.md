## Introduction

The Golfech case remains unresolved, but not in the strongest sense of that word. At 20:17 on 6 October 2010, two contractors outside a building at the Golfech nuclear power station in [Tarn-et-Garonne]({{ 'tarn-et-garonne/' | relative_url }}) reported a dark triangular phenomenon carrying three flashing white lights and a flashing red light near its centre. One witness recorded about 23 seconds on a mobile phone. An aircraft seemed the obvious explanation, yet GEIPAN—the French space agency unit that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena—later found that the available radar, geometry and witness estimates did not make an ordinary aircraft fit comfortably.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Tarn-et-Garonne_a4f8ba_golfech_ufo_evidence_41c628-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Golfech Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That did not make the event a confirmed unexplained craft. The recording failed to capture the claimed object clearly, the witnesses disagreed over its interpretation, important distance and altitude estimates were unreliable, and investigators lacked access to potentially decisive surveillance material. GEIPAN therefore classified Golfech as category C: unidentified because the information was insufficiently reliable, rather than category D for a phenomenon left unexplained after an adequately supported investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the two workers reported

The witnesses, identified publicly only as T1 and T2, were taking a break near an administrative building at the entrance to the power station. They described lights moving across the night sky and associated them with a black triangular form. According to GEIPAN’s final summary, the reported configuration consisted of three flashing white lights arranged around a flashing red light. Both witnesses said the phenomenon was silent, although investigators later noted that pumps, ventilation and other machinery created substantial background noise at the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

T1 gave the more extraordinary account. He described a large, sharply defined, matt-black triangular object, apparently very low and at times almost overhead. His estimates placed it only tens or hundreds of metres away, with an apparent width of roughly 10 to 15 degrees—many times the apparent diameter of the Moon. He reported acceleration and changes in direction but no engine noise, airflow, smell or environmental effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7459" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 T1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

T2 broadly agreed on the visible arrangement—a triangular appearance, white lights and a central red light—but was far less certain about what it represented. On the recording itself he repeatedly suggested that it might be an aircraft or a meteorological object. In his subsequent gendarmerie statement, he again identified it as an aircraft. During a later outside interview, however, he described a triangular object and said that the experience had disturbed him. GEIPAN regarded these shifts as a serious obstacle to judging his testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. The two men did not simply provide two independent, matching accounts of an extraordinary vehicle. They were together, reacted to each other in real time and strongly disagreed over the meaning of what they saw. T1 was already interested in UFOs; T2 later told the gendarmes that his colleague’s enthusiasm may have influenced the incident. Conversely, T2’s changing explanations may partly reflect a desire to distance himself from the publicity. The evidence does not establish which influence was stronger.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ladepeche.fr/article/2011/11/15/1215511-une-incroyable-histoire-d-ovni-au-dessus-de-la-centrale-de-golfech-fait-le-buzz-sur-internet.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ladepeche.fr">[ladepeche.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ladepeche.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Une histoire d&#x27;ovni à la centrale de Golfech fait le buzz sur internet</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Une histoire d&#x27;ovni à la centrale de Golfech fait le buzz sur internet</span></span></span>

## The video recorded the wrong lights

The mobile-phone film initially appeared to give Golfech an advantage over a testimony-only case. In practice, it became one of the main reasons for caution.

The file lasted just over 23 seconds and measured only 176 by 144 pixels, at fewer than eight frames per second with heavy compression. Frame-by-frame analysis found no detectable black triangular surface and no red light within the supposed triangle. Several visible light points remained fixed relative to one another when the camera moved, contradicting the idea that the recorded group itself was travelling continuously across the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN compared the filmed points with the power station’s lighting layout during its later site visit. The positions corresponded closely to fixed lamp posts. Investigators concluded that the prominent “triangle” visible in the video consisted of ground lights, not the aerial phenomenon described by the witnesses. The recording may contain a much fainter residual light higher in the frame, but the image quality was too poor to determine whether this was part of the reported event, a reflection within the camera or another artefact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This does not necessarily prove that nothing crossed the sky. GEIPAN reconstructed the cameraman’s position and found that he had aimed above the lamp posts, broadly where a moving phenomenon described by T1 might have passed. A slight panning motion also suggested that T2 was attempting to follow something. The agency proposed a plausible recording failure: unable to see the faint aerial lights on his phone screen, T2 may have centred the only visible lights—the lamp posts—while the actual phenomenon continued beyond them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The result is an unusual evidential split. The audio supports the fact that both men were reacting to something at the time, but the pictures do not verify its shape, altitude, speed or even its presence in the frame. T1 later treated the film as evidence for the triangle, although the technical analysis showed that the recorded triangle was unrelated to the claimed moving lights. GEIPAN warned that repeatedly viewing the footage may subsequently have influenced how the original sighting was remembered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why the aircraft theory caused problems

An aircraft remained the closest conventional match. Three white lights and a flashing red beacon can resemble normal aviation lighting, especially when perspective makes separated lights appear to define a triangular outline. The continuous industrial noise could also have masked an engine, and the witnesses may have perceived a dark surface between lights through a familiar visual effect in which the mind supplies a contour around isolated points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

T2’s immediate reaction strengthens this explanation. Before online debate, lengthy interviews or later reconstruction, he can be heard saying that it was an aircraft. His gendarmerie evidence maintained that interpretation. A conventional explanation therefore did not arise merely as an official attempt to dismiss an awkward report; it was proposed by one of the two observers during the event itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Yet the detailed investigation exposed difficulties. Military air-surveillance records reconstructed traffic around Agen airport, about 16 kilometres from Golfech. They showed local traffic around the relevant time and a high-altitude aircraft crossing the power station area at approximately 20:06, but no radar-detected aircraft that clearly matched the phenomenon at 20:17. A site visit also established that aircraft approaching Agen could not be seen from the witnesses’ precise position in the manner assumed during the initial inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

To reproduce T1’s reported angular size and proximity, an aircraft would have needed to be extremely low. GEIPAN calculated that one possible reconstruction put a Transall-sized aircraft at roughly 420 metres altitude and about 1.2 kilometres away. That was already problematic because a low flight over the protected nuclear site should have been detectable or reported. Accepting T1’s claim that the object passed nearly overhead made the fit worse: a transport aircraft at the implied range would have crossed the view rapidly and produced conspicuous noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN stressed that the altitude of isolated lights at night cannot be reliably determined without an independent distance reference. T1 and T2 supplied markedly different estimates, so calculations based on either account inherit that uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Nor does the sensitive location establish that the lights actually violated restricted airspace. ## Other explanations never became testable

GEIPAN considered a drone, an EDF maintenance device and a deliberate hoax, but none developed into a complete explanation.

A drone could account for low speed, low altitude and limited noise more readily than a full-sized aircraft. Against that, the reported light arrangement resembled standard aircraft lighting, while T1’s size estimates implied something much larger than a typical civilian drone available in 2010. Investigators could not identify a model, operator or operational reason for such a flight. Calling it a drone would therefore replace one unidentified vehicle with another rather than solve the case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

An EDF maintenance apparatus was suggested during the first inquiry because equipment operating around the site might have carried lights. The later investigation found no substantiated match. More importantly, no technical record was produced showing that such equipment was active along the reported path at 20:17. The hypothesis remained speculative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators also examined the possibility of a hoax because the video showed lamp posts, T1 had a pre-existing UFO interest and T2’s statements were inconsistent. GEIPAN nevertheless found reasons not to adopt that explanation. Both men risked professional consequences by making a false claim connected with a nuclear installation; their gendarmerie statements differed rather than presenting an obviously rehearsed story; and T1 later cooperated extensively with the inquiry. The final report treated deliberate deception as possible but unproved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The more economical interpretation is that the witnesses genuinely encountered ambiguous moving lights, then understood them differently. That inference fits the live audio, the agreement on a basic light pattern, T2’s immediate aircraft suggestion and the absence of usable imagery of a solid object. It does not establish what produced the lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Why GEIPAN classified the file as inadequate

GEIPAN’s final decision turned on the difference between **strangeness** and **consistency**. Strangeness measures how poorly a reported phenomenon matches familiar causes. Consistency reflects the quantity and reliability of the information supporting the account. Golfech scored moderately on both: the nearest conventional explanation had genuine problems, but the underlying evidence was too unstable to support a confident anomaly classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The agency identified several linked weaknesses:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the witnesses gave sharply different interpretations;
* T2’s position changed across the recording, police evidence and later interview;
* the video showed fixed lamp posts rather than the claimed aerial triangle;
* no reliable distance or altitude could be established;
* no photograph, video frame or physical trace demonstrated a solid black body;
* T2 could not be recontacted during the later investigation;
* investigators had no access to any power-station camera footage or details of its internal inquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The delay also mattered. GEIPAN began preliminary work soon after the sighting, but the decisive site visit did not take place until October 2013, three years later. Investigators could then measure sight lines, inspect noise levels and map the lamp posts, but they could no longer recover transient evidence such as complete surveillance recordings, staff recollections, equipment activity or additional witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The classification was not automatic. In April 2016, GEIPAN referred the borderline file to a panel of experts. Four supported category D1, judging the luminous phenomenon unexplained; eight chose category C because the inconsistencies prevented a consolidated conclusion. The majority decision placed Golfech in category C for insufficiently reliable information. That divided vote captures the case better than either “explained as an aircraft” or “officially unexplained UFO”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Did later investigation strengthen the claim?

Later work weakened some of the most dramatic claims while preserving a narrower mystery.

It weakened the case for a photographed triangular craft. Technical analysis established that the triangle visible in the mobile footage consisted of lamp posts. It also showed that no black structure or central red beacon could be recovered from the recording. The sighting therefore lost its apparent visual corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

It weakened confidence in precise claims about size and altitude. The witnesses’ estimates differed, and night-time light observations provide no dependable scale without a known reference. A phenomenon described as enormous and almost overhead might instead have been smaller and farther away. Once that uncertainty is admitted, aircraft and other ordinary-light configurations become less strained, even though no specific flight was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At the same time, the later inquiry weakened GEIPAN’s original “probable aircraft” classification. The site geometry did not support the assumed Agen approach path, and the available radar reconstruction contained no clear low-level aircraft matching the time and account. Investigators therefore could not retain aircraft as a sufficiently convincing identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

What survived was modest but real: two workers reacted contemporaneously to a configuration of moving, flashing lights; their basic descriptions overlapped despite their disagreement about its nature; and no recorded aircraft was shown to fit the report cleanly. ## The evidence gap is the case

Golfech occupies an important place in Tarn-et-Garonne’s UFO history because it demonstrates how a case can resist explanation without becoming strong evidence for an extraordinary object. The aircraft hypothesis encountered concrete objections, but every alternative depended even more heavily on uncertain testimony. The recording preserved the witnesses’ reactions while failing to preserve the phenomenon itself.

The missing evidence is unusually specific. A second usable recording, clear surveillance footage, an independently verified radar target, a third witness, reliable angular measurements or contemporaneous operational records could have shifted the assessment. None is publicly available. GEIPAN even recorded that it had received no information about possible power-station camera footage or the findings of any internal investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete86.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete86</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

For readers assessing the case, the most accurate conclusion is therefore neither “debunked” nor “confirmed unexplained”. The photographed triangle was effectively debunked as a group of fixed lights. The broader aerial observation was not. But because the surviving testimony cannot establish its distance, form or flight path, the failure to identify it does not carry the evidential force of a well-recorded anomaly. Category C is not a cautious synonym for an alien craft; it is a statement that the evidence gap is too large to decide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-10-02663" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGOLFECH (82) 06.10.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>
