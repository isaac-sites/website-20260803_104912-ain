## Introduction

Two [Seine-et-Marne]({{ 'which-seine-et-marne-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }}) sightings from 1994 remain more persuasive than most reports in the department’s official record. On 28 January, an Air France cockpit crew near Coulommiers saw an unfamiliar dark, changing form while an unidentified military-radar return was recorded in the wider area. On 24 June, three gendarmes near Ussy-sur-Marne reported a silent triangular object that apparently approached their patrol car before departing rapidly.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Seine-et-Marne_d85b24_unresolved_1994_case_7f48d4-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1994 Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Both cases are still classified as unexplained by GEIPAN, the investigation service of France’s space agency, CNES. Their importance lies in the quality of the witnesses, the existence of formal records and, in the Air France case, instrumental data. Yet neither case provides proof of an extraordinary aircraft, much less an extraterrestrial one. The radar contact cannot be securely matched to the object seen by the pilots, while the gendarmes’ account has no independent sensor record, photograph or outside witness. They are strong unresolved reports rather than solved mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The Air France encounter near Coulommiers

At 13:14 on 28 January 1994, Air France flight AF3532, an Airbus operating between Nice and London, was passing over the Coulommiers area. A chief steward who was in the cockpit noticed something to the left of the aircraft and initially suggested that it might be a weather balloon. The captain and co-pilot then observed it themselves, describing a brown or dark-red form that appeared to alter its shape before disappearing abruptly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

An air-defence chronology produced from information received that afternoon described a dark, bell-shaped object perhaps 10 to 20 nautical miles away and about 2,000 feet below the aircraft. It was reportedly visible for roughly a minute. Later estimates of its dimensions were extremely large, but those figures depended on an uncertain distance: without a known range, apparent angular size cannot be converted reliably into physical size.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Chronologie%20CODA.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Chronologie CODA</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Chronologie CODA</span></span></span>

The crew contacted air traffic control at Reims and were told that no known traffic was present in the relevant sector. The episode therefore cannot easily be dismissed as a straightforward encounter with another scheduled aircraft. This is one reason it has remained prominent among French aviation reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### The radar return: valuable but not a confirmation

The case’s strongest evidence is an unidentified return recorded by the military radar at Cinq-Mars-la-Pile. According to GEIPAN’s current summary, the track lasted about 50 seconds, crossed the path of AF3532, corresponded to no filed flight plan and vanished at approximately the same time as the visual phenomenon. Radar information of this kind is more valuable than a witness account alone because it may indicate that some physical reflector was present in the airspace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The crucial qualification is that investigators did **not** establish a secure visual-radar correlation. The radar track’s position and movement were not fully consistent with the object described from the cockpit. GEIPAN consequently warns that the two observations may have involved separate things. The radar evidence strengthens the case by showing unexplained activity near the aircraft’s route, but it does not prove that the radar was tracking the form seen by the crew.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because the case is sometimes retold as a perfectly matched radar-and-visual encounter. The official assessment is more cautious. There was an unusual visual report and an unidentified radar track with a suggestive temporal and geographical relationship, but the available data do not allow them to be fused into one certain event.

### Why a weather balloon was investigated

The steward’s initial suggestion made a balloon an obvious possibility. GEIPAN later asked Météo-France to reconstruct the course of the radiosonde launched from Trappes that day. The balloon was released at 12:18 local time and, at 13:14, had reached about 17,400 metres near Joigny, well to the south-east of the Air France aircraft. It did not burst until approximately 13:48.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Meteo%20radiosondage-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Objet...</span></span></span>

Météo-France also noted that high-altitude winds could deform a balloon, potentially accounting for an apparently changing outline. However, the calculated location did not match the sighting, and even a greatly expanded meteorological balloon would have been only a few metres across rather than the hundreds of metres suggested by the witnesses’ most dramatic estimates. GEIPAN therefore rejected that particular radiosonde as a satisfactory explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Meteo%20radiosondage-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Objet...</span></span></span>

A loose or damaged balloon envelope remains theoretically possible, as does an unusual atmospheric optical effect, but GEIPAN has found no evidence firm enough to support either proposal. These are residual possibilities, not established solutions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### The delayed testimony weakens the file

Despite the professional setting, AF3532 is not a flawless case. The captain apparently received an instruction to file a gendarmerie report, yet the principal formal witness statements were not made until about three years later, after the incident had attracted press attention. Delay creates opportunities for memory to become simplified, enlarged or unconsciously influenced by discussion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The steward who first noticed the phenomenon did not provide a published testimony, removing an important independent perspective. GEIPAN nevertheless found the captain’s and co-pilot’s accounts broadly coherent. Its current classification is D1: unexplained, moderately well supported and markedly strange, but weakened by late reporting, the missing steward’s statement and the uncertain relationship between the radar track and the visual sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Later investigation therefore produced a mixed result. It weakened the popular claim of unambiguous radar confirmation, but it also eliminated the specific radiosonde that seemed the most obvious conventional candidate. The case became more precisely unresolved rather than either confirmed or debunked.

## The gendarmes’ triangular-light report near Ussy-sur-Marne

At about 02:40 on 24 June 1994, three gendarmes on night patrol were travelling along the D3E towards Changis-sur-Marne. Near Ussy-sur-Marne, lights above the A4 motorway drew their attention. As the patrol approached, the lights appeared to resolve into three yellow-white points arranged as a triangle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/52019" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANUSSY-SUR-MARNE (77) 24.06.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The officers described a dark triangular form with a luminous centre and its point directed downwards. They could not determine its altitude or dimensions—an important limitation—but believed that it was initially stationary. After they stopped, it reportedly moved slowly towards them while maintaining roughly the same height, then stopped above or very close to their vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

When the patrol resumed its journey, the object was said to have moved westwards, passed above them and accelerated away so rapidly that only a faint point remained on the horizon. The officers reported no engine noise, smoke or smell, despite having the car windows open on a warm night. Their account placed the total observation at about ten minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/52019" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANUSSY-SUR-MARNE (77) 24.06.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### Why the police record carries weight

The strongest element is the documentary chain. The witnesses were serving gendarmes engaged in routine patrol duties, and the surviving file is a National Gendarmerie report rather than a story reconstructed solely through later UFO publications. It includes their narrative, a diagram of three lights joined by a triangular outline, a map of the patrol’s route and a requested Météo-France assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Professional status does not make anyone immune to visual error, especially at night. It does, however, give this report greater evidential value than an anonymous or casually recorded observation. Gendarmes are accustomed to recording locations, sequences and unusual incidents, and the account came from three officers sharing the same prolonged experience.

France’s system is specifically designed to preserve reports of this sort. The gendarmerie collects statements and local information before passing relevant files to GEIPAN, which may then consult aviation, military, meteorological and scientific partners. That process does not guarantee that a report is accurate, but it produces a more traceable record than most privately collected UFO testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

### The communications failure is suggestive, not measured evidence

The officers also reported that their vehicle’s radio equipment and portable set stopped working during the observation. They could not state exactly when the failure began or ended. This detail is often presented as evidence of an electromagnetic effect caused by the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The surviving file does not document a technical inspection, recorded voltage disturbance, fault log or timed comparison linking the failure to the object’s movements. It therefore establishes that the officers noticed a communications problem, not that an external field disabled their equipment. An ordinary radio fault, reception gap or power problem cannot be excluded from the available material.

This is a broader lesson in evaluating physical-effect claims. A malfunction reported during a strange sighting is relevant, but it becomes strong evidence only when the failure is independently recorded, technically examined and shown to coincide closely with the phenomenon. None of those additional steps is visible in the published Ussy-sur-Marne file.

### Weather checks did not identify a cause

Météo-France described the sky that night as becoming clear to partly cloudy, with no precipitation, light to moderate easterly winds and temperatures falling from about 20–21°C to 13–14°C. Nothing in that summary provides an obvious meteorological explanation for three structured lights apparently moving relative to the patrol.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

However, the absence of an obvious weather explanation does not establish that a solid triangular craft was present. At night, three separated lights can create an inferred outline even when the space between them is empty. Distance and altitude are particularly difficult to judge without a known background, and apparent hovering or sudden acceleration can result when a distant light changes direction, intensity or visibility.

Aircraft remain a general possibility for triangular light arrangements, but the reported silence, prolonged stationary appearance, close approach and very rapid departure make a routine aircraft identification difficult to fit. No specific aircraft, military operation or other source has been matched to the event in the public investigation. GEIPAN continues to list it as a D case: unexplained after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/52019" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANUSSY-SUR-MARNE (77) 24.06.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the two cases actually establish

The 1994 reports stand apart within Seine-et-Marne because each contains more than a brief personal recollection. AF3532 combines two trained flight-crew witnesses, contact with air traffic control, a documented military-radar anomaly and a later meteorological reconstruction. Ussy-sur-Marne combines three professional witnesses, a formal police record, a ten-minute sequence, diagrams, a route map and a weather enquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Their strengths are nevertheless different. The Air France case has instrumental information, but the radar return may not represent the observed phenomenon. The Ussy-sur-Marne case has a more immediate and detailed official narrative, but no independent instrument, photograph or unrelated witness confirms what the gendarmes perceived.

A balanced reading separates three propositions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Something unusual was reported:** strongly supported in both cases by identifiable professional witnesses and surviving official documentation.
* **A conventional source has not been demonstrated:** also supported; the investigated radiosonde does not fit AF3532, while no specific aircraft or atmospheric explanation has been established for Ussy-sur-Marne.
* **An extraordinary vehicle was objectively present:** not proved. The radar-visual match is uncertain, physical dimensions were not measured, the reported radio failure was not technically documented, and neither event produced imagery, recoverable material or fully independent corroboration.

</div>

GEIPAN’s classification reflects that middle position. A D or D1 case is unresolved because the available evidence has resisted a satisfactory identification; it is not an official finding that an exotic craft existed. CNES reports that only a small minority of all investigated French cases remain unidentified after investigation, while many others are explained, probably explained or left indeterminate because the data are inadequate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan« AF3532 » [AERO AFR] COULOMMIERS (77) 28.01.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

For Seine-et-Marne’s UFO history, the importance of 1994 is therefore not that it produced proof of visitors or secret technology. It produced two unusually durable evidence problems. Ordinary explanations remain possible, but none has yet accounted cleanly for all the reported details. At the same time, gaps in correlation, measurement and independent confirmation prevent the cases from carrying the larger conclusions sometimes built upon them.
