## Introduction

Some of [Loiret]({{ 'which-loiret-ufo-cases-still-resist/' | relative_url }})’s most dramatic aerial reports did not collapse because witnesses were dishonest or careless. They gained ordinary explanations when investigators reconstructed where the observer was looking, how the observer and light were moving, what the weather allowed them to see and which astronomical or aviation objects were present at the time.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Loiret_6d08aa_loiret_explained_sig_5d9565-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Explained Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The clearest example is the red object reported near Saint-Florent in June 1979. Four frightened witnesses believed that a strangely shaped light was descending towards the road. A later review found that its colour, position, shape and apparent descent matched the setting Moon. Other Loiret cases have been attributed to military aircraft, distress flares, balloons and probable atmospheric re-entry. Together, they show why a sincere description can be accurate in its basic details yet mistaken in its interpretation. They also provide a useful standard for judging the department’s genuinely unresolved reports: an explanation must fit the complete observation, not merely resemble one part of it.

## The Saint-Florent object that proved to be the Moon

At about 12.30 am on 30 June 1979, four people were travelling along the D54 between Saint-Florent and Villemurlin when they noticed a red phenomenon ahead of them. Different witnesses described it as a banana shape with upturned ends or as a drop. It appeared to descend towards the road and then move slowly. No engine noise or flashing navigation lights were reported. Alarmed, the group turned back towards Coullons and contacted the gendarmerie.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The initial investigation appeared to uncover a suggestive physical clue. At the apparent location of the phenomenon, gendarmes found the remains of a brushwood fire, still warm, which farmers had lit two days earlier. They considered whether glowing embers lifted by the air might have produced the red, pear-shaped light. Yet that theory did not fit the observation particularly well. A nearby fire could explain colour and perhaps shape, but not convincingly reproduce the object’s reported position and slow apparent movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The stronger explanation emerged when the old case was reconstructed with improved astronomical software. The Moon was setting almost exactly along the direction of the road, at an azimuth of about 281 degrees. Its low position explained why the witnesses saw the light ahead rather than high in the sky. Its reddened appearance was normal for a Moon close to the horizon, where its light passes through more atmosphere. The visible crescent also matched the witnesses’ banana-like outline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The reported “descent” was especially revealing. A celestial object nearing the horizon genuinely moves downwards over time, but the witnesses interpreted that slow astronomical motion as a nearby object approaching the landscape. Trees beside the road then hid the Moon, creating the impression that the phenomenon had disappeared behind local vegetation. GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that examines unusual aerospace reports, ultimately classified the case as a definite misidentification of the setting Moon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This finding does not mean that the witnesses invented the shape, colour or movement. On the contrary, several of those details were real. The error lay in distance and identity. A familiar object hundreds of thousands of kilometres away was mentally placed near the road because darkness provided few reliable depth cues.

## Why movement, darkness and fear distort interpretation

Many explained Loiret cases occurred while the witness was driving. That matters because the observer’s own motion alters the apparent motion of lights outside the vehicle. A distant object may seem to follow the road, stop when the car stops or accelerate when its viewing angle changes. Rain, windscreens, reflections and roadside trees further interrupt the view.

The Saint-Florent witnesses were travelling at night, became frightened and made decisions while trying to understand an unfamiliar-looking light. GEIPAN’s reassessment explicitly distinguished between seeing and interpretation: the witnesses’ visual report was not necessarily defective, but fatigue, driving and fear influenced what they thought the light represented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A later A19 sighting illustrates the same mechanism with an aircraft. At about 9.30 pm on 15 February 2018, a driver between Beaune-la-Rolande and Chilleurs-aux-Bois reported a triangular form with red, white and apparently pale-blue lights. The object seemed to make two unusual turns, cross the motorway and then travel alongside it before disappearing into cloud. The witness opened a window but heard no engine noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-02-50500?field_date_value=2023-02-16&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[A19] de BEAUNE-LA-ROLANDE (45) vers CHILLEURS-AUX-BOIS (45) 15.02.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Those details sounded stranger when described as the behaviour of an unknown object than when reconstructed as the view of a conventional aircraft from a moving car. The witness was driving at roughly 130 kilometres per hour, in darkness and active rain, with the windscreen wipers operating. He was also overtaking another vehicle during part of the sighting. Such conditions made it difficult to judge shape, distance and sound.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-02-50500?field_date_value=2023-02-16&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[A19] de BEAUNE-LA-ROLANDE (45) vers CHILLEURS-AUX-BOIS (45) 15.02.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN found that the light colours corresponded broadly to aviation lighting and that the object’s final direction pointed towards Orléans-Bricy air base, about 30 to 40 kilometres away. The changing apparent speed could be explained by the aircraft turning while the observer’s car also moved. A slow-moving military transport aircraft, possibly a C-130 Hercules, was therefore considered the probable source. The investigation could not confirm a particular flight because too much time had passed, so the case was placed in category B—probably explained—rather than treated as certain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-02-50500?field_date_value=2023-02-16&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[A19] de BEAUNE-LA-ROLANDE (45) vers CHILLEURS-AUX-BOIS (45) 15.02.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That distinction is important. An ordinary explanation can be strong without being complete. The investigators acknowledged a small mismatch concerning the reported number of red lights. They nevertheless judged the aircraft theory to fit the overall combination of direction, lighting, movement, weather and nearby military activity much better than an unknown craft.

## When a triangle was really an A400M

A more conclusive aircraft identification followed a sighting on 21 October 2021. A motorist travelling from Chevilly towards Sougy noticed a small green light moving slowly at low apparent altitude. It then seemed to accelerate suddenly. The witness stopped, left the vehicle and obtained a photograph, but described the object as silent and apparently triangular.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60144?page=%2C30&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D6] DE CHEVILLY (45) VERS SOUGY (45) 21.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators traced the report to a military Airbus A400M associated with Orléans-Bricy air base. Its navigation lights matched the reported colours, and its position was consistent with an aircraft approaching the base after low-altitude training. The sudden acceleration was apparent rather than real: as the aircraft turned, its motion became more sideways across the witness’s field of view, making its angular speed seem greater.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60144?page=%2C30&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D6] DE CHEVILLY (45) VERS SOUGY (45) 21.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The estimate of distance had been far more dramatic than the reconstruction. The witness initially thought the object might have been only about 30 metres away. GEIPAN calculated that it was probably around 3.6 kilometres distant. At that range, an aircraft can appear compact and its structural details can disappear into the dark, leaving only a simple shape inferred from the arrangement of lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60144?page=%2C30&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D6] DE CHEVILLY (45) VERS SOUGY (45) 21.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The absence of noise also ceased to be mysterious. Although an A400M is a loud aircraft, sound depends on distance, wind and the aircraft’s direction of travel. The wind was blowing away from the witness rather than carrying engine noise towards him. A low viewing angle also hid parts of the aircraft and some of its lights, encouraging the brain to complete the visible pattern as a triangle. GEIPAN classified the case in category A as an identified A400M transport aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/60144?page=%2C30&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D6] DE CHEVILLY (45) VERS SOUGY (45) 21.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This case demonstrates how “triangular object” need not describe the physical outline of anything. At night, observers often see points of light more clearly than the dark structure connecting them. The perceived shape may therefore be a mental outline drawn between lights rather than a directly observed solid craft.

## Flares, re-entry and other short-lived lights

Not every explained Loiret report involved the Moon or an aircraft. On 9 January 2020, reports from the department concerned luminous objects that GEIPAN judged were probably distress flares. Flares can appear to hover, descend unusually slowly or glow with an intense colour because they are designed to remain visible while falling beneath a parachute. The case was classified B, indicating a likely rather than absolutely demonstrated identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=enquete&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8%2C24&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">D5E4] DE PORGE (LE) (33) VERS SAUMOS (33) 07.10.2021 &#124; 07/10/2021 &#124; B &#124; Gironde &#124; Trainée de condensation &#124; 19/07/2024 &#124; 2021-10-07BOGNY...</span></span></span>

The explanatory value of a flare does not come merely from saying that it is “a light in the sky”. Investigators must look for the characteristic combination of ignition, sustained brightness, gradual descent, extinguishing and any grouping or repetition. Without those features, “flare” would be no more than a convenient label.

A different kind of dramatic event was reported near Puiseaux on 8 October 2002. At about 10.20 pm, witnesses in different places saw an exceptionally bright light followed by an object apparently falling along a path inclined at roughly 45 degrees. GEIPAN classified the report as a probable atmospheric re-entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2002-10-01594?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=91&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPUISEAUX (45) 08.10.2002 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Re-entry events and bright meteors can create reports across a wide area because observers separated by many kilometres see the same high-altitude phenomenon. The apparent fall may look close to each witness even though it is taking place far away. Fragmentation, persistent trails and changes in brightness can also make a natural or human-made object entering the atmosphere resemble a formation of independently manoeuvring lights.

The geographical spread of reports can therefore strengthen an ordinary explanation rather than an exotic one. Several observations at the same time, from different locations and along a compatible direction, can allow investigators to reconstruct a single distant trajectory. By contrast, one witness describing a low object near a particular field may initially make the event sound local and much closer than it was.

More recent entries in GEIPAN’s Loiret records continue the same pattern. A March 2025 report from Saint-Pryvé-Saint-Mesmin was classified as a probable illuminated balloon. Such cases show how new consumer objects—particularly balloons fitted with light-emitting diodes—can produce unfamiliar combinations of slow drift, changing brightness and apparent hovering even though the underlying mechanism is simple.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-PRYVE-SAINT-MESMIN (45) 09.03.2025 &#124; 09/03/2025 &#124; B &#124; Loiret &#124; Ballon LED &#124; 24/06/2025 &#124; 2025-03-09... ## Why stars and planets can seem to move

The same visual conditions that transformed the Moon at Saint-Florent can make bright stars or planets seem like active objects. A point of light near the horizon may flicker, change colour or appear to make small movements because turbulent layers of air continually bend its light. Thin cloud and moisture can enlarge or soften the image.

The autokinetic effect adds another difficulty. When a person stares at a small stationary light against a dark background with no fixed reference points, tiny involuntary eye movements can make the light appear to wander. Once an observer expects movement, later impressions of zigzagging or pursuit may feel compelling even though the source remains stationary.

Driving intensifies the illusion. A bright star visible through gaps in trees may repeatedly disappear and reappear. Because it remains in roughly the same direction while nearby objects rush past, it may seem to keep pace with the car. A turn in the road can then cause the “following” light to swing abruptly to one side or vanish.

This is why an astronomical explanation should be tested rather than casually asserted. The object must have been above the horizon at the stated time, in the reported direction and at a brightness compatible with the account. The Saint-Florent case became convincing because all those factors aligned—not simply because the Moon can sometimes be mistaken for something unusual.

## How an explanation becomes persuasive

The strongest explained cases in Loiret were resolved through several independent matches. Investigators compared the testimony with astronomical positions, aircraft routes, military activity, weather, wind, viewing direction and the observer’s own movement. A successful explanation accounted for the strange features as well as the ordinary ones.

Several recurring tests are particularly useful:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Position:** Was the proposed Moon, aircraft or flare actually where the witness was looking?
* **Timing:** Did it appear, turn, descend or disappear when the known object should have done so?
* **Appearance:** Do its colours and light pattern fit the description?
* **Apparent motion:** Can the observer’s movement, a turn or changing viewing angle account for acceleration or hovering?
* **Sound:** Was the source distant, masked by the vehicle or downwind from the observer?
* **Independent confirmation:** Do photographs, flight information or reports from other locations support the same reconstruction?
* **Residual problems:** Does the explanation leave major details unaccounted for, or only minor discrepancies expected from a brief night-time observation?

</div>

This approach prevents two opposite mistakes. One is accepting a report as extraordinary because it initially sounds vivid. The other is declaring it solved merely because a familiar object could produce a vaguely similar light. The A19 case remained only probable because no specific flight was confirmed. The Chevilly–Sougy case was stronger because investigators could connect the lights, route and movement to an A400M near Orléans-Bricy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-02-50500?field_date_value=2023-02-16&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[A19] de BEAUNE-LA-ROLANDE (45) vers CHILLEURS-AUX-BOIS (45) 15.02.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What explained cases reveal about unresolved reports

Explained sightings do not prove that every Loiret report has the same kind of answer. They show instead what a satisfactory answer should accomplish. It must explain the event as experienced by the witness: why it looked close, why it appeared silent, why its shape seemed unusual and why its movement felt purposeful.

They also demonstrate that witness sincerity and mistaken identification are entirely compatible. Four people can agree that they saw a red, descending object and still be looking at the Moon. A motorist can photograph a real green light, correctly remember its changes in apparent speed and nevertheless underestimate its distance by several kilometres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00635" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-FLORENT (45) 30.06.1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Conversely, an unresolved classification does not mean that an extraordinary craft has been established. It may mean that records are missing, the report was submitted too late, weather data are incomplete or no proposed explanation fits every important detail. GEIPAN’s classification system therefore separates identified cases, probably explained cases, cases lacking usable information and genuinely unresolved reports rather than treating all unidentified observations as one category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-PRYVE-SAINT-MESMIN (45) 09.03.2025 &#124; 09/03/2025 &#124; B &#124; Loiret &#124; Ballon LED &#124; 24/06/2025 &#124; 2025-03-09... AVESNES-LES-AUBERT (59) 01...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 9, 2025</span></span></span>

For Loiret’s UFO history, the explained cases are more than a collection of debunkings. They reveal the mechanisms that repeatedly create mystery in the department: long rural sightlines, dark roads, moving observers, low astronomical objects and military aircraft operating near Orléans-Bricy. Those mechanisms help narrow the field when more difficult reports are assessed. They remove cases that can be reconstructed conventionally while leaving the genuinely resistant ones to be judged on their own evidence.
