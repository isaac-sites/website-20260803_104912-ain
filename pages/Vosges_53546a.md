That contrast matters. Vosges offers a useful department-level case study in how sincere testimony can describe something genuinely alarming without establishing an exotic craft. It also shows why “unidentified” may mean either that a phenomenon resisted investigation or simply that too little reliable information survived to identify it.

## The strongest unresolved case

On 12 November 1992, at about 5 pm, a motorist travelling on the D23 from Sapois towards Vagney reported a large red, flashing mass approaching the vehicle rapidly and without audible noise. The encounter lasted only a few seconds, but the witness estimated that the phenomenon passed within about 50 metres. GEIPAN describes the report as an observation of a red mass coming very close to a vehicle and retains it in category D: unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1992-11-01273" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D23] de SAPOIS (88) vers VAGNEY (88) 12.11.1992 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is important within Vosges because it combines several features usually regarded as making a report more interesting: apparent proximity, movement towards the witness, silence and a strong emotional reaction. It is not merely a distant point of light. On the other hand, the official public summary contains no photograph, radar record, physical trace or independent witness account. The estimated distance and size therefore depend on a brief observation made by a frightened driver, circumstances in which perception can be especially uncertain.

A category D ruling should not be read as confirmation of an extraordinary vehicle. GEIPAN defines category D as a phenomenon that remains unidentified after investigation, while category C means that identification is prevented by missing or inadequate information. Its assessment considers both the residual strangeness of a report and the consistency, quantity and reliability of the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The Sapois–Vagney report is therefore the clearest unresolved entry in the department’s official record, but it is not a landmark case supported by instrumentation or multiple independent observers. Its status is best expressed narrowly: something unusual was reported, and the surviving evidence has not produced a satisfactory identification.

## The Épinal roadside encounter of 1976

An earlier report from Épinal has some of the same close-range, road-encounter character. At around 4.30 am on 10 July 1976, a motorist said that an unfamiliar craft was flying at low altitude above the road ahead. The witness described a bright red object about four or five metres long and roughly one metre in diameter, with a black band along its length, a raised cabin-like feature and a flashing light. After the driver stopped, the object appeared to reverse towards the car, prompting the witness to leave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/46896?customGetLattitude=46.94358292648825&amp;customGetLongitude=4.4989013671875&amp;customGetZoom=7&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=49.11702904077932&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.77013681219717&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.668457031250001&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=1.329345703125&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=57&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EPINAL (88) 10.07.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The description is vivid, but the official file illustrates the weakness of a detailed story unsupported by follow-up evidence. GEIPAN records that the gendarmerie obtained no additional information, and the case is classified C because reliable data are lacking. There is no publicly documented second witness, photograph, identified flight, measured trajectory or physical effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/46896?customGetLattitude=46.94358292648825&amp;customGetLongitude=4.4989013671875&amp;customGetZoom=7&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=49.11702904077932&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.77013681219717&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.668457031250001&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=1.329345703125&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=57&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EPINAL (88) 10.07.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

It is tempting to treat the witness’s estimate of dimensions as evidence that the object was nearby. In reality, an observer cannot reliably calculate the physical size or distance of an unfamiliar airborne light without a known scale. A small nearby object, a larger distant aircraft and a light seen through haze can produce similar angular impressions. GEIPAN itself stresses that witnesses frequently infer speed, distance and size from objects they have not identified, and that emotion, memory and cultural expectations can alter the resulting account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The Épinal case remains locally memorable because of its apparent interaction with the motorist, but its official classification is not “mysterious after a strong investigation”. It is “not identifiable because the investigation had too little material to work with”.

## The Dompaire triangle that became an aircraft

The Dompaire incident of 30–31 March 2007 initially sounded much stronger. Four young witnesses reported white and red lights forming what appeared to be a metallic triangle in a dark, stormy sky. They watched the phenomenon repeatedly over several hours and believed that it moved at changing speeds, travelled back and forth and at one stage passed only 15 to 20 metres above their vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The investigation found a much more ordinary but unusually presented cause. Air-traffic enquiries showed that a Swiss Hello AG MD-90, a development of the DC-9, had been conducting repeated “touch-and-go” training circuits at Épinal–Mirecourt Airport. GEIPAN records 30 practice approaches during the afternoon, followed by further circuits between 7.47 pm and 9.16 pm and a final departure at 9.45 pm. The witnesses’ movements repeatedly placed them beneath the aircraft’s route.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN concluded that the group had seen the training aircraft under unfamiliar conditions. Repeated circuits made the lights return in a way that could seem purposeful, while darkness, cloud and the absence of a clear view of the fuselage made it difficult to recognise an aeroplane. A later light seen near 2 am was probably a lamp or vehicle light, an interpretation consistent with the witnesses’ own account of several confusions during the night. The case is now category A, meaning identified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Dompaire is arguably the most instructive Vosges case because it had multiple witnesses, a long duration and a dramatic triangular interpretation. Those features did not prevent a conventional explanation. Instead, they gave investigators enough detail to compare the observations with actual aviation activity.

The incident also supplies the department’s clearest aviation connection. It involves a civilian aircraft using Épinal–Mirecourt rather than a secret military programme or unexplained radar target. No equally well-documented Vosges case in the official material establishes an unknown object tracked simultaneously by aircrew, military personnel and radar.

## How dramatic lights acquired ordinary explanations

Several Vosges files show recurring mechanisms that turn familiar lights into strange-looking phenomena.

In July 2007, a couple driving from Remiremont towards Dinozé watched a pale blue-white, misty oval or ring moving silently across low cloud. It appeared to follow their vehicle, remained visible intermittently for about 35 minutes and was observed over more than 20 kilometres. Investigators linked the effect to a multi-beam sky projector used by a nightclub in Épinal. Light projected onto the cloud base created luminous rings whose apparent movement changed with the witnesses’ own motion. GEIPAN classified the case A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-07-08259" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDE REMIREMONT (88) VERS DINOZE (88) 06.07.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Plombières-les-Bains in September 2013, two witnesses saw five bright red lights arriving successively and moving silently in the same direction. A video was posted online, but a field investigation examined the timing, wind direction, measurements and witness information and located the site of a private release of sky lanterns. GEIPAN classified the observation as fully explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08560" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPLOMBIERES-LES-BAINS (88) 08.09.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

In Thaon-les-Vosges in March 2021, a stationary, multicoloured point of light observed at about 11 pm was identified as Sirius. Bright stars low in the sky can flash red, blue and white because atmospheric turbulence refracts their light, particularly when they are viewed through a long path of unstable air close to the horizon. GEIPAN classified the case A and recorded relatively strong supporting information despite the phenomenon’s low strangeness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9980" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANTHAON-LES-VOSGES (88) 24.03.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A photograph taken at Hurbache in June 2009 produced another familiar kind of UFO claim: a bright spot noticed only after the image was examined. The photographer had not seen an object at the time. GEIPAN judged the vertical streak to be a probable camera or processing artefact and suggested that the bright point could have been sunlight reflecting from an aircraft, satellite or balloon. Because the exact cause could not be proved, the case was classified B, meaning probably rather than perfectly identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-06-02321" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHURBACHE (88) 01.06.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These cases do not imply that witnesses were dishonest. They show how cloud projections, lanterns, stars, camera processing and aircraft lights can look unlike their source when scale, distance and context are missing.

## The Mirecourt case changed after re-examination

At about 2 am on 22 November 1989, a woman near Mirecourt saw a bright red phenomenon travelling rapidly on a slightly descending path. She described two cylinder-like forms separated by a dark band, surrounded by sparks and flames and followed by a luminous trail. Her husband also caught sight of it before trees blocked the view. No sound was heard.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-11-01191" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMIRECOURT (88) 22.11.1989 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case was originally classified as unexplained, but GEIPAN later reopened it. Investigators concluded that the description was characteristic of an atmospheric re-entry and found that the projected return of the Soviet satellite Cosmos 2047 was compatible with the direction and approximate time. Although published re-entry times carried an uncertainty of several hours, the match was considered sufficiently strong for the case to be reclassified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-11-01191" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMIRECOURT (88) 22.11.1989 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Mirecourt demonstrates why historical UFO classifications should not be treated as permanent verdicts. Better software, orbital information and accumulated experience can turn an apparently structured “craft” into a recognised natural or technological event. The witness’s account of cylinders, a black division and fiery material was not necessarily an accurate description of solid components; it may have been the visual organisation of a bright, fragmenting object seen briefly through darkness and haze.

This reclassification also weakens any argument that Vosges has accumulated a steadily growing body of unexplained official cases. At least one of its more impressive historical entries moved in the opposite direction: from unexplained to almost certainly identified.

## What category C really means

The Saint-Michel-sur-Meurthe report of 31 December 2013 is a good example of the difference between an unexplained phenomenon and an under-documented one. A witness travelling in a vehicle at about 5.30 pm saw an apparently stationary orange light above the north-north-eastern horizon. Investigators found no bright star, Moon or obvious streetlight in the reported direction. They considered sunlight reflecting from an aircraft or satellite, including several candidate orbital objects, but could not confirm one. The case was therefore placed in category C for lack of information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-12-08633?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=162&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-MICHEL-SUR-MEURTHE (88) 31.12.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This does not put the light on the same evidential level as the Sapois–Vagney encounter. GEIPAN judged the Saint-Michel phenomenon to have low strangeness and regarded conventional explanations as plausible, but the moving observation platform, uncertain duration and absence of corroborating data prevented a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-12-08633?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=162&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-MICHEL-SUR-MEURTHE (88) 31.12.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The same distinction applies to the 1976 Épinal report. Both files remain technically unidentified, but principally because crucial evidence was never obtained. Treating every category C entry as a stubborn mystery would exaggerate the department’s unexplained record.

## Why Vosges generates persuasive misidentifications

The department’s landscape can make aerial interpretation difficult. Drivers frequently observe lights while moving through valleys, woodland and changing lines of sight. Hills and trees repeatedly conceal and reveal distant objects, creating the impression that a light has stopped, reversed or followed a vehicle. Low cloud provides a surface on which powerful ground lighting can form apparently solid shapes, as the Remiremont–Dinozé case demonstrates. Airports and sparsely used runways can also produce unusual aircraft activity that local observers do not expect, as happened near Dompaire.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Night-time observation removes many of the visual cues normally used to judge scale and distance. A witness may see navigation lights but not the aircraft joining them, or may interpret several lights as the corners of one dark triangular body. GEIPAN notes that apparent stationary motion can arise when both witness and object are moving, while emotion and prior expectations can make an already unfamiliar sight seem more structured and purposeful.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Modern technology has added further recurring explanations. Sky lanterns drift silently in groups; satellites flare or form moving chains; aircraft headlights can appear fixed when a plane is approaching almost directly; and cameras create reflections, compression artefacts and blurred trails that were not visible to the photographer. The official Vosges files contain examples of nearly all these mechanisms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-12-08633?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=162&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-MICHEL-SUR-MEURTHE (88) 31.12.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Official records versus local folklore

France is unusual in maintaining a public, state-backed system for collecting and examining reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena. GEIPAN was created within the French space agency CNES in 1977 and works with the gendarmerie, police, civil aviation, defence, meteorological specialists and volunteer investigators. It publishes anonymised testimony, supporting documents and conclusions rather than operating as a search for extraterrestrial visitors.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

That system gives Vosges UFO history a firmer documentary basis than collections built entirely from newspaper clippings or stories retold decades later. Gendarmerie statements, questionnaires, photographs, videos and investigation reports preserve what witnesses originally said and allow later researchers to see when a conclusion changed.

Local press and online retellings still play an important role in keeping incidents alive, but they can flatten crucial distinctions. A story advertised as an “unexplained Vosges UFO” may concern a category C report with almost no usable data, an old category D case subsequently identified, or a vivid witness account whose conventional explanation is less memorable than its opening mystery.

The best way to evaluate a local claim is therefore to ask four questions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* Was the report made close to the event, preferably to the gendarmerie or GEIPAN?
* Were there independent witnesses, photographs, radar traces or physical evidence?
* Did investigators check aircraft, astronomy, weather, satellites and local lighting?
* Is the classification current, or was it copied from an older version of the file?

</div>

These checks do not settle every case, but they separate documented uncertainty from folklore accumulated around an uncertain story.

## The balance of the record

Vosges has a genuine official UFO history, but the record does not point to a sustained local flap supported by aviation radar, military interception or repeated physical traces. Its most defensible unresolved incident is the short 1992 Sapois–Vagney roadside encounter. The 1976 Épinal report is striking but evidentially weak, while the 2013 Saint-Michel-sur-Meurthe light remains unclassified chiefly because the data could not distinguish between plausible conventional possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1992-11-01273" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D23] de SAPOIS (88) vers VAGNEY (88) 12.11.1992 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Against those cases stands a larger and more informative set of solved or probably solved observations: an MD-90 making repeated training circuits, a nightclub projector illuminating clouds, sky lanterns, Sirius, photographic artefacts and the likely re-entry of Cosmos 2047. These explanations were not imposed merely because they were conventional; they were developed by matching reported times, directions, weather, flight activity, orbital information and local sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The department’s lasting lesson is not that every strange light has an immediate answer. It is that the apparent drama of a sighting and the strength of its evidence are different things. In Vosges, later investigation has more often weakened extraordinary interpretations than strengthened them, while leaving a small residue of cases that remain open because testimony alone cannot establish what passed through the night sky.
