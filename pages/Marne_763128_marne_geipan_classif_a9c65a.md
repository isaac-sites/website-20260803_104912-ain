## Introduction

[Marne]({{ 'why-marnes-strangest-ufo-cases-remain/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO record is less a catalogue of enduring mysteries than a record of how unusual experiences are tested, explained and graded. The files published by GEIPAN, the unit of France’s national space agency that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena, include reports attributed to Venus, the Moon, meteors, the International Space Station, satellite trains, balloons, lanterns, lasers, helicopters, drones and even photographic artefacts. A large second group is labelled category C—not because officials found evidence of extraordinary craft, but because the information was too incomplete or unreliable for a defensible conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

That distinction changes how Marne’s UFO history should be read. A vivid account may finish as category A when astronomical or technical checks provide a close match. A comparatively ordinary-looking light may remain category C when the direction, time or witness statement is too vague to investigate. Classification therefore measures the state of the evidence after enquiry, not how dramatic the original story sounds.

## How GEIPAN’s A-to-D system works

GEIPAN assesses two qualities separately. The first is **residual strangeness**: how unusual the event remains after realistic explanations have been considered. The second is **consistency**: how strong and usable the evidence is. Consistency can be increased by independent witnesses, precise timings and viewing directions, photographs, video, radar information, physical traces or a detailed contemporary statement. It is weakened by delayed recollection, uncertain dates, vague locations, missing documents and the absence of information needed to reconstruct the sky or local environment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

Earlier versions of the system divided unresolved cases into D1 and D2. GEIPAN has also stressed that classifications can change when old cases are re-examined or new information appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">baisse cas d</span></span></span>

This explains an apparently surprising feature of Marne’s files: some reports once regarded as unexplained have later become straightforward category A cases. Better astronomical software, satellite records, mapping tools and systematic reconstruction can test possibilities that were difficult to verify when the original gendarmerie statement was taken.

## Astronomical objects produced some of the strongest illusions

Planets and the Moon account for several memorable Marne reports because they can appear exceptionally bright, coloured, enlarged or apparently mobile when seen near the horizon. Long observation does not necessarily protect a witness from misidentification. In fact, a distant astronomical object can remain visible for much longer than an aircraft or meteor, giving the observer time to develop an impression that it is hovering or following them.

A Reims report from 5 February 1977 illustrates the process. Two witnesses watched a silent luminous ball towards the west for an extended period. The file had originally been placed in category C, but a later review compared the reported direction, duration, appearance and brightness with the sky at the time. Venus occupied the relevant part of the sky and was unusually conspicuous. GEIPAN therefore reclassified the observation as category A. The review did not accuse the witnesses of invention or faulty eyesight; it concluded that they had accurately seen a bright object but misinterpreted what it was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-02-00392?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Suippes case of 16 May 1982 is even more instructive because it once carried an unexplained classification. Two women travelling by car described an orange, half-moon-shaped light that appeared to remain over the town and then to follow them in a series of sideways movements. Re-examination established that the Moon was rising in the observed direction. Its apparent pursuit of the vehicle was consistent with the familiar parallax illusion in which a very distant object seems to retain the same position relative to a moving observer. The case was changed from category D to category A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1982-05-00933" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other Marne entries reinforce the pattern. GEIPAN identifies a 1979 report at Merfy as the Moon, a 1981 Reims light as the Moon and a 2007 Moussy observation as Venus. These cases show why investigators need an accurate time, direction and viewing position: without them, an astronomical explanation may be strongly suspected but impossible to demonstrate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Brief moving lights were matched to meteors, spacecraft and satellites

Reports involving fast or steadily moving points of light can often be checked against time-specific records. This is where modern cases have an advantage over many older Marne files: satellite passes, launches and major meteors leave searchable external traces.

At Dormans on 19 November 2009, the reported moving light was identified as the International Space Station. Such passes can look unusual because the station is silent, very bright and apparently unaccompanied by the navigation lights expected on an aircraft. Its visibility and path, however, are predictable, allowing a category A identification when the witness timing and trajectory match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=67&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

A far more visually striking example occurred on the RN4 from Brugny-Vaudancourt towards Nancy on 27 March 2021. A motorist reported between roughly 50 and 100 silent white lights arranged one above another. GEIPAN identified the display as a Starlink satellite train. The incident demonstrates how new technologies repeatedly create new UFO forms: a line or procession of recently deployed satellites can look coordinated and artificial because it is coordinated and artificial, but it is not an unknown aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=67&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

At Les Petites-Loges on 8 November 2022, a very short observation of a rapidly moving luminous point was classified as a meteor. Duration is crucial in such cases. A light crossing part of the sky in seconds is more compatible with a meteor than with a planet, while the availability of reports from other locations can show that several witnesses observed the same atmospheric event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=67&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

These identifications are not based solely on resemblance. A convincing match normally combines the object’s direction, apparent speed, duration and time with external astronomical or spaceflight information. When one of those elements is missing, the same type of report may remain category C rather than being promoted to a speculative explanation.

## Balloons, lanterns and aircraft explained slower objects

Slow, silent or apparently stationary objects form another recurring Marne group. These are often harder to identify with complete certainty because their paths depend on local wind, viewing geometry and human activity. They therefore frequently receive category B rather than category A.

On 12 November 1977, two people at Châlons-sur-Marne observed an elongated luminous object that appeared initially stationary and then rose before disappearing into cloud. No unusual military activity was reported at the nearby camp. GEIPAN’s probable explanation was a weather balloon illuminated by the setting Sun, a combination that can make a pale object appear brilliant against a darkening sky. The file is classified B because the balloon interpretation fits well but was not independently proved by recovery or a specific launch record presented in the public summary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-11-00454" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Orange lights travelling together have repeatedly been attributed to flying lanterns. Marne examples include Saint-Brice-Courcelles in September 2011 and Fismes on Christmas Day 2014. Lanterns rise with the wind, produce a steady orange glow, make no engine noise and may extinguish abruptly. A release of several lanterns can therefore be described as a formation whose members follow the same route and disappear one after another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=67&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

At Vertus in September 2012, a slow and apparently silent object was judged probably to have been a low-flying helicopter. Silence alone is weak evidence against an aircraft explanation: wind direction, distance, terrain and background noise can make an engine difficult to hear, while a head-on or receding aircraft may seem almost stationary. GEIPAN accordingly used category B rather than claiming absolute identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=67&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The catalogue also records a Reims observation from April 2011 as probably caused by laser lighting, and an Épernay report from November 2024 was classified B as a probable drone in an update published in June 2026. ## Photographs can create objects that were never in the sky

A photograph does not automatically make a case more reliable. Investigators must first establish whether the recorded shape was visible to the witness or appeared only in the image. Lens reflections, internal glare, dust, insects, motion blur, compression and camera processing can all produce apparently structured forms.

A Reims case from 25 January 2009 involved luminous phenomena appearing in photographs. GEIPAN classified the images as photographic artefacts and placed the case in category A. This type of result is especially important in public discussions because an image may look more persuasive than a verbal account while actually supplying evidence about the camera rather than the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

Image analysis therefore begins with mundane questions: Was the object noticed before the photograph was taken? Does it appear in several frames? Does it maintain a position relative to the landscape or relative to the lens? Are there bright lamps capable of creating reflections? Is the original file available, with its metadata intact? A compressed copy posted online may be visually striking but far less useful than an unremarkable original image accompanied by an exact time, location and witness account.

## Why category C does not mean extraordinary

Category C is the most commonly misunderstood part of Marne’s record. It is not a halfway point between “probably conventional” and “genuinely mysterious”. It means that the file does not support a sufficiently reliable analysis. GEIPAN’s Marne catalogue applies it to reports from places including Bétheny, Reims, Avize, Taissy, Mareuil-le-Port, Sézanne, Bignicourt-sur-Marne, Broyes and Sommesous. The public descriptions repeatedly use phrases equivalent to “lack of information” or “lack of reliable information”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

Several recurring weaknesses lead to this outcome:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the report was made long after the event;
* the date or time is approximate;
* no precise direction or elevation was recorded;
* only one short statement survives;
* witnesses were not interviewed separately;
* no photograph, radar record or independent report is available;
* possible aircraft, astronomical or local-lighting explanations cannot be checked retrospectively;
* physical traces were documented without timely measurements, sampling or secure control of the site.

The Sézanne report of 30 May 2012, concerning flattened grass over an area several metres wide, is a good example. A ground mark may initially sound more substantial than a distant light, but without prompt examination, photographs with scale, soil or plant analysis, access history and evidence excluding weather, animals or human activity, it cannot establish what caused the disturbance. The file remained C because the trace did not come with enough information to discriminate between explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

Similarly, a Reims report from October 1980 described flashes occurring every second for about four minutes. That periodicity might suggest an aircraft beacon, rotating light, distant tower or another artificial source, but a missing direction, weather context or comparison with local installations prevents a secure retrospective finding. ## The Broyes and Sommesous file weakened on review

The paired motorist reports from Sommesous on 31 December 1999 and Broyes on 10 January 2000 are among Marne’s most dramatic official entries. Both involved intense light and alleged vehicle disturbances. In the Broyes account, the driver said the engine and radio stopped while the ignition remained on, then function returned after the light faded. The closeness in time and the similarity of the narratives encouraged officials to consider the incidents together.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=98&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Yet the later classification was C, not D. The problem was not that vehicle interference is inherently impossible or that the witnesses were automatically rejected. It was that the available material did not securely establish the sequence or cause. Investigators lacked sufficiently strong contemporary technical evidence linking an external aerial phenomenon to the reported malfunctions. There was no instrument record, independently observed object, preserved vehicle fault data or other measurement capable of separating an unusual external effect from electrical, mechanical, perceptual or reporting uncertainties. The public catalogue therefore describes the paired case as lacking reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

This is an important example of later reporting weakening rather than strengthening a claim. A case may remain narratively impressive while becoming evidentially less secure once investigators ask whether each crucial detail was recorded promptly and independently. Reclassification to C does not explain precisely what the motorists encountered. It says that the surviving file cannot justify calling the event a robust unexplained phenomenon.

## Reinvestigation changed Marne’s historical picture

Older files were often built primarily around gendarmerie statements. These could preserve valuable testimony, but they were not always designed to capture astronomical bearings, elevation angles, environmental measurements or the technical information required for modern reconstruction. GEIPAN has consequently revisited earlier cases using present-day classification criteria and digital tools.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">baisse cas d</span></span></span>

Marne shows the effect clearly. The 1977 Reims case moved from C to A after comparison with Venus. The 1982 Suippes report moved from D to A after reconstruction identified the rising Moon. These changes demonstrate that “unexplained” is not a permanent property of an event. It is a description of what could be concluded from the evidence available at a particular stage of the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-02-00392?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The reverse is also possible in principle: new documentation could strengthen an incomplete case. In practice, however, old category C reports are difficult to rescue. Memories fade, landscapes and lighting installations change, vehicles are repaired or scrapped, witnesses become unavailable and original photographs or negatives disappear. For many Marne cases, the absence of evidence is not evidence that nothing unusual happened, but it does impose a firm limit on what can responsibly be claimed.

## What Marne’s official files actually show

The most defensible reading of Marne’s catalogue is that ordinary phenomena can produce experiences that feel highly unusual, particularly at night, near the horizon or from a moving vehicle. The Moon may appear to follow a car. Venus may resemble a hovering luminous sphere. A sunlit balloon may seem to rise into cloud. Lanterns can resemble an organised formation. A satellite train can look like a fleet, while a photograph can introduce a bright form that the observer never saw.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-02-00392?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C148" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The files also show that official identification is not simply a matter of attaching a familiar label to every report. Category A requires a close, testable match; category B acknowledges a strong but not conclusive explanation; category C openly records investigative failure caused by weak evidence; and category D requires a phenomenon to remain unexplained after a sufficiently substantial enquiry. Marne’s conspicuous pattern is the combination of many A and B identifications with numerous C files, rather than a concentration of strongly supported unresolved cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">le présent niveau d&#x27;étrangeté et de...Read more...</span></span></span>

For readers assessing any Marne UFO claim, the decisive question is therefore not simply, “Was an explanation found?” It is, “Was enough reliable information collected to test the realistic explanations?” Where the answer is yes, planets, meteors, spacecraft, aircraft and artificial lights account for much of the record. Where the answer is no, the result is usually category C—an inconclusive file, not confirmed evidence of something extraordinary.

    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
