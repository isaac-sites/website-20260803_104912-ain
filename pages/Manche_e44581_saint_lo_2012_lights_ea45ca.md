## Introduction

On 4 April 2012, at about 8.15 pm, a man standing in his garden on the southern outskirts of Saint-Lô saw two bright points cross the twilight sky. One moved steadily; the other appeared to loop around it, make abrupt angular turns, approach it and then fall rapidly behind. The display lasted only about 15 seconds before both lights vanished into or behind cloud. France’s official investigators could not match the reported movements to aircraft, satellites, balloons, astronomical objects or other familiar causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-04-08222" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-LO (50) 04.04.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That conclusion makes Saint-Lô the strongest officially unexplained case in [Manche]({{ 'what-manches-ufo-record-actually-shows/' | relative_url }})’s published record. It does not establish that an extraordinary craft was present. The case rests on one brief visual observation, with no photograph, video or matching radar track. Its importance lies instead in the unusual relationship between the lights and in the unusually detailed attempt to test ordinary explanations. GEIPAN’s present file retains the case in classification D — unidentified after investigation — while the investigation report uses the older, more specific label D1.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-04-08222" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-LO (50) 04.04.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the witness reported

The witness was outside smoking a cigarette when two small yellowish-white lights attracted his attention. The sky was reportedly clear around him, although a large cloud lay towards the north. The lights travelled across roughly 30 degrees of sky, from an easterly direction towards the north, and remained continuously visible until they reached the cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The first light was not especially remarkable. It followed what the witness described as a straight, even course. The second was the reason the event became difficult to classify. It appeared to move repeatedly around the first, tracing ellipses, making sharp changes of direction and briefly coming into contact with it before dropping back. Both were described as very bright, point-like and silent, with no visible trail or halo.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The sighting ended quickly. After approximately 15 seconds, the lights disappeared through or behind the northern cloud. The witness moved within the garden to look beyond it, but they did not reappear. He could not estimate their distance, altitude or physical size, which is unsurprising for isolated lights viewed against the sky without a known reference object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Several features strengthen the account as testimony. The witness completed GEIPAN’s questionnaire on 6 April, only two days after the event, and had made notes and photographic reconstructions soon afterwards. He did not claim to know what the lights were, and the file records that he tried to consider the experience rationally rather than immediately assigning it an exotic origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

There were also major limitations from the outset. He was the only witness. No image was taken during the sighting, no instrument was used and no physical effect was reported. The entire case therefore depends on how accurately one person perceived and remembered a very brief pattern of lights at an unknown distance. GEIPAN nevertheless judged the report sufficiently detailed to investigate rather than placing it among cases that cannot be assessed for lack of information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary explanations did not fit

GEIPAN first reviewed the case remotely, then commissioned an investigator to visit the site. The investigation included measurements of the viewing directions, weather and astronomical checks, a request for military radar information, and a later cognitive interview intended to recover the witness’s recollection without leading him. The formal investigation report was completed in June 2014.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The investigation did not show that every conventional explanation was physically impossible. Rather, none available to GEIPAN reproduced the complete report: two bright points travelling together, with one apparently making rapid non-linear movements around the other.

**Astronomical objects were a poor match.** The sighting occurred before sunset — calculated in the report as 8.34 pm at Caen — and the principal visible astronomical object in the relevant part of the sky was a nearly full Moon to the east-south-east. A star, planet or the Moon could not account for two lights crossing a substantial portion of the sky in 15 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**A bright satellite pass also failed to match.** Investigators considered an Iridium satellite flare, which could briefly appear exceptionally bright, but their satellite check found no negative-magnitude pass at the relevant time. More importantly, a single satellite flare would not explain one point repeatedly circling or sharply changing position around another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**Space debris was judged unlikely.** A re-entry can produce several lights moving together and can sometimes give the impression of a formation. GEIPAN argued that a conspicuous re-entry would probably have generated reports across a much wider area. The direction described in the file was also considered inconsistent with the expected motion of a re-entering object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**Windborne lights did not fit the reported speed or direction.** Balloons and sky lanterns move with the wind and generally change their relative positions gradually. Regional data indicated northerly to north-easterly winds, whereas the reported lights crossed the sky rapidly and one seemed to execute abrupt manoeuvres. The same incompatibility weakened other drifting-atmosphere proposals.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**Aircraft were the most obvious comparison, but no satisfactory match emerged.** A pair of aircraft seen at different distances can appear to cross or change relative positions, especially when only their lights are visible. Aerobatic or model aircraft could also produce curved paths. However, the witness heard no engines, and the reported sharp turns seemed unlike the apparent motion expected from ordinary aircraft over a residential area. The investigators also considered ultralights, drones and model aircraft but rated these explanations as very weak.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The weakness of the aircraft explanation depends heavily on accepting the witness’s description literally. At an unknown range, a modest change in the viewing geometry of two independent lights can appear much more dramatic than their actual movement. GEIPAN’s report recognised that the available direction and trajectory data were too limited for an objective reconstruction. This is an important qualification: investigators could reject specific matches, but they could not calculate the lights’ true speeds, separation or flight paths.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## What the radar check established

The radar enquiry is the most substantial element distinguishing Saint-Lô from a routine unexplained-light report. GEIPAN asked military air-traffic control for recorded tracks around Saint-Lô between 8.00 and 8.30 pm. The returned plot contained four identified general-aviation tracks and two detections without complete identifying information, labelled X1 and X2 in the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The ordinary tracks had the speeds and altitudes expected of cruising commercial or private aircraft. Investigators examined whether either unidentified return could correspond to the lights. X2 appeared to travel at roughly 820 kilometres per hour, consistent with an aircraft, but its route did not match the witness’s viewing direction. X1 was slower, at about 120 kilometres per hour and 15,200 feet, but appeared at approximately 8.05 pm, around ten minutes too early, and lay south of the observation site rather than in the reported sector.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Consequently, the radar data neither confirmed the sighting nor revealed a corresponding anomalous object. Its value was mainly negative: the known tracks did not provide a convincing identification, while the two incomplete tracks were incompatible in time or direction.

That distinction is crucial. The case is sometimes described in a way that could imply an unexplained radar target accompanied the lights. The official report says the opposite. There was no radar return successfully correlated with the visual observation. The Saint-Lô case remains a single-witness visual event, not a combined visual-radar incident.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## The investigation’s weakest point

The field investigation improved the geographical information, but it took place more than two years after the sighting. During the cognitive interview, some recalled details — including the direction of travel — conflicted with the original questionnaire. The witness and investigator agreed that the notes and reconstructions made immediately after the event should take priority over the later memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This discrepancy does not prove that the report was false. It does demonstrate why the case cannot support precise claims about acceleration or manoeuvring. Human memory is reconstructive, and even an honest witness may alter directions, sequence or timing as an event is repeatedly recalled. GEIPAN itself highlighted the contradiction as an example of the uncertainty created when interviews occur long after an observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

There is a second problem: the apparent “orbiting” behaviour was seen only as angular movement on the witness’s field of view. Without measured distance, the report cannot establish that one physical object truly circled another. Two unrelated lights moving along separate paths could appear to interact because of perspective. Eye movement, changing attention between the points or brief loss of fixation could also exaggerate their relative motion.

GEIPAN considered a temporary visual effect, including phosphene-like disturbances or an ocular migraine, because of the short duration and looping apparent movement. Investigators rejected it as the preferred explanation because the witness reported good health and eyesight and no similar episode before or afterwards. Yet the absence of a known medical history cannot fully eliminate a one-off perceptual effect; it only means that the file contained no positive evidence for one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-04-08222" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-LO (50) 04.04.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why the case received an unexplained classification

GEIPAN classifies cases by weighing two broad qualities. “Consistency” reflects the quantity, reliability and objectivity of the information collected. “Strangeness” measures how poorly the best ordinary hypothesis accounts for the observation after investigation. Category D is used where a phenomenon remains unidentified despite enquiry, whereas category C applies when there is too little dependable information to decide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The 2014 Saint-Lô report assigned a consistency score of 0.7 and a strangeness score of 0.6. The consistency was limited by the single witness and lack of photographs or video. The strangeness came chiefly from the second light’s reported non-linear movement around the first. The report therefore classified the event D1, the former subcategory for a strange but not exceptionally well-corroborated unexplained case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s current public page, updated on 16 June 2026, displays the broader classification D and an updated strangeness value of 0.62, while its summary still describes the historical D1 conclusion. This appears to reflect the presentation of the current classification system rather than a newly identified cause: the published page continues to state that the phenomenon resisted the usual misidentification hypotheses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-04-08222" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-LO (50) 04.04.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The wording “unidentified after investigation” is deliberately narrower than “proved anomalous”. GEIPAN notes that D cases may be revisited when new information appears. Its wider statistics also show that such cases are a small minority of its archive: CNES currently reports about 3.3 per cent of cases as unidentified after investigation, compared with much larger shares that are identified, probably identified or unusable for lack of data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

## What the evidence does and does not prove

The strongest evidence is the witness’s prompt written report, the unusual and internally coherent description of two related lights, and the fact that investigators tested weather, satellite, astronomical and radar information rather than simply accepting the claim. No specific aircraft, satellite, balloon or atmospheric event was found that matched the account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete354.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete354</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The evidence does **not** establish the lights’ distance, altitude, speed or physical relationship. It does not show a structured object, and it provides no photograph, video, radar correlation, secondary witness or physical trace. The apparent ellipses and sharp turns are descriptions of visual angles, not measured trajectories.

Later reporting has neither substantially strengthened nor debunked the case. The official file remains available and was updated in 2026, but no new witness, instrument record or conventional identification is presented. The case has therefore stayed unresolved for the same reason it was classified D1 in 2014: the reported motion is difficult to reconcile with the checked hypotheses, yet the surviving evidence is too limited to reconstruct what physically occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-04-08222" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-LO (50) 04.04.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Within Manche’s UFO history, Saint-Lô is notable not because it proves an extraordinary visitation, but because it sits in the narrow space between a weak report and an identified mistake. It was detailed enough to survive a formal enquiry, but not independently recorded enough to support a firm conclusion. The most accurate verdict remains that two unusual lights were reported, the main ordinary explanations examined by GEIPAN did not fit cleanly, and the nature of the event is still unknown.
