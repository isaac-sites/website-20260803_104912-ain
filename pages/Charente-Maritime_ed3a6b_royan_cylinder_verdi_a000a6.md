## Introduction

The metal cylinder that fell near Royan in February 1985 was not space debris and did not provide evidence of an exotic craft. It was eventually identified as the rear section of a German wartime ground-launched smoke or rocket weapon whose remaining propellant had ignited decades after the Second World War. What makes the case important is not the object’s mystery but the way the explanation changed as stronger evidence became available.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Charente-Maritime_ed3a6b_royan_cylinder_verdi_a000a6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Royan Cylinder" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Unlike most sightings in [Charente-Maritime]({{ 'charente-maritime/' | relative_url }}), the Saint-Sulpice-de-Royan incident produced witnesses, impact marks, a recoverable object and several technical examinations. Investigators first ruled out known contemporary missiles, then provisionally linked the cylinder to a recently decayed Soviet rocket stage. Only later did markings, construction details and trajectory work point back to the German occupation of the Royan area. GEIPAN now classifies the case as category A: an identified phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## The fall, witnesses and recovered object

On 25 February 1985, two agricultural workers were in separate fields at Saint-Sulpice-de-Royan when they heard a whistling sound. One witness saw a rotating object approaching before it struck the ground. It landed within about 50 metres of him, cut a furrow roughly 1.8 metres long and up to 30 centimetres deep, hit a bank and bounced several times before stopping.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The cylinder was approximately 52 centimetres long and 15 centimetres across. One end carried a ring of 26 conical openings, while the opposite end appeared to have been torn away from a more substantial assembly. Its exterior was heavily oxidised, and dark deposits around the openings suggested that hot gases had passed through them. These features made it look less like random scrap metal than part of a manufactured propulsion system.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The most striking detail was its temperature. The witnesses could feel intense heat close to the surface and were unable to handle the object. Even the first gendarmes reportedly had to wait until the air-gendarmerie team arrived, about an hour after the initial call, before it could be removed safely. That heat naturally encouraged the idea that the cylinder had just travelled through the atmosphere or had been expelled by an operating rocket.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The incident was therefore much better documented than a typical report of a distant light. Investigators had:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* two nearby witnesses who heard and saw the descent;
* a physical object that could be retained for examination;
* measurable impact and rebound marks;
* prompt involvement by the local gendarmerie and the Rochefort air base;
* evidence that the object had recently undergone intense combustion.

</div>

There is a small archival complication over the date. GEIPAN’s current case page and database consistently give 25 February, whereas the opening lines of one scanned investigation report refer to the alert and fall as occurring on 26 February. The surviving testimony elsewhere in the file, and GEIPAN’s modern classification, support 25 February as the official case date. The discrepancy appears to be a clerical inconsistency rather than evidence of two separate events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Why investigators suspected a rocket component

The Rochefort air base examined the cylinder first. Missile specialists could not match it to a French weapon or to any foreign missile system known to them, and a Geiger-counter test detected no radioactivity. Checks were also made with the Landes missile-testing centre, regional air-traffic authorities and the military air bases at Cognac and Cazaux. None reported a launch, exercise or accident that could account for the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Those negative findings did not establish an extraterrestrial origin. They simply removed the most obvious contemporary explanations. The combination of apparent exhaust ports, scorching, great heat and a descent from above led GEPAN—the French national study group that preceded today’s GEIPAN—to investigate whether a piece of artificial space hardware had re-entered the atmosphere.

The timing produced a plausible lead. The Soviet satellite Cosmos 1629 had been launched on 21 February 1985 by a Proton rocket. Its payload carried catalogue number 15574, while an associated rocket body was catalogue number 15575 and was recorded as decaying from orbit around 24 February. Information supplied through NASA and the North American Aerospace Defense Command therefore appeared to place a suitable piece of orbital debris in the atmosphere at almost exactly the right time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

GEPAN provisionally concluded that the Royan cylinder might be part of the fourth-stage propulsion system used in the Cosmos 1629 launch. This was not a reckless guess. It joined three real observations: an object shaped like a propulsion component, evidence of intense heating and a known rocket body returning to Earth during the same period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Yet the match was circumstantial. A decaying rocket stage can re-enter anywhere along a broad orbital path, and temporal proximity alone cannot show that one particular fragment landed in a particular field. More importantly, the cylinder itself was available for direct testing. Once the material evidence began to contradict the orbital theory, the coincidental timing of the Soviet re-entry ceased to be persuasive.

## How later analysis changed the verdict

The cylinder was retained by GEPAN rather than discarded after the provisional space-debris finding. That decision was crucial. Further examinations were undertaken by the Centre for Research and Studies at Arcueil and other technical specialists. They discovered inscriptions and markings establishing that the component was German and dated from the Third Reich period. The proposed link to Cosmos 1629 was consequently abandoned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Additional analysis identified the object more specifically as the rear or propulsive section of a German ground-to-ground weapon described in the GEIPAN file as a “smoke launcher”. The term requires care. German *Nebelwerfer* weapons were literally named “smoke launchers”, reflecting their origins and association with chemical or smoke troops, but the family also fired high-explosive and other battlefield rockets. The label therefore does not mean that every such projectile merely released harmless smoke.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://shs.cairn.info/revue-historique-des-armees-2018-4-page-115?lang=fr" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: shs.cairn.info">[Cairn.info]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">shs.cairn.info</span><span class="citation-popover-title">revue historique des armees 2018 4 page 115</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">les traces des bombes-fusées des fronts de l&#x27;Atlantique...1 Sept 2022 — Bien que le terme Nebelwerfer signifie littéralement lanceur de...</span></span></span>

The cylinder’s ring of openings, heat damage and apparent separation from a larger body were consistent with a rocket-motor section rather than a spacecraft fuel tank. Investigators concluded that old propellant had ignited spontaneously, perhaps after warming or being disturbed by a minor shock. The resulting thrust launched the surviving motor section across the local countryside before it dropped into the field.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Trajectory calculations placed its probable starting point about 3.3 to 3.6 kilometres north-west of the impact site. According to GEIPAN’s reconstruction, that direction led towards marshland where German forces had reportedly disposed of munitions while trapped in the Royan pocket at the end of the war. The area had indeed been a heavily militarised German stronghold guarding the Gironde estuary until its capture in April 1945, making the survival of abandoned ordnance locally credible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-SULPICE-DE-ROYAN (17) 25.02.1985 &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The reconstruction is persuasive, although not every link is equally direct. The German origin rests on physical markings and technical examination. The exact launch point and the proposed history of disposal in the marshes are reconstructions based on range, direction and wartime circumstances; investigators did not observe the cylinder leaving a particular buried cache. The identification of the object is therefore much firmer than the detailed account of where it had lain during the preceding four decades.

## Why the early space-debris theory failed

The first explanation failed because it relied too heavily on correspondence in time and too little on correspondence in manufacture. A Soviet rocket body had fallen from orbit at approximately the right moment, but the Royan component bore German wartime markings and construction features. Once that direct evidence was recognised, the orbital association became an accidental coincidence.

Several features also became easier to understand under the wartime-weapon explanation:

**The intense heat:** it resulted from the motor’s own propellant burning shortly before impact, rather than necessarily from atmospheric re-entry.

**The whistling descent:** this was compatible with a heavy, irregular rocket component moving through the lower atmosphere after its brief powered flight.

**The multiple exhaust-like openings:** they belonged to a propulsion assembly, which had initially encouraged the correct general suspicion—rocket hardware—but the wrong historical source.

**The limited flight path:** trajectory reconstruction suggested a launch from only a few kilometres away, not the final stage of a journey from orbit.

**The surface condition:** heavy corrosion was consistent with an object that had spent decades in damp ground or marshland before ignition.

This distinction matters because “rocket component” and “space debris” are not interchangeable conclusions. Investigators correctly recognised that the cylinder had been part of a propulsion system. Their error was assigning it to the recent Soviet launch rather than to a weapon left in the Royan landscape since 1945.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete-R6.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## A rare case where physical evidence settled the question

The Royan cylinder occupies an unusual place in Charente-Maritime’s UFO history because the evidence became stronger after the initial excitement. In many sighting cases, investigators work with memories, sketches or distant photographs and have no way to test the reported phenomenon directly. Here, the central object remained available while the explanation evolved.

The investigation lasted nearly five years. During that period the case moved through three distinct stages:

1. **An unidentified falling object:** neither witnesses nor local military specialists recognised it.
2. **A provisional space-debris explanation:** the decay of rocket body 15575 after the Cosmos 1629 launch supplied an apparently good temporal match.


<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Charente-Maritime_ed3a6b_royan_cylinder_verdi_a000a6-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" alt="Royan Cylinder illustration 3" data-theme-src-dark="{{ "/assets/images/Charente-Maritime_ed3a6b_royan_cylinder_verdi_a000a6-Illustration-3-dark.svg" | relative_url }}" data-theme-src-light="{{ "/assets/images/Charente-Maritime_ed3a6b_royan_cylinder_verdi_a000a6-Illustration-3-light.svg" | relative_url }}" loading="lazy" decoding="async" fetchpriority="low">

3. **A physical identification:** German markings and specialist examination established a wartime military origin, while trajectory work linked the flight to the former Royan battlefield landscape. [GEIPAN](https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063)

This sequence shows why a provisional explanation should not be mistaken for a final verdict. The early space theory was evidence-based but incomplete. It was not replaced by speculation from outside the inquiry; it was overturned by closer examination of the recovered material.

The case also demonstrates that an ordinary explanation need not make an incident ordinary in every sense. A forty-year-old munition apparently igniting and launching itself from marshland is rare, hazardous and historically remarkable. What it does not require is an unknown aerospace vehicle. The surviving evidence points to an unusual episode of delayed wartime ordnance activity, not to technology beyond conventional human manufacture.

## What the case contributes to Charente-Maritime’s UFO record

Within the department, the Saint-Sulpice-de-Royan cylinder is the clearest example of why physical evidence can be more valuable than an initially impressive story. The witnesses appear to have reported a real and dangerous event accurately: a hot manufactured object descended with a whistle, struck the ground and bounced across a field. Their observation was not “debunked” in the sense of being dismissed as imaginary. What changed was the interpretation of the object’s origin.

The investigation also weakens a common assumption that official involvement implies an extraordinary cause. The gendarmerie, the Rochefort air base, GEPAN, NASA-related channels, NORAD information and specialist laboratories all entered the story because the object was genuinely difficult to identify. Their involvement records uncertainty and the effort to resolve it; it does not authenticate a UFO claim.

GEIPAN’s present category-A classification reflects the eventual strength of the identification. In its classification system, category A is used for phenomena that have been identified, rather than reports left unresolved through strangeness or lack of information. The Royan cylinder therefore belongs in the department’s UFO history not as an enduring unknown, but as a particularly instructive solved case. [GEIPAN+2GEIPAN](https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-02-01063)

Its lasting lesson is straightforward: the most persuasive explanation was not the first plausible one. A dramatic fall from the sky, a glowing-hot rocket-like object and a coincident Soviet re-entry initially formed a coherent picture. Markings on the metal and a reconstruction rooted in Royan’s wartime history formed a better one.

    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
