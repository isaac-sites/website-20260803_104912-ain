## Introduction

[Indre-et-Loire]({{ 'indre-et-loire/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO archive is notable less for an extraordinary unsolved encounter than for showing how ordinary phenomena can look extraordinary at night. In the files published by GEIPAN, the investigation unit of France’s national space agency, striking local reports have been attributed to the rising Moon, nightclub lasers, bright stars and sky lanterns. Other reports remain unclassified because the information is too incomplete to support any conclusion.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Indre-et-Loire_b45e62_geipan_explained_sig_21e1da-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction matters. A case labelled “unidentified” in everyday conversation may ultimately be explained, while a file that cannot be solved because essential details are missing is not evidence of an unknown craft. In the current published department record reviewed for this page, no Indre-et-Loire case meets GEIPAN’s standard for a well-documented unexplained event. The archive instead offers a useful lesson in how witness sincerity, visual illusion, atmospheric conditions and retrospective investigation can interact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[cnes.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

## What the GEIPAN classifications mean

GEIPAN, the Study and Information Group on Unidentified Aerospace Phenomena, has operated within the French space agency CNES since 1977. It receives testimony, creates a case file, conducts an initial assessment, investigates where possible, anonymises the material and publishes a classification. Its role is to analyse reported aerial phenomena using established scientific knowledge, not to prove extraterrestrial visitation or investigate paranormal claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

The four main classifications are easy to misunderstand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A** means the phenomenon has been identified after investigation.
* **B** means an explanation is considered probable, although not established with the same confidence as an A case.
* **C** means the case cannot be identified because too little reliable information is available.
* **D** means the phenomenon remains unidentified despite an investigation supported by sufficient information.

</div>

GEIPAN reaches these decisions by considering both the report’s **consistency**—the quantity and reliability of the available evidence—and its **residual strangeness** after ordinary explanations have been tested. A vivid story can therefore have low evidential value, while a multiple-witness report can still receive an A classification if its direction, timing and appearance match a known object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This is particularly important when reading older local files. Several Indre-et-Loire reports were first placed in C or D categories and reconsidered years later as better mapping, astronomical software and accumulated experience made specific explanations possible. A change of classification does not necessarily mean that the original investigators were careless. It may mean that they lacked the tools, reference cases or environmental information available to later analysts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

## The Moon at Neuvy-le-Roi

One of the strongest Indre-et-Loire examples began as a genuinely impressive multiple-witness report. At about 9.15 pm on 7 December 1979, people travelling on separate roads near Neuvy-le-Roi saw a large red-orange light low in the eastern sky. One driver described changing shapes, including something resembling a capital L and later a reddish parachute with a dark mass beneath it. A father and daughter in another car described an enormous orange-red ball that remained visible for much of their journey towards the town. Gendarmes took statements from three witnesses within days of the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

The witnesses’ descriptions made the phenomenon seem nearby and mobile. It appeared to travel beside one car, disappear behind farm buildings and re-emerge, while its apparent size was estimated as much greater than the Moon. Yet the same details eventually supplied the solution.

When GEIPAN re-examined the file, it reconstructed the witnesses’ routes and lines of sight. Both groups had been looking east or east-north-east at almost the same time. The phenomenon stayed at a very low angular elevation, was intermittently hidden by buildings or terrain, and seemed to keep pace with moving vehicles. That apparent pursuit is a familiar perspective effect: an extremely distant object changes direction so little relative to the observer that it can seem to accompany a car.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

Astronomical reconstruction showed the Moon rising at an azimuth of about 67 degrees, in the reported direction. It was in a gibbous phase, four days before the last quarter, which could produce the curved or parachute-like outline described when partly obscured by cloud. Its reddish colour was consistent with a low Moon seen through a long stretch of atmosphere. The familiar “Moon illusion” also helps explain why it appeared exceptionally large near the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

The case is valuable because the explanation does not require the witnesses to have been dishonest or foolish. GEIPAN explicitly found the testimony detailed and consistent and did not question the witnesses’ sincerity. It concluded that the observed position, duration, colour, shape and behaviour all corresponded to the rising Moon and reclassified the case from C to A. Strong testimony had recorded a real visual experience; it was the perceived distance and nature of the light that were mistaken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

## Lasers over Touraine

A very different source of apparent craft emerged in the department during the early 1990s: entertainment lasers projected into the sky. These could create blue or pale shapes on cloud layers, sweep from side to side and appear to hover above roads. To drivers unfamiliar with the technology, the projected light could look like a thin, structured object moving deliberately.

The best local reference case occurred near L’Île-Bouchard on 11 December 1993. GEIPAN’s archive identifies the phenomenon as a laser and classifies the case A. The investigation traced the displays to a nightclub device marketed as a “Sky Rose”, providing direct local evidence that such equipment was operating in the area and producing unusual aerial effects at the time. The case later became a comparison point for reassessing similar reports elsewhere in Indre-et-Loire and beyond.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=17&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPANGeipan Groupe d’études et d’informations sur les phénomènes aérospatiaux non identifiés.... That comparison was central to the re-examination of a report now listed as Saint-Branchs, dated 16 May 1993 and also known in earlier records as Saint-Germain-sur-Vienne or Cormery. Between midnight and 1 am, three people travelling in a car repeatedly noticed an elongated bluish form near the apparent height of the headlights. It moved continuously from side to side and remained difficult to place at any definite distance. The observation continued intermittently over roughly four kilometres, although only one formal witness statement was collected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-05-01301?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C408&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-BRANCHS (37) 16.05.1993 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN considered the appearance characteristic of a nightclub laser: bluish, thin, apparently insubstantial, illuminated from below and moving in regular lateral sweeps. Cloud towards the northern horizon could have acted as a projection surface even though the sky looked clearer along parts of the witnesses’ route. Investigators could not identify the exact projector a quarter of a century later, but the close match with documented laser cases led them to regard the explanation as very probable and to change the case from its former D status to A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-05-01301?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C408&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-BRANCHS (37) 16.05.1993 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The apparent contradiction in that wording—“very probable” but category A—also shows why readers should examine the investigation rather than rely only on a letter. In practice, the conclusion rests on a recognised visual pattern and a locally demonstrated technology, not on recovery of the individual machine that produced the light. The case has therefore weakened as evidence for an unknown object, even though a small historical uncertainty remains over the precise source nightclub.

## Stars, cameras and glowing formations

More recent files show that modern recording equipment does not automatically settle an observation. At Tournon-Saint-Pierre on 23 March 2020, a witness filmed a stationary light visible for an extended period in the south-western sky. Images extracted from the camcorder appeared to show changing forms and colours, features that could easily make an ordinary point of light seem structured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/60442?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C30" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN used a location photograph and digital mapping to estimate an azimuth of roughly 240 degrees and an elevation near 30 degrees. Astronomical software placed Procyon, one of the brightest stars in the night sky, close to that position at the stated time. The reported colour and brightness also matched the star. GEIPAN explained the shapes in the recording as optical and electronic artefacts caused by zooming and filming a point source out of focus; chromatic aberration produced the apparent colour changes. The case was classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/60442?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C30" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The file is a useful warning against treating enlarged video frames as direct pictures of an object’s physical outline. A distant star occupies little more than a point in a consumer camera’s image. Defocus, atmospheric turbulence, digital processing and movement of the camera can transform that point into discs, rings or shifting coloured forms that were not present in the sky.

Another local report, from Autrèche on 28 August 2011, illustrates how several ordinary lights can be mentally grouped into apparent formations. A couple saw silent orange lights moving across the sky and distinguished seven apparently triangular arrangements. GEIPAN noted that the number, colour, speed and direction were consistent with sky lanterns. The Saturday-night timing and a westerly wind strengthened that interpretation, although the launch itself was not traced; the case received a B rather than an A classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=158&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Together, the Procyon and Autrèche files show two different routes to perceived structure. A camera can manufacture detail around a single unresolved light, while the human visual system can organise separate lights into triangles or other meaningful shapes. Neither process implies that the observation was invented.

## Why incomplete cases are not unexplained cases

Indre-et-Loire’s archive also contains C cases, including files associated with Bléré, Chanceaux-près-Loches and Chambourg-sur-Indre. That label does not mean GEIPAN investigated a highly strange event and exhausted all conventional possibilities. It means the surviving evidence was insufficient to decide what happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=%2C103&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BLERE (37) 10.04.1990 Classification: C Département: Indre-et-Loire Date de mise à jour: 22/03/2007...</span></span></span>

A useful investigation normally needs a reasonably exact time, location, viewing direction, angular height, duration, apparent motion and weather conditions. Independent witness statements, original photographs and unaltered video can add consistency. Without such information, investigators may be unable to test whether the reported light corresponded to an aircraft, celestial object, satellite, meteor, lantern or ground-based source.

This creates a crucial hierarchy:

**Explained cases** have enough information to support an identification or a strong conventional hypothesis. **Incomplete cases** remain open because the evidence is inadequate. **Well-documented unexplained cases** retain significant strangeness after ordinary explanations have been tested against reliable data.

Only the last category corresponds to GEIPAN’s D classification. Treating every C file as a lingering mystery reverses the logic of the system: the very absence of reliable information is why the case cannot carry much evidential weight. GEIPAN may revisit C and D files when new evidence or improved methods become available, as the Neuvy-le-Roi and Saint-Branchs reclassifications demonstrate.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## What the official files change

The official record does not establish that nothing unusual has ever been seen over Indre-et-Loire. It shows something more precise: the department’s best documented published reports currently provide no robust official example of an unexplained craft. The memorable local files instead reveal recurring mechanisms—low celestial objects that seem to follow cars, projected light moving across cloud, bright stars distorted by cameras and lanterns interpreted as coordinated formations.

Later investigation has generally weakened rather than strengthened the extraordinary interpretation of these cases. Neuvy-le-Roi moved from insufficiently explained to an identification with the Moon. Saint-Branchs moved from an old D classification to a laser explanation after investigators could compare it with better-documented displays. More recent cases have been matched to Procyon or judged probably to involve sky lanterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete10.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Notes d&#x27;enquete10</span></span></span>

The broader lesson for Indre-et-Loire’s UFO history is not that witnesses are unreliable in every respect. Witnesses may accurately report colour, duration and emotional impact while misjudging distance, size or movement. Official files are most useful when they preserve that human experience but separate it from the physical conclusion. In Touraine, the surviving evidence points mainly towards sincere observations of misunderstood lights—not towards a documented local phenomenon that remains both strange and evidentially strong.
