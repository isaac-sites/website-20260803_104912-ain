## Introduction

Noyal-sur-Vilaine in 1990 and Étrelles in 2014 remain the two clearest officially unresolved aerial reports in [Ille-et-Vilaine]({{ 'which-ille-et-vilaine-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }}), but they are not equally well documented. At Noyal-sur-Vilaine, a lone motorist reported a large, silent triangular object that changed direction; the surviving public record is striking but extremely sparse. At Étrelles, two witnesses in the same vehicle described a dark mass with two dim lights, followed by an apparently abrupt, high-speed departure. That later case prompted interviews, a site reconstruction and checks against weather, aviation and meteor data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-10-01224" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNOYAL-SUR-VILAINE (35) 26.10.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Ille-et-Vilaine_a0a684_unresolved_noyal_etr_6fd046-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Unresolved Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Both cases remain open because investigators could not match the reports convincingly to a known phenomenon. That does not mean an extraordinary craft was established. “Unresolved” means that no proposed explanation adequately accounted for the information available, while crucial measurements or independent records needed for a firm conclusion were absent.

## What the witnesses reported

### Noyal-sur-Vilaine: a silent triangle in 1990

At about 19:40 on 26 October 1990, a 40-year-old man was driving home from work near Noyal-sur-Vilaine when he reported seeing a single object in the sky. GEIPAN’s public case data describe an estimated distance of roughly 500 metres to one kilometre, a completely silent observation and an apparent volume comparable to a large passenger aircraft. The witness characterised the object as triangular or conical, with variable motion rather than a simple steady passage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/4804" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNOYAL-SUR-VILAINE (35) 26.10.1990NOYAL-SUR-VILAINE (35) 26.10.1990. The official summary reduces the episode to its most important features: an aircraft-sized triangular object, a change of direction and movement without audible noise. No identification was found, and the case remains in category D—GEIPAN’s designation for a phenomenon that is still unidentified after examination rather than merely unanalysable for lack of information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-10-01224" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNOYAL-SUR-VILAINE (35) 26.10.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Those details make the case memorable, particularly because the observation occurred only ten days before the famous nationwide reports of 5 November 1990. That later wave is now attributed by CNES to an atmospheric re-entry, but the timing alone does not connect the two events. Noyal-sur-Vilaine involved a separate date, an individual report and an apparently manoeuvring geometric form. It should therefore not be absorbed automatically into the explanation of the November wave. The official catalogue continues to list the Noyal case separately as unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=46.124763699209396&amp;customGetLongitude=2.4169921874999996&amp;customGetZoom=6&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id=All&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=50.52739681329302&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=41.72213058512578&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=9.8876953125&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-5.053710937500001&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=38%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de casNOYAL-SUR-VILAINE (35) 26.10.1990, 26/10/1990, D, Ille-et-Vilaine, Phénomène étrange à très étrange/ de consistance...</span></span></span>

The difficulty is that almost everything depends on one person’s visual judgement. The public file contains no photograph, radar return, second witness, precise angular measurements or detailed investigative report. Estimates such as “the size of a Boeing 747” or a distance below one kilometre cannot be treated as physical measurements when the object’s true distance was unknown. A small, nearer light arrangement and a large, distant object can produce similar apparent sizes, especially during a short night-time encounter.

Noyal-sur-Vilaine is therefore unresolved in a narrow but legitimate sense: the description is unusual and no convincing identification was recorded, yet the surviving evidence is too limited to reconstruct the event securely. It is stronger than a vague light report, but much weaker than a case supported by independent observations or instruments.

### Étrelles: two lights beneath a dark mass

The Étrelles encounter occurred shortly after 23:00 on 1 June 2014 in the Piquet industrial zone north of the town. Two people were travelling together in a service vehicle at only about 15–20 kilometres per hour. They said that a phenomenon appeared ahead and almost above the car, paused very briefly, then vanished towards the east at what seemed to be extraordinary speed. The entire event lasted only two or three seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Both witnesses described two small, round lights, white with a slight green tint. The lights remained at a constant separation and appeared to be attached to, or positioned beneath, a compact dark mass. One witness believed that this mass blocked visible stars and estimated its apparent size as roughly twice that of a large people carrier. Neither witness heard an engine, rotor noise or any other sound associated with the phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Their accounts were similar but not identical. One witness concentrated mainly on the two lights and said he could not describe a definite surrounding shape. The other reported a vague rectangular or solid dark form and described a bent trajectory: first approaching from the right, then turning sharply before heading east. The investigators treated that claimed turn as important, because a genuine change of course would make a simple meteor explanation much less satisfactory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B02851%20%282014311435%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The witnesses reported the incident promptly. A gendarmerie statement was taken the following morning, and one witness contacted GEIPAN. The police record is valuable because it captures the basic description before a long period of retelling: two aligned lights, sudden acceleration or a sharp directional change, silence and a duration of only a few seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B02851%20%282014311435%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## What GEIPAN could establish

The Étrelles investigation was considerably more substantial than the surviving work on Noyal-sur-Vilaine. Two GEIPAN investigators visited the site on 30 June 2014. They interviewed the witnesses separately using a structured cognitive-interview approach, returned with them to the observation point and recorded estimated directions, elevations, landmarks and environmental conditions. Follow-up telephone interviews examined the timing and the reported change of course.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The reconstruction supported several relatively secure points. The witnesses had an open view from a slow-moving vehicle; the night was clear, calm and starry; nearby buildings and vegetation did not significantly obstruct the relevant part of the sky. Both witnesses identified broadly the same disappearance point on the eastern horizon. Their descriptions also agreed on two lights, silence and a startlingly rapid departure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Investigators checked military air-control information. The French national air-operations centre reported ordinary airliner traffic but no unusual radar trace at the relevant place and time. This finding did not confirm the witnesses’ object, because radar coverage and detectability are never absolute. It did, however, leave investigators without an identified aircraft that matched the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

A surveillance camera at a nearby aerospace-electronics site was also considered. Its recordings showed nothing abnormal, but the investigation established that the camera was directed towards the entrance gate rather than the area of sky in question. Its failure to record the phenomenon was therefore neutral rather than disconfirming evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Why the meteor explanation fell short

The most concrete conventional candidate was a bright meteor recorded that night. Astronomical information placed a fragmented meteor at 23:12:30 local time, potentially visible from Étrelles. Its division into two components offered an obvious possible match for the witnesses’ paired lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN did not accept the match because two principal discrepancies remained:

* **Timing:** the witnesses placed their observation at approximately 23:07, about five and a half minutes before the recorded meteor. One witness recalled checking a phone displaying 23:12 only after the sighting, several minutes of discussion and a subsequent call.
* **Direction:** investigators calculated the meteor at an azimuth of about 70 degrees and an elevation of 15 degrees, while the witnesses reconstructed the phenomenon nearer an azimuth of 109–120 degrees and an elevation of roughly 45 degrees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Neither discrepancy is beyond challenge. A remembered time can be wrong by several minutes, even when linked to a later phone call, and direction estimates made after a two-second event are not survey-grade measurements. The apparent turn could also reflect changing perspective, eye movements or an imperfect reconstruction of a fast luminous passage. GEIPAN itself stresses that human testimony must be evaluated through both the amount of information and its reliability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* No, GEIPAN is a technical department of CNES (Centre National d’Études Spatiales), a public, industrial and commercial institute in cha...</span></span></span>

Nevertheless, accepting the meteor requires several errors to operate together: an incorrect event time, a large directional error and the perception of a dark, star-obscuring mass around the lights. Investigators judged that combination possible enough to prevent a stronger classification, but not persuasive enough to close the case.

Other suggestions—an aircraft, helicopter, drone, artificial searchlight, astronomical object or unusual weather effect—were considered and found to fit poorly. Silence alone does not eliminate aircraft, because sound can be weak or delayed, but the reported position, short duration and sudden directional behaviour did not correspond neatly to ordinary local traffic. No proposal accounted convincingly for all the central features at once.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-06-50078" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANETRELLES (35) 01.06.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What GEIPAN could not prove

The investigation could establish that two people sincerely reported a brief and puzzling event under apparently good viewing conditions. It could not establish the physical size, distance, speed or even the definite solidity of what they saw.

The claimed kilometre-scale distance was an impression against a largely featureless night sky. If that estimate was wrong, the inferred dimensions and speed could be wrong by a very large factor. A phenomenon appearing to cross ten kilometres almost instantaneously sounds extraordinary only if its distance and path are already known. Neither was measured.

The two witnesses also do not count as wholly independent sources. They occupied the same vehicle, viewed the event from almost the same position and spoke immediately afterwards. Although they later tried to avoid influencing each other’s written accounts, their initial discussion included possible explanations and recognition that they had witnessed the same event. GEIPAN specifically cited this interdependence, alongside the extreme brevity of the observation and uncertainty over the initial trajectory, as reasons not to place the case in its strongest unexplained category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-06-50078" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANETRELLES (35) 01.06.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

There was no photograph, video, physical trace or confirming radar record. One witness briefly searched the surroundings after the lights disappeared but found nothing. The case consequently rests on testimony strengthened by prompt reporting and field reconstruction, not on independently measurable physical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B02851%20%282014311435%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s 2014 investigation described Étrelles as D1: unexplained, with high consistency but moderate strangeness, and explicitly stated that the material existence of an object was possible rather than certain. The current case page, updated in June 2026, displays the broader category D while retaining the same strangeness score of 0.62 and the original conclusion that no hypothesis fits well. This administrative presentation does not amount to new evidence for an extraordinary origin; the underlying limitations remain unchanged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-06-50078" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANETRELLES (35) 01.06.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## How strong is the surviving evidence?

The two reports occupy different positions on an evidence scale.

**Noyal-sur-Vilaine has a striking description but a thin record.** A defined triangular form, apparent manoeuvring, complete silence and an aircraft-like scale make it more specific than many reports of distant lights. Yet the public evidence is essentially one witness account with no visible chain of detailed testing. Its unresolved status says more about the impossibility of selecting a firm explanation than about the probability of an exotic one.

**Étrelles has a better investigative record but a very short observation.** Prompt gendarmerie testimony, two witnesses, separate interviews, a site visit and explicit checks of a meteor and air traffic all strengthen the case. The published investigation also records disagreements and uncertainties rather than smoothing them away. That transparency is one of its strongest features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete113.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete113</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

At the same time, two or three seconds is exceptionally little time in which to judge shape, distance and movement. The witnesses shared one viewing position, and the strongest claims—near-vertical position, solid mass, abrupt turn and extreme speed—depend on visual interpretation rather than measurement. Étrelles is therefore credible as an account of an unexplained perception, but not decisive evidence of an objectively extraordinary vehicle.

GEIPAN’s categories are designed around this distinction. Category C means that information is too poor for a useful judgement; category D means that a case remains unexplained after investigation. Within the traditional subdivisions, D1 denotes moderate evidential consistency, while D2 is reserved for stronger cases involving such features as independent witnesses, recordings or physical traces. ## Why the cases remain open

Noyal-sur-Vilaine remains open because its unusual core description has never been matched convincingly to a known source, while the available record is too sparse to test most alternatives retrospectively. Étrelles remains open for the opposite reason: investigators tested several plausible explanations, but none fitted all the reported timing, direction, appearance and movement. The best candidate—a fragmented meteor—was close enough to deserve serious attention but differed enough in reconstructed time and position to prevent confident identification.

Later reporting has neither substantially strengthened nor overturned the original claims. No corroborating image, radar detection or independent witness has emerged publicly for either event. Nor has GEIPAN published a conventional explanation that resolves them. The result is an evidential middle ground: two apparently sincere encounters, one minimally documented and one carefully investigated, which remain unexplained without becoming proof of an unknown craft.

Within Ille-et-Vilaine’s UFO history, that restraint is precisely why the cases matter. They show that an official “unresolved” label can survive honest investigation while still carrying strict limits. Noyal-sur-Vilaine is an open question because too little objective information survives. Étrelles is a better-supported open question, but its brevity and lack of independent instrumentation prevent the witnesses’ impressions of mass, distance and speed from being treated as established physical facts.
