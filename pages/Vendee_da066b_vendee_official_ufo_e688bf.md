## Introduction

Vendée’s official UFO record is less mysterious than its reputation might suggest. In the GEIPAN files published by France’s national space agency, most sufficiently documented reports are attributed to familiar objects or events: the Moon and bright planets, satellites and spacecraft, aircraft, sky lanterns, meteors, rocket re-entries and even effects created by public displays. A sizeable second group remains in category C, not because investigators found strong evidence of an extraordinary craft, but because the reports arrived too late or lacked accurate times, directions, photographs or independent witnesses.

A November 2025 export of GEIPAN’s public database contains 46 entries assigned to Vendée. Thirteen are classified A, meaning confidently explained; 15 are B, meaning the proposed explanation is highly probable; 17 are C, meaning the evidence is insufficient for analysis; and only one is listed as D, unexplained despite usable information. ## The cases identified with confidence

GEIPAN, the official French body that collects and investigates reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena, separates a witness’s experience from the eventual identification. A sighting may appear startling and still receive an A classification when its timing, position and appearance match a known source without significant ambiguity. Category B is used where the fit is strong but some uncertainty remains.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* PAN de catégorie D: observation inexpliquée malgré les éléments en possession du GEIPAN.... Le GEIPAN, de part son expérience, évalue...</span></span></span>

Vendée’s explained files repeatedly fall into a small number of recognisable families.

**The Moon, planets and stars.** Some of the oldest reports involved bright astronomical bodies seen through haze, cloud or an unfamiliar gap in the landscape. A February 1976 case, formerly associated with Luçon and once treated as unexplained, was later reclassified as an A case involving the Moon. GEIPAN judged its strangeness to be low when reviewed under modern criteria. A March 1993 report from Saint-Gilles-Croix-de-Vie, describing an imposing luminous object in a cloudy sky, was also identified as the Moon seen through thin cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-02-00288?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;page=%2C149" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The most recent clear example came from Foussais-Payré on 2 December 2024. Two witnesses recorded a bright object that appeared bluish, oval and capable of erratic movement. GEIPAN’s analysis found that the underlying source was Venus. The apparent motion came from a hand-held smartphone, while night mode and digital zoom altered the planet’s colour and shape. A supposed high-speed departure was contradicted by the footage, which showed the camera moving across fixed foreground features. The case was classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-12-51609" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This case matters because it shows that photographs do not automatically settle a sighting. Images can strengthen an investigation, but only when investigators account for exposure settings, stabilisation, zoom, camera movement and optical artefacts. A bright point with no surrounding reference can also appear to move through the autokinetic effect, a known illusion in which small eye movements make a stationary light seem mobile against a dark sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Satellites and spacecraft.** Vendée’s clear coastal skies and wide horizons make artificial satellites conspicuous, particularly shortly after sunset or before dawn, when an observer is in darkness but an orbiting object remains sunlit. On 17 March 2009, a report from Île-d’Yeu was identified as the combined passage of the International Space Station and the space shuttle. Two months later, a luminous object moving west to east over Montaigu, apparently making a brief zigzag before disappearing, was classified A as an ISS passage. The apparent deviation did not outweigh the match in time and trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-03-02252" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A Challans report from 8 August 2012 illustrates why similar events sometimes receive a B rather than an A. The witness saw two bright objects apparently travelling in parallel at high altitude. Satellite records showed several relevant passages, including an exceptionally bright ISS accompanied by docked cargo vehicles. GEIPAN considered satellites the probable explanation but could not determine exactly which objects produced every part of the observation, so the case remained B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-08331" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Sky lanterns.** Orange or flame-coloured lights moving silently in groups occur frequently in the later Vendée files. Reports from Montaigu and Barbâtre in May 2008, La Rabatelière on New Year’s Day 2014, Saint-Georges-de-Pointindoux in August 2014, Bessay in August 2015 and Talmont-Saint-Hilaire during the 2015–16 New Year celebrations were all classified as definite or probable lantern sightings in the published dataset. Their common features include warm colour, slow drift, lack of sound, changing formations and gradual extinction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">phénomène étrange non identifié.&#124;&#124;Le 15...Read more...</span></span></span>

The Saint-Georges-de-Pointindoux case is particularly instructive. Witnesses reported about a dozen red-orange points forming a geometric arrangement before disappearing. Rather than treating the formation itself as evidence of a structured object, GEIPAN compared the lights’ colour, movement and fading with wind-borne lanterns and assigned a B classification. Separate, less adequately documented observations associated with the episode were left in category C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-09128?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=%2C33&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Aircraft, meteors and other human activity.** A motorist travelling from La Ferrière towards La Roche-sur-Yon on 13 January 2017 reported successive intense lights. Investigation linked them to aircraft and classified the case A. A filmed object near Beaulieu-sous-la-Roche in July 2017, trailing orange light at dusk, was likewise identified as an aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-01-09625" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A rapid flash followed by a blue “ball” seen between Sainte-Gemme-la-Plaine and Moreilles on 26 August 2021 was assessed as a probable meteor, or bolide. Its extremely short duration was central to that conclusion. In Les Epesses in October 2019, a filmed circular form was traced not to an object in flight but to a smoke ring created by a pyrotechnic performance. These cases demonstrate the breadth of sources entering the official system: GEIPAN investigates what a witness could not identify, not merely reports that resemble a conventional flying craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-08-51234?field_agregation_index_value=lune&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C6&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Atmospheric re-entry also appears in the departmental record. An April 2003 observation at Angles was judged probably to have involved re-entering space debris. Such events can seem unusually large or slow because multiple glowing fragments remain visible together, inviting witnesses to perceive one structured vehicle rather than a dispersed stream of material. CNES identifies rocket stages, spacecraft debris and satellites among the recurring sources of apparently extraordinary aerial displays.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">phénomène étrange non identifié.&#124;&#124;Le 15...Read more...</span></span></span>

## Why some reports remain category C

Category C is often misunderstood as a lesser version of “unexplained”. GEIPAN defines it differently: these are reports that cannot be analysed because reliable information is missing. They are not equivalent to category D cases, in which investigators possess enough evidence to test ordinary explanations but still cannot identify the phenomenon. GEIPAN notes that category C sits outside the normal progression from explained to unexplained because no secure conclusion can be reached at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* PAN de catégorie D: observation inexpliquée malgré les éléments en possession du GEIPAN.... Le GEIPAN, de part son expérience, évalue...</span></span></span>

Vendée’s files contain several clear examples of how a potentially interesting story becomes untestable.

At L’Île-d’Olonne, a witness reported seeing a huge, silent triangular form marked by three white lights during a summer night sometime between 1990 and 1993. The account was submitted in 2008, roughly 18 years later. The witness had been about ten years old, the precise date and even the year were unknown, and the companion present at the scene did not see the object. Without a date, investigators could not reconstruct the sky, examine air traffic or seek reports from nearby locations. GEIPAN therefore classified the case C rather than treating its triangular appearance as evidence of an extraordinary machine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-07-02028" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Liez case is even older. A witness described a bright phenomenon seen in August 1947 that appeared to move, stop and depart rapidly. It was reported to GEIPAN in December 2014, more than 67 years after the event. Missing information about the date, weather and observing circumstances prevented meaningful checking, leading again to a C classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1947-08-09060?customGetLattitude=41.2370859051563&amp;customGetLongitude=7.550679783806908&amp;customGetZoom=5&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=50.708634400828224&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=31.765537409484374&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=20.22890244005691&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-5.127542872443095&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C159&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other Vendée category C files involve recurring lights near La Roche-sur-Yon, luminous objects above the sea at Saint-Jean-de-Monts, lights photographed at L’Épine and three red lights observed from a boat near Île-d’Yeu. Their descriptions may be sincere, but sincerity does not supply the missing variables needed to distinguish among aircraft, boats, planets, lanterns, satellites, reflections and atmospheric effects. The official database’s 17 category C entries therefore measure documentary weakness more than unexplained physical strangeness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">phénomène étrange non identifié.&#124;&#124;Le 15...Read more...</span></span></span>

The distinction is important for readers comparing UFO statistics. Combining C and D cases into a single “unexplained” total would substantially distort Vendée’s record. A report with no exact date and one distant witness is not evidentially equivalent to a multi-witness event with contemporaneous statements, photographs and verified environmental data.

## The one official D case

The public Vendée dataset contains one case classified D: an incident reported by the crew of a yacht off Les Sables-d’Olonne during the night of 3–4 July 1997. At about 4am, the person at the helm saw an intense light descend rapidly for two or three seconds, pause briefly and then depart horizontally at high apparent speed. After two crewmates were called, all three reportedly saw a second yellow-ochre, diamond-shaped phenomenon with lights at its corners, moving near cloud level for roughly ten to fifteen seconds. GEIPAN records that the two observations were not explained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1997-08-01465?field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=%2C381&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is the strongest officially unresolved Vendée file chiefly because it has more than a single retrospective witness. Three statements were collected, and the second phenomenon was reportedly shared. Its classification indicates that the available material was considered sufficiently substantial to resist simple dismissal.

Even so, D does not mean that an exotic vehicle was established. No radar confirmation, calibrated image, physical trace or independent observation from land is listed on the case page. Apparent distance, speed and altitude over dark water are especially difficult to judge because the horizon offers few stable reference points. The first light and the later diamond-shaped display may also have had separate causes. The official conclusion is therefore narrow: investigators did not identify the phenomena from the evidence available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1997-08-01465?field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=%2C381&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That limited conclusion is more informative than either extreme interpretation. It does not justify declaring the report solved, but neither does it establish a craft, intelligence or origin. It remains an unresolved witness case of moderate-to-strong consistency within GEIPAN’s classification system.

## What the records reveal about local patterns


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Taken together, the official cases show that Vendée’s landscape shapes what people report. The Atlantic coast and islands provide broad horizons with few visual reference points. That makes satellites, astronomical bodies, distant aircraft, meteors and lights over the sea particularly noticeable, while also making their altitude and distance difficult to estimate. Reports from roads add another source of uncertainty because the witness and foreground are moving simultaneously.

</div>

The timing of reports also follows recognisable human patterns. Lantern cases gather around weddings, festivals and New Year celebrations. Spacecraft and satellites become prominent when exceptionally bright passes occur. New aerospace activity introduces unfamiliar sights, including rocket exhaust clouds, satellite trains and re-entry fragments. CNES notes that technological change continually creates new kinds of reports even though their sources are conventional and human-made.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/dossiers/ovnis-pan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ovnis pan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ovnis pan</span></span></span>

The records also show how investigation changes outcomes. Exact times allow satellite passes, astronomical positions and aircraft movements to be reconstructed. Direction and elevation estimates can distinguish a planet from a moving object. Several independent witnesses can test whether apparent motion was shared. Original files preserve details that fade rapidly from memory. Photographs and videos help most when the full, unedited material and device information are available.

Conversely, the recurring category C cases show what cannot usually be repaired later. A vivid account submitted decades after the event may retain its emotional force while losing almost every checkable feature. Without an exact date, investigators cannot reliably examine the sky, weather, launches, aviation records or nearby reports. The absence of an explanation in such circumstances says little about the nature of the original stimulus.

## The clearest reading of Vendée’s official record

GEIPAN’s Vendée files support three firm conclusions. First, most cases with adequate evidence have ordinary explanations, especially astronomical objects, satellites, aircraft, lanterns and short-lived atmospheric or aerospace events. Secondly, many apparently unresolved reports are actually untestable category C files whose evidential gaps prevent any conclusion. Thirdly, one published departmental case—the 1997 yacht sighting off Les Sables-d’Olonne—remains genuinely unexplained within the official system, but its classification establishes only that no satisfactory identification was reached.

The most useful division is therefore not between believers and sceptics, but between explained, probably explained, insufficiently documented and properly unresolved reports. Vendée’s official archive is valuable precisely because it preserves those differences rather than turning every unfamiliar light into the same kind of mystery.
