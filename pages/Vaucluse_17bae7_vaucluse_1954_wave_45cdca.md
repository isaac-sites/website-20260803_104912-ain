## Introduction

France’s nationwide flying-saucer wave reached [Vaucluse]({{ 'why-vaucluses-strangest-sightings-still/' | relative_url }}) most visibly on 14 October 1954, when residents of Fontaine-de-Vaucluse reported a large, pale object apparently hovering above the village. Contemporary newspapers described a disc with a rounded top, changing coloured lights and no obvious sound or smoke. Two military jets from Orange-Caritat were sent to investigate, giving the story the appearance of an attempted aerial interception. Yet the pilots subsequently reported seeing no unknown craft, while a large scientific balloon travelling across southern France offered a strong conventional explanation. A separate fiery object reported during the same period was identified as a meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The episode matters because it shows how the 1954 wave worked at local level. A real observation, excited witnesses, hurried press reporting and military activity became woven into a much stronger story than the available evidence could securely support. Eleven days later, the mayor of Châteauneuf-du-Pape turned the saucer craze into lasting folklore by issuing a humorous ban on flying discs and cigars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/vaucluse/2010/07/25/l-arrete-du-25-octobre-1954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">l arrete du 25 octobre 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Dauphiné LibéréChateauneuf du Pape. ## What did Fontaine-de-Vaucluse witnesses report?

The alarm began around lunchtime on 14 October. According to accounts published the following day, children first drew attention to a white object moving or hovering slowly above Fontaine-de-Vaucluse. A crowd gathered, and some observers examined it through large binoculars. They described a broad white disc surmounted by a dome, likening the profile to a silver bowler hat. Its apparent outline reportedly shifted between a disc, a diamond and a domed shape as it changed orientation. Bright points around its lower edge were said to vary from white or violet to red.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The reported duration was unusually long for a fleeting light: versions of the story place the object over the area for roughly an hour or more. Witnesses said there was no smoke and no audible engine. These details helped distinguish the sighting, in the public imagination, from an ordinary aircraft passing overhead. They also encouraged newspapers to treat it as one of the more impressive incidents of the French wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

Some of the description nevertheless depends on viewing conditions that were never adequately documented. The surviving reports do not provide dependable measurements of elevation, angular size, distance or wind direction. Without those, an observer cannot determine whether a pale object is a modest balloon relatively nearby or an enormous craft much farther away. Descriptions such as “disc”, “dome” and “diamond” may record a changing viewing angle, optical effects in binoculars or attempts to interpret an unfamiliar object whose actual scale was unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The use of binoculars did not necessarily solve that problem. High-contrast objects viewed against a bright sky can acquire coloured fringes, distorted edges or an apparent structure that changes as focus and alignment shift. The Fontaine-de-Vaucluse reports are therefore valuable as witness testimony, but they are not the equivalent of calibrated astronomical or military observations.

## Did military jets chase the object?

The involvement of Orange-Caritat air base became the most dramatic element of the story. Fontaine-de-Vaucluse officials contacted the base, and two jets already available for flying duties were directed over the area. From the ground, their turns and changes of altitude appeared to some witnesses to be a pursuit. Early newspaper accounts even claimed that the pilots had sighted the object by radio, dived towards it and been left behind when it accelerated away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

That account was contradicted almost immediately. The French air authorities issued a statement saying that the two experienced officers had searched at different altitudes but had observed no unknown craft. Other contemporary reporting indicated that the pilots arrived only after the reported object had disappeared, rather than engaging it in a high-speed chase.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

This contradiction is central to any assessment of the case. The crowd saw jets manoeuvring in the same part of the sky where the object had been reported. It was easy to interpret that sequence as interception and pursuit, especially once journalists and bystanders exchanged information by telephone. The military account, however, does not support the claim that the pilots acquired, followed or were outrun by an extraordinary vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The scramble still has evidential value, but not in the way sometimes claimed. It confirms that local officials made a detailed enough report for the air base to check it. It does not confirm that military observers saw a saucer. The difference between responding to a report and independently verifying it is one of the most important distinctions in the whole Vaucluse episode.

## Why a balloon is the leading explanation

A scientific balloon was already being discussed at Orange on the day of the Fontaine-de-Vaucluse sighting. Contemporary reporting said that pilots knew of a balloon seen earlier travelling towards the south-east. A later local historical account connects the broader run of southern French observations with a notice from a physics institute in Milan seeking the recovery of a stratospheric balloon that had crossed into France. The balloon reportedly measured about 28 metres across, weighed around 110 kilograms and carried scientific equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

Such an object could account for several otherwise striking features:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Slow or apparently stationary movement:** a high-altitude balloon can seem motionless when viewed from a distance, especially when its movement is mostly towards or away from the observer.
* **Changing shape:** a large balloon, its suspended payload and rigging may present different profiles as they rotate or are seen through haze.
* **Silence:** a drifting balloon would produce no aircraft engine noise audible from the ground.
* **Brightness and colour changes:** sunlight reflected from a pale envelope or metallic equipment can change sharply with orientation, cloud cover and viewing instruments.
* **Disappearance into cloud:** a balloon entering haze or a cloud layer can appear to vanish suddenly without making any extraordinary manoeuvre.

</div>

The balloon hypothesis is not proven by a surviving recovery record tying a particular serial-numbered instrument directly to Fontaine-de-Vaucluse. The historical evidence available online does not include a precise trajectory calculation matching the balloon’s location to the village at the reported time. It is therefore more accurate to call it a strong explanation than a conclusively documented identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.avignoncitemillenaire.com/anecdotes-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avignoncitemillenaire.com">[Avignon Cité Millénaire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avignoncitemillenaire.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avignon Cité Millénaire Anecdotes 7-Soucoupes volantes &#124; Avignon Cité Millénaire</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Avignon Cité Millénaire Anecdotes 7-Soucoupes volantes &#124; Avignon Cité Millénaire</span></span></span>

Even so, the hypothesis fits the basic observation better than the later “faster than jets” narrative. The object was initially described as lingering silently in daylight for a prolonged period, behaviour compatible with a balloon but difficult to reconcile with the claim that it was demonstrating extreme speed. The spectacular acceleration appears chiefly in the crowd’s interpretation of the jets’ arrival, while the pilots themselves denied seeing an unknown object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The best cautious assessment is therefore that witnesses probably saw a genuine object, not an invented one, but that the object was plausibly a large research or meteorological balloon whose unfamiliar appearance was transformed by distance, optics and the expectations of the 1954 saucer wave.

## Where the meteor explanation fits

The balloon explanation should not be stretched to cover every unusual light reported in south-eastern France that October. Local retrospectives describe a second phenomenon, a conspicuous “ball of fire”, which encouraged further speculation but was identified by an observatory near Digne as a meteor. Contemporary material from Haute-Provence records an observatory observing a meteor during a series of reports involving bright objects crossing the regional sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.avignoncitemillenaire.com/anecdotes-7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: avignoncitemillenaire.com">[avignoncitemillenaire.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">avignoncitemillenaire.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Avignon Cité Millénaire Anecdotes 7-Soucoupes volantes &#124; Avignon Cité Millénaire</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Avignon Cité Millénaire Anecdotes 7-Soucoupes volantes &#124; Avignon Cité Millénaire</span></span></span>

A meteor and a balloon behave very differently. A meteor normally appears briefly, crosses part of the sky rapidly and may change from white to orange or red as it fragments or fades. A balloon can remain visible for a long time and drift slowly. Treating both as a single manoeuvring craft creates a seemingly complex event — one capable of hovering, changing shape and then racing across the sky — when witnesses may actually have observed separate conventional phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/16oct1954hauteprovencef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">France 1954, 16 octobre, Haute-Provence - Patrick GrossL&#x27;observatoire de Haute-Provence a observé ce météore sur son passage...</span></span></span>

This distinction helps explain why saucer waves can produce apparently contradictory reports. Once public attention is concentrated on the sky, unrelated objects are more likely to be noticed, reported and placed within the same story. A slow balloon seen in daylight, a bright meteor later in the day and military jets responding to a telephone call can become, through repetition, one coherent encounter with an elusive craft.

The meteor identification does not directly prove what hovered over Fontaine-de-Vaucluse. It does show that at least part of the surrounding cluster involved an astronomical event recognised by professional observers. That weakens attempts to treat every report from the period as mutually confirming evidence of a single unknown presence.

## How reporting amplified the encounter

The Fontaine-de-Vaucluse case survives largely through newspaper reports and later compilations rather than a complete investigation file containing original witness interviews, weather data, photographs and aircraft logs. The contemporary press preserved valuable detail, but it also preserved mutually incompatible versions of the same afternoon. One version had the jets spotting and chasing the object; another had them conducting an unsuccessful search after it was gone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The first version was naturally more memorable. A military pursuit supplied movement, authority and danger to what might otherwise have been a report of a distant white object. Once printed, the chase narrative was repeated in later UFO catalogues, sometimes with the uncertainty reduced and the alleged acceleration treated as an established fact. The official denial was also repeated, but it rarely carried the same dramatic force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The case also demonstrates how witness numbers can be misleading. A crowd may establish that something visible was present, but it does not produce dozens of independent observations if most people gathered after hearing the same cries, looked through the same binoculars and discussed what they thought they saw. Group observation can strengthen confidence that an object existed while simultaneously encouraging a shared interpretation of its shape and behaviour.

None of this requires dishonesty. Residents, journalists and military personnel could all report their experiences sincerely while disagreeing about the sequence. The most likely point of distortion is not fabrication but reconstruction: observers fitting jets, a slowly drifting object and its eventual disappearance into a familiar saucer-chase storyline.

## The anti-saucer by-law became the lasting legacy

On 25 October 1954, less than two weeks after the Fontaine-de-Vaucluse affair, Châteauneuf-du-Pape mayor Lucien Jeune issued the department’s most famous contribution to flying-saucer culture. The municipal order prohibited craft described as flying saucers or flying cigars, regardless of nationality, from flying over, landing in or taking off from the commune. Any offending craft that landed was to be impounded, with local rural guards charged with enforcing the order.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/vaucluse/2010/07/25/l-arrete-du-25-octobre-1954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">l arrete du 25 octobre 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Dauphiné LibéréChateauneuf du Pape. L’arrêté du 25 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

The wording imitated the formal style of municipal law, but its target made its humorous purpose obvious. Rather than documenting a landing in the vineyards, the order capitalised on a subject filling French newspapers. Later accounts have consistently treated it as a publicity-minded joke that helped keep the wine village in the public eye.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lesitinerairesdecharlotte.fr/saviez-vous-que-les-soucoupes-volantes-sont-interdites-a-chateauneuf-du-pape/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lesitinerairesdecharlotte.fr">[lesitinerairesdecharlotte.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lesitinerairesdecharlotte.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Saviez-vous que les soucoupes volantes sont interdites à</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Saviez-vous que les soucoupes volantes sont interdites à</span></span></span>

The order is sometimes presented as evidence that local authorities were seriously afraid of extraterrestrial visitors. Its real significance is cultural. It shows that by late October the saucer wave had become familiar enough to support satire: readers were expected to recognise references to discs, cigars, landings and mysterious nationalities without further explanation.

The by-law also outlasted the sightings that inspired it. Fontaine-de-Vaucluse’s detailed witness reports are now scattered through archives and specialist case collections, whereas the Châteauneuf-du-Pape ban continues to appear in journalism, tourism and wine writing. The joke has therefore become Vaucluse’s most durable 1954 saucer story, even though it provides no evidence for an actual unidentified craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/vaucluse/2010/07/25/l-arrete-du-25-octobre-1954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[ledauphine.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">l arrete du 25 octobre 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Dauphiné LibéréChateauneuf du Pape. ## How strong is the 1954 Vaucluse case today?

The 1954 wave left Vaucluse with a significant historical case, but not a strong case for an extraordinary aircraft. The underlying Fontaine-de-Vaucluse observation was probably real in the limited sense that many people saw an object in the sky. The description was sufficiently unusual to prompt a military check, and the conflicting accounts make the episode more complex than a simple newspaper invention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

The evidence becomes weaker when more ambitious claims are examined. There is no reliable confirmation that the pilots saw the object, no radar record in the surviving public accounts, no photograph, no recovered material and no continuous record establishing extraordinary acceleration. The official military statement directly contradicts the supposed jet pursuit. Meanwhile, a large scientific balloon was known to be crossing the region, and an observatory identified a separate fiery appearance as a meteor.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/14oct1954fontainedevauclusef.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 14 octobre, Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, Vaucluse...</span></span></span>

Later reporting has therefore weakened the most sensational version while preserving the case’s value as social and local history. Fontaine-de-Vaucluse illustrates how a strange but potentially ordinary object could acquire elaborate mechanical features through binocular observation and press description. Orange-Caritat shows how a precautionary military response could be remembered as confirmation. Châteauneuf-du-Pape shows how quickly the resulting excitement passed from news into humour and folklore.

The fairest classification is **plausibly explained but not documented well enough for absolute certainty**. A balloon is the best fit for the prolonged daylight object, and a meteor accounts for at least one other dramatic regional report. What remains unresolved is mainly the exact identity and path of the object seen from Fontaine-de-Vaucluse, not compelling evidence that an unknown craft outflew French military jets.

    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
