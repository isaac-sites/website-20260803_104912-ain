## Introduction

Official investigations have changed the meaning of many Côtes-d’Armor UFO reports. Apparent waves have often broken apart once investigators separated observations by date, time, direction and witness. Objects that seemed to belong to one prolonged mystery were reassessed as unrelated events involving lightning, Venus, aircraft, satellites, meteors, atmospheric re-entry, drones or lanterns. Other files were downgraded not because investigators proved an ordinary cause, but because the evidence was too incomplete to support any firm conclusion.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Cotes-d_Armor_789c20_geipan_reassessed_ca_f00621-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Solved Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The Plérin reports of late December 1994 are the clearest local example. Initially, several sightings made over four nights could be read as a cluster. GEIPAN’s later review instead treated each observation on its own evidence. One was probably lightning over the sea, another was consistent with Venus, while others remained unusable because their timings and descriptions were too weak. The result was not a single solved UFO case, but the dismantling of an apparent wave into several different evidential problems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-12-09759?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=10&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 31.12.199411 Dec 2017 — Or, la présence d&#x27;un orage en mer, visible depuis la côte, est avérée par l&#x27;enquête de gendarme...</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN tests a sighting

GEIPAN, the French national body within the space agency CNES that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena, does not classify a case solely by asking whether the witness appeared sincere. Its process begins with the testimony, then compares the reported time, position, movement, appearance and duration against known astronomical, meteorological, aeronautical and human-made phenomena. Depending on the case, investigators may consult the gendarmerie, civil aviation, defence bodies, weather services, astronomers, satellite data and image-analysis specialists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPANA classification methodology consolidated over decades</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Sighting reports and investigation conclusions are published on the GEIPAN website (www.geipan.fr) while maintaining the anonymity of wit...</span></span></span>

Two concepts shape the final judgement. **Residual strangeness** measures how poorly the best conventional hypothesis fits after investigation. **Consistency** measures whether the available information is detailed, coherent and reliable enough to support a conclusion. A striking story may therefore receive a weak classification if its timing is approximate, its direction is unknown or its documentation was created long after the event. Conversely, a relatively ordinary-looking light can be identified confidently when its path and timing match an aircraft or satellite precisely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPANSince 2008, a more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The categories are easy to misunderstand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A** means the phenomenon has been identified with a high degree of confidence.
* **B** means a particular explanation is considered probable.
* **C** means the evidence is insufficient or unreliable, so the case cannot be assessed properly.
* **D1 and D2** are unexplained cases, with D2 requiring stronger strangeness and stronger supporting evidence.

</div>

A category C report is therefore not halfway between “explained” and “alien”. It sits outside that scale because investigators lack the information needed to decide. GEIPAN itself stresses that C cases should be treated separately from A, B, D1 and D2 cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Around 200 lead to investigations with diffusion on the GEIPAN website (www.geipan.fr) of sighting reports and investigation conclusions...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters greatly in Côtes-d’Armor. Some of its most atmospheric reports survive as mysteries mainly because essential data were never recorded. The database describes files from Plérin, Ploubalay, Le Cambout and Plévenon as lacking reliable information. Those entries preserve the fact that something was reported, but they do not provide positive evidence for an extraordinary object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/52134" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 28.12.1994Department Côtes-d&#x27;Armor Classification C Date of update 11/12/2017 Type of phenomenon. ## Why the Plérin “wave” was split apart

The Plérin dossier demonstrates how grouping reports can create a stronger impression than the evidence warrants. The surviving records refer to observations on 28, 29, 30 and 31 December 1994, involving different witnesses and different times of day. Some occurred in the early evening; others were reported between roughly 6am and 6.45am. Treating all of them as appearances by one object would require assuming a connection that the witness evidence did not establish.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-12-09759?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=10&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 31.12.199411 Dec 2017 — Or, la présence d&#x27;un orage en mer, visible depuis la côte, est avérée par l&#x27;enquête de gendarme...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN therefore divided the material into separate phenomena rather than preserving it as one continuous incident. That analytical decision was as important as any proposed explanation. Once the reports were detached from one another, the apparent pattern weakened: a succession of unusual lights over several days became a set of observations with different likely causes and different evidential quality.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-12-09759?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=10&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 31.12.199411 Dec 2017 — Or, la présence d&#x27;un orage en mer, visible depuis la côte, est avérée par l&#x27;enquête de gendarme...</span></span></span>

The evening observation of 29 December was reassessed against weather evidence. A storm at sea, visible from the coast, was confirmed through the gendarmerie enquiry. GEIPAN concluded that flashes associated with that storm offered a probable explanation and classified the report B. This did not mean that the witnesses had fabricated anything. It meant that a real, distant meteorological event could account for lights that appeared unusual from their viewpoint.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-12-09759?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=10&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 31.12.199411 Dec 2017 — Or, la présence d&#x27;un orage en mer, visible depuis la côte, est avérée par l&#x27;enquête de gendarme...</span></span></span>

Part of the 31 December material was separately attributed, with probability rather than absolute proof, to Venus. Bright planets can appear much larger or more mobile than expected when they are viewed through haze, thin cloud, intervening branches or from a moving vehicle. Without a measured angle or fixed landmark, a witness may also underestimate how long a bright astronomical object has remained in the same area of sky. GEIPAN consequently recorded one Plérin file as a category B Venus identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casListe des nouveaux cas; VENUS — PLERIN (22) 31.12.1994, 31/12/1994, B · Côtes-d&#x27;Armor, Vénus; PLERIN (22) 31.12.1...</span></span></span>

Other Plérin reports did not receive equally neat solutions. The observation dated 28 December was placed in category C, with very low consistency, because GEIPAN judged that dependable information was missing. Another 31 December file was also classified C. These reports were not disproved; they were found too poorly documented to sustain either an identification or a strong unexplained classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/52134" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">PLERIN (22) 28.12.1994Department Côtes-d&#x27;Armor Classification C Date of update 11/12/2017 Type of phenomenon. This mixed outcome is the real lesson of the case. An official review does not always replace a dramatic account with one universal explanation. It may instead reveal that the original “case” was an artificial bundle: one weather event, one probable planet and several observations that cannot now be reconstructed.

## Timing and direction can overturn first impressions

Witness descriptions commonly emphasise colour, shape or apparent movement. Investigators often find that the more decisive facts are less dramatic: the exact minute, the compass direction, the elevation above the horizon and whether the observer was stationary. Those details allow a report to be checked against objects that were actually present in that part of the sky.

The October 2013 case at Plélo shows the value of this approach. The witness reported three lights moving together. A check of satellite-passage data found that the Chinese Yaogan 17 satellite triplet crossed at the relevant time on a trajectory corresponding to the observation. GEIPAN classified the case B, meaning that the satellite explanation was considered highly probable. The unusual three-object formation was not an obstacle to identification; it was the feature that made the match unusually specific.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-10-08576" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Aircraft cases work in much the same way, although perspective can make them difficult to recognise. A plane approaching almost head-on may appear to hover because its sideways movement is too small to detect. Landing lights can dominate the view and hide the usual red, green and flashing navigation lights. When it turns, the object may seem suddenly to accelerate, change form or vanish.

Two observations from Ploumilliau in September 2015 were eventually classified as probable aircraft. GEIPAN noted that the reported events were conspicuous enough to attract attention, including through sound, yet no wider group of witnesses reported anything comparably anomalous. The investigations treated the descriptions as compatible with planes rather than as evidence for a recurring unexplained craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-09-09329?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C55" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A Saint-Cast-le-Guildo report from 18 July 2024 received the stronger category A classification as a light aircraft. The contrast with older category C files is instructive: modern flight information, prompt reporting and better positional data can turn an ambiguous moving light into a specific identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;page=4%2C0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Ordinary causes do not all look ordinary

The solved and probably solved Côtes-d’Armor cases cover a broad range of phenomena. That variety helps explain why witnesses can reasonably fail to recognise what they see. There is no single stereotypical “misidentified UFO”. Different causes produce different colours, speeds, formations and apparent behaviours.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

**Satellites** may travel silently in straight lines, brighten suddenly as sunlight catches a reflective surface or appear in groups. Besides the Plélo triplet, a September 2025 observation at Belle-Isle-en-Terre was classified B as a satellite flare. The growing visibility of satellite trains and reflective spacecraft has added a recurring source of unfamiliar lights to recent casework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

**Atmospheric re-entry and bright meteors** can produce more spectacular effects. They may fragment, leave glowing trails or appear to move slowly because they cover a large distance high in the atmosphere. A department-wide event on 24 September 2004 was classified B as atmospheric re-entry. More recently, a report from Saint-Michel-en-Grève on 4 August 2024 received the same probable explanation, while a Pleudihen-sur-Rance observation on 22 December 2024 was identified as a bright meteor and classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

**Drones** create a different set of impressions. They can hover, turn sharply, carry bright LEDs and operate at heights that are hard to estimate at night. A Loudéac observation from January 2015 was classified B as a drone, while a Pleumeur-Bodou report from June that year was attributed probably to a toy drone. Such cases show why apparent hovering or abrupt manoeuvres are no longer as diagnostically unusual as they would have seemed in earlier decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=93%2C0&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Lanterns and balloons** can look purposeful because they drift together, change spacing and disappear one after another as their flames fail or cloud obscures them. GEIPAN’s Côtes-d’Armor records include probable lantern cases at Île-de-Bréhat, Saint-Brieuc and Saint-Brandan, as well as a Saint-Brieuc report attributed to a weather balloon. These explanations depend heavily on wind, duration and the way several lights move relative to one another.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Even birds can enter the record. A Callac observation from October 2013 was classified B as a probable bird. Under unusual lighting, a bird’s body or underside can catch the Sun while its wings become difficult to distinguish, producing an intermittent or oddly shaped luminous appearance. The importance of the case lies less in the particular identification than in the reminder that the observer’s description records an appearance, not necessarily the physical nature of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Reassessment can weaken or strengthen a case

Investigation is not automatically debunking. In principle, the same checks that identify aircraft or planets can strengthen an unresolved report if known traffic, weather and astronomical sources fail to match and the testimony remains detailed and internally coherent. GEIPAN states that D cases require both substantial residual strangeness and sufficient consistency, and that stronger D classifications normally involve field investigation and cognitive interviews with witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Around 200 lead to investigations with diffusion on the GEIPAN website (www.geipan.fr) of sighting reports and investigation conclusions...</span></span></span>

Côtes-d’Armor retains a small number of older D cases, notably Binic in March 1990 and Dinan in September 1999. Their presence means that official review did not explain every local report. Nevertheless, the label records an investigative outcome, not the discovery of an extraterrestrial vehicle. It means that no satisfactory conventional hypothesis was established from the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casListe des nouveaux cas; VENUS — PLERIN (22) 31.12.1994, 31/12/1994, B · Côtes-d&#x27;Armor, Vénus; PLERIN (22) 31.12.1...</span></span></span>

Older files face an additional problem: investigators cannot always reproduce the methods now expected for a strong unexplained classification. Witnesses may no longer be available, landscapes may have changed, and precise angular measurements were not taken at the time. A later review may therefore find a story genuinely puzzling while still recognising that its evidential foundations fall below modern standards.

GEIPAN’s published reviews explicitly allow classifications to change when new information appears. The organisation has also revisited older D cases because improved astronomical software, satellite records, mapping tools and aviation data can reveal possibilities that were unavailable during the first investigation. Reclassification is therefore not an admission that the original witnesses were dishonest; it is a normal consequence of testing an old report with better evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Around 200 lead to investigations with diffusion on the GEIPAN website (www.geipan.fr) of sighting reports and investigation conclusions...</span></span></span>

The reverse limitation also applies. A category C case cannot be made stronger simply through repeated retelling. Newspaper repetition, later summaries or inclusion in UFO catalogues may make an incident more familiar, but they do not restore a missing time, direction or independent witness statement. In some Côtes-d’Armor cases, later investigation weakened the mystery chiefly by exposing how little usable information had survived.

## What the reassessed record says about Côtes-d’Armor

The department’s official record does not support the idea of one recurring phenomenon responsible for all local reports. It instead shows several overlapping sources of confusion, each dominant under different conditions: bright planets near the horizon, aircraft seen at deceptive angles, satellites travelling in formation, meteors and re-entering debris, wind-borne lanterns, weather balloons, drones and occasional birds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Nor does the database show that every unexplained case was eventually solved. Binic and Dinan remain notable precisely because many surrounding reports were assigned conventional causes or judged too weak to assess. Their unresolved status should be preserved, but interpreted narrowly. It indicates a residue after investigation, not proof that the wider collection represents an extraordinary aerial wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casListe des nouveaux cas; VENUS — PLERIN (22) 31.12.1994, 31/12/1994, B · Côtes-d&#x27;Armor, Vénus; PLERIN (22) 31.12.1...</span></span></span>

The Plérin review provides the most useful model for reading the department’s UFO history. Reports that occur close together in place and time may feel connected, especially when circulated through local conversation or press coverage. Official investigation asks a stricter question: do the timelines, directions and physical characteristics actually require a common cause? In Plérin, the answer was no.

That reframing makes Côtes-d’Armor’s UFO record less sensational but more informative. It reveals how human observation interacts with an increasingly crowded sky, how missing data can preserve uncertainty, and how apparently coherent waves may dissolve when every report is tested separately. The remaining mysteries are best understood not as the cases investigators refused to explain, but as the limited set for which neither an adequate conventional match nor enough evidence for a confident judgement was available.
