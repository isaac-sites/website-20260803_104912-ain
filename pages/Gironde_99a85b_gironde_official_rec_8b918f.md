## Introduction

[Gironde]({{ 'why-girondes-strangest-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO archive does not reveal a coherent sequence of mysterious craft operating over Bordeaux, Arcachon Bay or the Médoc. It reveals something more ordinary, but more useful: repeated encounters with aircraft, planets, meteors, satellites, balloons, lanterns, drones and visual effects that initially looked unfamiliar to witnesses. A smaller group of reports remains uncertain chiefly because investigators lacked dependable information, not because the evidence pointed strongly towards an extraordinary vehicle.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Gironde_99a85b_gironde_official_rec_8b918f-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Record" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction is central to reading the public archive maintained by GEIPAN, the unidentified aerospace phenomena unit of the French space agency CNES. Its records preserve what witnesses reported, but they also show what happened when investigators checked directions, times, weather, astronomical positions, air traffic and the behaviour of familiar objects. In Gironde, those reconstructions usually reduce rather than strengthen the mystery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the classifications really mean


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

GEIPAN’s archive is an investigation database, not a list of confirmed anomalies. Category A is used when a phenomenon is identified with high confidence. Category B means that an ordinary explanation is considered probable, although not established as completely as in an A case. Category C denotes a report that cannot be assessed adequately because reliable information is missing. Only category D is intended for cases that remain unexplained despite sufficiently substantial investigation.

</div>

This matters because “unidentified” can describe two very different situations. A category D report has resisted explanation after the available evidence has been examined. A category C report is unresolved because the evidence is too weak, incomplete or inconsistent to support a sound conclusion. Treating both as equivalent would make a poorly remembered light as significant as a well-documented event that survived detailed checking.

The wider national figures reinforce this point. CNES reports that roughly a quarter of archived phenomena are clearly identified and about two-fifths probably identified. Nearly a third remain unidentified for lack of data, while only a small minority remain unexplained after investigation. Gironde’s records broadly fit that pattern: identified or probably identified reports form the visible centre of the archive, while weak category C files should not be mistaken for a hidden body of strong unexplained evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

## Aircraft, planets and meteors explain many reports

The most revealing Gironde cases are often those in which an observation sounded strange until investigators reconstructed the viewing conditions.

In October 2013, a motorist travelling from Bordeaux towards Bayonne saw a white point apparently moving south, disappearing and then seeming to return north. The witness noticed neither navigation lights nor an obvious aircraft shape. GEIPAN checked the position of the low evening Sun and data from flight-tracking records. It concluded that the witness had probably seen two aircraft crossing at different moments, their bodies brightly illuminated while their wings and normal identifying features were difficult to distinguish. What felt like one object reversing direction was more plausibly two separate aeroplanes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-10-08579" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan[A63] de BORDEAUX (33) VERS BAYONNE (64) 07.10.20137 Oct 2013 — Type de phénomène. Avion de ligne. Etrangeté. 0.38. Documents. Docu...</span></span></span>

A May 2018 report from Mios shows how an astronomical object can appear active rather than fixed. The witness filmed a bright light that seemed to move from side to side, rise and fall, approach and retreat, and change colour. GEIPAN matched its position closely to Jupiter, then explained the reported motion through autokinesis—the tendency of a stationary point of light to appear to move when viewed against a dark, featureless background. Atmospheric turbulence accounted for the colour and brightness changes, while cloud ended the observation. The case was classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-05-50527" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIOS (33) 03.05.20183 May 2018 — Département Gironde Classification A. Other Gironde entries identify Venus in reports from Pauillac, [Gujan-Mestras]({{ 'gujan-mestras/' | relative_url }}) and Saint-Christoly-Médoc. These cases are not trivial simply because the answer is a planet. Venus is exceptionally bright, is often seen near the horizon and can appear to pulse, drift or change colour through haze and atmospheric disturbance. Without fixed reference points, witnesses can also misjudge whether it is stationary, approaching or moving alongside them. The archive’s repeated planetary identifications therefore demonstrate a recurring mechanism rather than a series of unrelated mistakes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Fast, dramatic events also enter the system. A January 2022 report from Sainte-Hélène was classified as a fireball—a particularly bright meteor—while a 1991 set of observations from Gironde and the Hautes-Pyrénées was judged consistent with atmospheric re-entry. Such phenomena can produce multiple lights, fragmentation, changing colours and apparently level motion across the sky. Their brief duration leaves witnesses little opportunity to estimate distance or speed, making them easy to interpret as structured objects travelling at low altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1991-12-01255?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=%2C556&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan(DPT) GIRONDE, HAUTES-PYRENEES (33, 65) 10.12.1991Département. Gironde. Classification. B. Date de mise a jour. 17/01/2012. Type de...</span></span></span>

The same pattern extends to more recent technology and aerial clutter. The public Gironde listings include cases attributed to a satellite, rocket components, a helicopter, a condensation trail, Mylar balloons and a probable drone near Bazas in January 2025. The changing mixture reflects what is present in the sky at a particular period. Older reports may be dominated by aircraft and astronomical bodies; newer ones increasingly include consumer drones, satellite trains and lightweight illuminated objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Lanterns and balloons create convincing “craft”

Small airborne objects account for a conspicuous part of the modern Gironde archive. Reports involving Chinese lanterns appear at Andernos-les-Bains, Le Porge, Port-Sainte-Foy-et-Ponchapt, Saint-Sulpice-et-Cameyrac, Mios and other locations. Their behaviour readily produces UFO-like descriptions: orange or red lights, silence, slow movement, loose formations and disappearance without an obvious descent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The June 2018 Andernos-les-Bains case is a useful example. Witnesses saw groups of bright red lights moving slowly and silently before vanishing behind trees and houses. Investigators compared the apparent route with the wind and found the behaviour compatible with sky lanterns. The conclusion was category B rather than A because no lantern was recovered and no launch was directly documented, but the objects’ colour, speed, grouping and wind-borne direction supported a probable conventional explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-06-50550" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This illustrates why category B should not be translated as “still genuinely mysterious”. It often means that investigators found an explanation that fits the observation well, but cannot prove it through physical recovery, an identified operator or an independently recorded launch. In practical terms, the remaining uncertainty may concern which lantern, aircraft or drone was involved—not whether the report demonstrates an unknown form of technology.

Reflective balloons produce a different illusion. A Mylar balloon can rotate, flash sunlight, appear metallic and alternate between bright and dark surfaces. Its irregular motion may suggest control, especially when distance is underestimated. Gironde’s archive includes Mylar-balloon identifications at Mérignac and Saint-Médard-d’Eyrans, showing how an object with no propulsion can look manoeuvrable when seen briefly and without reliable scale.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why category C reports remain weak evidence

Gironde does contain category C entries, including reports from Hourtin, Carbon-Blanc and an aerial journey from Bordeaux towards Nantes. Their unresolved status may look intriguing in a search table, but GEIPAN explicitly associates the classification with missing reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=53&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A category C file may lack an exact viewing direction, a trustworthy time, sufficient duration, corroborating witnesses, an original photograph or enough detail to distinguish an aircraft from a planet. Sometimes a report is submitted long after the event, when weather observations, traffic records and memory are harder to test. In other cases, the description is simply too general: a light moved, changed appearance or disappeared, but no measurement anchors it to the sky.

That evidential weakness cannot be repaired by the vividness of the witness’s experience. A person may be completely sincere and still provide insufficient data for reconstruction. Distance and size are especially difficult to estimate for an isolated light because the observer usually knows neither its true dimensions nor its altitude. A small nearby object and a large distant one can occupy the same apparent angle. Without reference points, even speed may be little more than an impression.

Category C therefore means that no responsible conclusion can be reached. It does not mean that investigators exhausted ordinary explanations and found them inadequate. For readers trying to identify Gironde’s strongest cases, that is the most important caution in the entire archive: lack of explanation is not automatically evidence of an extraordinary cause.

## Reconstruction changes the meaning of a sighting

GEIPAN’s most valuable contribution is not assigning a letter but reconstructing what the witness could actually have seen. The process may combine maps, compass bearings, astronomical software, weather records, photographs, aircraft tracking and comparisons with the reported duration and movement. CNES also works with bodies including the gendarmerie, police, the French Air and Space Force, the national weather service and scientific specialists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

The Mios Jupiter case shows how several strange details can emerge from one ordinary source. The position matched the planet; atmospheric effects explained the flashes and colours; visual autokinesis accounted for apparent lateral movement; fluctuating brightness created an impression of approach and retreat; and clouds explained the sudden ending. The investigation did not dismiss the witness. It separated the observed stimulus from the visual interpretations added during perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-05-50527" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MIOS (33) 03.05.20183 May 2018 — Département Gironde Classification A. ## What the Gironde archive supports—and what it does not

Taken as a whole, the official record supports three firm conclusions.

First, Gironde residents have repeatedly seen real things that were difficult to identify at the time. Witness uncertainty should not be confused with fabrication. Aircraft viewed under unusual illumination, bright planets near the horizon, lanterns moving with the wind and fragmenting meteors can all produce genuinely puzzling experiences.

Second, detailed checking usually weakens the claim that an unusual light represented an unknown craft. The archive contains explicit identifications involving Venus, Jupiter, aircraft, balloons, lanterns, satellites, rocket debris, meteors, helicopters, condensation trails and drones. Several apparently complex behaviours—reversal, hovering, colour changes or silent formation flight—become understandable once the observation is reconstructed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=116&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Third, the remaining uncertain reports are uneven in quality. Some may retain genuine ambiguity, but category C files are unresolved because investigators lack the information needed to decide. They cannot carry the same evidential weight as a thoroughly documented case that remains unexplained after competing possibilities have been tested.

Gironde’s official UFO history is therefore not empty, but its significance lies mainly in misidentification, perception and investigation rather than proof of extraordinary vehicles. The archive shows how ordinary objects become extraordinary reports—and how a precise time, direction, weather record or traffic check can transform a compelling story into a probable reconstruction.
