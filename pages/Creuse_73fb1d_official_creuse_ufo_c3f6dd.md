## Introduction

Official records from [Creuse]({{ 'from-humanoids-to-airliners-in-creuse/' | relative_url }}) show that several convincing UFO reports had ordinary causes once investigators compared witness accounts with astronomy, aviation data, photography and known aerial activity. Bright stars produced a multi-brigade gendarmerie alert in 1989; a seemingly structured object photographed near Sermur in 2018 proved consistent with an airliner recorded during a six-second exposure; a roadside sighting in 2024 was identified as a crewed gas balloon; and reports from the nationwide event of 5 November 1990 fitted the re-entry of Soviet rocket debris. One 1988 file remained unclassified, not because officials found strong evidence of something extraordinary, but because the surviving information was too poor to analyse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-01-01165" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan(DPT) CREUSE (23) 27.01.1989 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This distinction matters. In the GEIPAN system, a category A case is explained without ambiguity, category B has a highly probable explanation, category C cannot be analysed because information is missing, and category D remains unexplained despite usable evidence. Creuse’s official portfolio is therefore mainly a record of identification and evidential limits, rather than a collection of strong unresolved cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Stars and the 1989 gendarmerie alert

The January 1989 case is the clearest example of official involvement making a sighting look stronger before ultimately helping to explain it. At about 8.30 pm, the gendarmerie group at Guéret asked brigades across Creuse to look eastwards at an unusual phenomenon. Officers at Évaux-les-Bains, Chambon-sur-Voueize, Chénérailles, Jarnages, Gouzon and Bellegarde-en-Marche reported very bright, scintillating points displaying varied colours. Similar observations continued on several evenings.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-01-01165" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan(DPT) CREUSE (23) 27.01.1989 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A report involving several police stations can sound like independent confirmation of an extraordinary object. In reality, the brigades had been directed to inspect the same part of the sky, so the reports were not six unrelated discoveries. They were coordinated observations of a persistent visual stimulus. That made comparison possible: the lights stayed in positions consistent with stars, returned on subsequent nights and displayed the rapid colour changes associated with atmospheric scintillation. GEIPAN classified the case A and recorded the phenomenon simply as astronomical.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-01-01165" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan(DPT) CREUSE (23) 27.01.1989 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Scintillation is the familiar twinkling caused when starlight passes through moving layers of air with different temperatures and densities. Near the horizon, the light travels through more atmosphere and may appear to flash red, green, blue or white. A bright star can therefore resemble a rotating beacon or a distant craft with coloured lights, especially when haze, temperature gradients or unstable air strengthen the effect.

The case also shows why official witnesses should not be dismissed, but should not be treated as immune to visual error. The gendarmes accurately reported what they saw: bright, coloured, flickering points. The mistake lay in interpreting an unfamiliar appearance as a potentially unusual aerial phenomenon. Their geographical spread actually helped resolve the incident, because observations from several brigades supported a fixed astronomical source rather than a nearby object travelling through Creuse.

## The Sermur photograph looked stranger than the object

Near Sermur on 6 August 2018, a witness photographed what appeared to be five aligned white elements accompanied by red points and interpreted the image as a flat rectangular structure moving through the sky. Unlike many distant-light reports, this case contained a photograph, camera information, stars that could be mapped and a stated direction of travel. Those details allowed a much more exact reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete550.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - SERMUR (23) 06.08.2018 [E-Cgei CR T,C S] compte rendu enquete.docx...</span></span></span>

The photograph was taken with a six-second exposure. During that interval, the moving light left a track while its flashing lamps appeared repeatedly along it. Investigators found five repetitions of white streaks and red points, spaced at roughly one-second intervals. That rhythm matched the flashing anti-collision lights of an aircraft. A continuous greenish line also matched the starboard navigation light, which was the side expected to face the witness if the aircraft was travelling north to south while remaining east of the observation point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete550.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - SERMUR (23) 06.08.2018 [E-Cgei CR T,C S] compte rendu enquete.docx...</span></span></span>

The apparent geometry was therefore produced by time rather than by a row of separate lights fixed to a rectangular craft. A camera with a long exposure records successive moments in one image. Steady lamps become lines; flashing lamps become dotted or segmented trails. The resulting pattern can look more structured than anything visible to the naked eye.

Other checks reinforced the aircraft explanation. The photographed light moved about five degrees during the six-second exposure, or roughly one degree per second, which GEIPAN considered compatible with an airliner at cruising altitude. Routes from Paris towards Africa also crossed the relevant area on a suitable track. Investigators could not identify the exact flight because the report was processed too late, but they assessed the aircraft hypothesis at 92 per cent and classified the case A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete550.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - SERMUR (23) 06.08.2018 [E-Cgei CR T,C S] compte rendu enquete.docx...</span></span></span>

The investigation also exposed a memory problem. The witness later described a diagonal, descending path, yet comparison with the background stars showed that the track was approximately parallel to the horizon. GEIPAN noted that the observation occurred in August but the questionnaire was completed in December. By then, the witness may have been describing an interpretation of the photograph rather than an exact memory of the original movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete550.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - SERMUR (23) 06.08.2018 [E-Cgei CR T,C S] compte rendu enquete.docx...</span></span></span>

This is one of Creuse’s most useful cases because the photograph did not merely fail to prove an extraordinary object; it preserved the mechanism of the mistake. The repeating flashes, colours, angular movement and orientation all supplied independent tests of the same explanation.

## Balloons and the value of checking the wider sky

A 15 September 2024 report made while travelling along the N145 between Saint-Priest-la-Plaine and La Souterraine was later classified A as a crewed gas balloon. GEIPAN’s database also records a related sighting on the same date from Rilhac-Rancon in neighbouring Haute-Vienne, identified in the same way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2023-02-16&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=3%2C9&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casMission &amp; Geipan... 2024-11-25, voir. That cross-border match is important. A balloon seen at a distance may appear motionless, unusually large or difficult to place against an empty sky. Its apparent shape can change with viewing angle, while sunlight on the envelope may make it far brighter than expected. A single witness may have little basis for estimating its height or distance. Reports from separated locations, however, can reveal that observers are looking at the same high-altitude object rather than at something close to either witness.

The 2024 case illustrates a modern advantage over older files: investigators can compare photographs, timings, known balloon activity and geographically separated reports while the information is still recent. The explanation does not depend on telling the witness that the object “must have been” a balloon. It depends on showing that a real balloon was in the appropriate sky and produced compatible observations along its route.

## The 1990 re-entry turned many lights into one apparent craft

On 5 November 1990, shortly after 7 pm, more than a thousand people across France reported a large luminous formation crossing a clear night sky. Reports reached the authorities through gendarmerie brigades, and some witnesses described an immense triangle, several lights moving together or an object apparently passing at low altitude. Creuse observations belonged to this much larger national event rather than to a separate local flap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The official inquiry considered astronomical, meteorological, civil-aviation and military information and requested orbital data. NASA supplied details for object 1990-094C, the third stage of a Soviet Proton rocket used to launch the Gorizont 21 communications satellite. Its predicted descent matched the time and broad path of the sightings. CNES announced that the spectacle was the stage breaking up during atmospheric re-entry. Contemporary reporting also carried the identification within days.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Un%20cas%20remarquable-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Later CNES modelling placed the glowing debris cloud at roughly 90 to 50 kilometres altitude, travelling at a speed that fell from about seven to two kilometres per second. Fragmentation produced many separate pieces and trails spread across tens of kilometres. Seen in perspective, those lights could be mentally joined into the outline of a single enormous object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The strongest source of disagreement was not whether a rocket stage re-entered: the orbital identification is well established. The dispute concerned reports that seemed inconsistent with it, including claims of hovering, turns, very low altitude or light falling on roads. GEIPAN’s later discussion argued that distance to an unidentified light is extremely difficult to judge at night. A very bright event tens of kilometres high may appear close, while a long debris train viewed from different positions can seem slow, stationary or differently shaped.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Some private investigators continued to argue that additional unidentified phenomena occurred simultaneously. That possibility cannot be disproved merely by identifying the rocket debris. However, it requires evidence separating a particular report from the known re-entry in time, direction and behaviour. The existence of unusual descriptions alone is weak separation evidence, because a mass sighting naturally produces a spread of estimates of colour, distance, duration and motion. GEIPAN acknowledged uncertainty in the exact lengthwise spread of the debris cloud, but judged the cross-track trajectory much more secure.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

For Creuse history, the lesson is that an event may be genuinely extraordinary to witness while having an ordinary physical cause. The 1990 display was not a trivial light or an invented story. It was a rare, spectacular aerospace event whose scale and unfamiliar appearance encouraged witnesses to perceive a structured craft.

## An unresolved label can mean unusable evidence

The Saint-Sulpice-le-Dunois report of 13 October 1988 is listed by GEIPAN as category C, with the reason given as insufficient reliable information. That classification does not mean investigators found a robust anomaly. It means they lacked enough dependable material to choose between competing explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=38&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is an essential distinction in reading official UFO databases. “Unexplained” and “unanalysable” are not interchangeable. A category D case has survived investigation while retaining unexplained features. A category C case has not passed that threshold because the date, direction, duration, witness detail or supporting records are too incomplete to perform meaningful checks. GEIPAN’s own definitions make that separation explicit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The 1988 file therefore carries less evidential weight than Creuse’s explained cases, not more. The 1989 observations can be tested against the stars; the Sermur photograph can be measured; the 2024 report can be compared with balloon activity; and the 1990 reports can be checked against orbital data. A sparse account offers fewer opportunities either to confirm an exotic claim or to demonstrate a conventional cause.

## What the official Creuse record actually shows

Taken together, the files reveal a consistent investigative pattern. Authorities did not explain Creuse sightings by applying one blanket theory. Different cases required different checks:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* repeated position and colour behaviour pointed to scintillating stars;
* exposure time, flash rhythm and navigation-light colours identified an airliner;
* reports from neighbouring areas supported a known balloon;
* orbital data and re-entry modelling accounted for a national display of fragmented rocket debris;
* missing documentation prevented a responsible conclusion in the 1988 case.

</div>

The most persuasive explanations are cumulative. No single feature proves the Sermur object was an aircraft, but the flash interval, red-white-green lighting, angular speed and flight direction all agree. No single testimony proves the 1989 lights were stars, but fixed positions, repetition over several nights and atmospheric scintillation form a coherent account. No individual 1990 witness needed to describe rocket debris perfectly for the independently tracked re-entry to remain the best explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete550.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - SERMUR (23) 06.08.2018 [E-Cgei CR T,C S] compte rendu enquete.docx...</span></span></span>

Creuse’s official files also caution against ranking cases simply by witness status or emotional impact. Gendarmes can misidentify stars; a photograph can create an artificial structure; several observers can share the same mistake; and an accurate account of bright lights can still be paired with an inaccurate estimate of distance. These are not accusations of dishonesty. They are predictable limits of perception when an unfamiliar light is seen without a known scale.

The department’s record is therefore most valuable as a compact study in how identification works. Ordinary causes became convincing UFO reports because they were viewed under unusual conditions, photographed in misleading ways or interpreted without sufficient distance and scale cues. Official checks weakened the extraordinary reading in most documented cases, while the one poorly documented file remained open only in the limited sense that there was too little evidence to decide.
