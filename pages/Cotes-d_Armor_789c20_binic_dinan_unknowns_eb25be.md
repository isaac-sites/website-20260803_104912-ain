## Introduction

Binic in 1990 and Dinan in 1999 are the two most significant unresolved cases in Côtes-d’Armor’s published official UFO record. Both remain classified D by GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena. That status means investigators did not find a satisfactory conventional explanation after examining the available evidence. It does **not** mean that either sighting has been identified as an exotic craft, still less as extraterrestrial technology.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-03-01196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBINIC (22) 22.03.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The cases are intriguing for different reasons. Binic involves several people reporting apparently related events, including a low-flying orange disc and unusual lights behind nearby hills. Dinan rests on a single experienced sky-watcher, but the official account says that checks with aerodromes and civil and military authorities failed to connect the observation with known aircraft or radar traffic. At the same time, neither file contains the kind of surviving physical or instrument data that would allow a firm identification today.

## What witnesses reported in Binic and Dinan

### Binic: repeated lights and a reported low-altitude disc

The Binic events occurred on 22 March 1990. GEIPAN’s summary says that three witnesses separately saw a strange luminous phenomenon, on more than one occasion. One witness reported being overflown at low altitude by an orange disc that produced an unusual rushing or rustling-air sound. The encounter frightened him sufficiently that he fell from his moped. Another witness reportedly saw a similar craft twice and noticed strange glows behind the surrounding hills. GEIPAN states that its investigation found no explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-03-01196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBINIC (22) 22.03.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is stronger than a simple report of a distant point of light. The claimed disc shape, colour, sound and low apparent altitude gave investigators several descriptive features to consider. The involvement of three witnesses also potentially raises the case’s evidential value, although the official summary makes clear that the observations were separate rather than one simultaneous, mutually confirmed view from the same position. That distinction matters: separate reports may support one another, but only if their timings, directions and descriptions can be shown to match closely.

The surviving public summary does not provide photographs, radar records, measured angles, a confirmed flight path or physical traces from the reported fall. Nor does it establish the object’s actual altitude or distance. A witness who sees an unfamiliar light against a dark sky normally has no reliable scale against which to judge size, range or speed. The description of being “overflown” is therefore an interpretation of the visual and acoustic experience, not an independently measured trajectory.

Binic nevertheless remains noteworthy within Côtes-d’Armor because the reported combination is unusual: repeated observations, more than one witness, apparent structure, sound and a close-pass impression. An independent database derived from GEIPAN’s public material currently lists Binic as one of only two category D cases among 34 departmental records, the other being Dinan.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/cas/binic-1990-0301196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">binic 1990 0301196</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">22 mars 1990... ### Dinan: a dark barrel-like form changing direction

The Dinan report concerns a night-time observation in 1999 by a 60-year-old amateur astronomer. While observing the Andromeda Galaxy, he noticed with the unaided eye a silent, non-luminous, brown object moving at a steady speed. According to GEIPAN, it initially followed a north-to-south path, then changed direction sharply towards the north-north-west before turning north. The witness’s sketch represented the form as barrel-like, and the observation lasted about one minute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/6228" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanDINAN (22) 07.09.1999DINAN (22) 07.09.1999; Date d&#x27;observation. 07/09/1999; Région. Bretagne; Département. Côtes-d&#x27;Armor; Class...</span></span></span>

The observer’s astronomical interest is relevant but should not be overstated. Someone accustomed to watching the night sky may be less likely than a casual observer to mistake a bright planet for a moving object. Dinan was not, however, a luminous astronomical-object report. It concerned a dark shape seen against the sky without optical magnification. Even an experienced observer can have difficulty estimating the form and path of a dim, unfamiliar object when its distance is unknown.

What makes Dinan particularly important is the reported official follow-up. GEIPAN says that investigators made detailed enquiries with neighbouring aerodromes and civil and military authorities. Neither air-traffic examination nor available radar traces allowed the sighting to be associated with an aircraft. The case was therefore left unexplained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1999-09-01538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanDINAN (22) 07.09.1999 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That negative result is meaningful, but narrower than it may first appear. It indicates that investigators did not find a matching recorded aircraft or radar contact. It does not demonstrate that the reported form was a solid craft, nor that every small, low, distant or poorly reflective object would necessarily have appeared on the radar systems consulted. Birds, balloons, lightweight airborne material and some aircraft operating outside an expected record can be difficult to exclude completely after the event.

There is also an unresolved archival problem in the public file. The case title and database date identify the observation as 7 September 1999, whereas the narrative describes the night of 7–8 August. The same mismatch appears in both GEIPAN’s current page and an independent reproduction of its data. It may be a transcription error, but without the underlying dated documents the correct night cannot safely be chosen. This matters because checks of weather, Moon position, aircraft movements and possible balloon activity depend on the exact date.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/cas/dinan-1999-0901538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[carteovni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">dinan 1999 0901538</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Observation OVNI à Dinan — septembre 1999 — CarteOvni.frObservation OVNI à Dinan — septembre 1999. 7 septembre 1999 Côtes-d&#x27;Armor (22) 👁️...</span></span></span>

## What a GEIPAN category D classification means

GEIPAN is a technical unit within CNES, France’s national space agency. Its purpose is to collect, investigate, archive and publish reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena. It works with witnesses and may draw on the gendarmerie, police, aviation organisations, defence bodies, weather specialists and scientific experts. GEIPAN explicitly says that it is not an extraterrestrial-research organisation and does not treat the word “UFO” as evidence that a physical vehicle was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes.fr.</span></span></span>

Under the current classification framework:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A** means a phenomenon has been identified with high confidence.
* **B** means an explanation is considered probable.
* **C** means the information is inadequate or unreliable for a sound assessment.
* **D** means the phenomenon remains unidentified after investigation.

</div>

GEIPAN’s more detailed method, introduced in 2008, assesses both the residual strangeness of a report and its “consistency”: the quantity, precision and reliability of the information supporting it. Modern unexplained cases may be divided into D1 and D2, while older records such as Binic and Dinan can retain the broader legacy D label.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This makes D different from “insufficient information”, but it should not be read as “confirmed extraordinary object”. A D classification records the outcome of an investigation: the available conventional hypotheses did not satisfactorily account for the reported experience. It does not specify what actually caused that experience.

The distinction is especially important with historical cases. GEIPAN acknowledges that older investigations lacked many tools now available to investigators, including convenient digital access to astronomical calculations, satellite trajectories, aviation data and geographical reconstruction. It also states that category D files can be revisited and reclassified if new information emerges. Many old unexplained cases have in fact been reconsidered and given ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Binic and Dinan therefore remain official unknowns in an administrative and evidential sense. Their files have not yielded a defensible identification, but the classification supplies no positive evidence for one particular extraordinary theory.

## Possible explanations and the missing evidence

No single conventional explanation clearly fits every reported feature of either case. Nevertheless, several possibilities remain open because the public evidence is too limited to eliminate them.

For Binic, an aircraft is an obvious hypothesis because the witness reported both a structured form and rushing-air noise. Unusual viewing geometry can hide an aircraft’s wings, merge its lights into a disc-like appearance or make its movement seem slower and lower than it is. GEIPAN itself gives documented examples in which conventional aircraft appeared stationary, disc-shaped or triangular because of lighting, motion and perceptual effects. Yet the published Binic summary does not identify a matching flight, and it is unclear whether the orange appearance and additional hillside lights can be fitted to one aviation explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-03-01196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBINIC (22) 22.03.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Atmospheric or distant-light effects might account for the glows behind hills, particularly in variable cloud or weather conditions. They are less obviously suited to the reported close orange disc unless separate phenomena were combined into one narrative. Multiple causes are possible: a conventional aircraft for one observation, distant illumination for another and memory or expectation linking them afterwards. The three witnesses’ precise independence, sightlines and timings would be essential to test that possibility, but those details are not available in the concise public record.

Dinan has a different range of plausible alternatives. A bird or group of birds can appear as a silent dark mass against a brighter sky and can alter course abruptly. A balloon or lightweight object may also change apparent direction with winds at different altitudes, although the reported steady movement and repeated turns would require closer analysis. A distant aircraft seen without visible navigation lights remains possible, but the official enquiries found no corresponding known traffic or radar trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1999-09-01538" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanDINAN (22) 07.09.1999 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Perception is also relevant. GEIPAN stresses that observers cannot reliably derive the distance, size or speed of an unidentified object without a known reference. The brain may reconstruct a path from limited visual information, especially at night, while emotion and later recollection can sharpen an initially ambiguous shape. This does not imply dishonesty or incompetence. It is one reason serious investigators distinguish a credible witness from a fully reliable measurement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The most important missing evidence across the two cases is:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* original, complete witness statements with exact timings and viewing directions;
* site diagrams showing terrain, horizons and lines of sight;
* contemporary weather and visibility records;
* complete aviation and radar documentation;
* photographs, video or independent instrumental detection;
* clear evidence that separately reported Binic observations described the same event;
* resolution of Dinan’s August-versus-September date discrepancy.

</div>

Without those materials, modern reanalysis can suggest possibilities but cannot confidently reconstruct either event. The absence of a solution is genuine, yet so is the absence of decisive evidence that the witnesses encountered one coherent, physically extraordinary object.

## Why these are Côtes-d’Armor’s strongest official unknowns

Binic and Dinan stand apart from most reported phenomena in Côtes-d’Armor because they survived official investigation as category D cases rather than being classified as identified, probably identified or too poorly documented to assess. Binic has the richer witness pattern and the more dramatic close-range claim. Dinan has the clearer account of aviation and radar checks, together with a witness accustomed to observing the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-03-01196" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBINIC (22) 22.03.1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Neither case, however, reaches the evidential standard of a well-instrumented aviation incident. Binic lacks publicly available objective records; Dinan depends on a single visual observation and contains a material date inconsistency. Later publication has preserved the cases and clarified their official standing, but it has not added decisive new evidence or strengthened a specific explanation.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore deliberately limited. Something unusual was reported at Binic and Dinan, and the official investigations did not identify it. The cases remain valuable because they show the difference between an unexplained report and proof of an extraordinary cause. Their D classifications keep the questions open, but they do not answer them.

    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
