## Introduction

Between 1977 and 1979, Côte-d’Or produced a small but revealing run of UFO reports. They did not describe one coherent “wave” or a single type of object. Instead, the official files range from a light conclusively identified as the Moon, through probable astronomical misidentifications and cases too incomplete to judge, to the unusual Arc-sur-Tille observation that remains officially unresolved.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Cote-d_Or_6e3ab6_late_1970s_ufo_cases_65aea5-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1970 s Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That mixture matters more than the raw number of reports. It shows how apparently dramatic experiences can separate under investigation: a “saucer” may become a low Moon seen through cloud, a light that seems to pursue a car may match a bright planet, and a striking account may remain unclassified simply because essential directions, timings or corroboration are missing. Arc-sur-Tille stands apart, but unresolved does not mean extraterrestrial. It means that the surviving evidence has not supported a sufficiently strong ordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47166" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977 T2VITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977; Observation date. 30/05/1977; region. ## The spread from Vitteaux to Arc-sur-Tille

The late-1970s reports were scattered across Côte-d’Or rather than concentrated around one obvious hotspot. The official record includes Vitteaux in May 1977, Chenôve in November 1977, Bierre-lès-Semur in July 1978, Dijon in September 1978, Argilly in June 1979 and Arc-sur-Tille in December 1979. Their final classifications span the full range from explained to unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47166" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977 T2VITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977; Observation date. 30/05/1977; region. Bourgogne; Department. Côte-d&#x27;Or; Cl...</span></span></span>

This is an important corrective to the idea of a unified local “UFO wave”. The reports occurred over two and a half years, in different settings and under different viewing conditions. Some involved motorists, others witnesses at home or outdoors. Some described stationary lights; others included apparent pursuit, rapid departure or a structured form near the ground. The similarities are mostly broad features common to many night-sky reports: brightness, low apparent altitude, unusual colour and difficulty judging distance.

GEIPAN, the unidentified-aerospace-phenomena unit of the French space agency CNES, does not classify cases merely by how strange a witness found them. Its system weighs both residual strangeness and “consistency” or evidential strength, including the quantity, precision and reliability of the available information. Category A means that the phenomenon has been identified without ambiguity; B indicates a highly probable explanation; C means that the report cannot be analysed reliably; and D is reserved for an observation still unexplained after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

The result is not a progression towards stronger evidence. It is a sorting process. Several reports weaken when viewing geometry and the night sky are reconstructed; two remain unusable because the documentation is inadequate; only one retains both sufficient substance and no accepted explanation.

## Moon, planets and the illusion of pursuit

### Vitteaux: an identified Moon

The Vitteaux report of 30 May 1977 is the clearest example of an ordinary object producing an extraordinary impression. GEIPAN ultimately classified it A and identified the phenomenon as the Moon. The importance of the case lies less in its surviving narrative than in its verdict: the most firmly resolved late-1970s report in Côte-d’Or was not an unusual craft but a familiar astronomical body viewed under misleading conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47166" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977 T2VITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977; Observation date. 30/05/1977; region. Bourgogne; Department. Côte-d&#x27;Or; Cl...</span></span></span>

The Moon is a recurrent source of UFO reports because observers frequently underestimate the distance of a bright object near the horizon. Atmospheric haze can turn it orange or red, while cloud can conceal parts of its disc, distort its outline or make it appear to brighten, fade and reappear. Without visible stars, landmarks or a clear horizon, a distant celestial body can be mentally placed just above fields, trees or buildings.

The key distinction is between seeing and interpreting. A witness may accurately report a conspicuous light while misjudging its size, distance and motion. GEIPAN’s own investigations stress that an explanation can combine a known physical object, such as the Moon or a planet, with ordinary effects of perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

### Bierre-lès-Semur: the “saucer” at four in the morning

The Bierre-lès-Semur case is the best documented Côte-d’Or example of how later reconstruction can overturn an initially mysterious verdict. At about 4 am on 18 July 1978, a student camping near the hamlet of Lucenay saw a stationary, fire-coloured light low in the sky. He described it as resembling a saucer or an inverted plate, fetched a camera and took three photographs. He then reported that it moved slowly and afterwards departed rapidly towards the south. No noise was heard. The photographs yielded no usable image, and gendarmes found no additional witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.197828 Jul 2017 — Témoignages. Témoignages. BIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.1978. Résumé. Courte observatio...</span></span></span>

The case was originally associated with Précy-sous-Thil and classified D. Decades later, GEIPAN re-examined it using improved mapping and astronomical software. The Moon was found at an azimuth of about 240 degrees—the direction of Dompierre-en-Morvan from the witness’s position—and only about two degrees above the horizon. Its position, low elevation and reddish-orange appearance closely matched the first and best-documented part of the report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.197828 Jul 2017 — Témoignages. Témoignages. BIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.1978. Résumé. Courte observatio...</span></span></span>

Cloud was also forming. A partly obscured lunar disc could therefore have appeared flattened or plate-like rather than circular. GEIPAN considered the reported final movement less secure because it was only briefly described. A moving gap in the clouds could have made the visible portion of the Moon appear to shift, accelerate or disappear in a new direction. The agency consequently reclassified the case B: a probable sighting of a reddish setting Moon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-07-00528" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanBIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.197828 Jul 2017 — Témoignages. Témoignages. BIERRE-LES-SEMUR (21) 18.07.1978. Résumé. Courte observatio...</span></span></span>

This reclassification is especially instructive because the original account contained features often treated as evidence against an astronomical explanation: an apparently structured shape, a change of speed and a rapid departure. Yet those details were not equally well supported. The stationary orange light had a precise celestial match; the dramatic departure was brief, lacked measurements and was not corroborated. Investigation did not require dismissing the witness. It required separating the firmly recorded observation from the witness’s interpretation of its final seconds.

### Argilly: why a planet can seem to follow a car

At about 11.30 pm on 3 June 1979, a motorist near Argilly saw an orange luminous ball in her rear-view mirror. She believed it was following her, became frightened and sounded her horn on reaching a settlement. One resident came outside but declined to approach the reported light. The witness continued to see it during the journey and reported physical sensations as well as the vehicle’s dashboard lighting going out. No second observation of the phenomenon was secured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00630" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanARGILLY (21) 03.06.19792 Jul 2008 — ARGILLY (21) 03.06.1979; Date d&#x27;observation. 03/06/1979; Région. GEIPAN’s astronomical chart showed several prominent objects in the relevant part of the sky, most notably Jupiter, then shining at a magnitude of approximately –1.47, as well as Pollux and Capella. The agency regarded an astronomical explanation as probable but could not confirm it because the report lacked enough precise positional and chronological information. It therefore remained category C rather than B. GEIPAN also considered the witness’s fear a likely contributor to the physical sensations and behaviour described.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-06-00630" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanARGILLY (21) 03.06.19792 Jul 2008 — ARGILLY (21) 03.06.1979; Date d&#x27;observation. 03/06/1979; Région. Bourgogne; Département. Côt...</span></span></span>

The impression that a distant light is pursuing a moving vehicle is a recognised feature of astronomical misidentifications. A nearby tree, house or road sign sweeps rapidly across the observer’s field of view as the car moves, but the Moon, Jupiter or a bright star is effectively at infinity and changes position very little. It therefore remains at roughly the same bearing through successive stretches of road. To a frightened driver, especially one checking mirrors or rounding bends, that stable bearing can suggest deliberate pursuit.

GEIPAN has described the same “following ball” effect in other vehicle cases and regards it as typical of confusion with the Moon or another distant astronomical source. Stopping the car may make the apparent object “stop”; restarting makes it seem to move again because it is the foreground landscape, not the celestial body, that supplies the relative motion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1984-09-01031" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Argilly nevertheless illustrates the limits of retrospective explanation. Jupiter is plausible, but plausibility is not identification. The recorded direction was broad, the witness was alone, and the claimed dashboard failure was not independently tested. A category C verdict therefore says less about the strangeness of the event than about the weakness of the information needed to decide between competing explanations.

## Explained, incomplete and unresolved verdicts

### Why category C is not another form of “unknown craft”

The Dijon report of 17 September 1978 and the Argilly report of June 1979 are both category C cases. In GEIPAN’s system, this does not mean that investigators exhausted conventional possibilities and found none adequate. It means that the surviving material is too incomplete or unreliable to support a firm analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=95%2C34&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DIJON (21) 17.09.1978 &#124; 17/09/1978 &#124; C &#124; Côte-d&#x27;Or &#124; Manque d&#x27;informations fiables &#124; 06/05/2010 &#124; 1978-09-17...</span></span></span>

That distinction is easily lost in retellings. A case can remain unidentified because no ordinary explanation fits, but it can also remain unidentified because the witness did not record a direction, angular height, exact duration or comparison with the Moon and stars. Old files add further difficulties: investigators may be unable to revisit the witness, reconstruct a route, recover photographs or establish whether times were recorded accurately.

The Dijon listing gives the date, location and category but publicly characterises the file chiefly by its lack of reliable information. There is therefore no sound basis for presenting it as a strong mystery. Its historical value is that it shows how a report may enter the official archive without becoming a usable case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=40%2C32&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-JEAN-D&#x27;ANGELY (17) 15.02.1975 &#124; --/02/1975 &#124; C &#124; Charente-Maritime &#124; Manque d&#x27;informations fiables &#124; 22/03/2007 &#124; 1975-02-01...</span></span></span>

Argilly contains more narrative detail, but the crucial geometry remains uncertain. A bright planet fits much of the account, while the apparent pursuit is a familiar perceptual effect. Yet the evidence is not precise enough to turn that hypothesis into a category A or B conclusion. The proper verdict is neither “alien vehicle” nor “definitively Jupiter”, but “probably astronomical and not demonstrable from the surviving file”.

### Chenôve: probable, but publicly underspecified

The Chenôve sighting of 10 November 1977 is classified B, meaning that GEIPAN considers a conventional explanation highly probable. The accessible case listing, however, labels the phenomenon only as an identified type not otherwise specified. That leaves the public with a verdict but little visible reasoning.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=10&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COSNES-ET-ROMAIN (54) 30.11.1977 &#124; 30/11/1977 &#124; C &#124; Meurthe-et-Moselle &#124; Manque d&#x27;informations fiables &#124; 04/11/2010 &#124; 1977-11-30...</span></span></span>

This is a useful warning against overstating either side. A category B classification weakens the case as evidence for an extraordinary craft, but the absence of a detailed public explanation makes it difficult for an outside reader to test the proposed solution independently. Chenôve should therefore be described as probably explained according to the official archive, not as a case whose mechanism can be confidently reconstructed from the currently accessible summary.

It also demonstrates that the strength of an archive is uneven. Some files, such as Bierre-lès-Semur, include a detailed later re-analysis with sky position, cloud conditions and viewing direction. Others preserve little more than a category and administrative record. Treating every database entry as equally informative would give a false impression of precision.

### Arc-sur-Tille: the exception that survived

The Arc-sur-Tille observation of 8 December 1979 is the outlier. At 9.52 am, two witnesses looking from their home reported a crescent-shaped object coloured dark blue and white near a small wood. They estimated it at four to five metres high, close to ground level and about 200 metres away. It appeared to sway for approximately a minute and a half before departing rapidly towards the north. They reported no special light or sound. Witnesses and later gendarmes found no trace on the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-12-00696" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanARC SUR TILLE (21) 19793 Sept 2008 — Il mesure 4 à 5 mètres de haut. Aucun bruit et aucune lumière particulière ne sont remarqués...</span></span></span>

Several details make this report harder to absorb into the astronomical pattern. It occurred in daylight rather than against a dark sky; the witnesses described a non-luminous, two-coloured shape; and the phenomenon was perceived beside landscape features rather than as an isolated point of light. The account also came from two people observing together, which gives it more substance than a solitary, briefly glimpsed light.

Even so, the evidence remains limited. The witnesses were not independent of one another, no photograph or instrument record exists, no physical trace was found and the estimated dimensions depended on an assumed distance. Visual size and distance are especially difficult to judge when the true nature of an object is unknown. A smaller nearby object, for example, can subtend the same angle as a larger distant one.

The category D verdict therefore carries a narrow meaning: GEIPAN did not find an ordinary explanation sufficiently compatible with the available account. It does not establish that the reported dimensions and distance were objectively measured, that an engineered craft was present or that the event had an extraterrestrial origin. Under GEIPAN’s methodology, unresolved cases are those retaining substantial strangeness after plausible hypotheses have been considered, provided the information is consistent enough to merit that conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

Arc-sur-Tille also lacks the features that would have made the case materially stronger: separate witnesses at different locations, photographs, radar data, an aviation report, recoverable physical effects or an independently verified trajectory. Its survival as category D is noteworthy within Côte-d’Or, but it remains a visual testimony rather than a multi-sensor incident.

## Why the apparent wave unravelled

Côte-d’Or’s late-1970s record becomes less mysterious when the reports are compared rather than retold separately. The cases do not reinforce one another as repeated observations of the same phenomenon. Their descriptions, times, evidential quality and eventual explanations diverge.

The most secure conclusions point towards ordinary causes. Vitteaux was identified as the Moon. Bierre-lès-Semur, once treated as unresolved, was later matched closely to a reddish Moon setting through cloud. Chenôve is officially considered probably identified. Argilly resembles a classic bright-object pursuit illusion, although its documentation is too weak for confirmation. Dijon is likewise incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47166" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GeipanVITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977 T2VITTEAUX (21) 30.05.1977; Observation date. 30/05/1977; region. Bourgogne; Department. Côte-d&#x27;Or; Cl...</span></span></span>

The later reclassification of Bierre-lès-Semur is particularly significant. In 2017, GEIPAN announced that it had revisited a group of old category D cases using improved digital tools, astronomical reconstruction and accumulated investigative experience. Bierre-lès-Semur moved from D to B after the Moon hypothesis was tested against the time, direction and horizon height. The change shows that an unresolved label can reflect the limitations of an earlier investigation rather than the permanent resistance of a case to explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/geipan-revisite-cas" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">geipan revisite cas</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">geipan revisite cas</span></span></span>

At the same time, the files do not justify sweeping sceptical certainty. A likely explanation is not the same as proof, and missing evidence cannot be replaced by assumption. Argilly remains C because the astronomical fit could not be demonstrated conclusively. Arc-sur-Tille remains D because no proposed conventional cause adequately reproduced the witnesses’ description.

The balanced reading is therefore neither that the 1977–79 reports reveal a hidden fleet nor that every witness merely “saw the Moon”. They form a mixed case family shaped by several processes:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* bright celestial objects appearing unusually large or low;
* cloud changing the apparent form and movement of the Moon;
* relative motion making distant lights seem to follow vehicles;
* fear influencing estimates, bodily sensations and interpretation;
* incomplete recording preventing later reconstruction;
* and, in one case, a sufficiently unusual account that no accepted explanation has yet displaced it.

</div>

That pattern gives the period its real place in Côte-d’Or’s UFO history. The late 1970s are valuable not as a single unexplained wave, but as a compact demonstration of how official investigation separates identification, probability, evidential failure and genuine residual uncertainty. Arc-sur-Tille remains the exception, while the wider cluster shows why an initially impressive run of reports can largely unravel once ordinary astronomical and perceptual explanations are tested against the details.
