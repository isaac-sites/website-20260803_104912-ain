## Introduction

[Corse-du-Sud]({{ 'corse-du-sud/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO record is unusual mainly because it is so small and so heavily weighted towards ordinary explanations. GEIPAN, the French space agency service that investigates unidentified aerial reports, has published files from the department involving a scientific balloon, a bright meteor, aircraft, a laser, rocket debris and a star. Two reports could not be assessed properly because dependable information was lacking. Only one geographically valid Corse-du-Sud case is presently classified as genuinely unexplained: the 2014 sighting near Zonza.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C53&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AJACCIO (2A) 14.12.1976 &#124; 14/12/1976 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Ballon scientifique ou technologique &#124; 13/09/2011 &#124; 1976-12-14...</span></span></span>

That does not make the Zonza report trivial, but it does put it in perspective. The official archive does not show a sustained local “flap”, a concentration of radar incidents or repeated encounters around an airport or military installation. It shows something more prosaic and more useful: how dramatically different kinds of evidence can sit under the same popular label of “UFO”, from conclusively identified objects to reports that remain mysterious only because there is too little information to test them.

## The small and uneven GEIPAN archive

GEIPAN is part of France’s National Centre for Space Studies, CNES. It generally uses the term unidentified aerospace phenomenon rather than “UFO”, because witnesses may have perceived a light, atmospheric effect or distant object rather than a physical craft. Its classifications are based on two linked questions: how strange the observation remains after conventional explanations have been tested, and how consistent or dependable the available evidence is.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The categories are easy to misunderstand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A** means the phenomenon has been identified with high confidence.
* **B** means a conventional explanation is probable, although not demonstrated as completely as in an A case.
* **C** means GEIPAN could not reach a judgement because the information was inadequate or unreliable.
* **D1 or D2** means the phenomenon remained unexplained after investigation, with D2 reserved for cases combining greater strangeness and stronger evidential consistency.

</div>

A C case is therefore not “almost unexplained”. It is an unusable file. GEIPAN itself stresses that C reports belong apart from A, B and D cases because investigators have not been able to make a meaningful assessment at all.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Even this small count requires caution. GEIPAN search results have also labelled reports from Bastia and San-Martino-di-Lota as belonging to Corse-du-Sud, although both places are officially in Haute-Corse. The Bastia entry was identified as an airliner, while the San-Martino-di-Lota report was classified C for lack of reliable information. Their appearance under Corse-du-Sud seems to be a database or historical coding problem rather than evidence from the department itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=title&amp;page=152&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This matters because a simple filtered count can overstate the department’s record. The archive is a working historical database assembled from reports created under different administrative codes and investigative systems. It should not be treated as a perfectly cleaned statistical dataset.

## Identified reports dominate the record

The clearest pattern is not mystery but misidentification. Objects that appeared remarkable to witnesses were usually recognised once investigators compared their appearance, direction, timing and behaviour with known phenomena.

The 1976 Ajaccio case, for example, is classified A as a scientific or technological balloon. Balloons can appear stationary or move slowly, change brightness as they rotate, and seem much larger than they are when viewed without a reliable distance reference. Those characteristics make them recurrent sources of unusual aerial reports, particularly when seen at altitude in clear light. The official conclusion does not imply that the witness invented anything; it means the described phenomenon was matched confidently to a conventional object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C53&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AJACCIO (2A) 14.12.1976 &#124; 14/12/1976 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Ballon scientifique ou technologique &#124; 13/09/2011 &#124; 1976-12-14...</span></span></span>

The department-wide report of 8 March 1987 was identified as a fireball: an exceptionally bright meteor. Such events can produce intense light, apparent colour changes, fragmentation and a path visible across a wide area. Their speed and brilliance can make them seem closer, lower or more structured than they really are. In classification terms, this is again an A case—an initially unidentified spectacle that ceased to be mysterious once it was compared with a known astronomical event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DEP) CORSE-DU-SUD (2A) 08.03.1987 &#124; 08/03/1987 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Bolide &#124; 15/03/2010 &#124; 1987-03-08... ERQUY (22) 20.04.2016 &#124; 20/04/2...</span></span></span>

The Tavaco case of August 1996 was placed in category B with a laser as the probable explanation. Unlike an A judgement, category B leaves some uncertainty, but GEIPAN considered the identified cause more likely than an unknown phenomenon. The Figari report of July 2007 was similarly judged probably attributable to an aircraft. The proximity of Figari airport makes aircraft identification especially relevant, although the B classification signals that the supporting evidence was not complete enough for absolute certainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=8%2C4&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPANTAVACO (2A) 08.08.1996 &#124; 08/08/1996 &#124; B &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Laser &#124; 22/03/2007 &#124; 1996-08-08...</span></span></span>

Two newer entries reinforce the same overall pattern. At Cuttoli-Corticchiato on 7 April 2021, the reported phenomenon was identified as rocket-related material. At San-Gavino-di-Tenda on 10 December 2025, GEIPAN concluded that the light was Sirius, the brightest star in the night sky. Both received category A classifications. These cases show why the archive cannot be read as a historical list frozen around the classic flying-saucer era: modern space activity and familiar astronomical objects continue to generate reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/60458?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=30&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Sirius identification is particularly instructive. Bright stars close to the horizon can appear to flash, change colour or move slightly because atmospheric turbulence distorts their light and because involuntary eye movements are more noticeable when the surrounding sky offers few reference points. A report can therefore be sincere, vivid and still arise from a stationary astronomical source. GEIPAN’s system is designed to separate the witness’s experience from the later physical explanation rather than dismissing the experience itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2024-08-12&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=3%2C0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPANSAN-GAVINO-DI-TENDA (20) 10.12.2025 &#124; 10/12/2025 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Sirius (étoile) &#124; 27/02/2026 &#124; 2025...</span></span></span>

## Weak files are not unexplained phenomena

The 1993 Ota report was classified C because GEIPAN lacked reliable information. The publicly searchable record does not support treating it as a strong mystery: the classification means the investigators could not determine what happened, not that conventional possibilities had been eliminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-09-01331?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=%2C95&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That distinction is essential in a department with so few files. Placing Ota alongside Zonza under a loose heading such as “unsolved cases” would conceal the most important difference between them. Zonza was investigated and still resisted a satisfactory explanation. Ota remained undecidable because the evidential foundation was too weak.

The misallocated San-Martino-di-Lota entry illustrates the same point from outside the department’s true boundaries. GEIPAN labelled it C for lack of dependable information. Even were it counted as Corse-du-Sud, it would add an incomplete report rather than another unexplained event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=29&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A category C file can result from missing timings, imprecise directions, delayed reporting, the absence of original witness material or an account that cannot be checked against weather, astronomical and aviation data. Without those basics, investigators may be unable either to identify a cause or to establish that the observation was genuinely unusual. GEIPAN permits C and D cases to be revisited should significant new information emerge, meaning the published classification is a reasoned status rather than an irreversible verdict.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/mentions_legales" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why Zonza stands alone

The report near Zonza on 28 August 2014 is the exception within Corse-du-Sud’s official record. A witness described a large, silent, dark circular formation moving slowly across the pre-dawn sky, accompanied by ordered groups of smaller shapes. He reported the sighting to the gendarmerie on the same day, and GEIPAN later conducted an investigation. The service classified the case D1: unexplained, with good consistency and medium-to-high strangeness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This gives the case more weight than a poorly documented anecdote. Prompt reporting reduces the risk that the account developed through years of retelling. The gendarmerie record preserved the witness’s early description, and the later site investigation allowed GEIPAN to assess the viewing environment and possible trajectory. Under GEIPAN’s post-2008 methodology, D cases receive more intensive treatment, including field investigation and expert review.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

However, D1 does not mean that an extraordinary craft was established. GEIPAN’s classification says that no proposed explanation adequately accounted for the report while the evidence was sufficient to preserve it as a meaningful anomaly. It does not identify the nature, origin or even exact physical structure of what was perceived.

The limitations remain substantial. The observation rested principally on one witness. A second person was reportedly present but was never identified and formally interviewed. There was no photograph, video, physical trace or publicly documented radar confirmation. Distance and altitude were unknown, making estimates of size and speed impossible to verify. Those weaknesses help explain why the case was D1 rather than the stronger D2 category. GEIPAN describes D1 cases as unexplained but of moderate evidential consistency or strangeness—for example, where only one witness is available or no photograph exists.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2021-08/FAQ%20-%20Press%20-%20English.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Later publication has therefore clarified the original claim without decisively strengthening it. The released documents show that the report was taken seriously and investigated rather than ignored. They also expose the gaps that prevent stronger conclusions. No subsequent official evidence has converted the sighting into a confirmed aircraft event, military incident or exotic vehicle.

## What the pattern says about evidence

The departmental archive supports three broad conclusions.

**First, most official reports are not enduring mysteries.** At least six of the geographically valid Corse-du-Sud entries have confident or probable conventional explanations: balloon, meteor, laser, aircraft, rocket material and Sirius. The range is more revealing than any one explanation. It demonstrates that “UFO” reports do not form a single kind of phenomenon; they are an observational category into which many unrelated causes initially fall.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C53&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AJACCIO (2A) 14.12.1976 &#124; 14/12/1976 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Ballon scientifique ou technologique &#124; 13/09/2011 &#124; 1976-12-14...</span></span></span>

**Second, unexplained and unexplainable are not the same.** Ota could not be judged because the information was inadequate. Zonza remained unexplained despite a fuller investigation. Treating both as equivalent would reward missing evidence by making a weak case appear as mysterious as an investigated anomaly. GEIPAN’s separation of C from D is intended to prevent precisely that mistake.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;order=title&amp;page=29&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nom du cas &#124; Date d&#x27;observation &#124; Classification &#124; Département &#124; Phénomene &#124; Date de mise a jour &#124; Date d&#x27;observation &#124; Lien... **Third, the archive does not establish a departmental hotspot.** The reports are spread across nearly five decades and do not form a clear temporal cluster. Their explanations are heterogeneous, and the only D1 case has no officially confirmed link to radar, aviation safety, an airbase or repeated observations by independent witnesses. On the published evidence, Corse-du-Sud is better understood as a department with one notable unresolved sighting amid a thin background of misidentifications and incomplete reports, not as the site of a sustained unexplained aerial pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C53&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AJACCIO (2A) 14.12.1976 &#124; 14/12/1976 &#124; A &#124; Corse-du-Sud &#124; Ballon scientifique ou technologique &#124; 13/09/2011 &#124; 1976-12-14...</span></span></span>

## How unusual is the official record?

In numerical terms, the department’s record is too small and administratively untidy to support ambitious statistical claims. The search results contain only a handful of genuine Corse-du-Sud cases, and at least two entries are apparently assigned to the wrong department. Reporting rates also depend on public awareness, willingness to contact authorities, the survival of older records and GEIPAN’s publication criteria—not simply on how often unusual things appeared in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=title&amp;page=152&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Its more meaningful feature is the contrast between classifications. The archive contains examples of nearly every important evidential outcome: confident identification, probable identification, insufficient information and one investigated anomaly. That makes Corse-du-Sud a compact demonstration of what an official UFO archive actually records.

It records experiences first and explanations second. Some experiences become identifiable once timings and trajectories are compared with known objects. Some remain indeterminate because essential details are missing. A very small remainder can resist the available explanations without thereby proving an extraordinary cause.

For Corse-du-Sud, that remainder currently consists of Zonza. Its status deserves neither dismissal nor inflation. It is a documented unresolved witness report with genuine investigative interest, but it stands in an archive whose dominant lesson is the frequency of mistaken identification and the decisive importance of evidence quality.

        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
