## Introduction

The Ternay sighting of 22 May 1981 became one of [Loir-et-Cher]({{ 'loir-et-cher/' | relative_url }})’s strongest official UFO cases because four family members watched the same luminous phenomenon for up to fifteen minutes, reported it almost immediately and helped gendarmes reconstruct the event the next morning. The witnesses described a dark, elongated form with intense white, blue-green and red-orange lights, apparently hovering near a small wood. No physical trace was found.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Loir-et-Cher_7a21fd_ternay_forestry_expl_a678b7-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Ternay" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

For decades, the case remained officially unexplained. A detailed GEIPAN re-examination published in 2018 changed that assessment. Using terrain profiles, aerial photographs and reconstructed sightlines, investigators concluded that the witnesses had probably seen the lights of forestry machinery working about two kilometres away, not an object 300–400 metres away in the air. The explanation is persuasive but not conclusive: no particular machine or operator was identified, and the exact date of the documented tree-felling remains uncertain. The case is therefore best described as **probably explained**, not definitively solved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## What the four witnesses reported

At about 11.40–11.45 pm, four relatives were travelling from Ternay towards Saint-Martin-des-Bois in a Peugeot 404 after a music rehearsal. One passenger first noticed a light and suggested that it might be an aircraft. The driver also initially considered an aeroplane approaching the Tours aviation area, but rejected that impression because the phenomenon appeared stationary and unlike an ordinary aircraft. He stopped the car so that the group could observe it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The broad outline was consistent across the four statements. They reported a single, strongly illuminated phenomenon close to a small wood, apparently low above the ground. The observation lasted roughly ten to fifteen minutes. All four regarded it as stationary during much of that period, although some described an apparent approach or changes of position before the car stopped. No one reported hearing an engine, rotor or other unusual sound.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The descriptions were similar but not identical:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* The driver described a dark, elongated, lens-like mass, perhaps eight metres long, with white, blue-green and red-orange lights.
* A second witness could not determine a clear shape and chiefly remembered bright white and red lights.
* The third and fourth witnesses described an oval or elongated lens, numerous coloured lights and blue reflections or beams beneath it.
* Several witnesses thought the red-orange lights marked the two ends of a larger dark body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

This agreement gave the report considerable weight, but it must be interpreted carefully. The witnesses were members of one family, travelling together and discussing the phenomenon as they watched it. Their accounts therefore confirm a shared visual event, but they are not equivalent to four observers independently encountering the same object from separate locations.

The conditions were also poor for estimating shape, height and distance. It was a very dark rural night under a heavily clouded sky, with no clear horizon. Two witnesses openly acknowledged that they could not describe the object precisely. Against such a background, separated points of light can be mentally joined into the outline of a single solid object, while the apparent distance of an unfamiliar light source may be wrong by a large margin. GEIPAN’s later analysis treated this loss of depth and horizon information as the central weakness in the original interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

### The disappearance and the mist

The most dramatic part of the account came after the main observation. The lights appeared to disappear behind the small wood. The driver and his daughter later approached from the other side by the D116, looking for the object. They reported that thick mist, smoke or cloud briefly obscured the wood. After turning back, the daughter noticed small red-orange, star-like lights roughly a metre above the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

These details encouraged the idea that a craft had descended or landed. Yet none of the witnesses actually saw an object touch the ground. “Landing” was an inference based on the lights becoming hidden by the trees and appearing on either side of them. The mist was not sampled or otherwise established as an effect produced by the phenomenon; damp, unsettled conditions were already present, and the witnesses themselves were unsure whether they had seen smoke or fog.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The small lights seen near the ground are potentially compatible with a terrestrial vehicle. They are also one of the weaker parts of the testimony because only one of the two returning witnesses included them in the quoted account, and the pair did not stop again despite having returned specifically to search for the phenomenon. GEIPAN regarded this as evidence that the final lights did not appear as extraordinary as the main display.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## How gendarmes investigated the scene

The speed and thoroughness of the initial response distinguish Ternay from many historical UFO stories. The principal witness contacted the gendarmerie within minutes of returning home. Officers went to his home and arranged a daylight examination. At about dawn, they accompanied him back to the area and spent several hours reconstructing the observation and searching the presumed disappearance site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The principal witness gave a formal statement on 23 May. The other three were interviewed separately over the following days. The resulting case file contained four witness accounts, two sketches, four site photographs and a location plan. That documentation allowed later investigators to compare what each person remembered and to reconstruct the roads, viewing directions and intervening terrain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

No indentation, scorching, damaged vegetation, residue or other physical trace was found. Nor did the investigation establish radar confirmation, an independent witness outside the family or an identifiable aircraft operating at the reported position. The lack of traces does not disprove an airborne light, but it substantially weakens the suggestion that an eight-metre vehicle landed beside the wood.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The original enquiry nevertheless had a significant limitation: it concentrated on the small wood that the witnesses believed was 300–400 metres away. That distance depended on their assumption that the phenomenon was beside or immediately behind the wood. The investigation did not establish that the lights and the wood were at the same range. This later proved decisive.

## Why the apparent distance was probably wrong

The 2018 reconstruction separated two features that the witnesses had visually combined: a nearby small wood used as a reference point, and a more distant area of rising farmland and woodland lying in roughly the same direction. GEIPAN’s terrain profiles showed that the ground south of the observers gradually rose towards fields near the locality of L’Airerie. A brightly illuminated vehicle there, approximately two kilometres away, could remain visible above or beside the nearer trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Because the true horizon was indistinct, lights on the higher ground could appear suspended against the dark sky. The witnesses then used the nearer wood to estimate the source as only 300–400 metres distant and about ten metres above the ground. Once that assumed distance was accepted, the spacing between the lights naturally suggested an object several metres long. At two kilometres, however, the same angular separation could represent the lamps fitted across a large ground vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Movement by the witnesses’ car provides a further explanation for the apparent approach. A nearby reference object changes position against a distant source as the observer moves, an effect known as parallax. The distant lights could therefore appear to move from open sky towards the small wood even if the vehicle itself moved only slowly. Once the witnesses stopped, real manoeuvres by the machine could account for the more limited movement and eventual disappearance behind intervening trees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

This reconstruction fits an otherwise puzzling feature of the testimony. The driver said that the red-orange light sometimes appeared at different ends and that its flashing seemed connected with changes in the object’s position. GEIPAN interpreted this as the changing orientation of a working vehicle: forward lamps, rear or warning lights and beams reflected from the ground or vegetation would become more or less visible as it turned.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Intermittent obstruction offers a straightforward explanation for the reported scintillation. Branches passing in front of powerful lamps could make them appear to blink, while illuminated foliage, dust or moisture could produce diffuse coloured reflections. This mechanism requires no deliberate flashing pattern and is consistent with witnesses seeing both steady lights and momentary bursts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## Why GEIPAN favoured forestry machinery

GEIPAN considered two related ground explanations: an agricultural tractor working at night, or forestry machinery undertaking cutting, clearance or maintenance. Both could carry multiple high-powered work lamps and move slowly through an area of fields and trees. Investigators regarded the forestry option as the better fit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The most important supporting evidence came from aerial photography. An image from May 1978 showed a substantial wooded patch near L’Airerie. A photograph from July 1981—about two months after the sighting—showed that part of it had been cleared. The comparison established that forestry operations occurred sometime within that three-year interval and suggested that cutting may already have been under way on the night of 22 May.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The proposed source also matches several observational features better than a conventional aircraft:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* It could remain nearly stationary for fifteen minutes.
* Its working lights could shine downwards and illuminate trees or ground.
* Turning could expose white front lamps, red or orange rear lights and differently tinted reflections.
* Trees could intermittently conceal the lamps.
* Its sound might be inaudible at about two kilometres, particularly from inside or beside a car with its engine running.
* It could disappear simply by moving behind vegetation, switching off its lights or leaving the working area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN scored the forestry hypothesis at 70 per cent in its internal comparison, against 50 per cent for agricultural machinery. The figure should not be read as a statistically calculated probability. It was the investigator’s structured judgement of how well each proposal accounted for the reported form, colours, brightness, movement, disappearance and evidence of activity at the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The re-examination ultimately changed the case from category D, meaning unexplained after investigation, to category B, meaning probably identified. The current GEIPAN case page records that B classification and gives the report comparatively moderate consistency but low strangeness once the ground explanation is considered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## Where the machinery explanation remains uncertain

The 2018 report reconstructed a plausible source but did not identify an actual machine. No forestry company record, operator testimony, work log or invoice was produced to confirm that illuminated machinery was operating near L’Airerie at 11.40 pm on the relevant night. The aerial photographs prove that trees were removed between May 1978 and July 1981; they do not prove that the work took place on 22 May 1981. GEIPAN explicitly acknowledged uncertainty over the exact date of the felling.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Night work is another assumption rather than a documented fact. The report suggests that cutting, maintenance or clearance may have continued after dark, possibly after beginning during daylight. That is feasible, especially where machinery was already deployed, but the enquiry did not establish why work would have continued so late or what type of equipment was used.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The reported blue and green lights are not an obvious match for standard vehicle lighting. GEIPAN noted this weakness in its own assessment. Coloured reflections from vegetation, wet surfaces, lamp housings or perceptual effects might account for them, but no reconstruction using a comparable period machine was conducted at the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

There is also some uncertainty in the proposed geometry. Investigators showed that a ground source in the more distant field could have been visible, but the original stopping points and exact sightlines were reconstructed many years later. Small errors in position can matter when trees, slopes and intervening features determine whether a light is visible. Modern aerial imagery and street-level photographs improved the analysis, although the landscape was not necessarily unchanged from 1981.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Finally, the explanation for the witnesses failing to find the source is possible rather than demonstrated. GEIPAN proposed that they looked to their right behind the nearby wood, while the distant working area lay farther south and to their left; alternatively, the machine may have switched off or moved behind trees. Each possibility resolves the difficulty, but there is no surviving observation showing which, if any, occurred.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## Did later reporting strengthen or weaken the case?

Later investigation strengthened confidence that the family saw a real and unusual display of lights. Their statements were taken promptly, the central features were broadly consistent, and geographical reconstruction confirmed that their viewing direction crossed a landscape capable of producing the reported appearance. There is no persuasive indication that the witnesses invented the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

What weakened was the original interpretation of an airborne craft. The witnesses’ estimated distance was not independently measured; it was inferred from the small wood. Once a more distant ground source was considered, the apparent low altitude, eight-metre size, approach, hovering and disappearance could all arise from mistaken depth perception, parallax and partial obstruction. The absence of physical traces then became unsurprising rather than anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete18.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CR AC_JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The most balanced verdict is therefore narrower than either “unexplained UFO” or “fully debunked”. The testimony is strong evidence that four people sincerely observed powerful, unfamiliar lights. The forestry explanation is the most coherent account presently available and is supported by the terrain and evidence of tree-cutting. However, because the responsible vehicle and its operation were never independently confirmed, it remains a probable reconstruction rather than a demonstrated identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/49012" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANTERNAY (41) 22.05.1981 T4 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Within Loir-et-Cher’s UFO history, Ternay matters precisely because good witnesses and a prompt official investigation did not guarantee a correct initial interpretation. It shows how a nearby visual reference can make distant ground lights appear airborne—and how revisiting an old file with better mapping and historical imagery can convert a compelling mystery into a plausible case of mistaken location.
