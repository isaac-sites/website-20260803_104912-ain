## Introduction

[Savoie]({{ 'why-savoies-ufo-mysteries-usually-fade/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO files prove something more modest, but more useful, than a hidden aerial mystery. They show that people across the department have reported genuinely puzzling lights and objects, and that careful investigation often turns those experiences into recognisable phenomena: planets, distress flares, satellites, aircraft contrails, balloons, lanterns, insects, paragliders and atmospheric re-entry events. Other reports remain unclassified because the available evidence is too poor to test properly.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Savoie_f57b8f_savoie_official_ufo_a0732b-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction is crucial. In the public archive maintained by GEIPAN, the French government unit that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena, an unexplained report is not automatically evidence of an extraordinary object. A weak file and a strong unexplained case occupy different categories. The searchable Savoie record is dominated by explained, probably explained and insufficiently documented cases, rather than a body of well-supported incidents that resisted investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=45.52221935752051&amp;customGetLongitude=5.278930664062501&amp;customGetZoom=8&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=46.638122462379656&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.40631625266138&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.146606445312501&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=3.4112548828125004&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN classifies a Savoie sighting

GEIPAN, part of France’s National Centre for Space Studies, does not classify a case according to how dramatic the witness’s story sounds. Its assessment uses two linked measures: the **consistency** of the evidence and the **residual strangeness** left after ordinary explanations have been considered.

Consistency reflects the quantity and reliability of the information collected. A report supported by accurate timings, several independent witnesses, original photographs, known viewing directions and relevant aviation or astronomical data will be stronger than a recollection submitted years later with no precise location or duration. Residual strangeness asks how unusual the event still appears after investigators compare it with known phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The resulting categories are easy to misunderstand:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A:** the phenomenon has been identified.
* **B:** an ordinary explanation is considered probable, although not proved beyond doubt.
* **C:** no identification is possible because essential information is missing or unreliable.
* **D:** the event remains unexplained despite an investigation based on sufficiently useful evidence.

</div>

GEIPAN has also used D1 and D2 subdivisions to distinguish between unexplained cases of moderate evidential strength and highly strange cases supported by stronger material, such as independent witnesses, recordings or physical traces. It stresses that classifications may change when new evidence appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANHow does GEIPAN classify observation cases? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This means the categories are not a scale running from “ordinary” to “alien”. Category C does not sit above B as a more mysterious result. It usually means that investigators cannot do enough with the file.

## Why category C does not prove an extraordinary object

Several Savoie entries are labelled C with the stated reason that reliable information is lacking. They include reports from Le Bourget-du-Lac in February 1964, La Motte-Servolex in March 1979, Salins-les-Thermes in August 1980, Tignes in February 2008 and Aix-les-Bains in August 2010. The archive preserves these reports, but their classification records an evidential failure rather than a successful demonstration of something anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=45.52221935752051&amp;customGetLongitude=5.278930664062501&amp;customGetZoom=8&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=46.638122462379656&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.40631625266138&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.146606445312501&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=3.4112548828125004&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The Saint-Étienne-de-Cuines report of 24 August 1980 illustrates the problem. A witness described a very bright object seen at about 3.30 am, with what appeared to be a pale pink parachute shape briefly visible beneath it. The bright object reportedly remained visible for more than ten minutes. The description is interesting, but GEIPAN classifies the case C because the information is insufficient for a dependable identification or reconstruction. There is no measurement establishing the object’s distance, altitude, actual size or physical connection to the apparent “parachute”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-08-00791" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-ETIENNE-DE-CUINES (73) 24.08.198024 Aug 1980 — Geipan Groupe. SAINT-ETIENNE-DE-CUINES (73) 24.08.1980 Date d&#x27;observation 24/0...</span></span></span>

Such files may remain unresolved indefinitely because the missing information cannot be recovered. A precise direction might have allowed an astronomical comparison. A reliable sequence of times could have been checked against aircraft or satellite movements. Independent witnesses might have shown whether the perceived shape was stable or subjective. Without those elements, competing explanations cannot be properly separated.

The honest conclusion is therefore not that an unknown craft was present. It is that the historical testimony is too incomplete to decide what was present. Category C protects that distinction by preventing speculation from being mistaken for an investigative result.

## Explained cases reveal the archive’s real value

The strongest evidence in Savoie’s official record often comes from cases that no longer look mysterious. These files show what investigators can establish when observations contain enough detail to compare with external information.

At Chambéry on 10 April 1997, a puzzling aerial light was ultimately classified A and identified as a distress flare. Elsewhere in the department’s archive, a 7 October 2001 event was attributed with high probability to atmospheric re-entry, a January 2010 sighting at Saint-François-Longchamp to a weather balloon, and a July 2022 report at Avrieux to the International Space Station.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=45.52221935752051&amp;customGetLongitude=5.278930664062501&amp;customGetZoom=8&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=46.638122462379656&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.40631625266138&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.146606445312501&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=3.4112548828125004&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Aiton’s 20 November 2021 case is particularly instructive because photographs alone did not settle the matter. The witness saw diffuse, dark formations against the evening sky and supplied eight images. GEIPAN’s published file includes an investigation report and a radar reconstruction. The result was category A: aircraft contrails, with very low residual strangeness and reasonably good consistency. The lesson is not that photographs are worthless, but that they become useful only when their time, direction, geometry and environmental setting can be checked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/10094" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAITON (73) 20.11.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Entremont-le-Vieux investigation shows how phone photography can create additional apparent detail rather than clarify it. Beginning in December 2024, a witness repeatedly observed white luminous forms and later photographed what seemed, under digital enlargement, to resemble an object. GEIPAN corrected an initially mistaken photograph date, compared the observations with the sky and identified the principal lights as Mars and Jupiter. The file was classified A, with low strangeness and comparatively good supporting information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-12-51614" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANENTREMONT-LE-VIEUX (73) 12.12.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is one of the archive’s most important findings. A sincere witness can accurately report seeing a bright light while misjudging what the light was, how far away it lay or whether magnified camera artefacts represented real structure. Investigation may validate the experience without validating the witness’s interpretation.

## Probable explanations are evidence, not dismissals

Category B cases are sometimes treated as though investigators merely guessed at an explanation. In reality, a B classification usually means that several details fit a known cause, but the decisive object cannot be independently recovered or confirmed.

On 6 July 2015, two people near Chambéry reported between 15 and 20 pink-orange lights moving silently in a line towards Aix-les-Bains. GEIPAN judged Thai-style sky lanterns to be the probable explanation. The colour, orderly release pattern, summer timing and movement matched lantern behaviour, while the direction was compatible with the recorded southerly wind. Because no individual lantern was recovered or traced to a specific event, the conclusion remained probable rather than certain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-07-09233" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The case also demonstrates a recurring problem in UFO testimony: estimating an unfamiliar object’s distance. A small, dim lantern nearby can be perceived as a larger, more distant object. Once the assumed distance is wrong, estimates of size and speed also become unreliable. GEIPAN specifically noted this effect in its Chambéry analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-07-09233" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other Savoie B files show similar reasoning. Reports have been attributed to a sky-tracer searchlight at Novalaise, a paraglider seen between Bourg-Saint-Maurice and Peisey-Nancroix, an insect photographed at Domessin, a weather balloon at Saint-François-Longchamp and an apparent vehicle at Aussois. Chambéry and La Motte-Servolex reports from June 2018 were assessed as LED balloons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=45.52221935752051&amp;customGetLongitude=5.278930664062501&amp;customGetZoom=8&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=46.638122462379656&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.40631625266138&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.146606445312501&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=3.4112548828125004&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

No single explanation accounts for every Savoie report. The pattern is instead one of repeated misidentification drawn from the department’s actual environment: recreational flying, celebrations, mountain transport, astronomy, aviation and objects crossing a dark sky with few dependable distance cues.

## The archive does not show a Savoie UFO hotspot

The current searchable material does not present Savoie as a concentration of strong category D incidents. The entries visible in the department’s official listing are principally A, B and C cases. That matters because a claim of a persistent local anomaly would require more than a succession of unexplained anecdotes. It would need well-documented events retaining significant strangeness after investigation, ideally with independent observations, reliable timing, instrument data or repeatable geographical features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=45.52221935752051&amp;customGetLongitude=5.278930664062501&amp;customGetZoom=8&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=46.638122462379656&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=44.40631625266138&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=7.146606445312501&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=3.4112548828125004&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The Aiton case, for example, shows radar material being used to resolve an observation as an ordinary aviation-related phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/10094" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAITON (73) 20.11.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Absence of a strong D cluster does not prove that every unusual thing ever seen in Savoie has been identified. GEIPAN receives only a fraction of all experiences, and the quality of submitted reports varies sharply. The organisation itself warns that changes in the proportion of unexplained cases may reflect reporting rates, stricter definitions and improved investigative tools rather than a change in whatever people are seeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## What recurring explanations tell us about Savoie

Taken together, the files reveal several practical reasons why ordinary phenomena can look exceptional in the department.

**Mountain horizons distort position.** Ridges can conceal the lower part of a light source, interrupt its path or make a distant object appear to move along the terrain. Without a visible horizon, witnesses may struggle to judge elevation and distance.

**Dark surroundings exaggerate brightness.** A planet, lantern or illuminated paraglider viewed against an unlit mountainside can appear isolated, close and unusually intense.

**Tourism and recreation add unfamiliar objects.** Paragliders, balloons, lanterns and mountain events introduce lights or shapes that an observer may not immediately recognise, particularly at night.

**Photography can manufacture apparent form.** Digital zoom, camera movement, focus errors and image processing can turn a point of light into a streak, disc or structured-looking patch. The Entremont-le-Vieux investigation demonstrates why the original viewing conditions matter more than an enlarged phone image considered in isolation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-12-51614" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANENTREMONT-LE-VIEUX (73) 12.12.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Modern checks improve identification.** Astronomical software, weather records, satellite predictions, radar information and precise photograph metadata allow recent sightings to be tested in ways that were rarely possible for reports collected in the 1960s or 1970s. GEIPAN has linked the decline in category D classifications nationally partly to stricter classification and more extensive investigation using modern tools.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

These factors do not imply that witnesses are dishonest or foolish. They show that aerial perception is a reconstruction made from limited visual cues. The stranger and less familiar the object, the harder it becomes to estimate its true dimensions and movement.

## What the official files really prove

Savoie’s records establish that unusual aerial experiences occur, that witnesses often report them sincerely and that some remain unresolved. They also establish that “unresolved” covers two very different situations: a well-investigated event that resists explanation, and a report that cannot be meaningfully analysed because too little evidence survives.

Most of the department’s official cases fall outside the first of those situations. Stronger files repeatedly produce conventional explanations, while many older mysteries remain in category C because their evidential foundations are weak. The archive therefore supports neither blanket dismissal nor extraordinary certainty.

Its most reliable conclusion is methodological. A strange description is the beginning of a case, not the result. What matters is whether the report contains enough independent, testable information to distinguish an unknown phenomenon from a planet, lantern, flare, aircraft, balloon, satellite or optical effect. In Savoie, that process has so far reduced the official mystery far more often than it has strengthened it.
