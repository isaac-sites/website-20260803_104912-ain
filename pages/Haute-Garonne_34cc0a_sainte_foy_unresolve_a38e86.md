## Introduction

The Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières sighting is unresolved because investigators could not make any ordinary explanation fit the witness’s full account with sufficient confidence. On 8 July 2018, a resident west of Toulouse reported a silent sequence of lights and dark geometric forms crossing much of the night sky in roughly 20 seconds. GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that investigates unidentified aerial phenomena, tested satellites, atmospheric re-entry, fireworks, aircraft, visual after-images and drones before placing the case in its unexplained D1 category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Il y a une diza...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 7, 2024</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Haute-Garonne_34cc0a_sainte_foy_unresolve_a38e86-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Sainte Foy" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That classification makes Sainte-Foy one of [Haute-Garonne]({{ 'why-haute-garonnes-strangest-sightings/' | relative_url }})’s most substantial modern unresolved files, but it does not establish that an extraordinary craft was present. The event was seen by only one person, produced no photograph, video or physical trace, and lacked the timely military radar record that might have confirmed or contradicted the reported flight path. Its real importance lies in the tension between an unusually detailed account and unusually limited independent evidence.

## The twenty-second sighting and witness account

The observation began at about 11.30 pm in the witness’s garden at Sainte-Foy-de-Peyrolières, around 25 kilometres west of central Toulouse. The sky was reportedly clear and well starred. Meteorological information consulted by GEIPAN indicated weak north-westerly wind, good horizontal visibility and no detected cloud layer below 7,500 metres at Toulouse-Blagnac. The witness was seated with his mother, although she did not see the main phenomenon because it unfolded too quickly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. The unusually prompt first report is one of the case’s strengths. At 11.41 pm, about ten minutes after the event, the witness left a 149-second telephone message for GEIPAN. This reduced the risk that the central story had been built entirely through years of retelling. He subsequently supplied a typed narrative, ten reconstruction drawings, annotated maps and a sky chart, completed a technical questionnaire and took part in an on-site reconstruction and cognitive interview in December 2018. Further measurements and exchanges continued over several years, and the case went before GEIPAN’s expert committee in 2022 and 2023.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Prompt reporting, however, did not eliminate uncertainty. The first voicemail described a much briefer event of two or three seconds involving four aligned lights, a reduction to two lights and a triangular appearance. The later account expanded the duration to about 20 seconds and divided it into five detailed phases. That does not prove invention: an initial message made in a shocked state may be compressed, while later questioning can recover omitted details. Nevertheless, the difference matters because estimates of speed, direction and shape depend heavily on timing that was reconstructed rather than instrumentally recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. There was also a small but relevant interruption in viewing. The witness later described jumping and having to clear or look past a large tree, losing sight of the phenomenon for about a second. Elsewhere, he indicated that he had not lost it from view. GEIPAN treated this as the account’s principal internal contradiction. In an event lasting only seconds, a one-second obstruction creates the possibility that lights seen before and after the tree were not the same object or formation, even though investigators considered that possibility unlikely because their appearance seemed continuous to the witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. ## Explanations tested by investigators

GEIPAN did not identify one obvious answer and stop. Its final report examined seven broad possibilities: a train of satellites, fragments from an atmospheric re-entry, fireworks, a single aircraft, several aircraft in formation, retinal persistence caused by bright lights, and a coordinated drone display. The opening shooting star was treated separately from the later phenomenon and may simply have directed the witness’s attention towards the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. **Satellites and atmospheric debris** were poor matches for the reported manoeuvres and changing geometry. CNES’s space-surveillance specialists found several orbiting objects moving generally north–south or south–north within the relevant period, but not at matching times or in a configuration corresponding to the groups of lights. They also found no atmospheric re-entry on that date. GRAVES, the French space-surveillance radar, did not provide evidence of a relevant low-orbit object. These checks substantially weakened a space-object explanation, but they did not constitute radar tracking of the reported phenomenon at low altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Il y a une diza...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 7, 2024</span></span></span>

**Fireworks** could superficially account for cascading lights and changing arrangements, particularly on a summer Sunday near the Toulouse area. Investigators found no identified display at the relevant time, however, and judged the later long horizontal movement incompatible with normal fireworks. The reported initial elevation of about 65 degrees would also have required a substantial professional launch if the display were distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Il y a une diza...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 7, 2024</span></span></span>

**A visual after-image** was considered because the witness had been looking towards the illuminated façade of the house before turning to the dark sky. Strong lights can leave temporary retinal images. GEIPAN rejected this as a complete explanation because the reported lights changed their relative positions and appeared to remain in an external part of the sky while the witness followed them. ## The radar gap that the investigation could not repair

Radar was potentially the best route to independent confirmation. The witness recognised this himself, telling investigators that radar detection was effectively the only evidence capable of supporting his account beyond testimony. GEIPAN asked the French Air Force’s National Air Operations Centre for a reconstruction of traffic, but the relevant record was either never received or was subsequently lost. By the time the request was renewed, the data-retention period had expired. The final report described this failure as particularly regrettable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Civil radar data were eventually obtained from Toulouse-Blagnac for an area centred on Toulouse. The recorded traffic did not reproduce the witness’s description of several aircraft around Sainte-Foy. However, the retrieved time window—21.50 to 22.10 UTC—may have been slightly too late or otherwise insufficiently aligned with the uncertain observation time. Its geographical coverage was also centred on Toulouse rather than constructed specifically around the whole apparent track reported from Sainte-Foy. It could weaken particular aircraft scenarios, but it could not establish that the relevant airspace was empty throughout the critical interval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. The distinction between the radar systems is important. Space-surveillance checks addressed satellites and orbital debris. Blagnac’s record covered identified civil traffic within a limited window and area. The unavailable military reconstruction was the dataset most likely to clarify whether unlisted, military or otherwise relevant aircraft were present. None of the radar work produced a target matching the witness’s object, but the coverage was not complete enough to turn “no match found” into “no conventional aircraft was there”.

Later investigation therefore improved the range of explanations tested without repairing the central evidential gap. More drawings, azimuth estimates and interviews made the testimony richer, but they remained products of the same witness. They could test his internal consistency; they could not independently verify the object’s existence, distance, speed or physical form.

## Why category D1 does not prove an exotic craft

GEIPAN’s classification system separates the strangeness of a report from its consistency. “Strangeness” concerns how poorly the best ordinary hypothesis explains the observation. “Consistency” concerns the quantity and quality of the available information, including witness numbers, precision, reliability and photographic or other supporting material. A D1 classification means that the case is considered strange and sufficiently documented to remain unexplained; D2 is reserved for still more unusual cases with stronger consistency. Classifications may be revised if new evidence appears.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">is above 0.5. In Sainte-Foy, consistency came from the witness’s prompt contact, extensive cooperation, detailed reconstructions and broad coherence during later questioning. It did not come from multiple independent observers or instruments. GEIPAN explicitly recorded one witness, no photograph, no video and no material evidence. The mother’s presence is not corroboration of the main event because she saw the preceding shooting star but not the subsequent formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete679.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">August 7, 2024 — 8 Jul 2024 — Réponse du COSE: « Objet: SAINTE-FOY-DE-PEYROLIERES (31) 08.07.2018: demande visibilité. Il y a une diza...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 7, 2024</span></span></span>

D1 therefore answers a limited question: did GEIPAN find a sufficiently persuasive conventional explanation for the reported experience? Its answer was no. It does not answer the much larger question of what objectively occupied the sky. “Unexplained” leaves open several possibilities, including an unusual aircraft configuration, a mistaken reconstruction of separate lights, an unrecorded drone or display, a perceptual effect not successfully reproduced, or a genuinely unfamiliar physical event.

This is also why witness credibility and physical proof must not be treated as interchangeable. A person may report an experience honestly and carefully while still misjudging distance, duration, continuity or speed. NASA’s independent scientific review of unidentified aerial phenomena reached the same general evidential point: eyewitness reports can be compelling and useful, but without calibrated, corroborating sensor data they cannot support definitive conclusions about an object’s nature or performance.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/uap-independent-study-team-final-report-0.pdf?emrc=66f0247c15428" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: nasa.gov">[NASA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">nasa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">uap independent study team final report 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UNIDENTIFIED ANOMALOUS PHENOMENA...</span></span></span>

For Haute-Garonne, Sainte-Foy is best described as a serious but evidentially bounded unresolved case. It is stronger than a vague light reported long afterwards because the witness contacted investigators quickly and submitted to an unusually extensive inquiry. It is weaker than a multi-witness or sensor-supported incident because every remarkable feature—dark structure, changing geometry, silence and extreme acceleration—ultimately depends on one brief visual observation. The investigation justified keeping the file open; it did not justify converting uncertainty into evidence of an exotic craft.
