## Introduction

The 1954 reports around Toul and Longwy did not form a single, closely documented incident. They were a small cluster of separate observations that newspapers connected during France’s autumn “flying-saucer” wave. The best-known account came from Roger Thiriet, a prison officer who said that on 10 October he encountered a small metallic craft beside a fogbound road near Toul. Reports from the Longwy area described luminous discs, strange sounds and, in one case, a scorched patch of ground.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Meurthe-et-Moselle_cbfe8a_toul_longwy_1954_wav_e4a1ea-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1954 Wave" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

These stories matter because they show how a regional UFO wave took shape. Poor visibility, delayed testimony, Cold War fears and repeated press comparisons made unrelated events appear mutually supporting. Yet the surviving evidence is largely newspaper-based. There are no photographs of the objects, no published laboratory result from the alleged Longwy-area ground trace and no surviving body of independent statements strong enough to establish that an extraordinary craft was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

## The encounters reported near Toul and Longwy

The central Toul story began before dawn on Sunday, 10 October 1954. Roger Thiriet, aged 40, was riding a motorised bicycle from Blénod-lès-Toul to his job at the Écrouves prison. Near the railway bridge over the old RN60, at the junction for Charmes-la-Côte, his headlamp reportedly illuminated an aluminium-coloured form about ten metres ahead. The morning was dark and heavily foggy, a detail recorded in the original newspaper account rather than added by later sceptics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

Thiriet described a circular object approximately 2 to 2.4 metres across and 1.4 metres high, topped by a dome and apparently fitted with two windows. He thought it was resting directly on or close to the road. After stopping his engine and moving towards it, he said, the object rose silently, without smoke or flame. He could follow its ascent for only a few metres before darkness and fog concealed it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

Several features made the story attractive to newspapers. The witness had a respectable occupation, gave unusually precise dimensions and claimed a close encounter rather than a distant light. He also portrayed himself as cautious: he waited more than a week before speaking because he feared ridicule. According to the contemporary article, the continuing stream of saucer stories eventually persuaded him to report his own experience. That delay, however understandable, meant that the account had already been exposed to days of national and regional UFO coverage before it was recorded publicly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

No physical trace was found. Thiriet examined the road when he returned from work but reported no mark, damage or residue. There was also no second witness at the roadside, no photograph and no independent measurement of the alleged object. The dimensions therefore depended on his estimate of distance in dense fog, illuminated mainly by a vehicle headlamp.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.estrepublicain.fr/edition-de-toul/2018/07/01/roger-et-le-mystere-de-la-soucoupe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: estrepublicain.fr">[L&#x27;Est Républicain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">estrepublicain.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L&#x27;Est Républicain Toul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L&#x27;Est RépublicainToul - Les incroyables histoires de Meurthe-et-Moselle. Roger et le mystère de la soucoupe...</span></span></span>

The Longwy material is less straightforward than later summaries sometimes suggest. Retrospective articles mention an observation on the same date, either at about 1.30 in the morning or later in the day, involving a disc or luminous object with a reddish trail. Some versions connect the afternoon observation with two of Thiriet’s colleagues, while others describe an unnamed observer near Longwy. The limited identification of the witnesses and the differences between retellings make it unsafe to treat these references as a firmly corroborated sighting of the same object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.estrepublicain.fr/edition-de-toul/2018/07/01/roger-et-le-mystere-de-la-soucoupe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: estrepublicain.fr">[L&#x27;Est Républicain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">estrepublicain.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L&#x27;Est Républicain Toul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L&#x27;Est RépublicainToul - Les incroyables histoires de Meurthe-et-Moselle. Roger et le mystère de la soucoupe...</span></span></span>

A more detailed northern [Meurthe-et-Moselle]({{ 'meurthe-et-moselle/' | relative_url }}) report followed during the night of 10–11 October near Doncourt, north-west of Longwy. Newspaper accounts said that people at an isolated farm were awakened at about 1.30 am by crackling and whistling sounds. Looking outside, they saw a flat, intensely luminous object apparently resting on a hillside by a wood. It was described through comparison rather than measurement—roughly three times the apparent size of the setting Sun—before rising vertically and disappearing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/11oct1954doncourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 11 octobre, Doncourt-Village, Moselle...</span></span></span>

Searchers later reported finding a circular burnt area about 1.5 metres across on a muddy track near the wood. Stones were said to have turned brick-red, while soil and vegetation appeared scorched. Samples were supposedly taken for laboratory examination. This sounds like the strongest physical element in the cluster, but no published analytical result has been located. The trace was not demonstrated to have been created during the observation, and accounts do not establish that witnesses saw the luminous object make direct contact with that exact patch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/11oct1954doncourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 11 octobre, Doncourt-Village, Moselle...</span></span></span>

These distinctions are important. The Toul case was a solitary close-range road encounter in fog. The loosely reported Longwy observations involved aerial lights. The Doncourt account involved a bright phenomenon on or beyond a hillside and a subsequently discovered burnt area. Their proximity in date and department encouraged comparison, but their reported appearances and evidential quality were not the same.

## How newspapers connected separate sightings

The original Toul article did more than reproduce a witness statement. Its opening placed the event within a “round” of saucers and assorted craft, noting that the Toul sky had previously seemed neglected except for an earlier disc reported near Gondreville. The language effectively recruited Toul into a story already under way: the remarkable point was not merely what Thiriet claimed to have seen, but that the national phenomenon had now reached the district.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

The same process operated around Longwy. Reports from Doncourt appeared beside dispatches about luminous discs, landed machines, stalled engines and alleged occupants elsewhere in France. A local reader therefore encountered each new story within a ready-made framework. A light on a hillside was not presented first as an unknown light; it became another “saucer”. A burnt patch was not initially treated as an isolated fire site; it became a possible landing trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/11oct1954doncourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 11 octobre, Doncourt-Village, Moselle...</span></span></span>

News agencies amplified that effect. The Doncourt report was shortened for national circulation, while place names were sometimes altered or confused. Later catalogues variously used Doncourt, Doncourt-Village, Doncourt-lès-Longuyon, Moncourt and even incorrect departmental labels. What appears in a modern list as several matching cases may therefore include duplicate versions of one newspaper story rather than several independent events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/11oct1954doncourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 11 octobre, Doncourt-Village, Moselle...</span></span></span>

The Toul account also acquired apparent support through the way reports were placed together. Thiriet’s silence ended after he heard about other sightings, and the newspaper immediately mentioned a reportedly similar object at Longwy. Yet similarity was asserted rather than demonstrated. A small aluminium-coloured structure apparently found on a road in headlamp beams is not obviously identical to a distant disc leaving a red trail. The connection came chiefly from the shared label “flying saucer” and the date of 10 October.

This does not mean that witnesses necessarily fabricated their experiences. It means the press acted as a pattern-making system. Reports encouraged further reports; familiar descriptions offered vocabulary to people trying to explain ambiguous experiences; and each newly published observation made earlier or later testimony seem less isolated. Research into UFO waves generally treats such sudden increases as reporting phenomena that must be studied alongside the underlying sightings, not simply counted as independent evidence of repeated external events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/UFO_Waves.__An_International_Bibliography__November__1___2015.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The surviving local journalism also reveals two contrasting tones. Contemporary coverage often emphasised witness respectability and mystery, while modern retrospectives use humour and openly question the more extravagant claims. Neither approach substitutes for an investigation. The older articles preserved valuable names, places and descriptions, but also relied on dramatic framing. The later pieces add historical distance, yet largely recycle the same newspaper material rather than providing newly recovered technical evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.estrepublicain.fr/edition-de-toul/2018/07/01/roger-et-le-mystere-de-la-soucoupe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: estrepublicain.fr">[L&#x27;Est Républicain]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">estrepublicain.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L&#x27;Est Républicain Toul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L&#x27;Est RépublicainToul - Les incroyables histoires de Meurthe-et-Moselle. ## Fog, military fears and later interpretation

Fog is central to assessing the Toul encounter. Thiriet’s description was detailed, but his own account states that visibility was poor enough to prevent him following the object after it had risen only a short distance. Headlights in fog create glare, reduce contrast and make distance difficult to judge. An unfamiliar roadside object, vehicle, structure or reflective surface may consequently appear closer, smaller or more sharply outlined than it is. The evidence does not identify a particular conventional object, but the conditions substantially weaken confidence in the reported dimensions and movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/press/estrepublicain20oct1954f.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: la presse, l&#x27;Est Républicain, France, le 20 octobre 1954...</span></span></span>

The alleged ascent is harder to evaluate because it was observed briefly and without a reference measurement. A change in illumination, the witness’s own movement or the obscuring effect of fog could have produced an impression of motion. Conversely, the surviving record does not prove that this happened. The responsible conclusion is narrower: the account cannot establish whether Thiriet saw a moving craft, misperceived an ordinary object or experienced some other visual episode.

The Longwy and Doncourt reports face different problems. A luminous object viewed from a farmhouse could have been much farther away than the witnesses believed, particularly if the hillside and forest edge were poorly visible. Bright astronomical objects, meteors, aircraft lights, flares and fires can all generate dramatic descriptions when their range is unknown. None perfectly explains every reported detail, especially the sounds and vertical departure, but those details survive through press summaries rather than separate, testable statements.

The scorched patch cannot settle the matter. A genuinely burnt area demonstrates that heat or fire affected the ground; it does not by itself identify the cause. A bonfire, deliberate burning, dumped hot material, industrial activity or an unrelated local event would need to be excluded. The promised laboratory analysis might have helped determine temperature or material changes, but no result is available in the accessible record. Claims that the trace proves a landing therefore go beyond the surviving evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/11oct1954doncourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 11 octobre, Doncourt-Village, Moselle...</span></span></span>

Cold War and military ideas also shaped interpretation. Thiriet reportedly rejected visitors from Mars and proposed instead that the machine was a secret weapon built by a foreign power, possibly operating from Antarctica. The Antarctic element was speculative, but the broader fear of hidden aircraft was understandable in 1954. Lorraine had recent memories of invasion and occupation, stood close to several national borders and was becoming an important NATO aviation zone. Toul-Rosières was used during 1954–55 by rotating United States Air Force units flying F-86H Sabre fighter-bombers, making advanced military aircraft a visible part of the region’s landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.estrepublicain.fr/edition-de-toul/2018/07/01/roger-et-le-mystere-de-la-soucoupe" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: estrepublicain.fr">[estrepublicain.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">estrepublicain.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">L&#x27;Est Républicain Toul</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L&#x27;Est RépublicainToul - Les incroyables histoires de Meurthe-et-Moselle. Roger et le mystère de la soucoupe...</span></span></span>

That military setting offers context rather than a direct solution. No sourced flight record connects a particular aircraft with the road encounter or the Longwy-area lights, and an F-86 does not resemble Thiriet’s claimed landed object. The airbase connection is nevertheless historically significant because witnesses and reporters did not have to choose only between aliens and imagination. Secret weapons, foreign reconnaissance and experimental aviation were already plausible cultural categories through which strange observations could be interpreted.

Later reporting has not materially strengthened the wave. It has preserved the original accounts and clarified some locations, but it has produced no object photographs, radar confirmation, trace analysis or additional independent testimony capable of resolving the principal uncertainties. In places, later cataloguing has made the record appear richer by repeating press versions under several names.

## What the 1954 wave means for Meurthe-et-Moselle

Within the department’s UFO history, the Toul and Longwy stories are most valuable as an example of how a “wave” can be assembled from unlike evidence. There was one named witness describing a close roadside object, several thinly reported aerial observations and a separate northern case involving light, sound and an unverified ground trace. Their rapid appearance during the national flap made them seem like parts of one regional event.

The evidence supports three restrained conclusions:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Something was reported:** the stories can be traced to contemporary regional and national newspaper coverage rather than solely to much later UFO books.
* **The reports were not mutually confirming in a strong sense:** they involved different settings, witnesses and descriptions, and some catalogue entries appear to be duplicates or confused place names.
* **No extraordinary explanation was established:** darkness, fog, uncertain distance, press influence and the absence of completed physical analysis leave ordinary or perceptual explanations open.

</div>

The Toul account remains unresolved only in the limited historical sense that the object Thiriet perceived cannot now be identified. “Unresolved” does not mean that a manufactured saucer was demonstrated. The Longwy-area material is weaker still because witness identities, timings and locations are inconsistently preserved. The Doncourt trace gives the cluster a tangible detail, but without chain of custody or laboratory findings it cannot carry the evidential weight later retellings sometimes place upon it.

Taken together, the reports show how Lorraine’s 1954 saucer wave took shape: an ambiguous encounter became news, the news encouraged other testimony, and newspapers joined observations across the department into a shared mystery. What survives is an illuminating chapter in Meurthe-et-Moselle’s social and media history, but not persuasive proof that a single unknown craft travelled between Toul and Longwy.
