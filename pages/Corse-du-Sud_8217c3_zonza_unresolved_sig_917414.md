## Introduction

The Zonza sighting remains unexplained because investigators could not match the witness’s account to aircraft, satellites, atmospheric debris, clouds or local lighting. At about 5 am on 28 August 2014, near Sainte-Lucie-de-Porto-Vecchio, a man reported a large, silent, dark circular form moving slowly towards Sardinia, accompanied by ordered groups of smaller dark shapes. He reported it to the gendarmerie that evening, and a GEIPAN investigator later visited the site.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

That official attention makes Zonza the most substantial unresolved case in [Corse-du-Sud]({{ 'corse-du-sud/' | relative_url }})’s published record. It does not, however, turn the sighting into proof of an extraordinary craft. There is no photograph, no identified second witness, no confirmed radar return and no usable estimate of distance or size. GEIPAN’s D1 classification means that a moderately strange case survived investigation with reasonable testimonial consistency—not that an extraterrestrial explanation was established.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANZONZA (2A) 28.08.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the witness reported before dawn

The observation took place at a campsite on the eastern side of southern Corsica. According to the investigation report, the witness had awakened because of the heat, used a computer in a communal bungalow and checked the time before walking back across the unlit grounds at about 5 am. Looking upwards between trees, he noticed a formation travelling on a straight course from the direction of Conca towards Sardinia. He estimated that it remained visible for approximately ten minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The central feature was described as an enormous black circle against a clear sky. Its rim appeared lighter, while between four and eight points of light formed another circle near its centre. The witness could not decide whether the dark form was a flat disc or something more spherical. In front of it were two rows of several smaller dark groups, apparently followed by further groups behind. He thought some components might have been triangular but repeatedly expressed uncertainty about that detail.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Everything reportedly moved at the same slow, constant speed. There was no engine noise, sudden acceleration, turn, hovering phase, landing or physical effect. The objects simply continued away until terrain and distance prevented further observation. This matters because the account contains no obvious sign of controlled manoeuvring beyond an orderly-looking formation and straight movement. GEIPAN itself noted that the reported motion showed no clearly intentional behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The witness’s first interpretation was military rather than extraterrestrial. Solenzara Air Base lies north of the area, and he associated the sighting with military activity linked in his mind to events in Syria and Iraq. He returned to bed afterwards and reported no fear, illness or environmental disturbance. Later that day, after speaking to colleagues and friends, he attended the local gendarmerie because he felt the observation deserved to be recorded despite fearing ridicule.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

### The unconfirmed second observer

The most tantalising part of the account is also one of its weakest. The witness said that he encountered another man near the campsite’s staff entrance and asked whether he was hallucinating. According to the witness, the man replied that he had seen it too, then continued on his way. The witness did not know his name, and neither the gendarmerie nor the GEIPAN investigator located him.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This cannot be treated as independent corroboration. Every detail about the exchange comes through the principal witness, and the unknown man provided no description, drawing or statement of his own. GEIPAN consequently recorded the case as having no confirmed additional witness. The alleged encounter may support the witness’s sincerity, but it cannot demonstrate that two people independently perceived the same shapes in the same way.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Which conventional explanations investigators tested

GEIPAN did more than record the narrative. An investigator was assigned on 5 September, roughly a week after the event, and later interviewed the witness, inspected the location and produced a questionnaire and reconstruction. Weather, astronomical conditions, satellites, atmospheric re-entry, civil aviation, military activity and local lighting were considered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**Aircraft and military traffic.** Figari Airport was reportedly closed to normal traffic between midnight and 6.30 am. Investigators were unable to obtain a direct explanation from officials at Solenzara Air Base, although they were told that fighter traffic in the wider region was heavy at the time. The report nevertheless states that military radar showed no aerial activity in the relevant zone and time period. GEIPAN regarded ordinary aircraft as a poor fit because of the silence, unusual appearance and absent radar trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

That finding is useful but not absolute. The published report does not reproduce primary radar logs, specify the radar coverage involved or explain whether small, low or non-transponding objects would necessarily have been detected. “No radar activity reported” therefore weakens a conventional aircraft explanation without proving that the sky was physically empty.

**Satellites and atmospheric debris.** Investigators checked for notable satellite passages and considered a re-entering spacecraft because a slow, extended formation can sometimes be produced by fragmenting debris. A Feng Yun satellite re-entry occurred that day, but GEIPAN found that its time and direction did not match the observation. Meteor activity and satellite re-entry were rated as very weak explanations because the witness described persistent dark masses, not a brief succession of luminous fragments.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

**Clouds.** The weather check found generally clear conditions, light winds and only limited cloud cover. A lenticular or otherwise unusually shaped cloud was considered because a dark mass can stand out against a brightening pre-dawn sky. ## Where the evidence is strongest

The best evidence for Zonza is not physical evidence but the speed and formality of the reporting. The witness gave a gendarmerie statement at 6.40 pm on the same day, about fourteen hours after the event. That reduces the risk of a story being substantially reshaped over years of repetition. His initial statement already contained the principal elements later recorded by GEIPAN: ordered lines of objects, a larger circle with lights, a course towards Sardinia and complete silence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B01426%20%282014311439%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The later interview added detail but did not radically transform the basic narrative. The witness remained uncertain where uncertainty was reasonable: he could not confirm the triangular shapes, count every component, determine whether the circle was flat or spherical, or estimate its true dimensions. Such admissions are generally more credible than artificially exact claims made under poor viewing conditions.

A field investigation also established that the described viewing geometry was possible. The campsite, trees and direction of travel were not merely reconstructed from memory at a distance; the investigator visited the area and recorded the witness’s position and sightline. GEIPAN assessed the testimony’s consistency at about 0.7 on its internal scale and the residual strangeness at about 0.6.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

These strengths justify treating the report as a serious unresolved observation. They support the conclusion that the witness probably experienced something unusual and described it in good faith. They do not establish what external stimulus produced the experience.

## The gaps that prevent a stronger conclusion

The largest problem is the complete absence of imagery. The witness considered going to fetch a camera but feared that the formation would disappear while he was away. No photograph or video was therefore taken despite an estimated ten-minute duration. Without images, investigators could not examine outlines, light spacing, motion frame by frame or whether the “large circle” was a coherent object rather than a perceptual grouping of separate features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B01426%20%282014311439%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Distance and scale are equally important omissions. The witness could not estimate either. A small nearby object and a much larger distant object can occupy the same angular area in the sky; speed and sound also depend heavily on range. Consequently, descriptions such as “enormous” express the witness’s impression but provide no defensible physical measurement. The investigation table explicitly records apparent size and distance as impossible to determine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The ten-minute duration is also an estimate made without a timed recording. Although the witness had recently seen the hour on a computer, the file provides no independently fixed start or finish time. A moderate timing error could affect comparisons with satellite, aviation or astronomical data.

There is a further inconsistency concerning the sky. The witness later said that the Moon illuminated the campsite well, while the investigation’s astronomical check stated that the Moon was not visible. Independent lunar records show that 28 August 2014 was only two days after new moon, with a thin waxing crescent rather than a bright night-time Moon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This discrepancy does not invalidate the entire account. The witness may simply have mistaken pre-dawn sky brightness or general visibility for moonlight. It does, however, demonstrate why even an apparently calm and sincere observer’s environmental recollections cannot automatically be treated as precise measurements.

The investigation also did not secure the alleged second witness, photographs from other campers, civilian radar material or direct confirmation from the military base. Local gendarmeries had received no comparable reports during August. For a phenomenon described as extensive, slow and visible for ten minutes, the absence of other traceable observers is a significant limitation, even allowing for the early hour and sparse population.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-07/ZONZA%20%2820%29%2028.08.2014%20%5BE-Cgei%20CR%20T%2CC%2CP%2CD%20S%5D%20compte%20rendu%20enquete%20v2_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">ZONZA (20) 28.08.2014 [E Cgei CR T,C,P,D S] compte rendu enquete v2 0</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Why unexplained does not mean extraterrestrial


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

GEIPAN currently lists Zonza as category D and describes it more specifically in the published conclusion as D1: unexplained, reasonably consistent and of medium-to-high strangeness. Its public guidance defines D1 cases as strange reports of moderate consistency, often involving a single witness without photographic or video recording. That description fits Zonza closely.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANZONZA (2A) 28.08.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

</div>

“Unexplained” is a statement about the limits of the available investigation. It means that the tested hypotheses did not adequately account for the testimony. It does not mean that every possible conventional cause was eliminated, still less that an exotic origin became the default answer.

Several broad possibilities remain open. The witness may have observed an unusual combination of distant aircraft, balloons, birds, thin cloud and celestial lights whose separate elements appeared to form a single organised system. He may have misjudged the duration, spacing or darkness of the shapes under pre-dawn contrast conditions. A less familiar military or civilian activity may have gone unconfirmed. There may also have been an external phenomenon that cannot now be reconstructed because the decisive measurements were never collected. None of these possibilities is demonstrated by the file.

GEIPAN’s own classification method reflects this distinction. It evaluates both residual strangeness and consistency, with stronger extraordinary-looking claims requiring stronger supporting information. A unique testimony without imagery can qualify as D1 when no satisfactory explanation is found, but it lacks the independent evidence needed for a more robust conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/25" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN &#124; GEIPANFebruary 26, 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D1/D2) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;év...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: February 26, 2021</span></span></span>

## What later reporting has changed

The core evidential position has not materially improved since the 2014 investigation. GEIPAN’s case page was updated in June 2026 but continues to list the event as unexplained. No authenticated photograph, named second observer or newly disclosed instrument record appears in the official file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08968" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANZONZA (2A) 28.08.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Later online retellings have spread the striking image of a giant dark circle escorted by formations, but they generally depend on the same gendarmerie statement and GEIPAN report rather than new evidence. Repetition therefore increases the story’s visibility, not its evidential weight. One later specialist discussion raised high-altitude balloons as a possible avenue, but no published reconstruction has demonstrated a balloon configuration matching the witness’s full description, timing and direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-zonza-2014-zonza-20-28-08-2014-e-cgei-cr-tcpd-s-compte-rendu-enquete-v2-0" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ufotransparency.com.</span></span></span>

The most defensible judgement is consequently narrow. Zonza remains an authentic unresolved entry in Corse-du-Sud’s official UFO history: promptly reported, investigated on site and not comfortably reduced to the standard explanations tested. Yet it remains evidentially fragile because the event survives only through one person’s account. Its interest lies in that tension—not in proof of an unknown vehicle, but in a reasonably detailed observation for which the evidence needed to settle the question was never obtained.
