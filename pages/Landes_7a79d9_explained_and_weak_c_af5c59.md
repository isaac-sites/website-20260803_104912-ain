## Introduction

Most UFO reports in [Landes]({{ 'what-landes-ufo-records-actually-reveal/' | relative_url }}) did not become lasting mysteries. They were either linked to familiar objects and lighting effects—balloons, lanterns, satellites and entertainment spotlights—or left unresolved because the original account contained too little dependable information. That distinction matters. A report can sound highly unusual without providing enough evidence to show that anything genuinely anomalous occurred.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Landes_7a79d9_explained_and_weak_c_af5c59-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Explained Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The official GEIPAN archive, maintained by the French space agency CNES, therefore tells a more instructive story than a simple catalogue of “explained” and “unexplained” sightings. Cases at Biscarrosse and between Bénesse-Maremne and Angresse show how apparently erratic or silent objects can be reconstructed from weather, movement and local conditions. Reports from Bourriot-Bergonce and Dax show the opposite problem: investigators could imagine several ordinary possibilities, but missing dates, delayed testimony or unanswered questions prevented a reliable conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-11-50853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBOURRIOT-BERGONCE (40) 10.11.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Familiar objects could look surprisingly strange

The Landes cases demonstrate that witnesses do not need to be careless or dishonest to misidentify an ordinary object. Distance removes useful detail, darkness conceals surroundings, and a light without a visible source can appear to move in ways that seem impossible. GEIPAN consequently evaluates both the residual strangeness of a report and its “consistency”: the quantity, reliability and objectivity of the information available after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">* La sous classification des cas D en D1/D2, en fonction de critères attribuant plus ou moins d’étrangeté résiduelle et de consistance au...</span></span></span>

A useful example occurred at Biscarrosse on 7 August 2021. A witness looking through a roof window watched a pale light make rapid, irregular movements for about an hour and a half. The description suggested a compact object darting around within a limited patch of sky. Yet the duration, the repeated changes of direction, the low cloud and the summer tourist setting pointed towards a sky-facing searchlight whose beam was sweeping across the cloud base.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-08-51221?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBISCARROSSE (40) 07.08.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also explained why the witness’s video did not settle the matter. A nearby illuminated white structure affected the camera’s exposure and focus, making the faint distant light difficult or impossible to record even though it remained visible to the eye. Weather observations showed cloud layers low enough to reflect a powerful beam, while several night-time venues operated in the wider area. Investigators could not identify the exact projector, so the case received category B—probably explained—rather than the more certain category A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-08-51221?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C33&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBISCARROSSE (40) 07.08.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This case is important because the strange movement was not evidence of extraordinary performance. It was part of the explanation. A beam crossing an uneven cloud layer can appear to stop, jump, reverse or accelerate, particularly when neither the beam nor its ground source is visible.

### A metallic sphere over Lake Cazaux

A more recent Biscarrosse report reached a firmer conclusion. On 20 August 2024, a holidaymaker at the lakeside beach of Maguide saw a metallic, apparently spherical object moving steadily above the opposite shore towards Sanguinet. It remained visible for roughly 20 seconds. A check with the French air-defence planning and operations centre found no aircraft movement matching the reported path.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-08-51577" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBISCARROSSE (40) 20.08.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The non-aircraft result did not make the object anomalous. Its round form, reflective surface, horizontal movement and direction of travel were all consistent with a Mylar party balloon being carried by the prevailing wind. The summer beach setting also supplied a plausible source. GEIPAN classified the report A, meaning that investigators considered the object identified without meaningful ambiguity.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-08-51577" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBISCARROSSE (40) 20.08.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is a useful warning against treating the elimination of one hypothesis as proof of an exotic alternative. Showing that an object was probably not an aircraft only narrowed the field. Shape, wind and setting provided a better explanation.

### Orange lights, lanterns and slow drift

An orange “ball” seen by a driver travelling from Bénesse-Maremne towards Angresse on 10 October 2011 produced another probable identification. The light appeared to move upwards and downwards, made no noise and was photographed by the witness. Investigators found that weak, variable winds were compatible with the movement of a flying lantern. After reviewing examples of such lanterns, the witness also accepted that explanation as plausible. GEIPAN classified the case B because the identification was persuasive but not directly proven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-10-02841?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=88&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D465] DE BENESSE (40) VERS ANGRESSE (40)10.10.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The observation took place early in the morning, an unusual time for a celebratory lantern. That reduced confidence but did not outweigh the object’s colour, silence and wind-compatible motion. The case illustrates how investigations weigh several imperfect clues rather than demanding that every detail fit perfectly.

Other Landes entries follow the same pattern. A Soustons report from December 2019 was attributed probably to a group of satellites, while another Soustons case and the 2021 Biscarrosse sighting were associated with sky-facing light displays. Such explanations are especially relevant when witnesses describe multiple lights moving together, repeated sweeps across one area, or steady points whose apparent spacing remains fixed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=141%2C21&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPANPENNES-MIRABEAU (LES) (13) 02.08.2016 &#124; 02/08/2016 &#124; C &#124; Bouches-du-Rhône &#124; Manque d&#x27;informations fiables...</span></span></span>

## Category C does not mean a deeper mystery

The most easily misunderstood GEIPAN label is category C. It does not mean that investigators found persuasive evidence for an extraordinary object. It means that the report could not be analysed reliably because essential information was missing or insufficiently dependable. GEIPAN distinguishes this from category D, in which a case remains unexplained despite having evidence substantial enough to test.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Nationally, category C is a major part of the archive rather than a rare exception. GEIPAN’s published statistics listed more than 1,000 cases classified as unidentified because of missing data, and its methodological material has historically placed roughly one-third of reports in the unusable or insufficient-information group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Several weaknesses commonly push a Landes report towards category C:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the report was made years after the observation;
* the exact date or time could not be established;
* only one witness supplied information;
* photographs showed the surroundings rather than the phenomenon;
* the object’s direction, elevation or duration was poorly defined;
* witnesses did not answer follow-up questions;
* no independent astronomical, meteorological or aviation record could be matched confidently.

</div>

These limitations do not show that the witness invented the event. They show that sincerity alone cannot reconstruct what was in the sky.

## What Bourriot-Bergonce reveals about delayed testimony

The Bourriot-Bergonce case concerned an observation said to have occurred on 10 November 1994 but reported in 2010, approximately 16 years later. The witness recalled being a child in the back of a car on a country road. Through the tops of trees, the witness saw an intense green light and a nearby luminous point that seemed to accelerate, change direction abruptly and disappear northwards. No other witness account was obtained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-11-50853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBOURRIOT-BERGONCE (40) 10.11.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The colour and speed initially suggested a meteor, bright fireball or atmospheric re-entry. The apparent change of direction was harder to reconcile with a single object, although two separate meteors occurring close together could have created that impression. GEIPAN did not promote this possibility to a probable explanation because there was no factual material with which to verify it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-11-50853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBOURRIOT-BERGONCE (40) 10.11.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The long delay was decisive. Investigators could not reconstruct precise weather, viewing geometry or the witness’s exact position with sufficient confidence, and memory may compress two events into one continuous sequence. GEIPAN concluded that a retrospective field investigation would not produce useful new evidence and classified the case C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-11-50853" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBOURRIOT-BERGONCE (40) 10.11.1994 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Bourriot-Bergonce therefore remains unidentified in a literal sense, but it is not evidentially comparable with the better-supported Cazères-sur-l’Adour category D case. Its dramatic acceleration and turn are claims embedded in a solitary childhood memory reported many years later, not movements confirmed by instruments or independent observers.

## Why the Dax report could not be settled

The Dax case of December 2018 demonstrates how a recent report can still become unusable. At about 1.30 am, a witness looking from a window reported three silent phenomena moving in sequence: two appeared white and oval, while another seemed black and triangular. They passed out of sight behind a neighbouring building after between 30 seconds and one minute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDAX (40) 11.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several ordinary explanations were considered but none could be established. A line of flying lanterns matched the sequential arrangement, yet their reported shapes and movement relative to the wind did not fit well. Reflections from electrical devices were possible because the observation began through glass, although the witness said the window was then opened. The bright “evening star” mentioned in the account was probably Sirius, but the witness’s suggestion that it reflected from the dark triangle could not be interpreted confidently.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDAX (40) 11.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

More serious problems concerned the basic record. The account left uncertainty over whether the sighting occurred during the night of 10–11 December or 11–12 December. The witness gave inconsistent information about when environmental photographs were taken and did not answer several follow-up questions. There was no image of the phenomenon and no second testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDAX (40) 11.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN noted that the shapes remained fairly strange, but the evidence had low consistency. It therefore classified the case C: not explained because reliable information was lacking. The wording is crucial. Investigators were not stating that three structured craft had passed over Dax. They were stating that one person had reported an unusual experience that could no longer be tested adequately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDAX (40) 11.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The report also shows how later comparisons can make a story seem stronger without adding evidence. The witness linked the sighting to a separate report by airline pilots over Ireland. GEIPAN found little similarity between the events and considered the Irish observation more suggestive of a bright meteor. The comparison did not corroborate the Dax account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-12-50688" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDAX (40) 11.12.2018 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Explained and weak cases are not failures

Cases involving balloons, lanterns or projected lights can appear disappointing only if the purpose of investigation is assumed to be finding extraordinary craft. GEIPAN’s actual role is to determine which known phenomena best fit the available observations and to state clearly when the evidence cannot support a conclusion. Its classifications are therefore outcomes of evidence assessment, not a scale running from “ordinary” to “alien”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Landes archive benefits from preserving both kinds of case. Explained reports reveal the circumstances that repeatedly create UFO impressions: reflective surfaces, low cloud, slow wind-driven movement, distant lights and missing visual references. Category C reports reveal where investigation breaks down: uncertainty over dates, single witnesses, long reporting delays and incomplete follow-up.

Together, they place the department’s rarer unresolved cases in perspective. A report should not be considered strong merely because no final explanation was reached. The most meaningful question is whether the available evidence was good enough to challenge ordinary explanations. At Bourriot-Bergonce and Dax, it was not. At Biscarrosse and between Bénesse-Maremne and Angresse, enough contextual detail survived to make ordinary objects the more convincing answer.
