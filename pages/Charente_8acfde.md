## Introduction

Two Charente reports remain officially unidentified after investigation: a silent brown oval seen over Angoulême in 2009 and a repeatedly appearing light observed at Ruffec in 2022. Neither produced photographs, physical traces, radar confirmation or supporting witnesses. Other intriguing reports, including high-speed radar echoes at Cognac air base in 1957, are classified as cases with insufficient reliable information rather than genuine unresolved anomalies.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the official record contains

GEIPAN was created by the French space agency CNES in 1977. It collects reports from witnesses and public authorities, checks possible astronomical, meteorological, aeronautical and human explanations, preserves case documents and publishes classifications. It does not regard an unidentified report as proof of an extraterrestrial vehicle. Its stated role is to investigate an observation, not to validate the interpretation attached to it by a witness or the media.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[cnes.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

## The 1957 Cognac radar echoes

Charente’s most striking aviation-related report occurred at Cognac air base on 17 December 1957. Four military personnel reportedly saw an unidentified echo appear on a radar display at 15:01 Universal Time. According to the later GEIPAN summary, the return travelled from a range of about 40 kilometres to 16 kilometres before disappearing among fixed echoes. Its roughly 30-second track implied a calculated speed of about 2,880 kilometres per hour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1957-12-00041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO CTL] COGNAC (16) 17.12.1957 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A second return appeared at 15:45, beginning at about 37 kilometres and disappearing at 25 kilometres after approximately 15 seconds. It produced the same estimated speed. Two witnesses prepared an illustrated report, giving the incident more documentary substance than a story reconstructed solely from memory decades later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1957-12-00041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO CTL] COGNAC (16) 17.12.1957 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Those figures make the incident sound dramatic, but the evidential weaknesses are decisive. There was no corresponding visual sighting, no surviving multi-radar confirmation mentioned in the public summary and too little technical information to exclude equipment effects, propagation anomalies, interference or an incorrectly interpreted conventional target. GEIPAN therefore classifies it C, explicitly citing insufficient information rather than treating the returns as an unexplained aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1957-12-00041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO CTL] COGNAC (16) 17.12.1957 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The location nevertheless gives the story enduring interest. Cognac-Châteaubernard is a major military aviation site associated with flight training and, in its modern role, remotely piloted aircraft operations. That makes careful checking of aircraft movements and radar conditions especially relevant to reports from the surrounding area. It does not, however, turn every unexplained light or return near Cognac into a military encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gouv.fr/air/actualites/lecole-pilotes-forces-aeriennes-suisses-sentraine-base-aerienne-709-cognac" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gouv.fr">[Ministère des Armées]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministère des ArméesL&#x27;école des pilotes des Forces aériennes suisses s&#x27;...L&#x27;école des pilotes des Forces aériennes suisses s&#x27;entraîne su...</span></span></span>

The 1957 case illustrates an important distinction. Instrumental evidence may appear more objective than eyewitness testimony, but a radar return is not self-explanatory. Without the equipment settings, weather conditions, calibration history, operator records and independent sensor data, a speed calculated from a plotted echo can be precise without being conclusive.

## Angoulême’s silent oval

At about 13:15 on 15 August 2009, a witness standing in a garden in Angoulême reported seeing a matt-brown, oval-shaped object travel in a straight line from east to west, towards Cognac. The object appeared to pass above nearby rooftops and made no audible sound. No second witness was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN considered several ordinary possibilities. The witness rejected an aircraft or helicopter, while investigators also examined nearby objects or activities such as a rugby ball, clay-pigeon shooting, a flying disc and model aircraft. A meteor-related explanation was considered because the observation occurred close to the annual Perseid meteor shower. None could be matched confidently to the account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case’s unresolved status rests partly on a procedural failure. The gendarmerie report reached GEIPAN too late for investigators to verify local air traffic with air-traffic control. That missing check is central: the official record does not show that aircraft were positively excluded, only that the witness did not believe the object was one and that investigators could no longer test the possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A meteor is not a particularly comfortable fit either. Meteors are normally brief luminous events, whereas the witness described a brown, apparently solid oval in daylight. Yet judgments about colour, altitude, shape and silence are vulnerable when an isolated object is viewed against the sky without a known scale. An object thought to be large and distant may instead be smaller and closer; apparent silence may simply reflect distance, wind or short exposure.

GEIPAN assigned the report a relatively high strangeness score and retained it as category D. The result is defensible in the narrow sense that no tested explanation was consolidated. Nevertheless, the case remains a single-witness observation without imagery, physical effects or independent sensor support. Its classification preserves a question; it does not transform the description into verified performance data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Ruffec’s repeating light

The Ruffec observation is Charente’s most recent officially unresolved case. At around 21:00 on 13 September 2022, a witness in a garden saw a white point of light towards the west. It appeared to travel through an arc, disappear and then repeat a similar movement in the same region of sky. Each cycle lasted roughly six seconds. After shifting slightly downwards and towards the west-south-west, the light disappeared permanently after about five minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Unlike many brief light reports, Ruffec received a comparatively detailed reconstruction. GEIPAN examined four principal possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* aircraft moving through the witness’s field of view;
* sunlight reflecting from Starlink satellites;
* a ground-based searchlight or entertainment projector;
* reflections involving ice crystals in the atmosphere.

</div>

The last two were considered poor fits. Investigators could not confirm the cloud layer that a projected light would normally illuminate, while the reported upper-level wind was considered too strong for a stable ice-crystal formation to remain in roughly the same place.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Aircraft were a more plausible starting point. The witness compared the appearance to an aircraft light, and investigators reconstructed numerous flights passing through the indicated area. However, the reported number and rhythm of appearances seemed greater than the actual traffic. GEIPAN therefore judged that several aircraft had probably not been mistaken for one object making repeated rotations, although it could not rule out every possible perceptual combination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Starlink hypothesis was also tested rather than dismissed casually. Satellites can brighten sharply when their surfaces reflect sunlight, producing lights that appear and vanish in sequence. In this instance, however, the geometry did not fit well. The Sun was about 29 degrees below the horizon, close to a limiting condition for such flares, but the expected direction was north-west. The witness was looking west, and the reported elevation of 45 to 60 degrees was much higher than the few degrees GEIPAN considered compatible with the proposed satellite effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN consequently retained the case as D, with the repeated, cyclic behaviour accounting for much of its residual strangeness. Even so, the evidence has firm limits: there was one witness, no photograph or video and no direct instrumental record of the light. The investigation shows that several routine explanations were tested and found inadequate, not that all conceivable ordinary explanations were eliminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Reports weakened by missing information

Most of Charente’s published cases are less resistant to explanation than the Angoulême and Ruffec reports. They remain uncertain largely because the original information is inadequate.

On 5 January 1977, two people travelling by car near Villebois-Lavalette watched a red-orange luminous phenomenon moving from south-east to north-west. They described sparks, no obvious sound and a gradual fading over roughly ten minutes. Yet other road users had reportedly seen sparks coming from an electricity line aligned in the same direction. The available evidence was insufficient to determine how the electrical activity related to the perceived light, so the case was classified C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-01-00378" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The incident is valuable because it shows how an initially exotic-looking account can contain the outline of a local explanation. An electrical fault could produce light, sparks, changing intensity and a misleading sense of movement, particularly when seen from a moving vehicle. Because the surviving record does not allow the geometry or timing to be reconstructed, that interpretation remains plausible rather than proven.

Another Angoulême report, from 11 December 2009, concerned a white light that rose and then appeared stationary for about ten minutes. The witness took a photograph, but GEIPAN found it unusable, and no independent witness supported the account. It was classified C for lack of detail. The mere existence of a photograph therefore added little: an image without usable resolution, exposure information, reference points or an identifiable subject cannot establish distance, movement or physical form.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-12-02472" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The departmental listing contains additional C cases, including reports from Jurignac, La Couronne and Cognac. Their presence should not be read as a concentration of proven anomalies. Category C commonly marks the point where investigation cannot proceed because testimony, timing, imagery or corroboration is too weak.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B1%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=9%2C32&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Was there a Charente UFO flap?

France experienced an extraordinary nationwide wave of “flying saucer” reports in 1954. Specialist catalogues include several claims associated with Charente, including reports around Ronsenac and Angoulême. Such catalogues are useful for locating possible newspaper references, but many entries are built from brief press notices, later compilations or accounts lacking original witness files. One catalogued Angoulême light from October 1954, for example, has been assessed by the compiler as probably a meteor rather than an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/3oct1954ronsenac.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[ufologie.patrickgross.org]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on patrickgross.org.</span></span></span>

The surviving evidence does not justify describing Charente as a major centre of the 1954 wave. It appears to have participated in the same national climate of intensive reporting, press attention and public expectation, but the department lacks a well-documented 1954 landing claim, physical-trace investigation or multi-sensor event in the accessible official record.

That context still matters. During a highly publicised wave, witnesses are more likely to report ambiguous lights, and newspapers are more likely to publish them under the language of flying saucers. This does not imply dishonesty. It means that social expectation can influence which events are noticed, remembered and given a particular label.

Charente’s clearer official chronology begins with the 1957 Cognac radar report, followed by scattered cases rather than a sustained sequence. The available record therefore looks less like a recurring departmental flap than a series of isolated observations separated by years or decades.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1957-12-00041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO CTL] COGNAC (16) 17.12.1957 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The role of gendarmes, aviation data and delayed reporting

France’s reporting structure gives local gendarmerie records unusual importance. A person who reports an unidentified phenomenon to the gendarmerie may be interviewed in detail, after which the material can be passed to GEIPAN. The purpose is not to pursue extraterrestrials but to preserve observations in a form that can be compared with aircraft movements, weather, astronomical objects and other witness reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie Nationale]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">comment la gendarmerie prend elle en compte les etrangetes dans le ciel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">comment la gendarmerie prend elle en compte les etrangetes dans le ciel</span></span></span>

Charente demonstrates both the value and the weakness of that system. The Angoulême oval entered the record through a gendarmerie statement, but the delay prevented air-traffic verification. The 1957 Cognac case retained an illustrated military report, yet still lacked enough technical material for a firm radar analysis. In Ruffec, a later investigation could reconstruct air traffic and satellite geometry, producing a much more transparent account of why candidate explanations were accepted or rejected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Timing is often more important than witness sincerity. Air-traffic records may be unavailable after long delays; temporary atmospheric conditions cannot be measured retrospectively; nearby events may go unrecognised; and witnesses’ estimates can become less precise. Prompt reporting does not guarantee an identification, but it preserves more routes to one.

Cognac’s military aviation activity also creates a recurring interpretive problem. Aircraft, training operations and remotely piloted systems enlarge the range of conventional aerial objects that may be seen locally. At the same time, the presence of trained personnel and radar equipment can make a report seem unusually authoritative. Each claim still has to be judged on its actual documentation rather than the reputation of the location or witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.defense.gouv.fr/air/actualites/lecole-pilotes-forces-aeriennes-suisses-sentraine-base-aerienne-709-cognac" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: defense.gouv.fr">[Ministère des Armées]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">defense.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ministère des ArméesL&#x27;école des pilotes des Forces aériennes suisses s&#x27;...L&#x27;école des pilotes des Forces aériennes suisses s&#x27;entraîne su...</span></span></span>

## What usually produces uncertainty

Charente’s cases point repeatedly towards a limited set of difficulties rather than one recurring exotic phenomenon.

**No reliable estimate of distance.** A light in an empty sky provides few scale cues. Witnesses may describe an object as large, fast or low without any measured range.

**Single-witness reporting.** Both surviving D cases depend on one observer. A sincere account can be detailed yet remain impossible to verify independently.

**Weak or absent imagery.** Ruffec and the 2009 Angoulême oval produced no photograph. The other Angoulême light was photographed, but the image was unusable. Evidence quality depends on what an image records, not merely on whether a camera was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Aircraft and satellites.** Charente includes civilian air routes, local aerodromes and military flying around Cognac. Modern satellite constellations add lights that can appear, brighten or disappear in unfamiliar patterns. Ruffec is notable precisely because investigators tested both air traffic and satellite reflections against the reported timing and direction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Astronomical and atmospheric events.** Meteors, bright planets, cloud effects and unusual reflections can all be perceived as structured or moving objects. GEIPAN considered a meteor in the Angoulême oval case but could not reconcile it securely with the testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Information loss.** Several Charente files were not resolved because the necessary data were missing rather than because ordinary causes had been exhaustively excluded. That difference separates a weak case from a genuinely difficult one.

## How strong are Charente’s unresolved cases?

The 2022 Ruffec case is the department’s most carefully tested unresolved report. Investigators reconstructed air traffic, considered satellite illumination and examined atmospheric and projected-light possibilities. Its repetitive behaviour remained unexplained within the available evidence. Its principal weakness is the complete lack of independent recording or corroboration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/61265?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=0&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRUFFEC (16) 13.09.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The 2009 Angoulême oval is visually more suggestive of a discrete object, but its investigation was weakened by late transmission of the gendarmerie statement. Without air-traffic data, imagery or another witness, its D classification is more dependent on the original description and the failure to find a convincing match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-08-02385" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANGOULEME (16) 15.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The 1957 Cognac radar echoes offer an instrument-based observation and multiple military personnel, yet they are not officially unresolved in the same sense. The lack of visual confirmation and technical radar data leaves the report in category C. Its calculated speed is interesting, but the public record does not establish that a physical object actually travelled along the inferred path.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1957-12-00041" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO CTL] COGNAC (16) 17.12.1957 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The remaining cases are weaker. The Villebois-Lavalette report has a possible association with sparking power lines, while the December 2009 Angoulême light lacks usable photographic or corroborating evidence. These reports are part of Charente’s UFO history, but they should not be presented as equivalent to investigated category D cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1977-01-00378" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## A balanced reading of the department’s record

Charente has produced a handful of genuinely interesting reports, especially the Cognac radar returns, the silent Angoulême oval and Ruffec’s cycling light. None has developed into a landmark case supported by converging visual, photographic, radar and physical evidence.

Later investigation has produced mixed results. It strengthened the Ruffec story by showing that obvious explanations were actively compared with the witness’s direction, timing and elevation. It weakened broader claims about the Angoulême oval by revealing that aircraft could not be checked after the delayed report. It placed the Cognac radar incident in a more cautious category because the surviving technical evidence was inadequate.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore limited. Charente contains two officially unidentified observations and several incomplete cases, but no public evidence confirming an extraordinary vehicle. “Unidentified” is accurate where GEIPAN could not secure an explanation. It should not be expanded into “unexplainable”, still less into proof of a non-human origin.

What makes the department useful within French UFO history is precisely this modest record. It shows how investigations move between testimony, military or civilian aviation, satellite geometry, weather and local infrastructure—and how a small missing piece of information can preserve a mystery for decades.
