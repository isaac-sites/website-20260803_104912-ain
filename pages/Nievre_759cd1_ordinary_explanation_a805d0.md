## Introduction

Most modern UFO reports in Nièvre that investigators could resolve were not caused by rare aircraft or unknown technology. They arose from familiar things seen under unfamiliar conditions: the International Space Station, bright planets, satellite reflections, drifting balloons and sky lanterns. These objects can look extraordinary because a dark sky removes the visual clues people normally use to judge distance, speed and size.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Nievre_759cd1_ordinary_explanation_a805d0-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Solved Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The official record is especially useful because several reports came with enough detail—time, direction, duration, photographs or weather conditions—to test ordinary explanations. The resulting identifications do not suggest that witnesses invented what they saw. In most cases, they accurately noticed a striking light but understandably misread its scale or movement. Nièvre’s solved sightings therefore show how investigation can turn a compelling personal experience into a testable astronomical or atmospheric event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=55&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## The International Space Station over Chaulgnes

On 25 June 2010, a witness at Chaulgnes reported a luminous spherical object crossing the night sky at about 23:40. GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena, identified it as a passage of the International Space Station and classified the case A, its category for a securely explained report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54607?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=%2C103&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHAULGNES (58) 25.06.2010Observation du déplacement d&#x27;un objet sphérique lumineux: passage de l&#x27; ISS. Description. Le 25 juin 2010...</span></span></span>

The ISS is unusually effective at creating UFO reports because it does not resemble an aircraft when seen with the naked eye. It normally appears as a bright, steady point, without the red, green or flashing navigation lights expected from a plane. It moves smoothly across a large part of the sky and may seem to appear or disappear abruptly when it enters or leaves the Earth’s shadow. GEIPAN notes that visible satellites commonly travel in a straight line for several minutes and can rival a bright star or, in the ISS’s case, Venus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

Those characteristics explain why the Chaulgnes sighting could feel more unusual than its eventual identification suggests. Calling the light “spherical” did not establish that a visible globe or structured craft was present. At great distance, any unresolved point source is perceived as a small disc or point of light. Without a known size, the observer also had no dependable way to judge its altitude or speed.

A second Nièvre case reinforces the identification pattern. GEIPAN lists a 24 May 2017 observation at Gien-sur-Cure as another category-A ISS case. Two securely identified station sightings in the same department show that the Chaulgnes report was not an isolated or improvised explanation: the ISS repeatedly produced the type of bright, silent and regular movement that witnesses associated with an unknown object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=55&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The decisive evidence in such cases is not simply that a satellite might have been visible. Investigators can compare the reported time and viewing direction with calculated orbital passages. Satellite paths are predictable, so agreement between the observed route and the computed pass can support a much stronger conclusion than resemblance alone. GEIPAN recommends recording the moment when a light passes a known star because precise timing and position allow this kind of check.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

## Two “objects” that were Venus and Jupiter

The Saint-Léger-des-Vignes case of 30 June 2015 shows that apparent movement is not always real movement. Between 23:30 and midnight, a witness watched two yellow-orange lights positioned one above the other. They seemed to move very slowly, remained visible for a long period and were photographed. GEIPAN reconstructed the sky and found Venus and Jupiter close together above the west-north-western horizon. The two lights sank towards the horizon exactly as the planets would, leading to a category-A identification as a planetary conjunction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-06-09214" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-LEGER-DES-VIGNES (58) 30.06.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The photographs did not strengthen an extraordinary interpretation. Instead, they gave investigators additional material to compare with sky charts. GEIPAN’s reconstructions for 23:33 and 23:55 showed the planets in the reported arrangement and approaching the horizon. This is an important difference between documentation and proof: an image may confirm that a light was present without showing that it was nearby, solid or behaving anomalously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-06-09214" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-LEGER-DES-VIGNES (58) 30.06.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Planets become convincing UFOs for several connected reasons. Venus can be much brighter than surrounding stars, while Jupiter is also conspicuous. Near the horizon, atmospheric disturbance can make their light flicker, change colour or appear less stable. With few surrounding reference points, a stationary light may also seem to drift. GEIPAN describes this illusion as autokinesis: small involuntary eye movements can make a fixed point appear to move against a dark, featureless background.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

The Saint-Léger-des-Vignes report was therefore not solved merely because “there were planets in the sky”. The explanation matched the number of lights, their relative positions, their slow apparent movement, their colour, the observation period and their disappearance near the horizon. That cumulative agreement is why the case received a secure rather than merely probable identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-06-09214" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-LEGER-DES-VIGNES (58) 30.06.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Balloons that seemed to change speed and direction

In daylight, ordinary airborne objects create a different problem. At Narcy on 23 August 2016, a witness and a roofer saw a very bright object moving through a clear morning sky. It appeared to approach them, changed direction, made no sound and eventually disappeared towards the horizon. The change of course was the feature that made the report seem most unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2016-08-09529" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNARCY (58) 23.08.2016 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Yet the witness also described movement resembling a balloon carried irregularly by the wind. Weather records showed changing winds around the time of the observation, making a wind-borne object a plausible explanation. GEIPAN concluded that it was probably a round object, particularly a child’s balloon, and classified the report B: likely explained, but with less certainty than a category-A case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2016-08-09529" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNARCY (58) 23.08.2016 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case demonstrates how distance uncertainty changes the apparent behaviour of an object. The observer did not know whether the bright object was a small balloon hundreds of metres away or something much larger and farther off. Without that information, perceived speed could not be calculated reliably. A lightweight object moving across changing air currents could also turn, rise or slow without following the steady course expected from an aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2016-08-09529" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNARCY (58) 23.08.2016 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A reflective balloon may look particularly strange because sunlight reaches it unevenly as it rotates. It can brighten suddenly, fade, appear silvery or white and briefly lose any recognisable outline. The lack of engine noise then seems significant, even though silence is exactly what a drifting balloon would produce. Narcy remained only a probable identification because the object was not recovered or photographed clearly, but the observed motion, weather and witness description all favoured an ordinary airborne object.

## Sky lanterns over Guérigny and Cosne-Cours-sur-Loire

GEIPAN attributes two other modern Nièvre reports to sky lanterns: Guérigny on 20 July 2013 and Cosne-Cours-sur-Loire on 5 July 2014. Both are category-B cases, meaning that the explanation was considered probable rather than demonstrated beyond reasonable doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=55&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Sky lanterns are small paper balloons lifted by heated air from a flame suspended beneath them. At night, the envelope may disappear against the darkness, leaving only an orange, yellow or reddish light. A single lantern can resemble a glowing craft. Several released together can appear to form an organised group, especially when they share the same air current.

Their movement is deceptive because it is neither completely stationary nor mechanically controlled. A lantern may rise slowly, pause in a layer of air, drift sideways, change direction as the wind varies and finally extinguish. When seen head-on, it may seem almost fixed; when the airflow turns it across the observer’s field of view, it can appear to accelerate.

Timing also matters. Both Nièvre reports occurred on summer Saturdays, when outdoor celebrations and lantern releases were plausible. That coincidence does not prove the explanation by itself, but it adds context when combined with the lights’ colour, slow drift, grouping and eventual fading. Investigators treat those features collectively rather than assuming that any orange light must be a lantern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=55&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

These cases also show why multiple witnesses do not automatically rule out a mundane cause. A lantern is a real, visible object that can surprise many people at once. Additional witnesses may confirm its presence and general direction while still lacking the distance or structural detail needed to recognise it.

## Satellite flashes at La Charité-sur-Loire

The most recent solved pattern in the department involved very brief white flashes rather than a continuously moving object. From their garden at La Charité-sur-Loire, a principal witness and, occasionally, his wife repeatedly saw point-like flashes in the night sky. GEIPAN examined two observations from 2 December 2023 because these were the only events supported by sufficiently precise dates and times.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-12-51510" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARITE-SUR-LOIRE (LA) (58) 02.12.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The investigation concluded that the flashes were probably reflections from satellites. One flash could be matched to the position of a satellite using Jupiter as a nearby reference point. The second could not be tied to one particular spacecraft because the recorded direction was less exact and numerous satellites were near the zenith. GEIPAN therefore classified the case B rather than A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-12-51510" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARITE-SUR-LOIRE (LA) (58) 02.12.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A satellite flare occurs when sunlight reflects from a flat or shiny surface towards a narrow area on the ground. To the observer, a faint or invisible satellite may suddenly become intensely bright and then fade within seconds. Older Iridium communications satellites were especially well known for predictable flares produced by their reflective antenna panels, but other spacecraft can create comparable glints. GEIPAN’s guidance notes that such flashes may become brighter than Venus and can be checked against calculated satellite positions when the location and time are recorded accurately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

La Charité-sur-Loire is a useful example of careful evidential grading. The repeated nature of the witness’s experience did not make the phenomenon more mysterious; repeated satellite flashes were entirely possible. At the same time, investigators did not claim to have identified every individual flash. They separated the well-located observation from the imprecise one and limited their conclusion to a probable class of cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-12-51510" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARITE-SUR-LOIRE (LA) (58) 02.12.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why familiar lights can appear extraordinary

The solved Nièvre reports share three underlying mechanisms.

**First, brightness encourages an assumption of closeness.** A brilliant object feels nearby even when it is a planet, space station or satellite hundreds or millions of kilometres away. If the witness assumes that it is close, its apparent motion may imply impossible speed or sudden manoeuvres.

**Second, the night sky provides poor scale.** A point of light has no visible wings, surface or surrounding landscape against which to judge its size. GEIPAN states that people cannot reliably estimate the distance of an unfamiliar object beyond roughly ten metres without additional clues. Size, distance and speed estimates based on a lone light are therefore highly uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Source details in endnotes.</span></span></span>

**Third, ordinary movement can look purposeful.** A satellite entering shadow seems to switch off. A balloon crossing variable winds appears to turn. A planet seems to drift because of autokinesis. A lantern’s flame fades as though a powered object has departed. The observation may be genuine while the inferred behaviour is not.

Nièvre’s modern record consequently weakens the idea that strangeness alone is evidence of an extraordinary craft. The best-resolved cases became less mysterious when investigators obtained exact times, directions, sky photographs, weather data and orbital calculations. Reports without those details were harder to settle, not necessarily because their objects were more unusual, but because fewer explanations could be tested.

That is the main value of these cases within Nièvre’s UFO history. They replace a simple division between “real” and “mistaken” sightings with a more accurate picture: witnesses often saw real lights, described their surprise honestly and still reached the wrong conclusion about distance or identity. The decisive step was not disbelief, but reconstruction.
