## Introduction

[Aisne]({{ 'which-aisne-ufo-stories-withstand/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO record is less dramatic than its folklore. The cases published by GEIPAN—the French space agency unit that investigates reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena—are dominated by probable conventional explanations and files that cannot be assessed properly because essential information is missing. The accessible record includes reports from Saint-Quentin, Laon, the Soissons area and smaller communes, but it does not reveal a strong local collection of well-corroborated unexplained encounters.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Aisne_b23b8e_aisne_geipan_files_39741a-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for GEIPAN Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction matters. GEIPAN’s archive is not simply a list of things that remained “unidentified” when first seen. Investigators compare witness accounts with astronomy, aircraft activity, satellites, meteorology, photographs, video and other available evidence. A case may end as explained, probably explained, insufficiently documented or genuinely unresolved. In Aisne, the recurring pattern is not extraordinary objects surviving rigorous checks; it is ordinary-looking causes, ambiguous visual impressions and reports too weak to support a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[cnes.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN separates weak cases from unexplained ones

GEIPAN is a technical department of CNES, France’s national space agency. Its remit is to collect, analyse, investigate, publish and archive reports of unusual aerial or aerospace phenomena. It works with organisations including the gendarmerie, police, the Air and Space Force, Météo-France and scientific specialists, but it is not an extraterrestrial-research body or a real-time national air-defence service.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

* **Category A** means the phenomenon has been identified without meaningful ambiguity.
* **Category B** means GEIPAN considers a particular explanation very probable, although the evidence may not permit absolute proof.
* **Category C** means the report cannot be analysed adequately because reliable information is lacking.
* **Category D** is reserved for a phenomenon that remains unexplained despite sufficient evidence and investigation. Older or revised records may use D, while more recent methodology distinguishes between cases of moderate and stronger evidential consistency.

GEIPAN assesses both **consistency**—the amount and reliability of the information—and **residual strangeness**, meaning how far the report still lies from known phenomena after investigation. A puzzling story with vague timing, no precise direction and no usable recording may therefore become Category C rather than D. Conversely, a report can sound unusual yet receive a B classification when one conventional cause fits most of the facts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années14 Dec 2016 — Depuis 2008, le GEIPAN établit la classification A, B, C, D1/D2 au...</span></span></span>

This prevents one of the most common misunderstandings in UFO reporting: “unidentified” does not always mean that investigators discovered something genuinely resistant to explanation. It may mean only that the witness supplied too little information to test the possibilities. Nationally, CNES reports that 24.6 per cent of archived phenomena are clearly identified, 39.7 per cent probably identified, 32.4 per cent unidentified for lack of data and only 3.3 per cent unidentified after investigation. Aisne’s better-known official files broadly resemble that evidence-heavy national pattern.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

## Saint-Quentin shows the limits of an older file

The Saint-Quentin case of 26 April 1981 involved three listed witnesses and a red, apparently spherical light seen at about 10pm. One witness described it as roughly half the apparent size of the Moon, moving slowly and displaying a trail or tail. The report entered the official archive through a gendarmerie statement and is classified B, meaning that GEIPAN regarded a conventional identification as probable rather than certain. The database labels the precise identified phenomenon only as unspecified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/3608" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-QUENTIN (02) 26.04.1981 T3SAINT-QUENTIN (02) 1981. Date d&#x27;observation. 26/04/1981. Région. Picardie. Département. Aisne. Clas...</span></span></span>

At first glance, several witnesses might appear to make the incident especially strong. Yet witness numbers alone do not settle what was seen. People observing together are not fully independent sensors: they share the same viewing conditions, may discuss the object during the sighting and can reinforce a common interpretation. The useful questions are whether the accounts were recorded separately, whether directions and angular elevations agree, whether the time is precise and whether aircraft, astronomical or atmospheric checks can reproduce the observation.

The Saint-Quentin file also illustrates the unevenness of historical documentation. Older cases frequently depend heavily on police or gendarmerie records, because investigators did not have today’s satellite databases, digital imagery, flight-tracking resources or standardised online questionnaires. GEIPAN itself notes that gendarmerie statements were often the central document in earlier investigations and that old classifications can be reconsidered when better methods or new information become available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">baisse cas d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Forte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années14 Dec 2016 — Depuis 2008, le GEIPAN établit la classification A, B, C, D1/D2 au...</span></span></span>

The responsible conclusion is therefore narrower than either “solved” or “extraordinary”. The case contains more substance than a lone undocumented anecdote, but its B status indicates that the official assessment favours an ordinary phenomenon. It does not provide the kind of residual anomaly that would justify treating Saint-Quentin as a landmark unexplained Aisne encounter.

## Laon: a strange-looking video becomes a probable bird

The Laon report of 20 May 2021 is one of the clearest demonstrations of what modern investigation can add. Shortly after midnight, a witness recorded a small pale or amber object apparently making rapid, irregular movements. The witness described abrupt changes of direction, a brief pause and a fast departure. On a phone screen, such motion can look highly unusual, particularly when the object is distant, poorly focused and filmed against a largely featureless night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9999" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LAON (02) 20.05.2021LAON (02) 20.05.2021; Date d&#x27;observation. 20/05/2021; Région. Picardie; Département. Aisne; Classification...</span></span></span>

Unlike many older reports, the file contains a technical questionnaire, the original video, still photographs, a reconstruction and an investigation report. GEIPAN classified it B and identified the probable phenomenon as a bird. The archive gives the case a relatively good consistency score of 0.70 but low residual strangeness of 0.28. In practical terms, investigators had enough material to examine the claim, and the better the case became documented, the less extraordinary it appeared.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9999" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LAON (02) 20.05.2021LAON (02) 20.05.2021; Date d&#x27;observation. 20/05/2021; Région. Picardie; Département. Aisne; Classification...</span></span></span>

This is an important result rather than a dismissal. A nearby bird illuminated by street lighting, moonlight or the camera’s processing can appear as an indistinct bright body. Its natural turns may look like extreme acceleration when distance and scale are unknown. Autofocus, image stabilisation, hand movement and digital zoom can further separate apparent movement in the recording from the object’s real path.

The Laon case therefore shows why video is not automatically decisive evidence. A recording can improve a file because it preserves motion and timing, but it can also expose how easily an ordinary nearby object becomes ambiguous when the image contains no reliable reference points. Here, later analysis weakened the unusual interpretation.

## Château-Thierry’s surrounding area: lights that fit astronomy

At Chartèves, near Château-Thierry, two witnesses reported multiple white lights containing or accompanied by red points on 20 May 2020. They believed the lights were moving in rapid or incomprehensible ways, at times appearing to rise, descend or remain stationary. The observation lasted long enough for the witnesses to compare the objects with stars and satellites.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9829" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARTEVES (02) 20.05.2020 T1CHARTEVES (02) 20.05.2020. Date d&#x27;observation. 20/05/2020. Région. Picardie. Département. Aisne. Classi...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN classified the case B and placed it under an astronomical explanation. The listed consistency score was 0.64 and residual strangeness 0.38. These values do not imply that every apparent movement was reconstructed with certainty. They indicate that the astronomical interpretation fitted the available evidence well enough to be considered probable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/9829" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHARTEVES (02) 20.05.2020 T1CHARTEVES (02) 20.05.2020. Date d&#x27;observation. 20/05/2020. Région. Picardie. Département. Aisne. Classi...</span></span></span>

The likely mechanism is familiar in night-sky observation. A bright star or planet seen without a stable foreground can seem to drift, dart or oscillate because of tiny involuntary eye movements, changes in posture and moving cloud. Atmospheric turbulence can add rapid colour fluctuations, particularly when a bright object is viewed lower in the sky. When several stars are visible, attention may also switch between them, creating the impression that one light has crossed a larger distance than it actually has.

Chartèves is valuable because the witnesses were not necessarily careless or unreliable. They reported what the lights genuinely appeared to do. The investigative issue is whether apparent motion in a dark, reference-free sky corresponds to physical movement. GEIPAN’s conclusion was that a conventional astronomical explanation remained more persuasive.

## Older reports can be explained without a named cause

The 8 September 1978 case associated with Corbeny and Chamouille is classified A, the strongest “identified” category. Five testimony entries are attached to the case, and the archive refers to a single object and a gendarmerie document. However, the public database’s phenomenon field gives only an unspecified identified phenomenon rather than a reader-friendly explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/2219" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CHAMOUILLE (02) 08.09.1978 T5Geipan... CORBENY (02) 08.09.1978. Date d&#x27;observation. 08/09/1978. Région. Picardie. Département. Ais...</span></span></span>

This creates an awkward but important archival distinction. The official classification may communicate more certainty than the summary page communicates detail. A reader can see that the investigators considered the observation explained, but may not be able to recover a concise statement of exactly what the explanation was from the indexed entry alone.

The same problem affects some other older B records, including Saint-Quentin. It should not be filled with speculation. An unspecified label does not entitle later writers to insert an aircraft, meteor, balloon or astronomical body merely because one seems plausible. It means the public-facing metadata is incomplete. For department-level history, this is a limitation of presentation and preservation, not evidence that the original classification concealed an extraordinary conclusion.

## Saint-Michel and the problem of projected light

At Saint-Michel on 15 November 2009, a witness reported a narrow blue light making curved movements or loops in the sky. It was silent and was observed near developed land and transport infrastructure. GEIPAN classified the report B and identified the likely cause as a moving light display or projected illumination.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7248" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Such observations can feel especially unnatural because a projected beam or bright patch is not constrained like a solid aircraft. It may sweep rapidly across cloud, reverse direction, trace loops or disappear when the beam moves away from the cloud layer. The apparent “object” can therefore execute movements that would be difficult for a physical craft, precisely because no object is travelling along the perceived path.

Saint-Michel reinforces a pattern seen well beyond Aisne: unusual movement is not, by itself, evidence of unusual technology. Investigators must first determine whether the witness was watching a solid body, a point light, illumination on cloud, a reflection or a camera artefact. Once that distinction changes, the significance of speed and manoeuvrability changes with it.

## The Category C cluster is not a hidden unexplained wave

Several Aisne entries are classified C because GEIPAN lacked enough reliable information to test them. They include Jussy on 23 August 2009, Baulne-en-Brie on 7 July 2013, Gauchy on 20 July 2013 and Condé-sur-Aisne on 6 August 2015. In each case, the database describes the problem as a lack of reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=28%2C11&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These files should not be grouped with genuinely resistant Category D cases. A Category C report may have no precise time, uncertain direction, an incomplete questionnaire, no original image, delayed reporting or insufficient cooperation from the witness. Without those basics, investigators cannot reliably compare the sighting with aircraft routes, astronomical positions or weather conditions.

Nor does the presence of several C reports around 2009–15 establish an Aisne “flap”. Increased reporting can reflect many things: publicity, easier online submission, widely visible satellites or lanterns, or several unrelated observations. A credible cluster requires more than dates on a list. It needs meaningful similarity in time, location, appearance and movement, preferably supported by independent witnesses or instrumental records.

The evidential lesson is almost the reverse of the sensational interpretation. Category C preserves uncertainty, but it preserves weak uncertainty. It tells readers that the file could not support either a secure conventional explanation or a robust anomaly.

## Why radar and aviation evidence remain limited

Aviation or military corroboration could materially strengthen an Aisne case, particularly given the department’s position beneath busy northern French and cross-Channel air routes. Useful corroboration would include a precise time, direction and elevation; independent pilot testimony; matching radar returns; air-traffic records; or documentation showing that known aircraft could not account for the report.

The accessible Aisne cases discussed here do not present that combination. Some contain multiple witnesses, gendarmerie records, photographs or video, but the official outcomes still favour identification, probable explanation or insufficient evidence. GEIPAN has access to institutional partners including civil and military authorities, yet its published mission is investigative rather than a guarantee that radar data exist for every report. Many sightings are reported too late, are too imprecisely timed or concern objects too small or low to produce useful radar confirmation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

A missing radar match is therefore not automatically suspicious, but neither should a visual report be described as “confirmed by radar” unless the file explicitly provides that evidence. In Aisne’s official record, radar is more notable as an absent strengthening factor than as support for a major local case.

## Why uncertainty is not extraordinary evidence

The strongest conclusion from Aisne’s GEIPAN files is methodological. Reports become valuable according to what can be checked, not according to how dramatic they sound. A small, fast light may become a probable bird when video is examined. Erratic points may fit astronomical objects once orientation and viewing conditions are reconstructed. A looping beam may be projected light. Other reports remain undecided because their data are too poor to test.

Three levels should therefore remain separate:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Probably explained:** the available evidence favours a known cause, even though every detail cannot be proved.
2. **Weakly unresolved:** the report cannot be assessed because essential information is absent.
3. **Strongly unexplained:** good-quality evidence survives serious attempts at identification.

</div>

Most of the prominent accessible Aisne entries fall into the first two groups. That does not prove that every witness saw something ordinary, and it does not invalidate the witnesses’ experiences. It means the official archive supplies no strong department-wide basis for claiming a well-supported series of extraordinary craft encounters.

This also places the celebrated but poorly documented Soissons story of 1954 in perspective. Its imagery is more dramatic than the later GEIPAN cases, but its surviving evidence is substantially weaker. Modern official files may appear less exciting precisely because questionnaires, recordings and comparison checks expose ordinary explanations or reveal that the original information was inadequate. For Aisne’s UFO history, that is the central finding: mystery is present, but robust unexplained evidence is scarce.
