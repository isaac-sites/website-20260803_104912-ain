## Introduction

The Authon-du-Perche sighting of 10 March 1980 is the most detailed official UFO case in [Eure-et-Loir]({{ 'why-eure-et-loirs-ufo-mysteries-often/' | relative_url }}), but it is not a single unexplained encounter. Several gendarmes reported changing lights from their barracks and later from a patrol car. The episode gained weight because trained officers participated and produced an official report. Yet a later review by GEIPAN, the French space agency’s investigation group, divided the night into three separate phases rather than treating every observation as one object. It concluded that the first and best-documented phase was very probably Venus. The two later phases were left without reliable explanations, not because they presented strong evidence of an extraordinary craft, but because the surviving information was too confused and incomplete to analyse properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That distinction makes Authon especially instructive. Multiple sincere witnesses can still misidentify a familiar astronomical object, while an “unresolved” remainder may merely reflect poor records rather than compelling evidence.

## What the gendarmes reported that night

The sequence began at about 21:30, when officers heard a radio message concerning an unidentified aerial phenomenon reported by a motorist on the D13 between Les Étilleux and Nogent-le-Rotrou. Gendarmes looked in the indicated direction but initially saw nothing. The motorist was never properly identified or interviewed in the surviving file, so GEIPAN later excluded this preliminary report from its analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At approximately 22:10, a gendarme at the Authon-du-Perche barracks noticed a luminous ball while closing the shutters of his accommodation. His wife also saw it. He alerted his superior and other officers, who watched from the barracks towards Les Étilleux. Their collective description presented the light as a ball or disc that changed from bright orange to red, appeared to move in successive jumps and then stopped abruptly. The original report also described it as roughly ten times larger than the visible stars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At about 22:40, four of the gendarmes left in a vehicle, travelling along the D9 towards Les Étilleux and then Coudray-au-Perche. During the journey they noticed a dark-red luminous mass, at one point described as three reddish-orange lights. They later stopped near Moulin Neuf, around five kilometres from the barracks, where they reported a further stationary, silent phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The official report’s description of this final observation was much more elaborate. It referred to an oval object apparently at low altitude, with dazzling lights in its lower portion, predominantly red-orange lighting above, red points arranged along a horizontal line and a surrounding glow or “halo”. The officers estimated a length of about ten metres, a height of three metres and an altitude of roughly 150 metres. Those figures, however, were visual estimates made at night with no established distance or physical reference against which the object’s size could be measured.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0132%20%281980308519%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

No supporting witnesses were located in the settlements through which the patrol passed. There were also no photographs, instrument readings, radar records, ground traces or independently measured bearings. The surviving evidence consists mainly of a three-page preliminary gendarmerie report and a much longer private investigation produced later in 1980 by the Groupe de Recherches Cosmographiques in Le Mans.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why GEIPAN divided the sighting into three phases

The officers understood the night as a connected pursuit of one unusual object. GEIPAN’s later reviewers considered that assumption unsafe. Changes in location, appearance and observing conditions made it more likely that the witnesses had encountered several distinct lights. The review therefore separated the case into:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. the luminous object watched from the Authon barracks;
2. the reddish lights noticed while the patrol was moving;
3. the oval or structured phenomenon reported after the car stopped near Moulin Neuf.

</div>

This separation is central to the modern assessment. A report involving “five gendarmes and a UFO” sounds like one well-corroborated event. In fact, not every officer observed every phase, and the descriptions were not preserved as five clean, independent accounts. The official report combined the witnesses’ impressions into a collective narrative. That prevents investigators from determining which details were independently noticed, which were agreed after discussion and which came from only one observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also treated the private investigation cautiously. Its transcripts and drawings preserve useful detail, but the agency did not control the interviews or know enough about how questions were asked. Some exchanges were confusing or suggestive, and individual accounts diverged on important points. In one surviving transcript, for example, one witness said the lights appeared bright orange to him while acknowledging that a colleague had described them as red. He was also less certain about the object’s outline, saying it appeared round at the barracks but only vaguely rectangular near Moulin Neuf.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Enquete%20privee%20G.R.C-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These differences do not imply dishonesty. They show why a group report is not automatically equivalent to five mutually confirming observations. Once witnesses talk together, pursue a light together and later answer questions about a shared episode, their accounts may overlap without providing fully independent corroboration.

## Why Venus fits the first phase

GEIPAN found that Venus was low in the relevant part of the sky and setting during the first observation. The night was reportedly clear enough for stars to be visible, yet the witnesses did not separately mention Venus, despite its prominence. For the reviewers, the simplest explanation was that the bright “object” was Venus itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several reported characteristics support that interpretation.

**A brilliant ball much larger than the stars.** Venus is normally the brightest planet seen from Earth and can appear exceptionally conspicuous against a dark sky. A point source has no obvious physical boundary to the naked eye, so witnesses cannot reliably translate its apparent glare into a true angular size. The gendarmes’ statement that the light looked ten times larger than surrounding stars recorded an impression, not a measurement.

**Orange and red colour changes.** A bright celestial object near the horizon is viewed through a much greater depth of atmosphere than one high in the sky. Turbulence and atmospheric dispersion can cause rapid changes in brightness and colour, sometimes spreading the light into reddish, greenish or rainbow-like flashes. Venus is susceptible to such effects when it is setting low over a land horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.skyatnightmagazine.com/advice/skills/green-flash-venus-what-is-how-see" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skyatnightmagazine.com">[Sky at Night Magazine]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skyatnightmagazine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Image: The Venus green flash effect can be observed over a land horizon.... It’s also not limited to a sea horizon and can be seen to oc...</span></span></span>

**Sudden jumps, pauses and sideways motion.** GEIPAN invoked the autokinetic illusion: when a small, isolated light is watched against a dark background with few stable visual references, it may seem to wander even though it is stationary. The effect is recognised in aviation because pilots can perceive a fixed light as moving during dark-night conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Strange shapes through binoculars.** Binoculars do not necessarily resolve a bright point into a reliable image. Poor focus, internal reflections, optical aberrations and movement of the instrument can transform an intense light into discs, spikes, coloured points or apparent structures. GEIPAN noted both problems with the binocular use and the astigmatism of one observer as possible contributors to the reported forms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Venus explanation therefore does not require every witness to have imagined the event. It proposes that they saw a real, bright light but misjudged its nature, size and movement under difficult night-time conditions.

## Did several gendarmes make Venus less likely?

The number and occupation of the witnesses made the case socially persuasive. Gendarmes are accustomed to observation, reporting and dealing with uncertain situations. Their participation ensured that the incident entered an official administrative system rather than surviving only as a newspaper anecdote. The report was completed on 25 March 1980 and distributed through official channels, including civil, military and judicial authorities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0132%20%281980308519%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Professional status, however, cannot provide missing astronomical reference points or protect the eye from normal visual illusions. The French gendarmerie itself describes witness testimony as the starting point of a GEIPAN investigation while warning that accounts can differ from what was physically present in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">les gendarmes les transmettent au GEIPAN qui va ensuite</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gendarmerie NationalComment la gendarmerie prend-elle en compte les étrangetés...July 2, 2022 — 2 Jul 2022 — Objets volants non identifi...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 2, 2022</span></span></span>

There is also a specific group dynamic in Authon. The officers had already heard a radio alert about an unexplained object before the first successful sighting. Once one gendarme called the others outside and labelled the light as unusual, each observer was looking within an established interpretive frame. This does not invalidate their testimony, but it reduces the force of the claim that several people independently discovered the same anomalous behaviour.

The first phase offers a good example. All saw a conspicuous light in broadly the same direction, but apparent jumps or stops could arise because several people were staring at the same isolated point while discussing its movement. Shared attention can produce agreement around a mistaken interpretation without any witness deliberately influencing the others.

## What remains unresolved, and why the evidence is weak

GEIPAN classified the second and third phases as category C: insufficient reliable data. This is different from category D, which is used for a phenomenon that remains unidentified despite sufficiently substantial information. The distinction matters because the case’s unresolved elements are sometimes presented as though official investigators had confirmed that an extraordinary object escaped explanation. GEIPAN’s actual conclusion was that the material could not support a dependable analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

For the second phase, the file lacks basic observational data such as bearings, elevation angles, angular sizes and the separation between the reddish lights. The witnesses were also travelling in a car through wooded, undulating country. Relative movement between the vehicle, roadside features and distant lights could have altered the apparent position and behaviour of whatever they saw. GEIPAN did not identify the source, but neither did it find enough information to establish that the lights formed a structured object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The third phase is weakened by a different problem: the official narrative is unusually precise about size and altitude while failing to show how those values were obtained. A ten-metre object at 150 metres and a much larger object farther away can create the same apparent image. Without a measured angle, known landmark, photograph or reliable distance estimate, dimensions stated in metres give a misleading impression of precision.

The witnesses’ individual descriptions also appear less uniform in the private interviews than in the official report. Accounts differed over colour, outline and visible structure. At least one witness was unsure whether the light illuminated the surroundings, while another suggested that warmth felt afterwards might simply have resulted from excitement inside an unheated vehicle. Such details illustrate the instability of the later narrative rather than providing physical effects that could be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Enquete%20privee%20G.R.C-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Several ordinary possibilities therefore remain open for phases two and three: distant vehicle or building lights distorted by terrain, separate astronomical objects, aircraft lights, reflections or combinations of more than one source. None can be demonstrated from the file. The proper conclusion is not that the observations were certainly mundane, but that the surviving record cannot distinguish among competing explanations.

## How the later review changed the case

Authon-du-Perche was originally treated as an unexplained category-D case under the name Nogent-le-Rotrou. GEIPAN later reopened it as part of a wider reconsideration of older files, using modern astronomical reconstruction and greater experience of recurring misidentifications. The published case is now listed overall as category A, with Venus named as the phenomenon and the first phase described as a very probable identification. The database also gives the case low scores for both strangeness and evidential consistency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The headline classification needs careful reading. It does not mean that Venus explains every light seen during the patrol. GEIPAN’s detailed account explicitly assigns the first phase to Venus and the second and third phases to category C because the evidence is inadequate. The overall category A reflects the identification of the principal, best-supported portion of the case rather than a complete reconstruction of the entire evening.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-03-01696?page=%2C444" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Later reporting therefore weakened the original UFO claim in two ways. First, it supplied a coherent astronomical explanation for the observation that began the officers’ pursuit. Secondly, it exposed how little independent information existed for the more dramatic descriptions recorded afterwards. Nothing in the later material added photographs, radar confirmation, physical traces or new independent witnesses.

## The lesson for Eure-et-Loir’s UFO history

Authon remains important not because it offers the department’s strongest unexplained object, but because it shows how authority and evidential strength can diverge. Five gendarmes, an official report and a night-time search created an impressive case on paper. Close examination revealed that the report merged different times, locations and witnesses, while its most secure phase matched a bright planet setting through disturbed air.

The remaining phases should not be described as debunked, because no firm identification was established. Nor should they be presented as a surviving core of extraordinary evidence. They are unresolved in the weaker, administrative sense: investigators lack the individual testimony and measured data needed to decide what happened.

The fairest assessment is therefore divided, just as GEIPAN’s review was. The first Authon observation was very probably Venus, its apparent movement and colour enhanced by atmospheric and visual effects. The later lights cannot now be identified confidently, but the deficiencies of the record make them poor evidence for an unusual craft. That combination—credible witnesses, a persuasive initial story and an evidential collapse under closer separation—is what makes Authon-du-Perche the most instructive UFO case in Eure-et-Loir.
