## Introduction

Several Dordogne reports initially sounded dramatic: two green discs near Eymet, a blue-violet ball that apparently shocked a witness, a fluorescent green oval passing at low altitude, and repeated “machines” filmed over Carlux. Later examination generally weakened exotic interpretations. Some cases were convincingly identified; others remained incomplete because the evidence was too sparse. In the searchable public GEIPAN material, Dordogne appears to have produced more explained or weakly documented reports than robust, persistently unexplained ones.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-04-00300" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le 24 avril 1976 vers 16h un couple dans la campagne remarque le bruit inhabituel d&#x27;un fort déplacement d&#x27;air suivi de l&#x27;apparition à 300...</span></span></span>

## What the official record actually means

France investigates reports of unusual things in the sky through GEIPAN, a unit of the national space agency CNES. It collects witness accounts directly and through organisations such as the gendarmerie and civil aviation, then compares them with weather, astronomical, aviation and other technical information. A report enters the system because the witness could not identify the phenomenon at the time, not because officials believe it involved an extraordinary vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[gouv.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">comment la gendarmerie prend elle en compte les etrangetes dans le ciel</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Gendarmerie NationalComment la gendarmerie prend-elle en compte les étrangetés...2 Jul 2022 — Une fois les témoignages établis par procè...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN calls these reports unidentified aerospace phenomena. Its present classification considers both **strangeness**, meaning what unusual features remain after investigation, and **consistency**, meaning the amount and reliability of the available information. Broadly:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A** indicates a phenomenon that has been identified with high confidence.
* **B** indicates a probable identification.
* **C** means there is not enough reliable information to reach a firm conclusion.
* **D1 or D2** is reserved for cases retaining significant unexplained features after investigation, with D2 representing the more strongly supported category.

</div>

The distinction between C and D is especially important. A case is not scientifically impressive merely because nobody has explained it. Missing times, uncertain directions, absent photographs or delayed testimony may leave a report unresolved only because it cannot be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That distinction shapes Dordogne’s history. The department’s official material contains interesting witness experiences, but much of it falls into three groups: identifiable objects, probable conventional explanations and reports too incomplete to carry much evidential weight.

## Eymet in 1976: the department’s most striking early case

On 24 April 1976, at about 4pm, a couple in the countryside near Eymet reportedly heard an unusual rush of air and saw two luminous green discs roughly 300 metres away. The description has the qualities that make a sighting memorable: daylight, two witnesses, apparent proximity, colour, movement and sound. It is one of the clearest candidates for a “landmark” Dordogne incident in the official archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-04-00300" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le 24 avril 1976 vers 16h un couple dans la campagne remarque le bruit inhabituel d&#x27;un fort déplacement d&#x27;air suivi de l&#x27;apparition à 300...</span></span></span>

Yet GEIPAN does not classify Eymet as an unexplained D case. It places it in category B and associates the observation with electricity or plasma. The surviving public summary is brief, so readers should resist turning the classification into greater certainty than the record supports. A B classification means the proposed explanation is considered probable, not proved beyond doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=43.87932553447948&amp;customGetLongitude=2.6586914062500004&amp;customGetZoom=6&amp;field_agregation_index_value=ovni&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=48.45835188280866&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=39.30029918615029&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=10.129394531250002&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-4.812011718750001&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=2&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The case matters because it illustrates a recurring problem in historical UFO research. A vivid narrative can survive for decades while the detailed investigative chain remains difficult for the public to reconstruct. Important questions remain hard to assess from a short summary alone: the exact weather, whether there were power lines or electrical disturbances nearby, how independently the two witnesses described the discs, and how soon their statements were recorded.

A second Dordogne report from the same year shows the opposite evidential problem. An observation at La Bachellerie on 24 December 1976 is listed as category C because reliable information is lacking. That does not strengthen the case for an unknown craft; it means the record is too thin to support either an ordinary or an extraordinary conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=25%2C23&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The 1988 luminous ball that appeared to affect a witness

The Saint-Michel-de-Villadeix incident of 1 December 1988 is Dordogne’s most instructive example of a case becoming less mysterious after re-examination. At about 8.20am, a man walking with his dog saw an intense blue-violet sphere near the road. He described it as brighter than an arc welder, moving rapidly and silently. During the three- or four-second event he felt pressure and an electric discharge, while his dog ran towards home and stopped responding to commands. The witness made a statement to the gendarmerie on the day and another ten days later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-12-01155?order=field_date&amp;page=125&amp;sort=asc&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-MICHEL-DE-VILLADEIX (24) 01.12.1988Dordogne... classification explicité dans une note d&#x27;enquête.... Le GEIPAN classe le cas...</span></span></span>

The incident was originally treated as unexplained. Later GEIPAN investigators reconsidered it as a probable electrical or plasma phenomenon. Their reasoning was unusually concrete:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the light appeared close to intersecting power lines and a transformer;
* its movement followed the direction of one line;
* nearby residents reportedly experienced a short voltage drop at the relevant time;
* the witness’s sensations were compatible with an electrical disturbance.

</div>

GEIPAN also gave greater weight to the witness’s immediate statement than to his later account. The second version introduced several balls and placed the phenomenon farther away, after the witness had been approached by neighbours and the media. Investigators regarded the earliest testimony as less vulnerable to memory change or outside influence. The case is now category B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1988-12-01155?order=field_date&amp;page=125&amp;sort=asc&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-MICHEL-DE-VILLADEIX (24) 01.12.1988Dordogne... classification explicité dans une note d&#x27;enquête.... Le GEIPAN classe le cas...</span></span></span>

This reassessment does not prove that a particular form of ball lightning travelled along the line. Electrical plasma events are themselves difficult to reconstruct retrospectively. But the revised interpretation fits the location, power disturbance and physical sensations better than the unsupported idea of a structured craft.

The case also reveals why witness sincerity is not the same as factual accuracy. Nothing in GEIPAN’s account suggests fabrication. A person can honestly report an extraordinary experience while misjudging distance, number, size or context. The changed second statement demonstrates how quickly a short event may develop into a more elaborate memory without deliberate dishonesty.

## Military aviation created one of Dordogne’s strangest “objects”

Shortly after midnight on 25 June 2019, witnesses at La Roche-Chalais saw what they described as a low-flying fluorescent green oval with red lights at the rear. It produced an unfamiliar humming or rushing-air sound and remained visible for about a minute. The appearance did not resemble an ordinary helicopter to them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-06-50780" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANROCHE-CHALAIS (LA) (24) 25.06.2019Dordogne. Classification. A. Date de mise a jour. 12/03/2021. Type de phénomène. Hélicoptère. Etr...</span></span></span>

The eventual explanation was highly specific. An international military exercise was taking place about 40 kilometres away and involved American CV-22 Osprey tiltrotor aircraft. Lights near the blade tips could produce two virtual green discs as the rotors turned. Local people had also reported helicopters and green light in the exercise area. The witnesses’ home lay on a plausible route between the training zone and the military base at Mont-de-Marsan. GEIPAN classified the case A: a military aircraft observed under unfamiliar conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-06-50780" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANROCHE-CHALAIS (LA) (24) 25.06.2019Dordogne. Classification. A. Date de mise a jour. 12/03/2021. Type de phénomène. Hélicoptère. Etr...</span></span></span>

This is an important Dordogne case precisely because the witnesses’ description was unusual but substantially accurate. They reported colour, shape, noise, direction and low altitude. The mistake lay not in seeing nothing, but in lacking the specialist knowledge needed to recognise a rare aircraft configuration at night.

It also demonstrates the value of aviation context. A generic explanation such as “probably a helicopter” would have been weak. Identifying a particular aircraft, exercise, route and lighting effect turned a vague suggestion into a strong reconstruction. The incident is Dordogne’s clearest military connection, but it supports misidentification rather than secret or anomalous aviation.

## Stars, planets and the illusion of movement

Between 11 and 20 November 2020, two witnesses near Carlux repeatedly observed and filmed lights that they interpreted as aerial machines. Some sessions lasted several hours. That duration might initially appear to strengthen the report, because the witnesses had ample time to watch. In practice, it pointed towards astronomy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-11-51125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCARLUX (24) 11.11.2020Département Dordogne Classification A Date. Le GEIPAN classe le cas &quot;A&quot; (Phénomène parfaitement expliqué): m...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN compared the reported positions with the night sky and found matches with Sirius, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn. Jupiter and Saturn were approaching their conspicuous 2020 conjunction, making the pair especially noticeable. The lights’ slow progress was consistent with the apparent motion of the sky caused by Earth’s rotation. Brief darting movements were attributed to autokinesis, a visual effect in which a fixed light seems to move when viewed against a dark, featureless background. GEIPAN classified the case A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-11-51125" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCARLUX (24) 11.11.2020Département Dordogne Classification A Date. Le GEIPAN classe le cas &quot;A&quot; (Phénomène parfaitement expliqué): m...</span></span></span>

Carlux is useful because it counters a common assumption that prolonged or repeated observation automatically establishes an object’s physical movement. A bright planet can be watched night after night. Magnification may make atmospheric shimmer and optical distortion look like changing structure. A camera can preserve the witness’s experience without preserving reliable information about distance or size.

A weaker astronomical case appears at Savignac-les-Églises in March 2009. The official index associates it with Saturn but leaves it in category C. That combination is not contradictory: investigators may have identified a plausible celestial source while lacking enough reliable testimony to make the identification firm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=131&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Balloons and lanterns account for many modern reports

Lightweight balloons and small flame-powered lanterns have become recurring sources of French UFO reports. They move silently, follow winds that may differ from conditions at ground level, change apparent shape as they rotate and can disappear abruptly behind cloud or when their light extinguishes.

At Salignac-Eyvigues on 10 September 2017, a witness saw an orange, apparently triangular object travel silently from west to east. Through binoculars it seemed brighter at the corners. GEIPAN considered a sky lantern the probable explanation because its colour and movement matched the wind, while the summer weekend date made a celebratory release plausible. The case was classified B rather than A because no particular lantern was independently traced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-09-50430" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other Dordogne entries reinforce the pattern. Prats-de-Carlux in September 2015 was classified A as a sky lantern, while Campsegret in June 2019 was classified B with the same probable cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=30&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At Saint-Michel-de-Montaigne in March 2020, the object was darker and stranger. A witness first described a rounded triangular form, then something like a dense palm head with moving projections. It drifted in the direction of the light wind and made no sound. GEIPAN proposed one or possibly two novelty balloons, with rotation accounting for the apparent change in shape. Because the reconstruction remained probable rather than conclusive, the case was classified B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-03-50928?field_date_value=2021-11-23&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=13" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The lesson is not that every irregular object is a balloon. It is that apparent structure is unreliable when an object is small, distant, rotating and seen without a known scale. A group of lights may look like the corners of a dark triangle; a novelty balloon may appear biological or mechanical; a brief loss of visibility may be mistaken for an instantaneous departure.

## Meteors, re-entries and errors of distance

Fast luminous events produce a different class of report. Witnesses may describe a fireball as being just beyond nearby trees or travelling horizontally at low altitude, although it is actually many kilometres away. Without depth cues, the mind tends to place an unfamiliar light within the visible landscape.

At Corgnac-sur-l’Isle on 16 November 1995, a witness saw a brilliant white ball with a red trail for two or three seconds. GEIPAN regarded it as a probable atmospheric re-entry and classified it B. At Hautefort in February 2009, another Dordogne report was classified B as a fireball.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1995-11-01403" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Dordogne should also be read against the nationwide event of 5 November 1990. At about 7pm, numerous French witnesses and gendarmerie units reported a large, apparently low object carrying multiple lights. CNES ultimately identified the spectacle as the atmospheric re-entry of the third stage of a Soviet Proton rocket. GEIPAN notes that observers often interpreted the separated fragments as lights fixed to one dark craft and seriously underestimated their altitude.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That event is relevant to local history even though it was not exclusive to Dordogne. It provides a controlled comparison for reports of large silent objects crossing a wide area. When multiple bright fragments move together, the brain naturally joins them into a single structured form. Agreement among witnesses about a “craft” therefore does not necessarily establish that a solid craft was present.

## What is missing from Dordogne’s UFO story

The public record reviewed here does not reveal a well-supported Dordogne case combining independent witnesses, instrument confirmation, physical traces and a lasting unexplained classification. There are intriguing descriptions, but the evidential chain usually breaks at one or more points.

Several limitations recur:

**Single or dependent witnesses.** Two people in the same household are valuable witnesses, but they share location, attention and discussion. Their accounts are not equivalent to independent observations from separated sites.

**No calibrated imagery.** Videos of distant lights often lack reliable information about range, size and motion. They can confirm that a light was recorded while adding little evidence about what it was.

**Delayed or changing testimony.** The Saint-Michel-de-Villadeix case shows why early statements matter. Later additions may reflect normal memory reconstruction, conversation or media attention.

**Incomplete environmental data.** Old cases may lack precise weather, aviation, power-network or astronomical records. That can prevent a convincing explanation without making an extraordinary explanation more likely.

**Archive selection.** GEIPAN’s database reflects reports that reached the official system and were suitable for publication. It is not a complete census of everything ever seen or printed in the Dordogne press.

This last point matters when considering the great French sighting wave of 1954. France experienced an exceptional burst of reports that autumn, but the readily accessible evidence does not establish Dordogne as one of its principal centres. National retellings can create the impression that every department experienced a dense, well-documented “flap”. For Dordogne, the safer conclusion is that local historical material remains fragmented and should not be inflated into a major departmental wave without contemporary newspaper files or gendarmerie records to support it. France’s 1954 wave was real as a social and reporting phenomenon, but disputed sighting patterns and later alignment theories do not by themselves validate individual incidents.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.completefrance.com/travel/flying-saucers-in-france-a-history-of-ufo-sightings/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: completefrance.com">[completefrance.com]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">completefrance.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">flying saucers in france a history of ufo sightings</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">flying saucers in france a history of ufo sightings</span></span></span>

## How later investigation changed the balance

Dordogne’s most valuable UFO history is a history of revision. Early classifications were not always treated as permanent. New astronomical software, aircraft information, meteorological reconstruction and experience with recurring misidentifications allowed investigators to revisit older reports. The 2024 case is especially revealing. A motorist saw a bright blinking point before dawn and at one stage thought it appeared to ignite. The witness doubted the balloon explanation because balloons were assumed not to fly in darkness. GEIPAN found the relevant balloon’s radar track, while the witness’s own video showed the burner flame. ## Dordogne’s place in French UFO history

Dordogne is not best understood as a hidden hotspot. Its record is a compact case study in why UFO reports remain culturally powerful even when conventional explanations are available. Rural roads, dark skies and unfamiliar aircraft can create vivid experiences. Planets may appear mobile; balloons can look engineered; military equipment can produce genuinely strange light patterns; electrical faults may create rare luminous effects; distant fireballs can seem close enough to touch.

The department also illustrates the three very different meanings of “unresolved”:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Not identified at the time:** the witness genuinely did not know what was seen.
* **Not proved but probably explained:** the evidence favours an ordinary source, as in several category B cases.
* **Not decidable:** the information is insufficient, as in category C cases.

</div>

None of these automatically means an exotic craft. Conversely, identifying an observation does not make the original experience foolish or worthless. The La Roche-Chalais witnesses apparently saw a real, rare aircraft under confusing conditions. The Saint-Michel-de-Villadeix witness experienced something sufficiently unusual to report immediately to the gendarmerie. The Carlux observers produced recordings that investigators could compare with the sky.

The fairest overall assessment is therefore restrained. Dordogne has a varied archive of unusual aerial observations and several memorable witness accounts, but later evidence has generally weakened rather than strengthened extraordinary interpretations. Its most persuasive cases are persuasive as demonstrations of investigation: they show how testimony, local geography, weather, astronomy, military activity and technical records must be brought together before a strange light becomes a meaningful mystery.
