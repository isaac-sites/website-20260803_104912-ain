## The 2011 divers’ sighting off the Golo

On the morning of 20 August 2011, five amateur divers travelled by inflatable boat from Taverna to a diving site known as Punta d’Arco, offshore from the mouth of the Golo on the eastern side of Haute-Corse. After a dive of roughly 45 minutes, they noticed a bright phenomenon that appeared motionless in a clear sky. According to the principal account, it remained visible for about five minutes, disappeared, then reappeared in a somewhat different form before vanishing again.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

At first glance, the case had several attractive features: five people were reportedly present, the weather was excellent, the object was seen in daylight, and a video made before the dive contained a small white point in the sky. Yet nearly every apparent strength came with an important limitation. Only one of the five divers was formally interviewed. The relevant observation itself was not filmed. The white point appeared in footage taken before the dive, so investigators could not establish that it was the same thing later reported by the group.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The delay was particularly damaging. GEIPAN received the report more than a month after the event and could no longer request useful radar information. A local investigator was not assigned until April 2012, about eight months after the sighting. By then, estimates of apparent size, exact direction and sequence depended heavily on memory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Investigators nevertheless checked several independent lines of enquiry. Bastia-Poretta air-traffic staff found that the period was exceptionally busy: between 10 and 11 a.m. there were five departures and six arrivals linking Corsica with the mainland, as well as numerous aircraft crossing the island’s wider airspace. Nothing abnormal had been recorded by the control tower, aviation gendarmerie, local gendarmerie brigades or Solenzara air base. The tower also had a view towards the coastal sector concerned, yet no separate report was logged.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

GEIPAN considered helicopters and a maritime-surveillance aircraft but found difficulties with each idea, particularly the reported silence and apparent dimensions. Its preferred explanation was a succession of airliners viewed almost head-on. Sunlight reflecting from aircraft surfaces, combined with approach lights and heat distortion, could make distant aircraft appear bright, nearly stationary and capable of changing shape. An aircraft approaching an observer has little apparent sideways motion, while much of its engine noise is directed behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The case was classified B: aircraft were judged probable rather than conclusively proved. That distinction is appropriate. GEIPAN did not match every reported appearance to a specific flight, and the other four witnesses were never interviewed. On the other hand, the reported large apparent size sat uneasily with the complete absence of reports from beaches, nearby holiday sites, aviation personnel and local media. Investigators considered it likely that the witness had overestimated the object’s angular size months after the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The 2011 incident is therefore one of Haute-Corse’s better documented cases, but its documentation weakened rather than strengthened the extraordinary interpretation. It shows how a real observation by sincere witnesses can remain impressive while still having a plausible conventional cause.

## Five lights over Bastia in 2013

A second substantial investigation began with an observation from a roof terrace in central Bastia on 5 August 2013. At approximately 11.50 p.m., four people saw five lights moving from north to south. One witness described an initial V formation followed by apparent geometrical changes. The lights were star-like, made no audible sound and remained visible for roughly 30 seconds before seeming to descend towards the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

Only one of the four observers gave a detailed account, but the investigator regarded that person as calm, sincere and not especially interested in UFOs. Credibility, however, did not settle the identity of the lights. The crucial evidence came from Bastia-Poretta airport.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

The final scheduled flight from Paris had been seriously delayed and landed at 12.02 a.m. Air-traffic staff explained that aircraft arriving from Paris commonly pass over or near Cap Corse, continue southwards offshore from Bastia and then turn towards the airport. The delayed Airbus A320 would therefore have crossed the relevant part of the sky at approximately the reported time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

GEIPAN concluded that the five lights were the aircraft’s position and approach lights. Their apparent geometry could change as the Airbus turned and descended, while distance and the direction of travel could account for the lack of audible engine noise. No anomaly was reported by air-traffic control or the aviation gendarmerie, no additional independent witness was located, and no related story appeared in the local press. The case was classified A: identified as flight AT514 during its landing approach.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

This case is an unusually clear example of retrospective identification. Without the delayed-flight record, a formation of five coordinated lights over a coastal city might have remained an intriguing anecdote. A single piece of time-specific aviation evidence transformed it into an ordinary aircraft seen from an unfamiliar angle.

## Venus, meteors and the problem of apparent motion

Two other cases illustrate recurring sources of misidentification. The March 1980 Favalello report was classified B with Venus as the probable explanation. Venus is exceptionally bright and, when seen low in a dark sky without useful reference points, can appear larger, closer or even in motion. Small involuntary eye movements can also make a fixed light seem to wander, an effect GEIPAN explicitly recognises when assessing unusual aerial reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=57&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Santo-Pietro-di-Venaco sighting of January 2003 involved an intense, rapidly moving, multicoloured light seen for only a few seconds. GEIPAN found that the short duration, high apparent speed and strong green and yellow colouring matched an atmospheric meteoroid. The case was classified B rather than D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2003-01-01603" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Meteor reports often sound more mechanical than observers expect. A bright fireball can fragment, change colour, leave a glowing trail or appear surprisingly low. Without an accurate duration and direction, later retellings may make it resemble a controlled object. The Santo-Pietro-di-Venaco file is especially useful because it was later revisited and moved away from an unexplained interpretation towards a conventional astronomical one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=1&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=2&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These cases also warn against treating the word “unidentified” as a permanent property. A phenomenon is unidentified only relative to the information available at a particular stage. Better astronomical calculations, flight records or comparison reports can change a classification years later. GEIPAN specifically allows older C and D files to be reconsidered when new information becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/mentions_legales" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Classification into A, B, C, D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page Des Mentions Légales &#124; GEIPAN * No, GEIPAN is a technical department of CNES (Centre National d’Études Spatiales), a public, i...</span></span></span>

## Why Haute-Corse produces convincing reports

The department’s geography creates difficult viewing conditions. Bastia and the eastern plain face open sea and busy air routes, while aircraft approaching Bastia-Poretta may be observed head-on, turning or descending against a dark horizon. In summer, dense passenger traffic adds more lights, reflections and unfamiliar flight paths. The 2011 investigation recorded intense daytime air traffic, and the 2013 case showed how one delayed arrival could closely reproduce a seemingly coordinated formation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Mountainous terrain also reduces reliable distance cues. Around Corte, Venaco and Vezzani, a light seen above a ridge may be difficult to place: it could be nearby and small, or distant and very bright. On the coast, the sea provides few fixed reference points, encouraging overestimation of both altitude and apparent size. GEIPAN’s method therefore places considerable weight on angular measurements, exact viewing direction, duration and independent corroboration rather than accepting a witness’s distance estimate at face value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Classification &#124; GEIPANSince 2008, a more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The department’s record further shows the importance of reporting quickly. Radar and detailed flight-plan data are not retained indefinitely. In the 2011 case, the reporting delay prevented a radar request; in the 2013 investigation, some flight-plan material had already been deleted because civil aviation retained it for only two months, although the decisive landing record survived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Photographs and video are not automatically decisive either. A tiny point of light without stable landmarks, original metadata or a continuous recording of the whole event may provide less information than a precise written account. In the divers’ case, the video confirmed only that a white point had been present before the dive; it could not prove that the point was unusual or connected with the later sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

## Are any Haute-Corse cases genuinely unexplained?

Some public files remain in category C, including Vezzani, Borgo, Vescovato and San-Martino-di-Lota. These cases are unresolved in the everyday sense that no secure identification was made, but they should not be presented as strong mysteries. GEIPAN’s category C specifically means that the evidence is too incomplete or unreliable to determine what happened.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=5%2C4&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That is different from a category D case, in which a reasonably consistent body of evidence survives investigation without an adequate explanation. The available Haute-Corse material is dominated instead by identified or probably identified events and low-information reports. The [official files]({{ 'official-files-d8abc5/' | relative_url }}) examined here provide no robust local case combining multiple independently interviewed witnesses, preserved radar data, clear imagery, physical effects and an unresolved final classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/mentions_legales" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Classification into A, B, C, D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Page Des Mentions Légales &#124; GEIPAN * No, GEIPAN is a technical department of CNES (Centre National d’Études Spatiales), a public, i...</span></span></span>

This does not imply that witnesses fabricated their experiences. GEIPAN’s published approach starts from the fact that people often experience genuine “strangeness” when ordinary objects or natural phenomena are seen under unfamiliar conditions. The investigation asks what could reproduce the observation, not whether the witness is foolish or dishonest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/440" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The fairest assessment of Haute-Corse is therefore restrained. The department has produced memorable reports, particularly around Bastia and the eastern coast, and a few older accounts remain indeterminate because their records are weak. Yet the strongest investigated examples have generally moved towards aviation or astronomical explanations. Later reporting has not built a compelling case for an exceptional local phenomenon; in the best-documented incidents, it has mostly reduced the mystery.
