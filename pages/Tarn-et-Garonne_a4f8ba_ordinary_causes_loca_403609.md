## Introduction

Most investigated UFO reports from [Tarn-et-Garonne]({{ 'tarn-et-garonne/' | relative_url }}) have turned out to involve ordinary objects or natural events: bright meteors, illuminated balloons, distant aircraft, the Moon, projected lights and even birds. These explanations matter because the original descriptions could sound far stranger than their eventual causes. Witnesses reported silent shapes, hovering lights, apparent triangles and objects that seemed only tens of metres away.

The department’s official files therefore offer a useful warning against judging a sighting by vivid language alone. GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that investigates unidentified aerospace phenomena, compares testimony with astronomy, weather, aircraft movements, geography, video and local events. Its conclusions range from category A, where an identification is considered secure, to category B, where an ordinary explanation is probable rather than certain. Across France, CNES currently lists about 24.6 per cent of investigated cases as fully identified and 39.7 per cent as probably identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

## Meteors can look close, shaped and silent

A meteor or atmospheric re-entry does not always resemble the familiar thin streak of a “shooting star”. A bright fireball may appear as a glowing oval, teardrop or compact object, sometimes changing size or brightness as it crosses the sky. Because observers usually have no reliable distance reference, something many kilometres above them can seem low, nearby and structured.

The Montauban sighting of 9 September 2011 illustrates this problem particularly well. At about 10.10 pm, several people in the same neighbourhood saw a luminous phenomenon travelling rapidly from south to north. Two independent accounts described a white teardrop or bean-like form, visible for between several seconds and half a minute. It appeared silent, did not illuminate its surroundings and seemed to shrink as it moved away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-09-02828?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=87&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MONTAUBAN (82) 09.09.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators initially considered illuminated lanterns connected with Montauban’s local festivities, but the witnesses rejected that interpretation and the reported speed did not fit a slowly drifting lantern. GEIPAN’s later analysis instead favoured an atmospheric entry by a meteoroid or piece of space debris. The case was classified B: probably a bright fireball, rather than conclusively identified from direct instrumental evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-09-02828?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=87&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MONTAUBAN (82) 09.09.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The incident shows why multiple witnesses do not necessarily prove that an unusual vehicle was present. People watching the same brief light from roughly the same area share the same lack of scale and distance cues. Their agreement that the event was fast, bright and silent strengthens the evidence that something real crossed the sky, but it does not by itself establish what that object was.

A similar mechanism probably explains the Caussade report of 31 July 2011. A witness described a very fast, white, oval phenomenon that made no audible sound and quickly disappeared towards the horizon. GEIPAN found its speed, brightness and direction compatible with atmospheric entry, possibly associated with the Perseid meteor period, and forwarded the information to specialists at the Institute of Celestial Mechanics and Ephemeris Calculation and the National Museum of Natural History.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-07-02802" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCAUSSADE (82) 31.07.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These cases also explain why the absence of noise is weak evidence against a meteor. Light reaches an observer almost immediately, whereas any sound generated far away arrives later and may never be heard at all. A short observation can therefore end before the witness has any reason to associate a delayed sound with what was seen.

## Balloons can resemble coherent craft

The Mas-Grenier case of 8 August 2020 is one of Tarn-et-Garonne’s clearest examples of an apparently structured object being produced by several ordinary objects moving together. At about 10 pm, witnesses saw what they described as a luminous, jellyfish-shaped object passing silently above a cherry tree. It carried bluish halos, followed a broadly straight course while undulating and gradually became a point of light towards the southern horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-08-51050" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMAS-GRENIER (82) 08.08.2020 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That account could easily be retold as a close encounter with a single manoeuvring craft. The investigation produced a much more specific chain of evidence. Weather records showed a weak north-to-south wind matching the reported direction. Aircraft tracking did not show a relevant overflight. A village function room lay a few streets north of the observation point, and the local council confirmed that a wedding had taken place that evening. More decisively, the wedding party had released bunches of white balloons, each fitted with an LED light, near the reported time. A photograph of the preparations was also supplied. GEIPAN classified the case A: a certain misidentification of a cluster of illuminated balloons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-08-51050" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMAS-GRENIER (82) 08.08.2020 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The “jellyfish” impression was not irrational. A bouquet of balloons can maintain a loose collective outline while its individual parts bob, rotate and change spacing. Lights attached to the balloons create the appearance of illuminated features on a larger body, while darkness hides the strings and unlit surfaces linking the visible points. Slow wind-driven movement also explains the silence and apparent wavering.

Illuminated lanterns caused other local reports. At Montauban on 27 September 2014, GEIPAN judged a reported light to be probably a flying lantern and assigned it category B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-09-09009" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MONTAUBAN (82) 27.09.2014 &#124; GEIPANSeptember 27, 2014 — ## MONTAUBAN (82) 27.09.2014... Lanterne thai... Le GEIPAN classe ce cas &quot;...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 27, 2014</span></span></span> The Albias file from January 2009 likewise records a second, independent observation whose characteristics probably matched a lantern, although the primary event that night had a different explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-01-02730" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANALBIAS (82) 17.01.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These distinctions are important. Mas-Grenier was not resolved merely because balloons seemed possible; investigators found the actual local release. Where no release was independently confirmed, GEIPAN used the more cautious category B.

## Aircraft can appear stationary or triangular

Aircraft are easiest to recognise when they cross the observer’s field of view sideways. When an aircraft approaches almost directly, however, its angular position may change very little. A bright landing light can then appear stationary for many minutes, while wings, fuselage and navigation lights remain difficult to see.

That geometry was central to the A62 case of 11 August 2013. While travelling between the Valence-d’Agen area and Toulouse at around 7 am, a driver and passenger watched a white point that appeared almost motionless. Two short white trails extended behind it, producing what looked like a triangle without a base. The observation lasted about twenty minutes, ending when the car’s changing position carried the witnesses past it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-08-08537" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan[A62] de VALENCE-D&#x27;AGEN (82) vers TOULOUSE (31) 11.08.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN examined the high-altitude weather conditions and compared the appearance with known aircraft displaying short condensation trails. It concluded that the object was certainly an airliner and classified the report A. The apparent triangular form did not require a triangular vehicle: the luminous point and two diverging contrails supplied the visible corners and sides, while the witnesses’ viewpoint concealed the ordinary aircraft generating them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-08-08537" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan[A62] de VALENCE-D&#x27;AGEN (82) vers TOULOUSE (31) 11.08.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This case highlights a broader problem with reports made from moving cars. The witness is not observing from a fixed platform. Road bends, vehicle speed, foreground objects and changes in viewing angle can make a distant object seem to stop, follow the car or move unexpectedly. The aircraft’s true speed may be high even while its movement against the distant sky appears slight.

## The Moon can become an unfamiliar object

The Moon seems too obvious to cause a UFO report, yet it becomes surprisingly difficult to recognise when seen low on the horizon, partly obscured or viewed through cloud and haze. Atmospheric scattering can turn it orange or red, while buildings, trees and uneven cloud may hide enough of its disc to create a semicircle, dome or glowing segment.

At Albias shortly after midnight on 17 January 2009, a witness reported an orange half-circle close to the eastern horizon. Astronomical reconstruction showed the Moon in the reported part of the sky, about 60 per cent illuminated and in a waning gibbous phase. GEIPAN classified the observation A as the Moon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2009-01-02730" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANALBIAS (82) 17.01.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The useful point is not simply that the witness “failed to recognise the Moon”. Recognition depends on context. A familiar object can look unfamiliar when it appears at an unexpected time or orientation, when only part of it is visible, or when its apparent colour differs from everyday experience. Once the observer interprets it as something nearby, its slow astronomical movement may be perceived as hovering.

## Birds can survive the video test

The Valence-d’Agen report of 9 April 2023 is particularly instructive because it included video. In late afternoon, a witness saw two white objects moving for about two minutes; roughly twenty minutes later, another similar object appeared and was filmed. GEIPAN regarded the case as reasonably well documented and considered both balloons and birds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-04-51433?field_date_value=2024-08-12&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VALENCE D&#x27;AGEN (82) 09.04.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Balloons matched the white appearance and brightness, but their circling movement would have required local swirling winds that were not supported by the available weather model. Birds provided a better overall fit. Their white undersides could account for the flashes of colour, while circling in rising air currents could produce alternating loops and straight segments. An ornithological specialist noted that the apparent circles looked smaller than usual, so the match was not perfect. GEIPAN also reported possible wingbeats in the video, although it did not treat them as certain. The case was therefore classified B: probably birds, not conclusively proved birds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-04-51433?field_date_value=2024-08-12&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VALENCE D&#x27;AGEN (82) 09.04.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This finding weakens the common assumption that video automatically settles a sighting. A small distant target may occupy only a few pixels. Autofocus, compression and digital zoom can alter its outline, while hand movement makes the object’s own motion difficult to separate from the camera’s. In the Valence-d’Agen footage, GEIPAN explicitly noted that camera movement complicated assessment of the object’s path.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-04-51433?field_date_value=2024-08-12&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C8" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VALENCE D&#x27;AGEN (82) 09.04.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Video can still be valuable. It may establish duration, direction, brightness changes or repeated movements more reliably than memory. But its evidential value depends on scale, focus, steadiness, original file quality and whether investigators can reconstruct where the camera was pointing. A blurred recording is evidence that something was filmed, not necessarily evidence that the object had the shape later perceived in the enlarged image.

## Several witnesses can share the same mistake

Multiple witnesses improve a report when their accounts are genuinely independent, recorded promptly and compared without cross-contamination. They do not eliminate ordinary explanations. The witnesses may stand together, discuss the object while watching it and adopt the same interpretation. They also share the same viewing conditions: darkness, cloud, distance, glare and the absence of a stable scale reference.

Mas-Grenier had several witnesses, yet a documented balloon release explained the event. Montauban’s 2011 fireball produced independent reports, but the shared description was still compatible with a natural atmospheric event. At Valence-d’Agen, two people were present, although only one formal account was collected and only one person recorded the later object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-08-51050" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMAS-GRENIER (82) 08.08.2020 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The strongest investigations therefore ask a different question from “How many people saw it?” They examine whether witnesses observed from different locations, whether their timings and bearings agree, whether they described the event before discussing it together, and whether external records support one explanation. Several similar accounts can confirm the presence of a real stimulus while leaving its nature entirely ordinary.

## What the local pattern actually shows

Tarn-et-Garonne’s identified cases do not point to one recurring object. They reveal several recurring mechanisms of misperception:

* **Brief, rapid lights** are often compatible with meteors or atmospheric re-entry.
* **Slow, silent clusters** may be lanterns or LED balloons carried by the wind.
* **Apparently fixed lights and triangular trails** can result from an aircraft approaching along the observer’s line of sight.
* **Orange or partial shapes near the horizon** may be the Moon seen through haze or obstruction.
* **Small white objects circling in daylight** may be birds using rising air.
* **Moving or repeated beams** can come from ground-based lighting; GEIPAN’s department listing, for example, attributes a Pompignan report from November 2016 to a laser and a Labastide-Saint-Pierre case from July 2024 to a sky-tracer searchlight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=18&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

None of this proves that every unresolved Tarn-et-Garonne report has a mundane cause. Cases lacking dependable timings, directions, original files or independent records may remain unclassified because the necessary checks are no longer possible. Nor should a plausible suggestion be treated as a demonstrated solution.

The stronger lesson is narrower: descriptions such as “silent”, “triangular”, “hovering”, “close” or “seen by several people” are not diagnostic by themselves. In Tarn-et-Garonne, those same features have arisen from confirmed balloons, an identified airliner, the Moon and probable birds or meteors. The department’s official record becomes less mysterious as more contextual evidence is added—not because witnesses necessarily invented anything, but because ordinary objects can look extraordinary when distance, scale and motion are uncertain.
