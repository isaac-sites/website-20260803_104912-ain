## Introduction

[Moselle]({{ 'which-moselle-ufo-cases-still-resist/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO record does not prove that extraordinary craft have crossed the department. It proves something more limited, but still useful: when reports are checked against aircraft movements, orbital objects, astronomy, balloons and witness circumstances, many apparently mysterious sightings become ordinary. A smaller residue remains officially unidentified because the available evidence has not yielded a satisfactory explanation.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Moselle_b6f07c_moselle_geipan_files_2118c6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for GEIPAN Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

GEIPAN, the French space agency unit responsible for unidentified aerospace phenomena, lists several Moselle cases in its unexplained category, notably Guébling in 1978, Metz in 1979, Ars-sur-Moselle in 1990 and Rémilly in 1993. Yet the same archive contains many more reports classified as identified, probably identified or too poorly documented to assess. That balance is essential. An unexplained file records the limit of an investigation, not confirmation of an alien vehicle or even proof that a solid object was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-01-00474" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGUEBLING (57) 07.01.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN separates mystery from missing information

GEIPAN does not classify a sighting simply according to how unusual it sounded to the witness. Its method considers two linked questions: how strange the observation remains after comparison with known phenomena, and how consistent the supporting evidence is. Consistency depends on the quantity, precision and reliability of the information collected, including the number and independence of witnesses, the coherence of their accounts and the presence of photographs, radar records or other objective material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The archive’s main categories are:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A:** the phenomenon has been identified with high confidence.
* **B:** a known explanation is considered probable.
* **C:** no identification can be made because the information is inadequate.
* **D:** the phenomenon remains unidentified after investigation.

</div>

Since 2008, GEIPAN has further distinguished D1 and D2 cases according to the strength and strangeness of the evidence, although many older Moselle pages still display the broader legacy label “D”. A case can also be reconsidered if new information emerges or older records are re-examined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This makes the difference between C and D especially important. A C case is not a compelling mystery merely because it lacks an explanation. It is usually a report that cannot be tested properly: the time may be uncertain, the direction missing, the witness unavailable or the description too vague. Moselle’s archive contains numerous such entries, including reports from Bouzonville, Phalsbourg, Metz, Guénange, Hayange, Florange, Thionville and Fameck. Their status means “insufficient evidence”, not “unexplainable phenomenon”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B1%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=34&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also stresses that testimony is vulnerable to ordinary perceptual effects. Distance and speed are difficult to estimate for an unidentified light, moving observers can make an aircraft appear stationary, and memory may incorporate later interpretations. This does not imply dishonesty. It explains why a sincere, detailed account can still describe an aircraft, planet or satellite in unfamiliar terms.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## The Moselle observations still listed as unexplained

### Guébling, 7 January 1978

At about 4.50 am, a witness travelling by moped near Guébling reported three very bright lights close to ground level. According to GEIPAN’s summary, one light separated from the others, approached and appeared to follow the witness as far as the entrance to a neighbouring village, where it disappeared. No sound was heard. Investigators checked whether a helicopter had been operating in the area and found no corresponding flight. The phenomenon remained unidentified and the case was classified D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-01-00474" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGUEBLING (57) 07.01.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case is memorable because it involved apparent interaction: the light did not merely remain in the distance but seemed to pursue the rider. That impression, however, is also one of its evidential weaknesses. With a single moving witness and no confirmed distance to the light, a fixed or distant source can appear to maintain its position relative to an observer. The official page records the helicopter check but does not present radar data, photographs, physical traces or an independent second witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-01-00474" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGUEBLING (57) 07.01.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Guébling therefore deserves its unresolved status without being treated as strong proof of an unknown machine. The report contains genuine residual strangeness, particularly the low apparent altitude and silent pursuit, but the public file offers limited objective information with which to test alternative explanations decades later.

### Metz, 3 December 1979

The Metz case has a broader witness base. At approximately 4.50 pm, two people reported a triangular object producing two trails of red smoke. They said it stopped for several seconds while passing above a telecommunications tower and then resumed its movement. They followed it visually until it disappeared, with the red trails reportedly remaining visible. A third person saw a bright point at roughly the same time, although GEIPAN could not establish that this was the same phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-12-01703?field_is_revisited_value=1&amp;page=%2C33" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMETZ (57) 1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators found no matching air traffic, leaving the observation classified D. Its daylight or twilight timing, reported shape, smoke-like trails and multiple witnesses make it more substantial than a brief report of an isolated nocturnal light. The claimed halt above a prominent tower is the detail that most resists a simple aircraft explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-12-01703?field_is_revisited_value=1&amp;page=%2C33" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMETZ (57) 1979 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Even so, the evidence is not as convergent as a quick summary may suggest. The third witness described only a bright point, and GEIPAN explicitly states that the two observations could not be correlated. Nor does the public summary cite radar confirmation, photographs or independently measured movement. The absence of identified traffic weakens the ordinary-aircraft hypothesis, but it does not establish that no aircraft, atmospheric effect or misjudged trajectory was involved.

### Ars-sur-Moselle, 27 February 1990

A young couple driving near Ars-sur-Moselle reported a silent, round or oval object resembling a rugby ball, equipped with three powerful white lights. They estimated its diameter at roughly 2.5 to 3 metres and said it travelled slowly at very low altitude. After they followed it for about ten minutes, it allegedly departed at very high speed. GEIPAN’s investigation did not match the report to a known aircraft or another recognised phenomenon, and the case remains classified D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-02-01195" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANARS SUR MOSELLE (57) 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is arguably Moselle’s most vivid official unresolved account. It combines two witnesses, prolonged observation, apparent structure, low altitude, silence and an abrupt change in speed. Those features make it much harder to dismiss as a momentary glimpse of a star or satellite.

They do not, however, provide independently verified measurements. The estimated size assumes a distance that the witnesses could not objectively know. A bright object against a dark sky may appear closer, lower and more sharply defined than it is. Following the light by car also complicates judgements of motion: changes in road direction and relative position can create striking apparent accelerations or periods of hovering. GEIPAN itself regards such moving-observer effects as an important source of apparently extraordinary aircraft behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The case remains interesting because no specific conventional source was demonstrated, not because the reported dimensions or acceleration were technically confirmed. Its evidential foundation is still principally the couple’s testimony.

### Rémilly, 7 August 1993

Rémilly is also retained in the archive as a D case involving a phenomenon judged strange or very strange with medium-to-strong consistency. The online search record confirms its unexplained classification, but the readily accessible case listing provides less descriptive material than the public summaries for Guébling, Metz and Ars-sur-Moselle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1993-08-01318" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">REMILLY (57) 07.08.1993 &#124; GEIPANAugust 7, 1993 — Geipan Groupe d’études et d’informations sur les phénomènes aérospatiaux non ident...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 7, 1993</span></span></span>

That scarcity is itself significant. A classification may be visible while the evidence needed by an outside reader to evaluate it remains buried in scanned testimony, incompletely indexed or difficult to reconstruct. Rémilly should therefore be counted among Moselle’s official unresolved files, but it cannot responsibly be presented as equally well documented merely because it carries the same letter classification.

This illustrates a wider problem with historical archive comparisons. “D” is an investigative conclusion, not a standard measure showing that every case contains the same quantity or quality of evidence. Some files include several witness statements and explicit checks; others offer little more than a classification and a brief description.

## Why the solved cases are just as important

The unexplained cases make sense only when read beside the reports that GEIPAN has resolved. Moselle’s archive includes identifications involving aircraft, the International Space Station, planets, atmospheric re-entry, lanterns and balloons. These are not irrelevant false alarms. They demonstrate the recurring ways in which familiar phenomena acquire unfamiliar shapes, movements and apparent behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=106&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

In Kanfen in July 2020, for example, GEIPAN classified the reported phenomenon as an aircraft with the highest-confidence A rating. At Walscheid in July 2022, the observation was identified as the International Space Station. Moulins-lès-Metz’s April 2012 report was probably a balloon, while cases at Maizières-lès-Metz, Florange, Metz and Yutz were attributed or probably attributed to sky lanterns. Other Moselle entries include Venus at Hayes, Jupiter at Audun-le-Tiche and an atmospheric re-entry at Basse-Ham.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-07-51021" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These explanations cover several of the features that witnesses commonly find puzzling:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Apparent hovering:** an aircraft approaching nearly head-on may show little sideways motion.
* **Sudden disappearance:** an aircraft can turn away, enter cloud or switch the visible orientation of its lights.
* **Silent movement:** distance, wind direction and background noise can make an aircraft inaudible.
* **Formation or repeated lights:** lanterns, satellites or aircraft on similar routes can appear coordinated.
* **Unexpected brightness:** the International Space Station and some satellites can brighten dramatically in sunlight.
* **Object-like shape:** several lights may be mentally grouped into the outline of a triangle, oval or structured body.

</div>

GEIPAN’s own published methodology gives an example from outside Moselle in which a C-130 transport aircraft was described as a stationary saucer or enormous slow triangle. Radar data established the aircraft’s presence, while geometry, reflected moonlight and errors in judging angular speed explained why it looked so unlike an ordinary plane. The example is relevant to Moselle because it shows that a dramatic witness description and a conventional cause can coexist.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The contrast also prevents an evidential double standard. It would be unreasonable to accept witness estimates of extraordinary speed and proximity as exact in unresolved cases while disregarding comparable testimony whenever flight records or orbital calculations reveal an ordinary source. The same limits of perception apply in both groups.

## What the archive actually proves

The Moselle files support three firm conclusions.

First, the overwhelming lesson of the modern record is explanatory variety. Reports initially experienced as highly unusual have been connected to aircraft, planets, orbital objects, re-entering material, lanterns and balloons. No single explanation accounts for every sighting, but known phenomena account for a substantial and demonstrable share.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=106&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Second, a small number of observations retain meaningful unanswered questions. Guébling’s pursuing light, Metz’s triangular smoke-trailing phenomenon and the low-flying silent object reported at Ars-sur-Moselle were not matched to known causes in their official investigations. Their continued D status means that GEIPAN regarded both their strangeness and evidential consistency as sufficient to avoid placing them among poorly documented C reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-01-00474" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANGUEBLING (57) 07.01.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Third, none of these classifications establishes an exotic origin. The public files do not provide recovered material, calibrated imagery, confirmed radar tracks or physical traces demonstrating technology beyond known capabilities. The strongest evidence consists mainly of witness statements supported, in some cases, by checks that ruled out particular aircraft movements. Eliminating one hypothesis does not automatically validate a more extraordinary one.

GEIPAN itself states that unexplained cases require periodic re-evaluation and that classifications can change when better information or investigative tools become available. Nationally, it has re-examined older D files and explained many of them, while the proportion of recent cases left unexplained has fallen as methods and digital resources have improved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Moselle’s unresolved files are therefore best understood as open historical questions rather than a body of proof. They identify observations for which the surviving evidence has resisted a satisfactory conventional match. Their value lies in showing exactly where explanation stopped—and, equally importantly, how often it did not.
