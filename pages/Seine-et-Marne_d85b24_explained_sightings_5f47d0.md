## Introduction

Some of [Seine-et-Marne]({{ 'which-seine-et-marne-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }})’s most convincing UFO reports were not invented, exaggerated or made by obviously unreliable witnesses. People genuinely saw brilliant lights, apparent formations and objects that seemed to hover, accelerate or vanish. What changed was the identification: astronomical checks, orbital data, weather records, aviation context, photographs and video showed that several dramatic observations had familiar causes.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Seine-et-Marne_d85b24_explained_sightings_5f47d0-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Solved Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The clearest examples are strikingly varied. The immense “craft” crossing France on 5 November 1990 was debris from a Soviet rocket stage re-entering the atmosphere. A luminous object watched from Meaux by a resident and gendarmes was Venus. Orange lights over Courtry closely matched flying lanterns. Green discs filmed above Mitry-Mory were probably spots cast on cloud by a ground-based projector. These cases matter because they show that a sincere and detailed account can accurately preserve the appearance of an event while still misidentifying its distance, scale or nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The 1990 “giant craft” was falling rocket debris

The evening of 5 November 1990 produced one of the most spectacular UFO episodes ever recorded in France. At about 7pm, thousands of people saw a vast procession of lights travelling across a clear sky. Reports often described an enormous dark triangle or elongated vehicle, apparently moving silently at low altitude. Seine-et-Marne lay beneath the observed path, and GEIPAN’s national case file includes testimony from Lagny-sur-Marne among the reports gathered from across the country.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The scale and consistency of the reports initially made the incident seem unusually strong. Gendarmerie brigades contacted the Centre national d’études spatiales, or CNES, in large numbers. The former official investigation service SEPRA received calls for more than a week, while aviation, military, meteorological and orbital authorities were consulted. Even technically experienced observers found the display remarkable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The decisive evidence came from orbital tracking rather than from trying to judge the witnesses’ credibility. NASA identified an object associated with the Soviet Gorizont 21 launch as having re-entered on 5 November. CNES orbit calculations then confirmed a trajectory across France from the Bay of Biscay towards Alsace. The explanation was released to the press on 9 November, and contemporary reporting described the event as the disintegration of pieces of a Soviet rocket.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

What witnesses had seen was a cloud of incandescent debris from the third stage of a Proton rocket. GEIPAN estimates that the material crossed the upper atmosphere at roughly 50 to 100 kilometres altitude, slowing from about seven to two kilometres per second. Separate fragments produced multiple bright points and trails spread across tens of kilometres. Seen from the ground, those points appeared to travel together as parts of one structured object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This explains why the sighting could look like a solid triangular machine rather than a loose collection of debris. Human vision naturally groups lights that move in a coordinated way and may supply an apparent outline between them. With no visible surface, known dimensions or reliable distance cue, observers interpreted the dark space between the lights as the body of a craft. GEIPAN notes that many accounts consequently described a low-flying dark object fitted with several lights, even though the luminous fragments were extremely high and widely separated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The explanation fits several independent features at once: the time, direction and duration of the passage; the number of lights; the reconstructed orbital path; and the apparent size remaining comparatively stable as the phenomenon crossed the sky. That last detail is important. A nearby vehicle passing overhead should show a pronounced change in apparent size, whereas a very distant debris field may not. GEIPAN therefore classifies the event as category A, meaning securely identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The fact that some witnesses rejected the conclusion does not create contrary physical evidence. The experience felt close, structured and potentially threatening, while the explanation placed it tens of kilometres above them. That gap between lived impression and measured geometry is precisely what makes the event so instructive: the testimony conveyed how the display looked, but not how far away it was.

## Venus became a moving light over Meaux

On the morning of 19 December 1978, a witness in Meaux watched an unfamiliar bright point from a balcony for more than an hour. It appeared to oscillate in the clear south-eastern sky before becoming lost in the Sun’s glare. Gendarmes attended the apartment and also saw a luminous point in the reported direction, at an estimated elevation of about 35 degrees.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Police in Paris had investigated a similar bright object the previous day and concluded that it was astronomical. A celestial-position check for the Meaux observation placed Venus in the same south-eastern sector at roughly 25 to 30 degrees elevation. GEIPAN consequently classified the report as category A: a misidentification of Venus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The involvement of gendarmes strengthens the evidence that a light was present; it does not establish that the light was an unusual aircraft. This distinction is central to solved UFO cases. Multiple credible people can corroborate an observation while sharing the same mistaken interpretation because they are all viewing the same unfamiliar appearance under the same conditions.

Venus is especially effective at producing such reports because it is exceptionally bright and can remain visible as the surrounding sky grows lighter. Without nearby reference points, a small luminous source can also seem to drift, oscillate or change position. GEIPAN describes several perception effects that complicate such observations, including apparent motion in isolated lights and attempts to infer distance and speed from objects whose size is unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A related illusion occurs when an observer is travelling. A distant star or planet changes little in direction while nearby trees, buildings and road features move rapidly across the field of view. The light may therefore seem to follow the vehicle, stop when the vehicle stops or manoeuvre around landmarks. In another officially explained Venus case, GEIPAN called this the “following ball” illusion and showed how an initial underestimate of distance made the apparent pursuit seem physically real.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1980-04-00764?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B11%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=3&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Geipan(DPT) BAS-RHIN (67) 11.04.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Meaux file is valuable because the explanation does not depend on dismissing the witnesses’ account. The long duration, fixed general direction and astronomical position are the very details that identify the source. What appeared at first to support the presence of a hovering object instead became evidence for a planet.

## Lanterns can appear to stop and shoot away

At about 11pm on 29 August 2009, a group on a terrace in Courtry saw a red-orange light followed by roughly ten more. The lights travelled together, appeared to stop for around 30 seconds and then disappeared individually. They made no audible sound. Fifteen people reportedly watched, although only one submitted a formal account and photographs to GEIPAN several years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54205?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COURTRY (77) 29.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The description closely matched flying lanterns: warm orange colour, several lights released in succession, wind-driven movement and individual extinction as each flame went out. The photographs were also compatible with that explanation. Weather information indicating light gusts could account for changes in the formation’s apparent movement, including the period when the lights seemed to pause. The date—a summer weekend evening—also provided a plausible setting for a celebration. GEIPAN classified the case as category B, meaning that lanterns were the probable rather than conclusively demonstrated explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54205?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COURTRY (77) 29.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The most dramatic element was the apparent departure. The witness interpreted the abrupt loss of a light as acceleration away from Earth, arguing that no known technology could move so quickly. Yet a lantern does not need to accelerate to disappear instantly. Its flame may go out, become obscured by cloud or turn too faint to see. Because the observer cannot perceive its unlit fabric at night, extinction looks like an object crossing an enormous distance in a fraction of a second.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54205?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COURTRY (77) 29.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Lanterns also expose a common misunderstanding about silence. A distant, slow-moving object without an engine will naturally be inaudible. Silence therefore cannot by itself distinguish an exotic craft from a lantern, balloon, distant aircraft or high-altitude event.

The Courtry case was not solved by finding the person who launched the lights. It was assessed by comparing the complete pattern against known behaviour. That makes category B appropriately cautious: the explanation accounts for the colour, number, motion, setting, photographs and staggered disappearance, but the original lanterns were not recovered or independently traced.

## Projected lights turned clouds into a screen

A more recent report from Mitry-Mory shows how modern entertainment lighting can create apparently airborne objects. At 4.40am on 25 November 2024, a witness saw several green circular lights moving back and forth across an overcast sky. The phenomenon lasted about 30 seconds, and 11 seconds were recorded on video.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN found that the circles had the appearance of light spots projected onto a cloud base by a sky-tracer, a powerful moving projector used for displays or advertising. Their rapid movement in different directions was consistent with an automated lighting sequence. Weather data confirmed complete cloud cover, providing the surface on which the beam could become visible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This mechanism can be deceptive because the observer sees the illuminated patch rather than the beam’s source. The spot may appear to be an independent object moving within or above the cloud. Several projectors, or one instrument producing a programmed pattern, can create groups of discs that converge, separate and reverse direction without the constraints expected of aircraft.

The Mitry-Mory video increased the case’s evidential quality, but it did not make the phenomenon harder to explain. Instead, the recording preserved features—the diffuse green colour, circular form and sweeping paths—that favoured projection. GEIPAN gave the report good “consistency”, its term for the quantity and reliability of available information, while rating its strangeness comparatively low.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators could not identify the precise projector from publicly available information, so the case remained category B rather than A. This is an important limit. A strong probable explanation does not require pretending that every link in the chain has been proved. It means the known mechanism explains the observed characteristics substantially better than the alternatives presently supported by evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Familiar objects can form unfamiliar shapes

Other Seine-et-Marne cases show that the same transformation can occur with aircraft and even birds. Three white-orange lights seen forming an equilateral triangle over Noisiel in March 2009 included one blinking point and followed a route near the visual approach path to Lognes–Émerainville aerodrome. GEIPAN judged an approaching aircraft to be the probable source. Because the report was submitted ten months later, radar checks could no longer be undertaken.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/node/53932?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation_textuel&amp;page=%2C314&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NOISIEL (77) 13.03.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The triangular impression did not require a triangular vehicle. Three visible lights are enough for the observer to infer a single outline between them, particularly when they maintain roughly the same relative positions. The 1990 rocket re-entry created the same perceptual effect on a much larger scale: coordinated points became the lights of an imagined solid body.

At Émerainville in August 2017, a witness using binoculars described a stationary vertical cylinder with red-orange and white sections. GEIPAN found that a distant airliner could appear cigar-shaped when its wings were not reflecting sunlight towards the observer. Changing reflections could also make it visible for a time and then apparently vanish. Its “stationary” appearance was difficult to assess because a distant aircraft moving partly along the observer’s line of sight shows little sideways motion. The case was classified A as an aircraft observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-08-09706" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">EMERAINVILLE (77) 04.08.2017 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

In November 2021, two intense white lights over Vulaines-sur-Seine appeared almost motionless before fading. GEIPAN considered landing or search lights from aircraft, possibly military aircraft returning after a large exercise. A head-on aircraft can appear stationary because most of its movement is towards the observer rather than across the sky. When it turns, the narrow cone of its forward light moves away from the observer and the “object” fades, even though the aircraft remains present. Missing radar and route information prevented a definitive identification, so the result was category B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-11-51271?field_date_value=2004-04-23&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=7" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VULAINES-SUR-SEINE (77) 12.11.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Fontainebleau in October 2023, four brilliant points performed what the witness called a disorderly aerial ballet against a grey sky. GEIPAN’s probable explanation was a group of white egrets passing through a sunlit break between cloud layers. Their colour, erratic paths and sudden disappearance were consistent with birds entering and leaving the illuminated area; nearby wetlands also supported the presence of such birds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51489?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=24&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">FONTAINEBLEAU (77) 29.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These cases demonstrate that apparent shape is often an interpretation rather than a directly observed surface. A triangle may be three lamps, a cylinder may be a sunlit fuselage and dancing “stars” may be birds catching the light. The more limited the contrast and distance information, the more readily the brain completes an incomplete pattern.

## Why darkness makes distance unreliable

The recurring problem in these reports is not poor eyesight but missing scale. In daylight, distance can be estimated through perspective, surface detail, overlap, shadows and comparison with familiar objects. A small isolated light in a dark or cloudy sky supplies few of those clues. Unless its true size is known, its distance cannot be reliably calculated from its apparent size alone.

That uncertainty spreads into every later judgement. A witness who assumes that a light is a few hundred metres away will infer that it is relatively small, slow and perhaps hovering. The same angular movement, if produced tens of kilometres away, implies an enormous object travelling at great speed. An error in assumed distance therefore becomes an error in size, altitude and performance. GEIPAN explicitly lists the tendency to estimate distance and then derive speed for an unrecognised object among the main weaknesses of human aerial testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Brightness is also misleading. The 1990 debris field seemed nearby partly because it was powerful enough to dominate the sky. The mind treated brilliance as a cue to proximity, although the fragments were high in the atmosphere. At Courtry, the reverse occurred: the loss of brightness was interpreted as rapid movement away rather than the extinguishing of a flame.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Apparent motion can be equally unreliable. A point without a stable visual reference may seem to sway. A distant object moving towards the observer may look fixed. A projector spot can cross cloud almost instantly because no physical craft is travelling from one position to another; only the direction of the beam is changing. An aircraft light can vanish when its angle changes, while a bird can disappear the moment it enters shadow.

Emotion can then reinforce the first interpretation. GEIPAN describes strangeness and emotional reaction as mutually amplifying: once an observation is interpreted as an extraordinary machine, ordinary features such as silence, fading or formation movement may be re-read as evidence of advanced performance. Memory and later discussion can add further structure to an initially ambiguous sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What a convincing ordinary explanation requires

An explanation is strongest when it accounts for several independent details rather than merely naming something common. “It was probably an aircraft” is weak unless the proposed aircraft can match the direction, duration, lights and apparent behaviour. The solved and probably solved Seine-et-Marne cases succeeded because investigators tested mechanisms against the reports.

The most useful checks were:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Astronomical position:** Venus was present in the precise sector and elevation reported from Meaux.
* **Orbital reconstruction:** the Proton stage’s timing and track matched the national display of 5 November 1990.
* **Weather conditions:** wind supported the movement of lanterns at Courtry, while complete cloud cover supplied a screen for the Mitry-Mory projection.
* **Lighting behaviour:** blinking, fading, reflections and directional beams matched aircraft near Noisiel, Émerainville and Vulaines-sur-Seine.
* **Local environment:** an aerodrome approach path supported the Noisiel interpretation, while wetlands and known bird habitat strengthened the Fontainebleau explanation.
* **Images and video:** Courtry’s photographs and Mitry-Mory’s recording preserved characteristics that could be compared with known phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

</div>

GEIPAN distinguishes between category A cases, where an identification approaches proof, and category B cases, where one known explanation is highly probable but cannot be demonstrated completely. It also separates “strangeness” from “consistency”. A report can be well documented yet not especially mysterious, as at Mitry-Mory; another may sound highly unusual but contain too little reliable information to support either identification or an unexplained classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This prevents two opposite errors. The first is treating every unresolved case as evidence of an extraordinary vehicle. The second is declaring every report solved through an unsupported guess. In Seine-et-Marne, the ordinary explanations are persuasive where they reproduce the actual observation—not simply because rockets, planets, lanterns, projectors, aircraft and birds are familiar things.

## What the solved cases change

These explanations weaken the claim that dramatic appearance, numerous witnesses or official involvement necessarily indicate an extraordinary craft. The 1990 event had thousands of observers and generated calls from gendarmerie brigades, yet orbital evidence identified it. The Meaux light was seen by both a resident and attending officers, yet its position matched Venus. Fifteen people watched the Courtry formation, but group observation did not prevent a shared misidentification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

They do not show that every Seine-et-Marne report has been explained. The department also contains cases for which the published evidence has not produced a satisfactory identification. The lesson is narrower and more useful: a report should not be rated by drama alone. Its value depends on whether investigators can establish geometry, timing, environmental conditions, independent records and the reliability of the surviving documentation.

The solved sightings are therefore not peripheral failures to the department’s UFO history. They provide the comparisons needed to judge its unresolved cases. They show how an enormous craft can emerge from widely separated rocket fragments, how a planet can appear to manoeuvre, how a flame going out can resemble impossible acceleration, and how cloud can become a screen for apparently autonomous discs. Understanding those mechanisms makes the remaining mysteries smaller in number, but more clearly defined.
