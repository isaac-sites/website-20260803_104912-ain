## Introduction

The fluorescent green “UFO” reported at La Roche-Chalais in northern [Dordogne]({{ 'why-dordognes-ufo-mysteries-often-faded/' | relative_url }}) on 25 June 2019 was almost certainly a military aircraft. A witness awakened by an unfamiliar droning sound saw a low, slow-moving oval of green light, with smaller red lights behind it, crossing the night sky towards the south-west. The appearance was extraordinary, but the timing, route, sound and lighting all matched a rare American CV-22 Osprey taking part in the international military exercise Athéna 2019. France’s official UFO investigation service, GEIPAN, ultimately classified the case as identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. The case matters within Dordogne’s UFO history because it demonstrates how a sincere and apparently detailed observation can be transformed by unfamiliar aviation technology. The witnesses did not simply mistake an ordinary aeroplane light for something exotic. They encountered an unusual tiltrotor aircraft whose illuminated spinning blades can resemble glowing discs, operating after midnight on a route that local residents would have had little reason to expect.

## What witnesses saw at La Roche-Chalais

At approximately 12.30 am, a resident in a hamlet south-east of La Roche-Chalais was awakened by a buzzing or droning noise outside her open bedroom window. She initially considered familiar possibilities, including the local medical helicopter, but the object she then saw did not look or sound like the helicopters she knew. She described a fluorescent green oval, apparently flying at low altitude and modest speed, with several weaker red signals towards the rear. The passage lasted about a minute before the object disappeared towards the south-west. Her partner also came to the window, although only one full witness statement entered the official file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. Several details made the experience compelling. It involved both light and sound, continued long enough for the primary witness to wake another person, and seemed to show a structured object rather than a distant point. The estimated size was about six metres, although size and altitude are notoriously difficult to judge at night without a known reference. No photograph was taken, and the investigation recorded no direct radar trace linking a particular aircraft to the house. Those limitations matter: the identification rests on a strong convergence of circumstances rather than on an individually confirmed flight track.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. The witness’s interpretation was also understandable. A green oval accompanied by an unfamiliar mechanical hum does not resemble the everyday appearance of a passenger aircraft. Even the suggestion of a conventional helicopter seemed inadequate to her because the sound, shape and colour were unlike anything she had previously encountered. GEIPAN did not treat that reaction as foolish or dishonest. Its investigation instead asked what real aircraft present in the region could produce precisely those unusual features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. ## How the CV-22 created green discs

The CV-22 Osprey is neither a conventional helicopter nor an ordinary fixed-wing aeroplane. It is a tiltrotor: its two large engine-and-rotor assemblies can point upwards for vertical take-off and hovering, then rotate forwards for faster aeroplane-like flight. The United States Air Force uses the CV-22 for long-range special-forces transport, including low-level operations by day or night. Its hybrid configuration gives it a silhouette, motion and acoustic character unfamiliar to most members of the public.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.afsoc.af.mil/About-Us/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/2742374/cv-22-osprey/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: afsoc.af.mil">[Air Force Special Operations Command]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">afsoc.af.mil</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Air Force Special Operations Command CV-22 Osprey</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Air Force Special Operations CommandCV-22 Osprey - AFSOC - Air ForceThe CV-22 Osprey is a tiltrotor aircraft that combines the vertical t...</span></span></span>

The decisive feature at La Roche-Chalais was the lighting fitted near the rotor-blade tips. CV-22 blades have controllable, night-vision-compatible tip lights intended to help crews and ground personnel see the rotor area during darkness. As each illuminated point spins rapidly, persistence of vision makes its circular path appear continuous. Instead of seeing individual green lamps, an observer can therefore perceive a bright ring or “virtual disc”. Two rotors produce two such discs, but their apparent separation and shape vary with viewing angle, distance, darkness and the visibility of the aircraft body. From some directions they can overlap visually and resemble a single oval.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://theaviationist.com/2013/08/16/cv-22-flying-saucer/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: theaviationist.com">[The Aviationist]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">theaviationist.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">The AviationistThis is not a flying saucer but a CV-22 tilt-rotor aircraft at nightAugust 16, 2013 — 16 Aug 2013 — Once the rotor is work...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 16, 2013</span></span></span>

This mechanism accounts for the most distinctive element of the report without forcing the testimony into the shape of an ordinary helicopter. The witness saw fluorescent green light because the rotor-tip lights were green. She perceived an oval because rotating points of light generated luminous rotor circles viewed obliquely. The weaker red lights behind the green area were consistent with aircraft anti-collision or navigation lighting visible near the tail. GEIPAN noted that a red anti-collision light on the aircraft’s rear section would have been on the side presented to the witnesses during the reported right-to-left passage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. The sound fits as well. An Osprey’s two large prop-rotors create a powerful combination of blade noise, low-frequency vibration and displaced air. To a person familiar only with common civilian helicopters, it may register as a strange mixture of buzzing, breath-like airflow and propulsion noise rather than as an immediately recognisable aircraft. ## Why the military exercise solved the case

An unusual aircraft is only a useful explanation if there is evidence that one was operating in the right region at the right time. In this case, the observation coincided with Athéna 2019, an international special-forces aviation exercise centred on Mont-de-Marsan air base. An official French aeronautical notice created temporary restricted and danger zones for the exercise between 17 and 27 June. It warned of numerous French and foreign military aircraft operating at different heights and under varied flight conditions across a wide area, including sectors around Angoulême and Dignac north of Dordogne.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.sia.aviation-civile.gouv.fr/pub/media/store/documents/file/l/f/lf_sup_2019_147_fr.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: sia.aviation-civile.gouv.fr">[SIA]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">sia.aviation-civile.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">SIASIA Aviation Civile</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SIASIA Aviation Civile</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: May 27, 2019</span></span></span>

The relevant zones were active late on 24 June and into the early hours of 25 June. GEIPAN established that operations were taking place around Angoulême and Dignac during the period surrounding the La Roche-Chalais sighting. Aircraft involved in Athéna reportedly included three CV-22 Ospreys alongside French helicopters, transport aircraft, drones and fighters. ## How strong is the identification?

GEIPAN classified the case as **A**, its category for an identified phenomenon. Its assessment gave substantial weight to the exact match between the green colour and the Osprey’s blade-tip lighting, the compatible low-level speed and size impression, the straight trajectory, the south-westerly direction and the documented military activity. The official report regarded the case’s remaining strangeness as low once those factors were combined.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. ## What the case changes in Dordogne’s UFO history

La Roche-Chalais is best understood as a successfully resolved UFO report, not as a trivial sighting that should never have been filed. At the moment of observation, the phenomenon was genuinely unidentified to the witnesses. Its unusual appearance arose from the interaction of real technology, darkness, viewing angle and incomplete public knowledge about a military operation.

The case also warns against relying on visual shape alone. At night, the visible “object” may be formed mainly by moving lights rather than by the physical outline of the aircraft. Two rotating illuminated blade paths can become an oval or pair of discs; a dark fuselage can disappear; and small red aircraft lights may seem detached from the main green form. What appears to be the body of a craft may therefore be an optical pattern generated around it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete579.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">February 2, 2021 — 9 Oct 2019 — domicile de La Roche-Chalais. Military exercises add a second layer of confusion. They place uncommon aircraft over rural areas, sometimes at low altitude and outside familiar daytime traffic patterns. Special-forces training is also not always widely advertised to residents, even when formal aviation notices have been issued. In the Athéna case, a mayor knew of the activity but had been asked not to disclose it beforehand, leaving many local observers without the contextual information needed to recognise what they were seeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.charentelibre.fr/charente/dirac/survol-d-helicopteres-au-dessus-de-dirac-il-s-agissait-d-une-manoeuvre-militaire-internationale-6052959.php" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: charentelibre.fr">[Charente Libre]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">charentelibre.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Charente Libre Survol d&#x27;hélicoptères au-dessus de Dirac: il s&#x27;agissait d</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Charente Libre Survol d&#x27;hélicoptères au-dessus de Dirac: il s&#x27;agissait d</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 25, 2019</span></span></span>

For investigations elsewhere in Dordogne, the practical lesson is clear. Reports involving structured lights, rotor-like noise or low-level night movement should be compared not only with ordinary civilian traffic but also with temporary restricted zones, military exercise notices, airbase routes and sightings from neighbouring departments. The La Roche-Chalais explanation became convincing because investigators connected a witness account in Dordogne with aviation documentation and contemporary reports from Charente.

It therefore strengthens a broader evidence-led reading of the department’s UFO record. An initially spectacular description can survive largely intact—the green oval, the red lights, the strange sound and the slow passage—while its interpretation changes completely. Later investigation did not show that the witness imagined the experience. It showed that an unfamiliar CV-22 Osprey could look remarkably like the kind of luminous craft people have learned to call a UFO.
