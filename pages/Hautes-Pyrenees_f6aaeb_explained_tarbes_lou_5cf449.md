## Introduction

Several of the most dramatic modern UFO reports around Tarbes and Lourdes appear to have been produced by ordinary objects seen under unusually misleading conditions. A silent triangle over Tarbes was probably a flock of migrating birds reflecting city light. Bright “moving” objects near Lourdes matched Venus, while a rapidly crossing orange ball was consistent with a meteor. These explanations do not imply that witnesses invented what they saw. They show how darkness, uncertain distance and brief viewing times can turn familiar phenomena into convincing structured craft.

The cases matter within the UFO history of Hautes-Pyrénées because investigators could test them against astronomy, weather, migration patterns, photographs and viewing directions. The result is a useful contrast with reports that remain unidentified only because evidence is missing. Here, later investigation generally weakened the extraordinary interpretations while explaining why the original experiences felt so persuasive.

## The Tarbes triangle was probably a formation of birds

On 6 November 2018, a woman walking her dog in Tarbes reported seeing what appeared to be a small, dark triangular craft. Five faint white lights formed a V. The phenomenon moved slowly and silently, apparently passed above her, then seemed to travel between buildings in the Martinet district. The description initially sounds more difficult to explain than a simple distant light. The witness did not merely report luminous points; she believed she could see a black surface connecting them. She also rejected an aircraft or drone because there was no engine noise, no flashing navigation lights and no obvious mechanical movement. Yet those same details led GEIPAN towards an ornithological explanation rather than an exotic vehicle.

A flock of large birds flying in a V can create the impression of a single triangular body. Against a dark sky, the observer may see only pale undersides or patches of plumage catching illumination from streets below. The eye then joins those separate points into one coherent outline. The five dim, slightly blurred lights described at Tarbes were consistent with intermittent reflection from birds rather than fixed lamps mounted on a craft. Silence and relatively slow apparent movement also fitted a flock better than a low-flying aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete577.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 4, 2021 — Le PAN est décrit comme un triangle noir avec 5 lumières blanches disposées en forme de V.... The date strengthened that interpretation. Early November falls within the autumn migration period for several large species, including cranes and geese. Hautes-Pyrénées lies close to major routes towards the Iberian Peninsula, and cranes regularly gather at the Puydarrieux reservoir before crossing or moving along the Pyrenean corridor. Local bird organisations describe crane passage in the department during October and November, with the strongest regional movement often occurring in the first two-thirds of November.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lpo.fr/lpo-locales/la-lpo-en-nouvelle-aquitaine/lpo-aquitaine/actus-aquitaine/actu-2023-aquitaine/organbidexka-un-bilan-en-demi-teinte" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lpo.fr">[lpo.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lpo.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Organbidexka: un bilan en demi-teinte!</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">LPO (Ligue pour la Protection des Oiseaux) - Agir pour la biodiversitéDecember 7, 2023 — Du 15 juillet au 15 novembre, notre équipe a étu...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: December 7, 2023</span></span></span>

No flock was independently recorded at the precise place and minute of the sighting, and no photograph captured the formation. ### Why the triangle appeared low and solid

The witness estimated that the phenomenon was only about five storeys high and believed it passed between two buildings. GEIPAN did not treat that distance estimate as a reliable measurement. At night, an observer viewing an unfamiliar silhouette has no dependable way to separate a small nearby object from a much larger distant one unless there are clear reference points at the same distance.

The buildings provided an apparent frame, but not necessarily a true intersection with the flight path. A more distant formation could have disappeared behind roofs or entered a restricted line of sight, creating the impression that it had travelled through the gap. Once the lights were mentally grouped as parts of one object, the dark space between them could be perceived as a physical surface.

There were also uncertainties in the reconstruction. The account reached GEIPAN in September 2019, nearly a year after the stated event. The witness said she had made notes, which supports the recorded date, but some remembered conditions did not match available records. She recalled a clear, starry sky; meteorological data from Tarbes–Lourdes–Pyrénées indicated considerable cloud, fog around 23:00 and visibility of only about 800 metres before conditions improved later. GEIPAN noted that either the date, the recalled weather or both might have become imprecise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete577.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 4, 2021 — Le PAN est décrit comme un triangle noir avec 5 lumières blanches disposées en forme de V.... This discrepancy does not discredit the witness. It demonstrates why delayed testimony is difficult to use for exact geometry. People can retain the central image of an event while becoming less accurate about time, cloud cover, direction or duration. In this case, the remembered “black triangle” remained vivid, but the details needed to calculate its true height and route were much less secure.

## Venus created moving lights near Lourdes

A report made from the D940 while travelling from Lamarque-Pontacq towards Lourdes on 13 February 2011 involved a luminous ball close to the horizon. The motorist perceived movement and changes of colour, features that can make a planet seem unlike an astronomical object. GEIPAN nevertheless classified the case as a confirmed sighting of Venus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-02-08605" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Venus is especially effective at producing UFO reports because it can be far brighter than any star. Near the horizon, its light passes through a thicker and more turbulent layer of atmosphere. Refraction and variations in air density can make it shimmer, change colour or appear to jump slightly. Trees, bends in the road and the motion of a car can add apparent lateral movement. A stationary planet may therefore seem to pace the vehicle, descend towards the landscape or change position whenever foreground objects interrupt the view.

The Lourdes road case is instructive because the witness was not simply looking at a fixed light from a stationary position. Movement of the observer changed the relationship between Venus and nearby landscape features. This parallax is negligible for Venus itself, which is effectively at an infinite distance for everyday viewing, but substantial for trees, ridges, buildings and roadside objects. The background light consequently appears to shift relative to the foreground.

A second Lourdes report, covering observations on 22 and 23 September 2013, combined two different astronomical phenomena. On the first evening, a bright orange or luminous object remained visible in a direction matching Venus. GEIPAN calculated that the witness’s estimated bearing was approximately 241 degrees, while Venus lay at about 235 degrees. Photographs were also consistent with a bright point source, and a weaker light above and to the right corresponded to Saturn.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08606" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The close positional match is stronger evidence than resemblance alone. A bright UFO description might fit several possible causes, but when the reported direction, time and arrangement of neighbouring lights reproduce the actual sky, the planetary explanation becomes difficult to avoid. GEIPAN classified the overall case as category A, meaning that the reported phenomena were identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08606" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Local reporting has produced similar rapid resolutions. In December 2018, a bright object photographed over Hautes-Pyrénées was initially presented as mysterious before being identified as Venus. Such episodes illustrate how a familiar planet can generate renewed attention whenever it is unusually conspicuous, particularly when a photograph lacks wider landscape or sky context.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ladepeche.fr/article/2018/12/24/2930385-ovni-ciel-hautes-pyrenees-temoin-raconte.html" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ladepeche.fr">[ladepeche.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ladepeche.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2930385 ovni ciel hautes pyrenees temoin raconte</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">2930385 ovni ciel hautes pyrenees temoin raconte</span></span></span>

## A meteor turned a second Lourdes sighting into a fast-moving craft

The same witness who observed Venus near Lourdes in September 2013 reported a very different event the following evening: an orange ball crossed the sky rapidly and disappeared within seconds. GEIPAN considered this consistent with a bolide, the term used for an exceptionally bright meteor produced when a larger fragment of natural material enters the atmosphere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08606" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This distinction matters because witness accounts often group successive strange lights into a single narrative. In reality, the first Lourdes phenomenon behaved like a distant planet: bright, persistent and fixed in the astronomical sky. The second behaved like a meteor: sudden, fast and short-lived. The fact that both looked orange did not indicate that they were connected or controlled by the same object.

Bright meteors are frequently misjudged as nearby. They may illuminate clouds or landscape, fragment into several points and leave a glowing trail. Because there is no familiar size reference, a witness can interpret a phenomenon tens of kilometres high as a low object crossing a neighbouring valley. GEIPAN notes in other investigated French cases that observers commonly underestimate the altitude and distance of atmospheric entries, even when the same event is visible across several regions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-02-09100" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Lourdes case page carries a database label referring to a bolide, while its written conclusion identifies both Venus and a bolide. That apparent inconsistency reflects the combined nature of the report rather than uncertainty about a single object: two observations on consecutive evenings required two separate explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08606" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary objects can feel extraordinary

The Tarbes and Lourdes cases involved different sources, but the misperceptions followed a common pattern. The observer saw something genuine; the error arose when the mind attempted to infer its distance, size and structure from incomplete visual information.

**Darkness removes scale.** A light in a black sky carries almost no direct information about its distance. Venus can be interpreted as a nearby hovering lamp, while a high meteor can appear to pass just beyond a ridge. A flock of birds may seem enormous or very low because the observer cannot see the individual bodies clearly.

**The visual system constructs shapes.** Five lights arranged in a V are not experienced merely as five unrelated points. The brain favours a single, stable object and may fill in the unlit area between them. That process helps people recognise everyday objects quickly, but it can also transform a loose formation into an apparent solid triangle.

**Movement is judged relative to the foreground.** A bright planet seen from a moving car appears to change position against trees, buildings and bends in the road. A distant light that remains visible beside the vehicle can even seem to follow it. Conversely, a flock crossing above an urban area may appear to fly between buildings because roofs and walls repeatedly block the observer’s view.

**Brief events encourage rough reconstruction.** A ten-second observation leaves little opportunity to test competing interpretations. The witness must attend to shape, sound, direction and possible danger at once. Estimates of duration, angular size and altitude are commonly made afterwards rather than measured during the event.

**Memory preserves meaning better than geometry.** The emotional core — “a silent triangle passed over me” — may remain strong even when the exact date, weather or direction becomes uncertain. The year-long delay in the Tarbes report limited checks of air traffic, satellite passages and local bird movements, while the discrepancy over cloud cover showed how peripheral details can change without any deliberate exaggeration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete577.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCompte rendu d&#x27;enquête</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">May 4, 2021 — Le PAN est décrit comme un triangle noir avec 5 lumières blanches disposées en forme de V.... ## What the explanations establish — and what they do not

The Tarbes bird explanation is persuasive but probabilistic. It accounts for the V shape, faint reflected lights, silence, slow movement and November timing, yet no direct record proves that a particular flock passed over the neighbourhood. The correct conclusion is therefore “probably birds”, not “birds demonstrated beyond doubt”. GEIPAN’s category B classification preserves that distinction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-11-50827" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">L’hypothèse d’une méprise avec des oiseaux migrateurs est renforcée par le fait que l’observation ait eu lieu début novembre, c’est-à-dir...</span></span></span>

The Venus cases are considerably firmer. In the 2013 Lourdes observation, the calculated direction agreed closely with the planet’s position, the images were compatible with a point-like astronomical source and the neighbouring light matched Saturn. The D940 report likewise fitted the familiar pattern of a bright planet apparently moving and changing colour near the horizon. These were classified as identified category A cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08606" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The meteor identification was based mainly on behaviour rather than recovery of physical material: a bright orange ball moving rapidly for a few seconds is characteristic of a bolide. That is a strong conventional explanation, although the surviving public account contains less independent evidence than the Venus reconstruction.

Taken together, these reports show why the explained cases form an important part of Hautes-Pyrénées UFO history. They reveal that a sighting can be sincere, vivid and highly structured without requiring an unusual aircraft. They also demonstrate the value of reporting promptly. Precise time, direction, photographs with a wide field of view and immediate checks of weather or astronomical conditions can turn an extraordinary memory into a testable event. Where those details survive, the skies over Tarbes and Lourdes have often proved less mysterious than they first appeared.
