## What Essonne’s case record actually shows

GEIPAN is a technical unit within the French National Centre for Space Studies, or CNES. It collects witness statements, conducts investigations, publishes anonymised files and classifies reports according to how strange the event remains after analysis and how reliable the available information is. Category A means an identification is regarded as secure; B means a probable identification; C means the evidence is inadequate or unusable; and D is reserved for phenomena that remain unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

That distinction matters in Essonne because an unresolved report is not automatically a strong report. A case can remain open simply because there was one witness, no photograph, no precise bearing, a delayed statement or no opportunity to inspect the location promptly. Conversely, a dramatic-looking account can receive an A or B classification when its direction, timing and movement correspond closely to an astronomical body or known local activity.

## Saint-Hilaire, 1981: the department’s most ambiguous encounter

During the night of 3–4 October 1981, a couple driving near the “cabane à Pierrot” crossroads at Saint-Hilaire noticed flashes in a field, followed by three red lights apparently moving towards them. They heard no sound and left quickly because they were frightened. The gendarmerie was informed several days later and found no physical traces when officers visited the site. Only one combined witness account was formally recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1981-10-01678" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-HILAIRE (91) 04.10.1981 &#124; GEIPANClassification. Comprendre un phénomène... Essonne. Classification. C. Date de mise a jour...</span></span></span>

The case was originally classified as D, suggesting that it remained unexplained. A later review changed it to C. The reason was not that investigators had discovered a definitive conventional source, but that the phenomenon appeared to be on or near the ground. Nothing established that it had arrived by air, departed through the air or was an airborne object at all. GEIPAN concluded that identifying unexplained activity on private ground, without evidence of an aerospace component, fell outside its proper remit.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1981-10-01678" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-HILAIRE (91) 04.10.1981 &#124; GEIPANClassification. Comprendre un phénomène... Essonne. Classification. C. Date de mise a jour...</span></span></span>

Investigators nevertheless considered vehicle-based explanations. Changes between white flashes and red lights could have resulted from a vehicle turning, exposing different lamps to the witnesses. Farm machinery, motorcycles or an unauthorised vehicle entering the field were all possible, although activity at about 2.30 a.m. on a Sunday appeared unusual. No one interviewed the owner or occupier of the land, leaving an obvious investigative gap.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1981-10-01678" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-HILAIRE (91) 04.10.1981 &#124; GEIPANClassification. Comprendre un phénomène... Essonne. Classification. C. Date de mise a jour...</span></span></span>

The description that the lights were coming closer is also less decisive than it first sounds. A light that becomes brighter as it turns towards an observer can create the impression of forward movement even if its actual position changes little. Without angular measurements, a second independent statement, ground traces or a clear account of how the lights disappeared, the direction and distance cannot be reconstructed reliably.

Saint-Hilaire is therefore neither convincingly explained nor a strong unexplained UFO case. Its present C classification is a judgement about evidential limits. The event may have been genuinely puzzling to the witnesses, but the surviving record cannot establish whether it involved a vehicle, people in the field, an unusual lighting arrangement or something else.

### Briis-sous-Forges to Limours, 2026

A particularly clear modern example occurred on 1 January 2026. A motorist travelling between Briis-sous-Forges and Limours saw a yellow-orange sphere low ahead, apparently descending rapidly and seeming to accompany the moving car before disappearing near the horizon. This case is important because it involved more than simply naming a bright object. The analysis addressed the precise feature that made the sighting feel anomalous: apparent movement coordinated with the witness’s car.

### Gif-sur-Yvette and the second Moon

At Gif-sur-Yvette on 5 December 2003, a witness briefly saw a white oval light close to the Moon, similar to it in colour and intensity and nearly comparable in apparent size. The observation lasted only around 15 to 20 seconds and was not reported to GEIPAN until 2014.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2003-12-08708?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=59&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Investigators judged a paraselene, commonly called a moon dog, to be the probable explanation. This is an atmospheric optical effect in which moonlight interacting with ice crystals produces a bright patch beside the Moon. The B classification reflects uncertainty rather than a perfect reconstruction: the report was made roughly a decade after the event, there was no photograph, and the remembered position of the Moon did not agree fully with astronomical calculations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2003-12-08708?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=59&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The delay is central to the assessment. Memories of a striking experience may remain vivid while details such as cloud cover, direction and the Moon’s location shift over time. Later testimony did not strengthen the case; it provided enough material for a plausible explanation while also exposing significant memory-related weaknesses.

## Orange lights, lanterns and illuminated balloons

Orange or red lights moving silently across the night sky form one of the most persistent patterns in modern UFO reporting. In Essonne, several such cases occurred during periods when inexpensive sky lanterns and light-emitting balloons had become widely available.

At Évry on 12 May 2014, a mother and daughter watched an orange, fire-like mass moving steadily towards the north-east. It kept roughly the same apparent height and disappeared behind trees after about a minute. Its colour, duration and wind-compatible direction led GEIPAN to classify it B as a probable sky lantern, although investigators noted that the reported radiating shape and the fact that it occurred on a Monday were less typical. An aircraft approaching Orly was retained as another possibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-05-08695" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANEVRY (91) 12.05.2014EVRY (91) 12.05.2014. The case also illustrates the limited value of failed night photography. The witness attempted to photograph the light using a Nintendo DS, but the image showed nothing useful. GEIPAN considered that unsurprising because the device’s small camera was poorly suited to dim nocturnal subjects. An unsuccessful photograph neither confirms that the phenomenon was extraordinary nor proves that nothing was present.

On Christmas Eve 2015, three people in a high-rise flat at Évry saw a silent light changing between green, blue and orange as it passed overhead and disappeared behind the building. The direction matched the reported south-westerly wind, while the changing colours and festive date supported the interpretation of an LED balloon. GEIPAN classified the report B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-12-09391?field_date_value=2007-03-01&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=22" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A similar pattern appeared at Palaiseau on 17 February 2022, where a rapidly observed luminous ball was judged probably to have been an LED balloon. The evidence consisted of one witness, no usable image and a very short observation, so the conclusion remained probable rather than certain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-02-51310" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At Vigneux-sur-Seine on 30 September 2023, a witness reported a red-orange oval that looked as though it were burning, moved silently for about 30 seconds and drifted towards the north-east. GEIPAN found that the observed movement matched the wind and that the colour, persistence and lack of sound were characteristic of a sky lantern. The case was classified A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-09-51491" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Taken together, these reports do not establish a formal “flap” centred on one night or locality. They show a recurring class of sightings generated by slow, lightweight objects whose flames or LEDs look conspicuous against a dark sky. Their distance is difficult to judge, so a small lantern can be perceived as a large object travelling at considerable altitude.

## The Saclay plateau creates its own unusual lights

Northern Essonne contains one of France’s densest concentrations of scientific and technical institutions. That setting can add credibility to UFO stories—particularly when witnesses associate unusual lights with research centres, restricted facilities or aerospace work—but it can also supply direct conventional explanations.

The clearest example occurred at Villejust on 29 August 2017. Several witnesses watched a vertical green streak repeatedly appearing through cloud for about an hour. Photographs later seemed to contain additional white or blue “spheres”. GEIPAN identified the main light as the IPRAL atmospheric-research laser operated by the SIRTA observatory at École Polytechnique. The observatory confirmed that the laser was operating at the relevant time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-08-50421?page=26%2C16&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANVILLEJUST (91) 29.08.2017 &#124; GEIPANClassification. Their relative positions did not change across photographs taken several minutes apart, and doubled points could be explained by camera movement during exposure. The case received an A classification because investigators could match the direction, colour and behaviour of the beam with a known local instrument and independently confirm its use.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2017-08-50421?page=26%2C16&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANVILLEJUST (91) 29.08.2017 &#124; GEIPANClassification. This is a useful warning against treating proximity to laboratories or military-linked research as evidence of secret craft. In this case, the unusual light really was connected with an advanced scientific facility, but not in the way a more sensational account might suggest. It was a publicly understood atmospheric measurement system whose beam became visible where it intersected cloud.

## Orly, aircraft and the problem of viewing angle

Essonne lies directly south of Paris and partly beneath the wider air-traffic environment associated with Paris-Orly Airport. Aircraft are therefore an obvious hypothesis for moving lights, but “it was probably a plane” is not sufficient unless the reported appearance can also be explained.

An approaching aircraft can look stationary when flying nearly towards an observer. Its landing lights may dominate while its wings and navigation lights remain difficult to see. As the viewing angle changes, the apparent object can suddenly move sideways or vanish. GEIPAN’s analysis of the May 2014 Évry report retained this as a secondary possibility because an aircraft approaching Orly from the relevant direction could have presented a large frontal light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-05-08695" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANEVRY (91) 12.05.2014EVRY (91) 12.05.2014. The general investigative principle is illustrated by GEIPAN’s national work with military and civil aviation data. The unit can consult radar information in suitable cases and stresses that an identification must account for the reported strangeness, not merely place an aircraft nearby. Perspective effects, reflected moonlight, obscured wings and misjudged angular speed can transform a recognisable aircraft into a seemingly stationary saucer or slow triangle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

No major published Essonne case in the evidence reviewed here depends on confirmed radar returns tracking an unknown object. Nor is there a strong departmental case based on simultaneous reports from a pilot, air-traffic controller and independent ground witnesses. Aviation is important chiefly as a recurring source of possible misidentification and as a source of records that could, in a better-documented event, test a witness account.

## Fixed lights mistaken for objects in the sky

A long observation does not necessarily make a case stronger. At Évry on 7 August 2015, a witness watched four brilliant silver lights arranged in two horizontal pairs from about 3 a.m. until 4.30 a.m. They appeared fixed and were seen through foliage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-08-09275" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANEVRY (91) 07.08.2015EVRY (91) 07.08.2015. Further discussion established that a sports ground lay behind the trees. GEIPAN judged that the lights were probably ground-based illumination, perhaps associated with overnight work or lamps left on unusually late. The branches helped conceal the surrounding landscape and prevented the witness from recognising the source as part of a larger installation. The report was classified B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-08-09275" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANEVRY (91) 07.08.2015EVRY (91) 07.08.2015. This case reverses a common assumption. Ninety minutes of visibility sounds more impressive than a ten-second flash, but a phenomenon that remains perfectly fixed for a long time may be easier to reconcile with a terrestrial lamp. Duration improves evidence only when accompanied by useful observation: changes in direction, reliable bearings, comparison with landmarks, photographs or independent witnesses.

## How official investigations change the story

Essonne’s case history is especially useful for showing that official classification is not fixed permanently. Older reports can be re-examined using planetarium software, improved maps, weather archives and a better understanding of perception. The Épinay-sous-Sénart case moved towards a secure Venus identification, while Saint-Hilaire moved away from an unexplained classification because investigators recognised that the record did not establish an aerial phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete50.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete50</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ce courrier est réceptionné en date... ## Is there a genuine Essonne UFO hotspot?

The available official cases do not demonstrate that Essonne experiences an exceptional concentration of unexplained aerial phenomena. What the department does possess is an unusually varied observational environment: dense suburbs, rural southern landscapes, major roads, nearby air traffic, celebration lights and the scientific infrastructure of the Saclay plateau.

Those features create many opportunities for ambiguous sightings. A low Moon may appear to pursue a car on a country road; Venus can acquire apparent structure through binoculars; a lantern can cross an urban skyline without sound; sports lighting may seem suspended behind trees; and a research laser can produce a vivid green column in cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete50.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Notes d&#x27;enquete50</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Ce courrier est réceptionné en date... No clear aerial phase was observed, the landowner was not questioned, no traces were found and only one formal account survived. Its status is best described as indeterminate, not as a confirmed encounter with an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1981-10-01678" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSAINT-HILAIRE (91) 04.10.1981 &#124; GEIPANClassification. Essonne’s UFO history is therefore significant mainly as a compact study in evidential sorting. The department has produced memorable reports and sincere witness reactions, but later analysis has generally weakened extraordinary interpretations. The official record leaves room for uncertainty in individual incidents while offering no persuasive departmental pattern of technologically anomalous vehicles, military incursions or repeated unexplained radar events.
