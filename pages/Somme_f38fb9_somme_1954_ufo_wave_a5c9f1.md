## Introduction

Autumn 1954 gave [Somme]({{ 'why-sommes-ufo-record-still-resists-easy/' | relative_url }}) its most important concentration of unidentified aerial reports, but the wave was not a single coherent mystery. It mixed rumours, newspaper jokes, mistaken balloons and poorly documented lights with two substantially stronger incidents: the daylight Harponville–Contay encounter of 7 September and the military observation at Doullens on 19 October. Both are still classified as unexplained by GEIPAN, the French government’s specialist unit for unidentified aerospace phenomena.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

What made these two cases different was not simply that witnesses found them strange. Harponville–Contay involved two men watching a structured object at relatively close range for several minutes in clear daylight, followed by a prompt gendarmerie investigation. Doullens involved several servicemen at a radar station who independently observed a coloured phenomenon beneath cloud cover. Neither case proves that an extraordinary craft visited Somme. Both nevertheless contain enough detail and witness consistency to resist the easy explanations that account for weaker reports from the same period.

## How the national wave reached Somme

France experienced an exceptional surge of flying-saucer stories during September and October 1954. Reports of discs, luminous balls, cigar-shaped objects and alleged landings appeared almost daily in the press. Somme became part of that national atmosphere very early: the Harponville–Contay incident occurred on 7 September, before many of the country’s most widely publicised cases and before the wave reached its greatest intensity. Contemporary newspapers were reporting the Somme encounter by 9 September.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The timing matters. Harponville–Contay cannot easily be dismissed as a late imitation produced after weeks of overwhelming local publicity. Flying-saucer ideas were already familiar in France, but this particular report preceded the most intense phase of the national wave. That strengthens the case against a simple explanation based on witnesses copying the detailed form of a recently celebrated incident.

It does not remove the possibility of expectation, exaggeration or misidentification. Newspaper coverage was already shaping how unusual objects were described, and once the Harponville story appeared it helped stimulate further rumours within Somme. On the following evening, claims circulated that another saucer had landed near Estrées-Deniécourt and Foucaucourt-en-Santerre. Reports spoke of numerous observers and even a man who had touched the object, but an official inquiry encountered reluctant or disappearing witnesses. Later accounts treated the episode as a prank for which the alleged participants admitted responsibility.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/8sep1954estreesdeniecourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 8 septembre, Estrées-Deniecourt, Somme...</span></span></span>

That rapid progression—from a serious report to an apparent copycat story—captures the difficulty of interpreting a “wave”. A cluster may contain sincere observations, ordinary objects recast as saucers, rumours that become more detailed as they spread, and deliberate jokes. The reports are historically connected through publicity and public attention, but they need not share one physical cause.

Somme’s newspaper record also contains examples of stories that lost their mystery quickly. In October, debris from an object said to have exploded near Boismont, close to Saint-Valery-sur-Somme, was identified as material from a Royal Air Force weather balloon. It was initially absorbed into the saucer narrative but became an example of how unfamiliar aviation or meteorological equipment could generate convincing local excitement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/7oct1954beruges.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Another false saucer!&quot; The mysterious craft which broke out in the Picardy sky, and whose debris was scattered in a pasture at Boismont...</span></span></span>

Other entries survive with so little information that no responsible conclusion is possible. A later catalogue lists reports from Long, Abbeville and Arry at the end of October, but describes some of them as wholly insufficient for assessment and notes a possible meteor in the Arry case. Such fragments establish that people were watching the sky; they do not establish that anything exceptional was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/1954.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">31 Long, Somme. Oct. 31 Abbeville, Somme. Evening. Totally insufficient information. Oct. 16 Ham, Somme. Evening. Oct. 16 Saint-Sulpice...</span></span></span>

The value of the 1954 Somme wave therefore lies less in its raw number of reports than in the contrast between them. Harponville–Contay and Doullens stand out precisely because most neighbouring stories were weaker, second-hand, humorous, admitted hoaxes or eventually explained.

## Harponville–Contay: the daylight roadside encounter

At about 7.30 am on 7 September 1954, two men were cycling to work on the minor road between Harponville and Contay. GEIPAN’s summary describes clear weather without fog. In a field approximately 200 metres from the road, the witnesses noticed a dark circular form that appeared to be at or immediately above ground level and was moving with a slight oscillation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The men did not merely glimpse it while passing. They approached until they were roughly 100 metres away. Their descriptions referred to a broad cylindrical or disc-like body with a slightly domed upper surface and an opening around its side. Contemporary retellings compared its shape to an unfinished haystack with an upturned plate placed on top—a rural comparison that conveyed form rather than claiming technical knowledge. Later sources commonly estimated the object at about ten metres across and three metres high, although measurements made by witnesses at an uncertain distance should be treated cautiously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The reported departure is the most unusual part of the account. The object supposedly rose silently at an angle for about 15 metres, then climbed vertically. The witnesses said that grey-blue smoke escaped from a pipe-like feature underneath as it ascended. The entire observation lasted about four or five minutes, after which the frightened men went directly to the gendarmerie.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several features make this Somme’s strongest civilian case:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Two witnesses were present.** Their agreement does not guarantee accuracy, but it reduces the likelihood of a wholly private illusion or invented experience.
* **The observation occurred in daylight and clear weather.** This removes many common night-sky ambiguities involving bright planets, stars and distant lights.
* **The duration was substantial.** Several minutes offered more opportunity for inspection than a meteor, flash or rapidly passing aircraft would allow.
* **The object appeared structured.** The witnesses described shape, movement and a possible opening rather than only a light.
* **The report was made promptly.** Immediate contact with the gendarmerie is more valuable than an account reconstructed decades later.
* **Investigators did not question the men’s general credibility.** GEIPAN records no reason to regard them as dishonest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

</div>

The case also has important weaknesses. The gendarmes found no marks in the field, despite the initial impression that the object had been on or close to the ground. No independent witness was located, no photograph was taken and no material evidence was recovered. The object’s dimensions and distance were estimates, while descriptions reproduced in later UFO books acquired small discrepancies in the witnesses’ names, the time and whether the object was actually resting on the field.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A helicopter is the most frequently discussed conventional comparison. Oscillation, hovering, a door-like feature and a climbing departure could all suggest a rotary-wing aircraft seen from an unfamiliar angle. One witness reportedly said that he had seen helicopters before and did not believe this was one. That judgement is relevant, but it cannot settle the question: early helicopters varied greatly in appearance, and unfamiliarity with a particular model could still produce a mistaken description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The helicopter theory nevertheless struggles with the reported silence at roughly 100 metres, the broad low profile and the vertical departure accompanied by smoke. Conversely, the absence of ground traces and the ordinary physical character of smoke make the account less like evidence of a technology beyond human understanding than some later retellings imply. No surviving information confirms local military movements, identifies a suitable aircraft or establishes that the witnesses’ distance estimate was correct.

GEIPAN retains the incident in category D, meaning that it remains unexplained after assessment and has medium-to-strong evidential consistency. That is a judgement about the unresolved report, not an endorsement of an extraterrestrial interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Doullens: valuable military testimony without radar proof

The Doullens incident occurred between about 1.30 and 1.40 am on 19 October 1954 at a military radar station. Several servicemen, including a private, a senior corporal and a sergeant, observed an unusual luminous phenomenon beneath a continuous layer of stratocumulus cloud whose base was estimated at around 1,000 metres. The crucial limitation is stated directly in the official record: the station’s radar was not operating that night.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The first phase was apparently seen only by the sentry. At approximately 1.34 am, he saw a green light emerge from the direction of the clouds to the east-north-east, near Mondicourt. He reported that it crossed a large part of the sky in about half a second while changing from green through orange to a dull red. It then appeared to halt abruptly towards the east.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The stationary phase allowed the sentry to alert his superiors, making the later observation a multiple-witness event. For two or three minutes, the phenomenon appeared clearly outlined against the cloud layer. The sergeant described an elliptical or disc-like underside tilted from the horizontal; another witness emphasised movement rather than a fixed shape. Its light reportedly cycled through pale green, orange and yellow at intervals of roughly five seconds, while the phenomenon seemed to oscillate or shift vertically and horizontally.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Military status adds value but must not be exaggerated. Servicemen at a radar installation were accustomed to watching the sky and reporting unusual activity, and the involvement of ranks above the original sentry reduces the chance that the entire account was a casual invention. Yet they were still making a visual observation at night, without a functioning instrument, beneath cloud cover and at an unknown distance.

The lack of radar data is especially important because the case is sometimes loosely described as a “radar-station sighting”. That phrase is geographically true but can create the false impression that the object was tracked electronically. It was not. There is no recorded range, speed, altitude or flight path independent of human visual estimates.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN considered two principal conventional explanations. The first was an astronomical misidentification involving the Moon and Jupiter. Both lay in roughly the reported direction, and Jupiter was particularly bright and close to the Moon that night. Thin or uneven cloud can distort celestial lights, create colour effects and make a fixed object appear to move. The witnesses’ failure to mention the Moon as a separate object also makes this possibility worth taking seriously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

However, the hypothesis does not comfortably explain every reported detail. The rapid initial traverse, abrupt stop, repeated colour sequence and apparent short-range movement are difficult to reconcile with a stationary astronomical source unless substantial errors of perception entered the account. The first high-speed phase also rested on one observer’s testimony, so it may not have been the same phenomenon later seen by the other men.

A helicopter offers another partial match. Alternating visibility of red and green navigation lights could produce changing colours, while manoeuvring beneath clouds could create the impression of hovering or oscillation. Against this, the witnesses reported no engine noise and described speeds during parts of the episode that would have been extreme for a helicopter. Those speed estimates depend entirely on assumed distance, however; a small nearby light and a larger distant object can produce the same angular movement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The official analysis estimated that, if the phenomenon really was about 800 metres high, its apparent angular size would imply a body roughly 15 to 20 metres across at a distance of about one kilometre. This calculation illustrates rather than proves its scale. The altitude was inferred from an estimated cloud base, and the assumption that the object was immediately beneath those clouds may have been wrong.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN ultimately classified Doullens as category D, describing it as strange but distant and of medium consistency. The unresolved status reflects the awkward combination of several trained witnesses and unusual reported behaviour, counterbalanced by poor viewing conditions, uncertain geometry, no radar return and plausible but incomplete celestial or aircraft explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Which case carries more weight?

Harponville–Contay and Doullens are strong for different reasons. The first offers better viewing conditions and a closer, apparently structured object. The second offers multiple military observers and a recorded sequence of changing motion and colour. Neither combines good visual testimony with photographs, radar, physical traces or independently measured flight data.

Harponville–Contay is arguably the stronger of the two because daylight, clear weather and several minutes of observation remove many of the uncertainties that affect luminous night-time cases. Its prompt gendarmerie inquiry also anchors the core account in a contemporary official document. The absence of ground marks and corroborating witnesses remains a serious limitation, but the event is difficult to reduce to a planet, meteor or distant point of light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Doullens benefits from the professional setting and the participation of several servicemen, yet its evidential reputation sometimes exceeds what the file supports. The radar was unavailable, most distance and speed claims were inferred, and the Moon–Jupiter pairing provides a genuine source of doubt. The case remains interesting because that explanation does not reproduce the complete testimony, not because military equipment confirmed an impossible aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-10-09217?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOULLENS (80) 19.10.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The broader Somme wave makes these distinctions easier to see. The Estrées-Deniécourt story appears to have collapsed under inquiry; the Boismont “explosion” was traced to a balloon; several other reports contain too little information to assess. Against that background, the survival of Harponville–Contay and Doullens as category D cases is meaningful. It shows that later review did not simply preserve everything reported in 1954 as mysterious.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufologie.patrickgross.org/1954/8sep1954estreesdeniecourtf.htm" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufologie.patrickgross.org">[Ufologie]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufologie.patrickgross.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Les OVNIS vus de près: France 1954, 8 septembre, Estrées-Deniecourt, Somme...</span></span></span>

## What the 1954 wave actually establishes

Somme’s 1954 record supports three firm conclusions. First, the department participated in a genuine social and reporting wave: unusual aerial stories appeared repeatedly, spread quickly through newspapers and encouraged further public attention. Second, a substantial proportion of the material was weak, humorous, mistaken or fabricated. Third, two cases retained enough first-hand detail to remain unresolved in the official archive.

None of this establishes a common fleet or single cause. A weather balloon near Saint-Valery-sur-Somme, an apparent prank near Estrées-Deniécourt, a possible astronomical misidentification at Doullens and an unidentified daylight object near Contay can all belong to the same historical wave without representing the same physical phenomenon. Treating the wave as one event obscures the differences in evidence that make it useful.

Later reporting has neither decisively strengthened nor destroyed the two leading cases. Digitisation and GEIPAN review have made the original gendarmerie material easier to examine and corrected some exaggerated popular versions. They have also exposed unresolved inconsistencies: estimated sizes became treated as measurements, the Doullens radar setting was sometimes mistaken for radar confirmation, and secondary catalogues introduced errors in dates and witness details.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1954-09-00008" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[CD47] DE HARPONVILLE (80) VERS CONTAY (80) 07.09.1954 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The most defensible assessment is therefore restrained. Harponville–Contay is a comparatively strong close-range civilian report with no satisfactory established explanation. Doullens is a worthwhile multiple-witness military case whose astronomical and aircraft alternatives remain incomplete but plausible in part. Both are officially unexplained; neither supplies conclusive evidence of an exotic craft. Their importance lies in showing why Somme’s 1954 wave cannot be dismissed wholesale—and why its most memorable stories still require case-by-case scepticism.
