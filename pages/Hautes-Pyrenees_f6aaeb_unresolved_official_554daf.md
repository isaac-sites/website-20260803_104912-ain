## Introduction

An unexplained sighting does not necessarily amount to strong evidence of an unknown craft. In Hautes-Pyrénées, the clearest example is the Bagnères-de-Bigorre report of September 2022. A witness described a pale-yellow luminous ball moving past a fifth-floor window, but the official investigation could not establish its direction, apparent size, brightness, distance or exact path. GEIPAN therefore classified the case as category C: not explained, but not sufficiently documented to analyse.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to reading the department’s official UFO record. Some cases remain unidentified because good information survives after ordinary explanations have been tested. Others remain unidentified because investigators never obtained enough information to perform those tests. The second kind should not be treated as evidence that something extraordinary occurred. It is evidence that the available record has reached its limit.

## What categories A, B and C actually mean

GEIPAN, the unit of France’s national space agency CNES that examines reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena, does not use “unexplained” as a single catch-all verdict. Its classifications reflect both the strangeness of a reported event and the **consistency** of the evidence supporting it. Consistency depends on the amount of information available and the assessed reliability of its sources, including the number and independence of witnesses, the precision of their answers and the existence of photographs or other records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The categories most relevant to the Hautes-Pyrénées archive are:

* **Category A:** the observation has been explained without significant ambiguity.
* **Category B:** investigators regard a conventional explanation as highly probable, although it may not be proved beyond every possible doubt.
* **Category C:** the report cannot be properly analysed because essential information is absent or insufficiently reliable.
* **Category D:** no satisfactory explanation has been found despite evidence considered sufficiently substantial to support the reported strangeness. GEIPAN further distinguishes D1 cases of medium consistency from D2 cases supported by particularly strong or multiple evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Category C is therefore not halfway between “explained” and “alien”. It is a failure to reach an evidential decision. A case may sound striking in narrative form while remaining impossible to test. Without viewing angles, timings, environmental details or independent confirmation, investigators may be unable to compare it properly with aircraft, celestial objects, reflections, balloons, birds, meteors or lights on the ground.

Category B is also commonly misunderstood. It does not mean that investigators identified a particular aircraft by registration number or recovered the object responsible. It means that the known explanation fits the report well enough to be considered very probable. Conversely, category D is not a declaration that an extraordinary vehicle existed. It records that the reported phenomenon remained unexplained after an investigation judged sufficiently well supported to justify that conclusion. GEIPAN itself cautions that classifications other than fully established A cases can change when fresh information or better analysis becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/baisse-cas-d" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANForte baisse des CAS D sur les 10 dernières années &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This framework prevents two opposite errors. The first is to dismiss every unidentified report as worthless. The second is to treat every absence of an explanation as positive evidence for an exotic one. Hautes-Pyrénées contains reports across this spectrum, but the unresolved Bagnères file belongs firmly at the information-poor end.

## The incomplete Bagnères-de-Bigorre report of 2022

During the early hours of 23 September 2022, a visitor staying at a thermal residence in Bagnères-de-Bigorre noticed something bright through the curtains while watching television. The witness initially thought it was the Moon. About five minutes later, after approaching the window, the witness reported seeing a pale-yellow luminous ball with a thin brown and yellow outline. It appeared to move horizontally to the left and vanished after roughly five seconds. No photograph was taken, no physical trace was reported and no second witness was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The report reached GEIPAN through the local gendarmerie two days later. That quick approach to the authorities is useful because it produced a contemporary written statement rather than a story first recorded years afterwards. It also shows that the witness was genuinely concerned by the experience. Neither point, however, establishes what was seen. Sincerity and accurate identification are different questions.

GEIPAN asked the witness to complete its technical questionnaire in November 2022 and sent a further request in March 2023. By the time its investigation report was prepared in April 2024, no reply had been received. The gendarmerie interview therefore remained the sole source available for analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

That record preserved the basic narrative but omitted measurements needed to reconstruct the event. Investigators did not know the object’s azimuth, meaning its compass direction; its angular elevation above or below the horizon; its apparent size; the precise extent of its movement; its brightness; its possible sound; or a reliable estimate of distance. The witness supplied no sketch or photograph showing the view from the window. Descriptions such as “to my left”, “above the street” and “at eye level” were too broad to convert into a dependable trajectory.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Weather records offered some context. At the Tarbes–Lourdes–Pyrénées airport station, about 18 kilometres away, visibility was reported as very good to excellent, winds were light and no cloud was detected by the station’s ceilometer. Those conditions do not identify the phenomenon. Nor do they rule out local effects that might not have been present at the airport station. They merely remove some obvious possibilities, such as a distant light heavily distorted by extensive recorded cloud cover, while leaving numerous other explanations untested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN ultimately considered no specific hypothesis because the basic geometry was missing. Its report assessed the consistency of the case as weak: one witness, no image or video, and numerous absent technical details. It classified the observation as C because there were insufficient data, not because all conventional explanations had been investigated and rejected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

That is the most important fact about the case. “No hypothesis considered” can sound more mysterious than “probable balloon” or “probable reflection”, but here it signals a more elementary problem. Investigators could not reliably determine which hypotheses deserved testing.

## What the missing measurements could have changed

The absent details were not administrative trivia. Each would have narrowed the possible origins of the light.

**A measured direction and elevation** could have allowed comparison with the Moon, bright planets, stars, known flight paths and fixed lights on surrounding buildings or hills. The witness’s first impression that the light might be the Moon is relevant, but without an exact viewing direction it cannot be evaluated properly.

**Apparent size** would have helped distinguish a small bright point from a visibly extended object. Witnesses naturally describe what they perceive as a “ball”, but a point source affected by glare, eyesight, glass, curtains or atmospheric conditions can acquire a circular appearance. Without an angular estimate—often obtained by comparing the object with a fingertip or a familiar feature—“ball” tells investigators little about physical size.

**A reconstructed trajectory** could have shown whether the apparent movement was consistent with an aircraft, a nearby object, the witness changing position at the window, or a light being intermittently obscured. The report states that the phenomenon seemed stationary and then moved when the witness approached. That sequence is potentially important, but it cannot be tested without a plan of the room, window and external sightline.

**Brightness, duration and sound** would have helped compare the event with meteors, aircraft landing lights, drones, lanterns and local reflections. A five-second observation is particularly vulnerable to uncertainty because there is little time to establish scale, speed or distance. A silent light may be genuinely silent, too distant for sound to arrive during the observation, or associated with a noise masked by the television or the building.

None of these missing facts proves that the sighting had an ordinary cause. They show why an ordinary cause could neither be confirmed nor responsibly excluded.

## Why an official report is not the same as official confirmation

The presence of a gendarmerie statement and a CNES investigation gives the Bagnères case a stronger documentary trail than an anonymous social-media post. It confirms that a named person formally reported an experience at a particular place and time and that GEIPAN assessed the available material. It does not mean that the gendarmerie witnessed the event, verified the object or endorsed the witness’s interpretation.

A police interview mainly records testimony. It may capture a report close to the event, but it is not automatically designed to obtain all the observational measurements needed for aerospace analysis. GEIPAN’s technical questionnaire is intended to fill that gap by gathering details in a standardised form. Its methodology relies on the number and precision of answers, supporting images, witness independence and internal coherence when judging the evidential consistency of a case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The Bagnères file shows what happens when that second stage fails. The official paper trail exists, but it contains too little geometry to identify the phenomenon. The authority of the institutions involved cannot supply information that was never recorded.

This also explains why some apparently less dramatic Hautes-Pyrénées cases can receive firmer conclusions. A modest light observed with a known direction, duration and movement may be matched confidently to a meteor, planet or aircraft. A more colourful account may remain unresolved simply because its descriptive details are too vague. The strength of a case depends less on how unusual the story sounds than on whether its claims can be checked.

## Delayed testimony and fragile documentary trails

Time affects UFO investigation in two different ways. A witness may report promptly to the gendarmerie, as happened at Bagnères, yet the technical investigation may still stall if later questions go unanswered. Conversely, a detailed questionnaire completed months after an event may contain more measurements but introduce uncertainty about memory.

GEIPAN has acknowledged this problem in other investigations. In a case from Crozon, it noted that a questionnaire completed five months after the observation and a reconstruction conducted about a year later could have altered or disturbed elements of the testimony. Its use of cognitive interviewing is intended partly to help witnesses recover contextual information while reducing the influence of assumptions and prior knowledge, but no interview method can recreate measurements that were never made at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/06_PY_sli.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANCOPEIPAN_Entretien Cognitif Inte</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCOPEIPAN_EntretienCognitifInte...September 25, 2014 — 24 Jul 2014 — La probabilité de récupération d&#x27;un souvenir en mémoire peut êt...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 25, 2014</span></span></span>

Post-sighting internet research can add another complication. The Bagnères witness told the gendarmerie that, after the incident, they searched online and found apparently similar phenomena reported elsewhere. That does not invalidate the original observation, which had already occurred. It does mean that any later retelling could be influenced by images, labels and stories encountered afterwards. The original gendarmerie statement is therefore valuable as an early record, even though it is technically incomplete.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Older Hautes-Pyrénées reports often present an even greater problem. A surviving newspaper clipping may preserve a date, a town and a dramatic description while omitting the original witness statement, weather conditions, exact location or later correction. A local archive can establish that a story was published; it cannot by itself establish that the reported object behaved as described. Where the investigative file has disappeared or never existed, modern certainty is usually impossible.

## When does an unexplained sighting mean anything?

An unresolved report becomes more evidentially significant when uncertainty survives **despite** good documentation. Useful indicators include several witnesses who observed independently, precise and mutually consistent timelines, measured viewing directions, original photographs with usable metadata, radar or other instrument records, and prompt access to the location for reconstruction. Physical traces matter only when they are properly documented, sampled and linked convincingly to the event rather than merely found nearby.

The Bagnères case offers almost the opposite pattern: a single short visual observation, no image, no independent confirmation, no reconstruction and no response to requests for technical detail. Its category C label is appropriate because the experience cannot be reduced to a verified explanation, but neither can its unusual features be established with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

For readers assessing unresolved reports across Hautes-Pyrénées, three questions are more useful than simply asking whether a case was “explained”:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **What facts were actually recorded?** A vivid narrative is not the same as measured direction, duration and angular size.
2. **Which explanations were tested, and against what data?** A category D case should have survived meaningful comparison with known phenomena; a category C case may never have reached that stage.
3. **What new evidence could change the conclusion?** A second witness, an original photograph, a precise viewpoint or a contemporaneous flight record may transform an untestable story into an analysable case.

</div>

Official openness about evidential failure is a strength, not an embarrassment. Classifying a report as C avoids inventing an explanation unsupported by the record, while also avoiding the equally unwarranted claim that the observation represents a genuinely extraordinary object.

## The defensible verdict on the unresolved files

The unresolved portion of the Hautes-Pyrénées record should not be presented as a hidden reserve of strong UFO evidence. Some reports may describe experiences whose causes genuinely remain unknown. In the publicly documented Bagnères case, however, the central uncertainty arose because investigators lacked the information required to distinguish among possibilities.

Later reporting did not strengthen the original claim. The 2024 investigation added weather context and formally identified the missing data, but it produced no photograph, corroborating witness, radar return or testable trajectory. Its main contribution was to define the limits of the evidence more clearly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete663.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMicrosoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAGNERES-DE-BIGORRE (65) 23.09.2022 CR final EN COURS MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The responsible conclusion is narrower than either belief or dismissal: a witness reported a disturbing luminous phenomenon; the report was preserved by the gendarmerie and reviewed by GEIPAN; its origin was not identified; and the available documentation is too weak to support a larger claim about what crossed the sky—or appeared to cross it—over Bagnères-de-Bigorre that night.
