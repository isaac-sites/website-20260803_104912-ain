## Introduction

The sighting reported at Choisy-le-Roi on 21 May 2012 is the strongest published unresolved UFO case in [Val-de-Marne]({{ 'what-val-de-marnes-ufo-files-actually/' | relative_url }}). At about 10.12 pm, a lone motorist described a silent, illuminated object that appeared to stop above the road, changed shape and colour, then climbed into cloud. France’s official investigation service, GEIPAN, considered a festive balloon the only plausible conventional explanation but concluded that it did not adequately reproduce the reported behaviour. The case was therefore retained in category D, historically described more specifically as D1: unexplained after investigation, but supported by only moderate evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Val-de-Marne_555905_choisy_unresolved_ca_5c9eaa-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Choisy Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That status makes Choisy-le-Roi important within Val-de-Marne, where other prominent reports have generally been explained, judged probably explained or left unusable through lack of information. It does not, however, establish that an exotic craft was present. The file rests on one brief visual observation, without photographs, independent witnesses, radar data or physical evidence.

## What the witness reported in 2012

The observation began while the witness, a 40-year-old man, was driving through a built-up part of Choisy-le-Roi. GEIPAN records the weather as very cloudy or overcast. At 10.12 pm, he noticed a luminous phenomenon moving approximately from north to south. When he stopped at a red light, he obtained a clearer view and described a roughly cylindrical form, tapering downwards in a way he compared to a pointed icon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The witness then got out of his vehicle. He said that the phenomenon had become stationary in front of him and that an intense light appeared to emerge from several small window-like openings. An orange luminous sphere or glow surrounded the object. After remaining still for several seconds, it reportedly rose, appeared to change into an inverted V shape, turned white and disappeared into the low cloud layer. GEIPAN gives the total duration as approximately 15 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The published testimony record describes the object as silent and gives an estimated distance of 45 metres. Its apparent dimensions were recorded as roughly five to seven metres high, with a base about five metres across. These figures should be read as the witness’s estimates rather than measured dimensions. With an unfamiliar object against a cloudy night sky, there was no verified reference distance from which its true size could be calculated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7727" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several features made the report more unusual than a routine account of a distant light:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the witness perceived a structured, three-dimensional object rather than a point;
* he described illuminated openings and an orange surrounding glow;
* the phenomenon appeared to halt above the road;
* it was reportedly silent at what seemed to be close range;
* its shape and colour appeared to change as it departed.

</div>

Those details are the core of the mystery. They are also dependent entirely on one person’s perception during a short, surprising event.

## Why GEIPAN retained category D

GEIPAN, the unidentified aerospace phenomena unit of the French space agency CNES, does not classify a case solely according to whether the witness recognised what was seen. Investigators assess two separate qualities: **residual strangeness**, meaning how poorly the report fits known phenomena after plausible explanations have been tested, and **consistency**, meaning the quantity, precision and reliability of the available information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLe glossaire du GeipanLa classification d&#x27;un cas nécessite l&#x27;évaluation, d&#x27;une part, de son degré de consistance (C) et, d&#x27;autre pa...</span></span></span>

The current Choisy-le-Roi record gives a strangeness score of 0.60 and a consistency score of 0.70. In GEIPAN’s framework, an unexplained classification can be retained when the information is considered sufficiently consistent to support the remaining level of strangeness. The case page now displays the broader category D, while its written conclusion and older database entries retain the D1 wording used for a strange but moderately supported case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The distinction matters. A category C case is one in which investigators cannot reach a useful conclusion because the information is inadequate. A D or D1 case is different: GEIPAN considers the observation sufficiently documented to analyse, yet finds that the plausible explanations tested do not fit well enough. D1 commonly includes precisely the limitation found here—a single witness with a detailed account but no photograph or recording.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLe glossaire du GeipanLa classification d&#x27;un cas nécessite l&#x27;évaluation, d&#x27;une part, de son degré de consistance (C) et, d&#x27;autre pa...</span></span></span>

For Choisy-le-Roi, the official summary identifies a festive balloon as the only plausible conventional hypothesis considered. Such a balloon could account for silence, slow movement, bright internal lighting, drifting and apparent changes caused by rotation or partial obscuration. GEIPAN nevertheless judged the hypothesis difficult to reconcile with the full account, especially the apparent stop in front of the witness, the strongly structured form with illuminated “windows”, and the subsequent change of shape during ascent. It therefore concluded that the reported phenomenon could not be explained and classified it as an object of unknown nature.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case’s standing is reinforced by comparison with the rest of Val-de-Marne’s published record. The much-publicised Maisons-Alfort lights of 2011 were classified B and attributed probably to a Thai lantern. A Saint-Maur-des-Fossés report from July 2012 was also classified B, with a balloon proposed. Other department cases, including reports from Maisons-Alfort and Saint-Maur, have been placed in category C because too little reliable information was available. GEIPAN search results continue to single out Choisy-le-Roi as the department’s D-classified case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=51&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why category D is not proof of an exotic craft

The strongest reason for caution is that the file contains no evidence independent of the witness’s recollection. There is one testimony, no photograph or video, no second observer whose account can be compared, and no publicly documented radar or aviation record confirming that an object occupied the reported position. GEIPAN itself notes the surprising absence of other witnesses, given that the event supposedly occurred over a road in a densely populated town.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The apparent distance and dimensions are particularly uncertain. Humans can estimate the size of an unfamiliar object only when its distance is known, and estimate distance reliably only when its size or surrounding position is known. At night, against cloud, that circular problem becomes acute. A small illuminated balloon tens or hundreds of metres away can appear much larger and closer, while its rotation may create the impression that its outline is changing. GEIPAN has itself warned in discussions of lantern and balloon cases that witness distance estimates are often unreliable when the observed object cannot first be identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span></span></span>

The reported stationary phase also has alternatives that cannot be fully excluded. A drifting object may appear motionless when viewed head-on, when its movement briefly aligns with the observer’s line of sight, or when a moving witness stops and the visual geometry changes. GEIPAN recognises an “apparent stationary” or pivot effect in which relative movement can make an airborne object seem fixed against the sky. Whether that mechanism applies here cannot now be demonstrated, but the absence of recorded imagery prevents it from being properly tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/missions-methodes-et-resultat" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">missions methodes et resultat</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMISSIONS, METHODES ET RESULTATS25 Feb 2021 — PAN de catégorie D: observation inexpliquée malgré les éléments en possession du GEIP...</span></span></span>

Nor does the description of “windows” necessarily prove that the object possessed a manufactured cabin. Brief night observations encourage the visual system to organise separate bright areas into a coherent structure. Reflections, folds in balloon material, internal lights or gaps in a glow can be perceived as openings on a solid body. This does not show that the witness was careless or dishonest; it shows why a precise account can remain sincerely reported while still containing perceptual interpretation.

GEIPAN’s classification therefore means that its investigators did not find a conventional hypothesis that satisfactorily matched all the reported characteristics. It does not mean that every ordinary explanation was experimentally ruled out, still less that the object was extraterrestrial. ## What evidence is still missing

The Choisy-le-Roi case could have been substantially strengthened—or resolved—by information that was not obtained or has not been published.

**Independent witnesses** would have been the most valuable addition. Accounts from people positioned elsewhere could have tested whether the object was truly above the road, provided intersecting sightlines for estimating altitude and distance, and shown whether the apparent shape change was visible from more than one angle.

**Photographs or video** could have established angular size, motion relative to buildings or street furniture, changes in brightness and whether the “windows” were stable features. Even poor smartphone footage might have helped distinguish a compact structure from an illuminated balloon rotating in the wind.

**Precise positional reconstruction** would have allowed investigators to compare the sightline with local roads, buildings, cranes, illuminated signs and approach paths. The public record gives directions and an estimated distance, but it does not supply enough independently verifiable geometry for readers to reconstruct the event conclusively.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7727" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Contemporaneous wind and cloud-layer data** could have tested the balloon theory more firmly. A light object should broadly follow the airflow at its altitude. If the reported north-to-south passage and later ascent contradicted measured winds, the balloon explanation would weaken; if they matched, it would gain credibility. The case page records overcast conditions but does not publicly present a detailed wind comparison.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Aviation or radar confirmation** would have transformed the evidential quality of the case. Choisy-le-Roi lies in a sky busy with traffic associated with the Paris region and nearby Orly Airport, but the published file does not report a correlated radar target, pilot observation or air-traffic incident. Without that second sensor channel, the case remains a civilian visual report rather than an aviation encounter.

## The fairest assessment

Choisy-le-Roi remains Val-de-Marne’s key UFO mystery because it sits in the narrow space between an explained sighting and an unusably weak one. The witness supplied a detailed sequence, the description contained several genuinely unusual features, and GEIPAN found that the leading balloon hypothesis did not reproduce the whole report. The current case page, updated on 16 June 2026, still lists the observation in category D with relatively high consistency for a single-witness file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-05-08357" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHOISY-LE-ROI (94) 21.05.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Yet its evidential ceiling is equally clear. The event lasted only about 15 seconds. It was seen by one person, under a covered night sky, with no recording and no confirmed instrumental trace. The estimated closeness and size cannot be independently checked, and several key features—stationarity, shape change and apparent windows—could have been influenced by perspective, rotation, lighting or rapid perceptual interpretation.

The most defensible conclusion is therefore neither that the sighting was “just a balloon” nor that it demonstrated an unknown craft. It is an officially unresolved observation whose reported characteristics resisted the explanation tested, but whose missing corroboration prevents a stronger claim. Within Val-de-Marne’s UFO history, that combination makes Choisy-le-Roi the department’s most substantial open case—and a useful example of how “unidentified” can be meaningful without being conclusive.
