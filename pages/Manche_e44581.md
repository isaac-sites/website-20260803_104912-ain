## Introduction

That pattern matters. Manche is a coastal department with busy civilian and military airspace, ports, maritime traffic and long, unobstructed horizons. ## What the official record contains

GEIPAN, the unit of the French space agency CNES responsible for unidentified aerospace phenomena, collects direct witness reports, investigates suitable cases and publishes anonymised files. Its categories distinguish identified or probably identified events from cases lacking reliable information and those that remain unexplained after investigation. GEIPAN stresses that testimony is the starting point of an enquiry, but that estimates of an unknown light’s distance, size and speed are inherently unreliable without familiar reference points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/what-did-i-see/step-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The published Manche cases form a small and uneven sequence rather than a sustained departmental “flap”. The official list includes reports from Chef-du-Pont and Saint-Germain-le-Gaillard in 1979; Cerisy-la-Salle in 1983; Brix in 1989; several locations in 1993 and 1994; Saint-Samson-de-Bonfossé in 2009; Isigny-le-Buat in 2010; Saint-Lô and Tourlaville in 2012; Saint-Germain-sur-Ay in 2013; Saint-Côme-du-Mont in 2014; and Carentan-les-Marais in 2024. The classifications range from straightforward identifications to cases left unusable through lack of information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=22%2C27&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Saint-Lô in 2012: the strongest unresolved case

The sighting at Saint-Lô on 4 April 2012 is the most significant officially unresolved Manche case. A witness reported two luminous points in the night sky. According to the GEIPAN summary, one followed an approximately straight course while the second appeared to move around it. The case was classified D1, the label then used for an unexplained phenomenon supported by a moderate or substantial amount of usable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=80&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The unusual feature was not merely that two lights were visible. Aircraft, satellites and celestial objects routinely appear as lights at night. What made the account harder to resolve was the reported relative movement: one point seemed to circle or manoeuvre around the other while both progressed across the sky. Investigators could not match that complete description convincingly to a known satellite passage, conventional aircraft configuration or simple astronomical object.

Even so, the classification has important limits. It records that GEIPAN did not find a satisfactory explanation from the available information; it does not certify that the lights were solid craft, that their apparent motions were measured independently, or that they displayed technology beyond conventional aviation. The case appears to have relied chiefly on one witness rather than simultaneous radar records, calibrated imagery or several observers at separated locations. A point of light against a dark sky can also appear to drift, stop or change direction because of involuntary eye movements and the absence of stable visual references. GEIPAN specifically warns about these perceptual effects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">etape 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">etape 1</span></span></span>

Saint-Lô therefore deserves to be called unresolved, but not conclusive. It is stronger than a vague recollection and weaker than a multi-sensor aviation incident. Later reporting has not produced independent radar data, additional contemporary witnesses or higher-quality recordings that materially strengthen the original claim. Its evidential status remains much as it was when classified: genuinely unidentified within the file, but open to ordinary explanations that could not be tested retrospectively.

## The 1979 reports: striking descriptions, weak reconstruction

Two of the earliest published Manche files date from spring 1979. At Chef-du-Pont on 22 March, the reported phenomenon was a luminous ball making back-and-forth movements. GEIPAN placed the case in category C because reliable information was insufficient for a firm conclusion. The public case page identifies a gendarmerie record but does not present the event as a solved or strongly evidenced encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-03-00612" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A little over a month later, on 27 April, a witness at Saint-Germain-le-Gaillard described the passage of an object of large apparent dimensions. This case was also classified C. That classification is crucial: “large” describes the witness’s impression, not a verified physical measurement. Without a known distance, even a small nearby object or an arrangement of lights can be perceived as something enormous farther away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=135&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The two reports are sometimes tempting to join into a local wave because they occurred within weeks of each other. The available record does not justify that conclusion. The descriptions differ, the supporting material is limited and there is no demonstrated common trajectory, repeated witness group or shared aviation event. They are best treated as two unresolved reports with insufficient data, not as evidence of coordinated activity over the department.

The 1983 Cerisy-la-Salle “ball of fire” belongs to the same weak-evidence category. A fiery object may represent a meteor or atmospheric re-entry, but the historic file did not contain enough dependable information to establish an identification. GEIPAN explains that bright meteors can remain visible for several seconds, change colour, fragment and appear far closer to the observer than they really are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=22%2C27&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Brix in 1989: a dramatic trace with an earthly cause

The Brix case of October 1989 is one of Manche’s most instructive incidents because it began with apparently physical evidence. A farmer found a large burnt area in a meadow. Cases involving traces on the ground can seem stronger than sightings of distant lights, since something measurable has clearly occurred.

The enquiry, however, did not support an extraordinary aerial event. GEIPAN’s published summary concluded that the damage was probably caused by the accidental ignition of hydrocarbons in part of the field. The file contains an enquiry report, soil analysis, photographs, press material and a police record, yet the final classification was B: probably identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/50208" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Brix illustrates why the amount of documentation should not be confused with evidence for a UFO. A heavily documented case may become less mysterious as testing progresses. In this instance, the physical trace allowed investigators to consider combustion and contamination rather than relying solely on memory. The later evidence weakened an exotic interpretation instead of strengthening it.

It also demonstrates the value of preserving mundane explanations in departmental UFO history. A focus only on unexplained cases would miss how investigations actually work: hypotheses are narrowed by examining soil, burn patterns, local activity and possible fuels. The interesting outcome is not that something inexplicable scorched the field, but that an apparently sensational trace proved compatible with an accidental fire.

## The 1990s cluster: Venus, a nightclub light and a child’s balloon

Several Manche reports from 1993 and 1994 were resolved or substantially weakened by familiar causes.

At Beaumont-Hague in February 1993, the observation was judged probably to be Venus. The planet is one of the most frequent sources of unusual-light reports because it can be exceptionally bright and appears low in the sky around dusk or dawn. Atmospheric turbulence can make it seem to change colour or shape. When the observer has no reliable distance cue, a stationary planet may also be interpreted as a hovering object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=49.42700809484137&amp;customGetLongitude=-0.9608752280473711&amp;customGetZoom=7&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=51.49757451349519&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=47.35644167618756&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=2.2526257485151295&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-4.174376204609872&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=14&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At Gavray in April 1993, investigators identified the source as a beam from a nightclub searchlight. Such beams can illuminate haze or low cloud, producing moving patches or columns of light with no obvious visible connection to the ground. GEIPAN classified the case A, indicating that the phenomenon was identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;page=%2C125&amp;select-category-export=nothing" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The Saint-Sauveur-le-Vicomte report of November 1993 remained category C. Witnesses described a luminous line surrounded by points of light, but the available information was not sufficient for reliable reconstruction. It is a useful example of why a structured appearance does not automatically imply a structured craft. Separate lights can be mentally grouped into a single outline, especially in darkness, while aircraft lights, reflections or objects at different distances may appear connected.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C377&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At La Haye-Pesnel in May 1994, the likely explanation was a child’s balloon. Balloons can seem to hover, accelerate, reverse direction or climb unexpectedly when carried through variable winds and turbulence near buildings or trees. Their apparent behaviour becomes particularly misleading when observers assume that they are much larger and farther away than they actually are.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=49.42700809484137&amp;customGetLongitude=-0.9608752280473711&amp;customGetZoom=7&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=51.49757451349519&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=47.35644167618756&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=2.2526257485151295&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-4.174376204609872&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=14&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Taken together, these cases do not form a coherent UFO flap. They show several unrelated mechanisms producing superficially similar impressions: an astronomical light, projected illumination, an inadequately documented arrangement of lights and a wind-borne object.

## Coastal skies, aircraft and luminous trains

Manche’s geography strongly shapes what witnesses see. Much of the department has open views towards the sea, while Cherbourg, Granville and the bay of Mont-Saint-Michel are crossed by aircraft, helicopters and maritime traffic. A distant aircraft seen head-on may appear stationary; a vessel’s lights can seem suspended above a dark horizon; and condensation trails illuminated by a low Sun can appear as bright, slow-moving objects.

The official files repeatedly contain these kinds of explanations. At Saint-Germain-sur-Ay in September 2013, a bright moving point was attributed to an aircraft trail. At Saint-Côme-du-Mont in November 2014, several white-yellow streaks were identified as aircraft condensation trails.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=96&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Tourlaville, now part of Cherbourg-en-Cotentin, produced a different kind of report in May 2012. Witnesses saw orange luminous phenomena moving through the sky. GEIPAN considered the probable explanation to be sky lanterns. Released in groups, these small flame-heated balloons can maintain similar spacing, brighten and fade at different times, or appear to form the lights of a single dark object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?customGetLattitude=49.42700809484137&amp;customGetLongitude=-0.9608752280473711&amp;customGetZoom=7&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=51.49757451349519&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=47.35644167618756&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=2.2526257485151295&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-4.174376204609872&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=14&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Cherbourg also appears in documentation relating to a widely observed bright atmospheric event in October 2011. Witnesses at Port Chantereyne described a silent white or green light, in some accounts with fragmentation, crossing the sky for several seconds. The wider enquiry identified the event as a bright meteor rather than a local craft. The case is a reminder that a phenomenon seen from Manche may be regional or national in scale, tens of kilometres above the ground, even when it appears to pass close to the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete94.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete94</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete94</span></span></span>

More recently, strings of Starlink satellites have generated reports of an “extraordinary luminous train” over Granville and elsewhere. GEIPAN lists satellite formations among common modern misidentifications: shortly after launch, multiple satellites can appear as a precise line of evenly spaced lights, an arrangement that looks artificial because it is artificial, but is not unidentified once orbital data are checked.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">etape 1</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">etape 1</span></span></span>

## Carentan-les-Marais in 2024: a case weakened by missing evidence

The newest published Manche case concerns an observation at Carentan-les-Marais at about 1.35 am on 12 September 2024. A witness reported a luminous phenomenon making unusual movements when viewed from home. GEIPAN ultimately classified the case C because it lacked sufficiently reliable information. The file was published with an update dated 30 March 2026.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51563" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

An important evidential problem is that the photographs and video examined by investigators were not recordings of the original September event. They came from another observation made by the same witness on 12 November 2024, which the witness considered similar. That creates a break between the claim and the supporting imagery: the later recording may reveal something about the witness’s recurring interpretation of lights, but it cannot establish what was present two months earlier.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51563" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Category C should not be read as “mysterious object confirmed”. It means that the information was too incomplete or uncertain for GEIPAN to decide reliably between possible explanations. In practical terms, the case is weaker than Saint-Lô. It lacks contemporaneous visual evidence tied securely to the reported date, and later material cannot repair that gap.

## Is there a military or radar connection?

Manche’s military and maritime importance makes the question reasonable, particularly around Cherbourg and the northern Cotentin. Yet the published departmental cases do not contain a strong equivalent of the classic radar-visual incidents found elsewhere in UFO history. No Manche case in the official group examined here is supported by a public combination of military radar tracking, airborne interception, pilot observation and ground witnesses.

That absence does not prove that military personnel have never submitted reports or that every relevant record is public. It does mean that claims of a recognised Manche “military UFO corridor” go beyond the available evidence. Ordinary aviation remains relevant chiefly as a source of possible explanations: aircraft approaching or receding, helicopters, formation lights, flares and illuminated trails can all appear unusual when their distance and orientation are unknown.

The bay and Channel also complicate localisation. An observer may place a light over land when it is actually above the sea, or may interpret a vessel, helicopter or aircraft near the horizon as something hovering close by. In a GEIPAN enquiry concerning an observation across the bay of Mont-Saint-Michel, investigators explicitly considered aircraft or helicopters associated with Granville’s airfield and maritime traffic as the two principal hypotheses after rejecting a satellite and an object drifting with the wind.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete450.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete450</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete450</span></span></span>

## What Manche’s cases actually show

The departmental record supports four broad conclusions.

**There is one notable unresolved case.** Saint-Lô in 2012 remains the clearest official anomaly because the reported relationship between two moving lights resisted the available checks. Its unresolved status is meaningful, but the lack of independent instrumental evidence limits stronger conclusions.

**Most apparent patterns dissolve under investigation.** The 1993–94 reports involved Venus, a nightclub searchlight, a probable balloon and one poorly documented light formation. Similar dates do not establish a common cause.

**Physical traces are not automatically better UFO evidence.** The burnt field at Brix offered material that could be examined, and that examination favoured accidental hydrocarbon combustion.

**Missing information is the dominant weakness.** Chef-du-Pont, Saint-Germain-le-Gaillard, Cerisy-la-Salle, Saint-Sauveur-le-Vicomte, Saint-Samson-de-Bonfossé, Isigny-le-Buat and Carentan-les-Marais were not validated as extraordinary events. Their files remained inconclusive largely because investigators lacked dependable timing, geometry, corroboration or contemporaneous records.

Manche’s UFO history is therefore best understood neither as a catalogue of proven exotic craft nor as a collection in which every witness was simply mistaken. It is a record of observations made under difficult conditions, followed by enquiries of very unequal evidential strength. Some yielded convincing conventional explanations; some could not be reconstructed; and one, at Saint-Lô, remains officially unexplained without providing enough evidence to determine what the witnesses actually saw.
