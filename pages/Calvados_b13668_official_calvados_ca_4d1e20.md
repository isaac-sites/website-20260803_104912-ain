## Introduction

France’s official files do not reveal a hidden concentration of extraordinary craft over [Calvados]({{ 'calvados-ufos-between-legend-and/' | relative_url }}). They show something more useful: how impressive-looking reports can become ordinary once investigators reconstruct the viewing conditions, compare directions with weather and local activity, and distinguish a genuinely unexplained event from one that is merely under-documented.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Calvados_b13668_official_calvados_ca_4d1e20-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Official Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The clearest Calvados examples involve lasers or powerful event projectors, lightweight debris, lanterns and probable drones. Other reports were placed in the insufficient-information category because the witness account could not support a reliable reconstruction. This distinction matters. A case classified as unusable is not evidence of an unknown phenomenon; it means that too little dependable information survives to decide what was seen. The official record therefore weakens several dramatic interpretations while illustrating how easily distance, darkness, cloud layers and unfamiliar lighting can mislead even sincere witnesses.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=24%2C4&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN classifies reported phenomena

GEIPAN is the unidentified aerospace phenomena investigation and information group operated by France’s national space agency, CNES. It receives reports directly from witnesses and through bodies including the gendarmerie, police, civil aviation and the armed forces. Investigators may compare testimony with meteorological observations, astronomical data, aircraft movements, photographs, maps and local enquiries before publishing a case file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

Its classification system is best read as a judgement about the evidence, not as a scale running from mundane to alien:

* **Category A** means that the reported phenomenon has been identified with high confidence.

CNES’s published national figures underline the importance of Category C. It reports that 24.6 per cent of phenomena are clearly identified and 39.7 per cent probably identified, while 32.4 per cent remain unidentified because of insufficient data. Only 3.3 per cent are listed as unexplained after investigation. In other words, the largest “unidentified” group consists not of strong mysteries but of reports that cannot be tested adequately.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also records two separate measures in many newer files. **Strangeness** describes how unusual the reported phenomenon appears, while **consistency** concerns the amount of reliable, objectified information available. A strange account with one witness, no image and uncertain timing may therefore have little investigative value. Conversely, an initially puzzling report supported by photographs, precise directions and weather data may be resolved quite firmly. The Calvados files repeatedly demonstrate this difference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51490?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=4%2C27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAYEUX (14) 22.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Lasers, sky roses and lights projected onto cloud

Some of the most visually striking official cases in Calvados were eventually linked to ground-based lighting. These reports are especially instructive because a projected patch of light can appear to be a self-contained object high in the sky. The beam itself may be invisible in clear air, leaving only a luminous disc, oval or group of moving patches on a cloud layer.

### The Honfleur–Blonville laser case

On 20 August 2000, a report made while travelling from Honfleur towards Blonville-sur-Mer was later classified Category A and identified as a laser. The public catalogue gives little narrative detail, but its importance lies in the final status: this was not simply a vague light dismissed without inquiry. GEIPAN recorded it as a positively identified lighting phenomenon and revisited the file before its 2017 update.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/52806?field_is_revisited_value=1&amp;page=%2C4" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANde HONFLEUR (14) vers BLONVILLE-SUR-MER (14) 20.08.2000 &#124; GEIPANAugust 20, 2000 — Geipan Groupe d’études et d’informations sur les...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: August 20, 2000</span></span></span>

The location makes such an explanation plausible. The Honfleur–Deauville–Trouville coast contains hotels, nightlife venues, seasonal entertainment and outdoor events capable of using strong promotional projectors. From a moving vehicle, apparent motion produced by the observer’s own travel can combine with the scanning movement of a beam. With no reliable distance reference against a dark sky, a patch projected on cloud may seem much larger, faster or higher than it really is.

The case should not be stretched beyond what the official file supports: the publicly indexed material does not preserve a detailed reconstruction comparable with more recent investigations. Its value is narrower. It establishes that at least one seemingly anomalous coastal report in Calvados was officially resolved as laser illumination rather than an aircraft or unknown vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=24%2C4&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### Saint-Gatien-des-Bois changed from unexplained to probably explained

The 27 November 2011 Saint-Gatien-des-Bois case is more revealing because GEIPAN later reversed its original assessment. At about 6.50 pm, a motorist and her children saw an intense, stationary white phenomenon above the village church. Another witness subsequently provided a separate account. The absence of sound and the apparently fixed luminous forms initially made the event difficult to identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The first investigation rejected the possibility of powerful event projectors because the weather information then consulted appeared to show no cloud in the relevant area. Without cloud, there would be no surface on which the light could form the observed patches. The case was consequently classified D, meaning unexplained despite investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A later review reached a different conclusion. More detailed meteorological work indicated that banks of low stratocumulus cloud were probably present. That single change transformed the interpretation. Circular white patches, variable in number, could have been the impact points of a rapidly moving sky projector on the cloud base. Very clear air would explain why witnesses saw the illuminated patches but not the beams connecting them to the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators also identified a nearby practical source. The golf course north of Saint-Gatien was known to hold outdoor events, and a contractor working there at the time offered a projector model whose technical characteristics matched the reported appearance. GEIPAN could not prove that the machine was operating at the exact moment of the sighting. However, its presence, the probable cloud layer, the shapes and brightness, the absence of noise and the compatibility of the viewing directions produced a coherent explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The revised classification was Category B: probable projected light on cloud. This is one of the strongest lessons in the Calvados archive. “Unexplained” was not treated as a permanent verdict. When improved weather analysis supplied a missing piece of evidence, GEIPAN reassessed the case and published the change rather than preserving its more mysterious earlier status.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

There is still a legitimate limitation. No invoice, operator’s statement or equipment log proves that the projector was switched on that evening. The official conclusion is therefore probable rather than certain. Yet the explanation accounts for several otherwise odd features at once, especially the bright shapes without visible connecting beams. It is more economical than assuming a silent object hovering above the village.

## Ordinary objects that looked extraordinary

Not all official explanations involve lights. A daytime Caen report from 2021 shows how an object carried by wind can appear controlled or structured when viewed without an accurate sense of scale.

Two people eating on a rooftop terrace on 19 October watched a dark, silent object pass at apparently low altitude for roughly ten minutes. They described it as flat underneath and rounded above. It followed a mostly straight course, turned slightly and then appeared to rise. Neither witness photographed it, but both produced drawings and trajectory reconstructions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-10-51258?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C43&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CAEN (14) 19.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN compared the movement with local weather conditions and concluded that the object was lightweight, rigid debris, possibly a piece of black expanded PVC from building work. The shape could remain stable because rigid sheet material would not flutter like a plastic bag. Its dark colour matched the observation, while its northward path corresponded with the direction of strong wind gusts. The apparent climb could result from changing perspective or the debris being lifted by the airflow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-10-51258?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=%2C43&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">CAEN (14) 19.10.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The Bayeux drone report

The Bayeux case of 22 October 2023 demonstrates how modern technology has added another common source of unfamiliar aerial lights. Shortly after midnight, a lone witness saw a red light moving quickly, then apparently stopping. Red and green lights became visible and alternated at roughly one-second intervals. A pale yellow conical beam then appeared to sweep towards the ground before the light moved away in an apparent zigzag.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51490?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=4%2C27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAYEUX (14) 22.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At face value, the account contained several features often described as highly anomalous: abrupt changes of movement, hovering, coloured lights and a downward beam. GEIPAN considered both a helicopter and a drone. The red and green lights were compatible with aviation navigation lighting, but the reported blinking green light fitted a drone better than a helicopter, whose right-side green position light should normally remain steady. The ability to hover and carry a downward-facing searchlight also favoured a drone.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51490?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=4%2C27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAYEUX (14) 22.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The report was classified Category B rather than A. There was only one witness and no photograph or video, so investigators could not identify a particular aircraft or operator. The apparent instantaneous acceleration and zigzag departure may also have been affected by perspective: a small nearby drone can cross a large angle of the observer’s vision quickly while travelling at an ordinary speed.

The distinction is significant. GEIPAN did not claim to have proved which drone was present. It judged the drone hypothesis to be the best match for the observed lighting and behaviour. This is stronger than saying “it could have been anything”, but weaker than a confirmed identification supported by flight records or recovered equipment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51490?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=4%2C27&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BAYEUX (14) 22.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why some Calvados reports remain unusable

A substantial portion of the department’s official catalogue is classified C for lack of reliable information. Examples include La Caine in 1983, Lisieux in 1991, Honfleur in 1998, Saint-Julien-le-Faucon in 2000, La Graverie in 2008, several reports from Hérouville-Saint-Clair, Cesny-Bois-Halbout in 2011, Vire in 2013, Saint-Gatien-des-Bois in December 2013 and Lasson in 2015.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=140&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The December 2013 Saint-Gatien file states the problem directly: GEIPAN classified it C because detailed information and independent corroboration were lacking. That result should not be confused with the much better documented November 2011 Saint-Gatien case, which was investigated, revisited and probably explained as projected light. Two reports from the same place can therefore have entirely different evidential value.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-12-08618" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A report usually becomes difficult or impossible to analyse when several basic elements are absent:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the time is approximate rather than recorded;
* the viewing direction or elevation is unknown;
* the witness cannot estimate angular size;
* no photograph, video or contemporaneous sketch exists;
* the account is submitted long after the event;
* there is only one observer and no independent confirmation;
* weather, aircraft or local-event records cannot be matched confidently;
* the description uses impressions such as “huge” or “very fast” without measurable reference points.

</div>

Under those conditions, investigators may be unable to distinguish among a star, aircraft, lantern, satellite, reflection, projected light or genuinely unusual event. Classification C therefore expresses an evidence gap, not a positive finding that conventional explanations have failed.

This is particularly important for historical Calvados claims. Older gendarmerie statements may preserve a sincere description but lack the exact geometry, photographs and environmental data now requested by GEIPAN. The survival of an official document makes the report historically real—it proves that someone reported an experience—but does not prove the witness’s interpretation of what caused it.

## What the official Calvados record actually establishes

The official portfolio supports three broad conclusions.

First, spectacular appearance is a poor guide to evidential strength. The Saint-Gatien lights looked stationary, intense and silent, yet detailed cloud analysis made a projector explanation probable. The Bayeux object seemed to hover, zigzag and shine a beam, but its navigation lights and behaviour pointed towards a drone. The Caen object appeared structured and airborne for several minutes, yet its path followed strong winds and matched rigid lightweight debris.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Second, local conditions matter. Calvados combines coastal cloud, clear maritime air, tourist and entertainment venues, busy aviation routes and densely populated areas around Caen. These conditions create repeated opportunities for ordinary lights or objects to appear unfamiliar. Low cloud can become a screen for projectors; clear air can hide the connecting beam; coastal perspective can distort distance; and aircraft, drones or lanterns can be difficult to recognise when seen only as lights.

Third, an official classification is not a declaration of absolute certainty. Category B explanations remain probabilistic, and Category C reports remain undecidable. Even Category A may identify a class of object—such as wind-driven debris—without recovering the particular item. The strength of the system lies in stating these limits rather than forcing every report into either “alien” or “debunked”.

The Calvados files consequently leave little support for a recent, well-documented core of unexplained incidents. The best-developed cases tend to acquire ordinary explanations, while many of the remaining unidentified entries are weak because their evidence cannot sustain detailed testing. That does not show that witnesses imagined their experiences. It shows that the experience of seeing something baffling and the question of what physically caused it are separate issues.

## The lasting lesson from the files

Official investigation has generally weakened, not strengthened, extraordinary readings of recent Calvados sightings. Lasers and event projectors account for some of the most dramatic luminous displays. Lanterns, trails, flares, atmospheric entries, drones and debris explain others. Where the necessary measurements are missing, GEIPAN usually avoids calling the case a genuine mystery and records it as insufficiently documented.

The 2011 Saint-Gatien revision is the clearest example of responsible uncertainty. An unexplained classification was changed after better meteorological analysis revealed that the original rejection of projected light rested on an incomplete picture of the cloud cover. The source device was never proved beyond doubt, so the final result remained probable rather than certain. That combination of revision, explanation and restraint is the most informative feature of Calvados’s official UFO record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2011-11-02855" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-GATIEN-DES-BOIS (14) 27.11.2011 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
