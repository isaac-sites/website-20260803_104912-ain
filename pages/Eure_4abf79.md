## Introduction

The wider official record is more sobering. Most reports from Eure are either too incomplete to resolve or have eventually been attributed to ordinary causes such as aircraft, lanterns, kites and camera reflections. The department therefore offers a useful cross-section of French UFO history: one famous but weakly archived legend, one unusually interesting military case, and numerous examples showing why witness testimony alone rarely settles what happened.

### What can reasonably be accepted?

There is good evidence that a report was made and publicised in August 1954. Miserey appears to have been a named, local witness rather than an anonymous storyteller invented decades later. The town, approximate time and broad description remained relatively stable in subsequent retellings. The case also predates France’s official UFO investigation service, so the absence of a modern GEIPAN file is unsurprising.

What is missing is more decisive. There is no surviving photograph, film, instrument reading or physical trace. The original statements of the supposed supporting witnesses are not readily available for comparison. Estimates such as the object’s altitude, distance and size were made without triangulation. Even the duration and number of smaller objects vary across accounts. The surviving record therefore establishes a striking report, not a verified aerial event of the dimensions later claimed.

Astronomical or meteorological explanations have occasionally been suggested in general discussions of the case, but no reconstruction has conclusively matched every reported feature. Conversely, the available evidence is too incomplete to justify rejecting ordinary explanations. A bright astronomical body, illuminated cloud, balloon, aircraft lights or several unrelated stimuli could have been interpreted as one structured scene. The proper assessment is **historically important but evidentially unresolved**.

## The 1978 Évreux case: a visual report with radar support

Eure’s most substantial official case occurred on 23 April 1978 at Base aérienne 105, the military airfield at Évreux-Fauville. Between 20:35 and 20:51 Universal Time, an operations duty officer reportedly saw a red luminous point between 10 and 15 degrees above the horizon, roughly north-west of the base. Personnel in the control tower also observed it as its brightness diminished.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-01879" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At 20:44, the base radar registered a weak return about 18 nautical miles away. The echo appeared to move towards the north-west at an estimated 30 to 40 knots and remained detectable for approximately seven minutes. The officer prepared a written report the following day. GEIPAN’s published reassessment rejects a straightforward helicopter or balloon identification and classifies the case as D1: unexplained after investigation, with moderate strangeness and reasonably good consistency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-01879" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This case matters because it did not begin with a casual observer who later contacted a UFO group. It arose inside an operational military environment, involved more than one visual observer and included an apparent instrument contact. Those features make it considerably stronger than a typical report of a distant light.

They do not make it conclusive. The radar recording itself has not been preserved, leaving only the written description. The return was rated at very low intensity, and a second radar station at Cinq-Mars-la-Pile did not confirm it. GEIPAN therefore acknowledges that a false or secondary radar echo cannot be excluded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-01879" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Venus was visible in roughly the same general sector that evening. GEIPAN nevertheless judged a direct confusion unlikely because the reported light was higher in the sky and apparently moved from an azimuth of about 300 to 330 degrees. That comparison weakens the simplest astronomical explanation, but it does not prove that the visual light and radar return were the same physical object. Their overlap in time and direction is suggestive rather than demonstrated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-01879" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The case is therefore best described as **Eure’s strongest officially unresolved sighting**, but not as a confirmed radar-tracked craft. Its value lies precisely in the tension between credible circumstances and inadequate surviving instrument data.

## What the wider GEIPAN record shows

France’s official investigation body, GEIPAN, was created within the national space agency CNES in 1977. It collects reports, investigates possible explanations and publishes case files. Its classifications distinguish identified cases, probable identifications, reports that cannot be resolved because information is insufficient, and the small minority that remain unexplained after investigation. Across its national archive, CNES reports that only about 3.3 per cent fall into the last category.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span></span></span>

Eure’s published cases follow that broader pattern. The archive contains interesting experiences, but very few withstand scrutiny as strong unknowns.

**Le Vaudreuil, 5 April 1978.** A factory guard reported a white ball making rapid movements high above a forest on five occasions before fading away. No supporting witness or further evidence was obtained, and the case was classified C because reliable information was lacking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-00505" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Bacqueville, 4 January 1994.** A frightened witness described a rotating cone with coloured bands, later flashes, bodily sensations and unusual behaviour by his dog. The report was once treated as unexplained, but GEIPAN’s later review downgraded it to C. Investigators considered a promotional searchlight, aircraft or helicopter, while noting that missing duration, angular measurements and disappearance details prevented a firm conclusion. The witness’s intense fear was also judged likely to have affected later perceptions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01342" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Évreux, 21 November 2018.** A driver on the N154 briefly saw a rounded form with red and green lights rise over a field and move away. GEIPAN considered aircraft or agricultural machinery plausible, particularly because the sighting occurred near the air base during the sugar-beet harvest. The late report meant that radar data could no longer be requested, so the file remained C rather than being declared a genuine unknown.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-11-50692" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These cases illustrate an important distinction. “Unresolved” can mean that an investigation found a well-documented phenomenon that resisted explanation, as in the 1978 air-base case. More often, it means that essential information was never collected. A C-classification is an evidence gap, not positive evidence for an extraordinary object.

## Ordinary explanations that repeatedly work

Several recent Eure investigations show how apparently structured or intelligently moving phenomena can emerge from familiar objects and optical effects.

At Berville-sur-Mer in June 2019, a witness filmed three intense red-orange lights passing silently at intervals of about five minutes. Their apparent speed initially seemed too high for floating lanterns. Video analysis, fading behaviour, wind direction and the timing on a summer weekend nevertheless led GEIPAN to conclude that they were very probably sky lanterns, likely closer to the witness than assumed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-06-50768?field_date_value=2019-08-31&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C65" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

At Verneuil d’Avre et d’Iton in March 2023, several people saw a dark, pointed triangular object apparently hovering for around five minutes. A photograph made the report look more substantial than a simple verbal account. Municipal police, however, confirmed that a kite had been flown from the grounds of the nearby Saint-Nicolas abbey. A police photograph matched the witness image, and investigators reconstructed the location by comparing sight lines.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-03-51425" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Another driver repeatedly filmed a moving light while travelling from Grand Bourgtheroulde towards Rouen in February 2023. Frame-by-frame analysis showed that the light appeared when an oncoming lorry’s powerful headlamps entered the image. The supposed object moved in step with a lens reflection across the camera’s optical centre. GEIPAN classified it as a photographic artefact, not an object in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-02-51517?field_date_value=2024-08-12&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=6" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Even an apparently dramatic physical trace has received a mundane interpretation. In October 1989 investigators examined a large burned area in a Normandy field connected with a witness report of intense blue light. Soil samples contained kerosene, and the official inquiry concluded that an accidental hydrocarbon fire was the likely cause. Although the GEIPAN database title preserves a geographical inconsistency between Brix and an Eure-linked witness file, the investigative lesson is clear: laboratory analysis can overturn the initial impression of a mysterious landing trace.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1989-10-01668" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why aviation matters so much in Eure


<div class="content-callout content-callout--key content-callout--takeaway" markdown="1">

Évreux-Fauville is a major military transport base, and aviation is an unavoidable part of interpreting sightings around central Eure. Aircraft seen almost head-on can seem stationary; landing or navigation lights can obscure the outline of the airframe; mist and low cloud can enlarge or diffuse lights; and a turning aircraft can appear to change shape or direction abruptly.

</div>

The base does not automatically explain every report. Indeed, its operational staff produced the department’s strongest unresolved file in 1978. But proximity to military flying makes aircraft the first hypothesis to test, not evidence that witnesses have seen secret or non-human technology. GEIPAN works with the French Air and Space Force, civil aviation, weather services and other institutions precisely because traffic records, radar data and atmospheric conditions are often decisive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

Radar also requires caution. It can supply range and movement data unavailable to the eye, but weak returns may arise from propagation effects, reflections, weather or equipment limitations. A visual sighting and a radar mark become compelling only when their time, direction, elevation and motion correspond closely and the original technical data survive. The 1978 Évreux incident approaches that standard but does not fully meet it because the echo was weak, unrecorded and unconfirmed elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-04-01879" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Vernon adds a different aerospace connection. The town housed an important post-war ballistic and aerodynamic research establishment, which encouraged later speculation that the 1954 sighting was somehow linked to military technology. That proximity is historically interesting, but no documentary evidence has surfaced showing that an experimental launch or classified aircraft caused the report. A nearby technical facility is a line of inquiry, not an explanation by itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufomotion.xyz/Documentaires-OVNI/galleries/file_uploader/pdf/Michel__Aim_____A_propos_des_Soucoupes_Volantes__MOC__1958_.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufomotion.xyz">[UFOmotion]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufomotion.xyz</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Michel Aim A propos des Soucoupes Volantes MOC 1958</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Michel Aim A propos des Soucoupes Volantes MOC 1958</span></span></span>

## How official investigation changes the story

The development of French investigation procedures is visible across Eure’s case history. The Vernon report entered the public record through newspapers and UFO writers, with little surviving technical documentation. By 1978, military reports could be forwarded to the newly created GEPAN, the predecessor of GEIPAN. Modern witnesses can submit questionnaires, photographs and video, while investigators reconstruct astronomy, weather, geography, air traffic and camera optics.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span></span></span>

Gendarmerie procedure emphasises exact time, duration, location, position against the landscape, shape, colour, movement and sound. Statements may be recorded in formal reports and passed to GEIPAN. The system reflects a central problem in UFO investigation: witnesses may be honest and observant while still misjudging distance, trajectory or size, especially when viewing an unfamiliar light without reference points.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

Eure’s reviewed files show that later investigation often weakens an original claim without accusing the witness of fabrication. The Bacqueville case moved from an unexplained category to insufficient evidence. The triangular Verneuil object became a kite after police confirmation. The Grand Bourgtheroulde light became a lens reflection when its movement was compared with oncoming headlights. These are not dismissals based on ridicule; they are examples of evidence changing the classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01342" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The fairest assessment of Eure’s UFO history

Eure does not display a well-supported series of craft landings, repeated pilot encounters or a sustained departmental “flap” comparable with France’s national wave of autumn 1954. Its reputation rests mainly on two episodes: Vernon in August 1954 and the Évreux-Fauville visual-radar case in April 1978.

Vernon is the more spectacular story but the weaker case. It is rooted in contemporary reporting and a named witness, yet lacks surviving independent statements, measurements or physical evidence. Its later fame owes much to influential UFO authors repeating and elaborating the original account.

Évreux-Fauville is less dramatic but more evidentially valuable. Military observers, prompt reporting and a radar return give it unusual weight. Its unresolved status is justified, although the faint, unpreserved echo and lack of confirmation prevent strong conclusions.

The rest of the department’s record is dominated by incomplete reports and successful identifications. Aircraft, lanterns, kites, agricultural activity, fear, poor viewing conditions and camera artefacts recur because they can produce experiences that are genuinely puzzling in the moment. Eure’s UFO history is therefore not a story of proof, but of gradations: a celebrated historical mystery, one serious official unknown, several weak cases and a growing body of investigations showing how much can be learned when photographs, witness geometry, environmental data and timely records are available.
