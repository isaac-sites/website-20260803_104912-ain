## Introduction

The Grandvillars–Delle black-sphere report remains the [Territoire de Belfort]({{ 'territoire-de-belfort/' | relative_url }})’s principal officially unresolved sighting, but it is unresolved for a limited and important reason: the evidence was too weak to support either identification or serious anomaly. At about 1.30 am on 14 April 2013, a motorist on the N1019 reported seeing a dark, apparently spherical object cross her view for no more than about five seconds before trees hid it. No passenger confirmed the sighting, no independent witness came forward, and no photograph, reliable angular measurement, speed estimate or subsequent physical record established what was present.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Territoire_de_Belfor_8990a1_grandvillars_black_s_77b66e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Black Sphere" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that examines unusual aerial reports, therefore placed the case in category C: not identified because reliable information and corroboration were missing. This is not the same as category D, which is used when a sufficiently documented case remains unexplained after investigation. The distinction makes this brief roadside observation more valuable as a lesson in evidential limits than as evidence of an extraordinary object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2021-08/GEIPAN-informationenglish.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## What the driver reported on the road to Delle

The witness was a 30-year-old woman returning home with her husband and son after a meal with friends near Illzach. She said that she was driving towards Delle on the N1019, roughly one or two kilometres from the Swiss border. On a gentle downhill section, she was travelling at about 90 kilometres per hour before slowing to around 70 during the observation. The night appeared clear, and lights from the nearby customs area illuminated part of the sky and landscape ahead.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

According to her written account, a black sphere appeared from the right, seemingly from behind trees about 200 metres ahead, and moved horizontally towards the left. She blinked several times because she initially wondered whether her vision was at fault, then braked for a better look. The object continued moving until it disappeared behind trees on the left. She estimated that the whole episode lasted five seconds or less.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

The structured questionnaire described one non-luminous, black, spherical object with an apparent size comparable to a bowl. Elsewhere in the same form, however, the witness estimated its distance at roughly one kilometre rather than 200 metres. Its speed was not measured, its altitude was not established, and no sound or environmental effect was documented.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

That difference between the narrative distance and the later estimate is not evidence that the witness was dishonest. It illustrates the difficulty of reconstructing a brief night-time observation from a moving car. A dark silhouette contains few visual cues from which to calculate range. Without a known distance, an apparent size such as “the size of a bowl” cannot be converted into an actual diameter: a small nearby bird, a larger distant object and a shadowed feature against the sky might produce comparable angular impressions.

The witness’s immediate reaction was cautious rather than sensational. She told her husband that she thought she might have hallucinated and later said that she wanted an explanation rather than asserting that she had seen an extraterrestrial vehicle. She also stated that she had not consumed alcohol, did not wear glasses, was not taking medication or drugs and had extensive driving experience. Those details make the report a sincere account worth recording, but they do not supply the missing measurements needed to identify the stimulus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

## Why the other occupants did not provide corroboration

Although the driver was not alone in the vehicle, the case still rests on a single observation. Her son was asleep in the rear, while her husband was struggling to stay awake and did not report seeing the sphere. The event was so brief that the driver had little opportunity to direct another person’s attention towards it before trees blocked the view. GEIPAN’s summary accordingly states that no other testimony was obtained.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

This matters because independent witnesses can test several parts of a sighting at once. Agreement about direction, shape, duration and movement would show that the perception was not confined to one observer. Differences between witnesses can also reveal whether reflections, fatigue or viewing position affected what each person saw. Here, the passengers establish that the journey took place, but not that an external sphere crossed the road or sky.

The absence of corroboration does not disprove the report. It does mean that the case cannot distinguish confidently between several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a real but ordinary object, such as a nocturnal bird seen in silhouette;
* a nearby object whose distance and size were misjudged;
* a fleeting visual effect involving trees, lighting or the windscreen;
* a momentary perceptual error associated with fatigue and night driving;
* or a physical object that cannot now be identified.

</div>

GEIPAN explicitly mentioned an illusion, a nocturnal bird or a fatigue-related visual problem as possibilities. It did not claim to have demonstrated any one of them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Missing measurements, witnesses and records

The public file is more substantial than a one-line anecdote because it contains a completed questionnaire, a hand-marked map and references to environmental photographs and a sketch. Yet almost every item capable of testing the sighting remains approximate or absent. The witness supplied a general location near the Delle interchange and customs area, but the material does not establish a measured line of sight, angular elevation, angular width, exact trajectory or reference object of known dimensions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

The most consequential gaps are straightforward:

**No usable size measurement.** “The size of a bowl” describes an apparent impression, but the questionnaire does not record whether that comparison was made at arm’s length or by some other method. Without angular size and reliable range, the object’s physical dimensions cannot be calculated.

**No reliable distance.** The account refers both to something about 200 metres ahead and to an estimated object distance of one kilometre. Neither figure was triangulated or tied to a fixed point on the landscape.

**No speed calculation.** A leftward passage lasting only a few seconds sounds rapid, but apparent motion depends on distance, observer movement and viewing angle. The witness herself was driving at motorway speed and then braking, creating additional relative movement.

**No independent record.** There was no photograph or video of the object, no radar report, no trace, and no second witness who observed it. The two environment photographs listed in the questionnaire could assist with orientation, but they do not record the event itself.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20terre-R570.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Questionnaire terre R570</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Questionnaire terre R570</span></span></span>

**No completed official cross-check.** GEIPAN stated that it had not received the expected gendarmerie report when it assessed the case. That missing document might have fixed the date of the initial declaration, preserved additional questioning or confirmed whether local checks were attempted. The currently published file still presents only the questionnaire as its documentary attachment and retains the category C classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The file’s later website update does not appear to have strengthened the underlying evidence. GEIPAN’s current case page gives an update date of 23 June 2021, but the description, classification and documentary list remain essentially unchanged: the event is still labelled as lacking reliable information, with no published independent witness statement or later identification. The safest conclusion is that the administrative record was refreshed without a decisive new evidential development.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/7946" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why ordinary explanations cannot be tested properly

GEIPAN wrote that the black sphere did not fit an obvious “classic” misidentification, but also judged the case to be of low relative strangeness. That combination can sound contradictory until the limits of the observation are considered. The report did not resemble the most familiar luminous cases involving planets, aircraft lights, meteors or lanterns because the object was described as completely black and non-luminous. Yet its behaviour was not especially exotic: it followed a straight path, showed no abrupt acceleration, produced no reported effects and vanished behind ordinary landscape cover rather than disappearing in open sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A bird is therefore plausible without being proven. At night, a bird crossing a brighter patch of sky can appear as a featureless black shape. Its outline may look rounded during a brief glimpse, particularly when wing movement is not resolved. Distance compression, peripheral vision and the observer’s own motion can make a nearby animal seem larger or farther away. Nothing in the file confirms a bird, however, because the record lacks a precise silhouette, flight pattern, wing detail or repeat observation.

A windscreen-related effect is similarly possible but untestable. Reflections usually involve light rather than an opaque black sphere, so the description does not point neatly to a standard dashboard reflection. Nevertheless, the witness was looking through glass while travelling past illuminated infrastructure, trees and customs lights. Without reconstruction photographs showing the exact sightline, interior lighting and windscreen geometry, an optical artefact cannot be ruled in or out.

Fatigue deserves consideration because the journey followed a long day and the sighting occurred at 1.30 am. GEIPAN specifically treated fatigue as a possible weakness in the testimony. Fatigue does not automatically produce hallucinations, nor does it invalidate a witness’s account. It can, however, reduce attention, slow visual processing and make an ambiguous stimulus harder to interpret during a glimpse lasting only seconds. In this case it is a risk factor, not an explanation established by evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The central problem is therefore not that investigators exhausted all ordinary possibilities and found them inadequate. It is that none of the possibilities could be tested to a useful standard. The same missing data that prevent an ordinary identification also prevent a strong claim that the sphere behaved anomalously.

## Why category C does not mean a strong mystery

GEIPAN’s classification system separates the strangeness of a report from the consistency of its evidence. Category A is used where an explanation is effectively established, category B where an explanation is probable, and categories D1 or D2 where a sufficiently supported case remains unexplained. Category C is reserved for reports that are not workable because reliable data are lacking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2021-08/GEIPAN-informationenglish.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This distinction is particularly important for the Grandvillars case. In everyday language, “unidentified” may suggest that investigators encountered something resistant to explanation. In category C, it often means that the evidential process stopped earlier: the available information did not permit investigators to compare hypotheses confidently.

GEIPAN’s own assessment placed the case low on both practical consistency and strangeness. The observation was made by one person, from a moving vehicle, for a few seconds, after a long day. The object’s movement was straight rather than erratic, and its disappearance behind trees offered no anomalous endpoint. GEIPAN concluded that this limited degree of strangeness did not justify a deeper field investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55945" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

That judgement should not be misrepresented as a debunking. GEIPAN did not establish that the witness saw a bird, suffered a visual disturbance or misread a reflection. Equally, it did not validate the sphere as a structured craft. Category C preserves both uncertainties: the reported perception is unexplained, but the case does not contain enough dependable information to make that unexplained status scientifically weighty.

CNES’s broader figures show why the distinction matters. Its current GEIPAN summary places nearly a third of archived phenomena in the “unidentified for lack of data” group, compared with only a small percentage that remain unidentified after investigation. A category C entry is therefore a common outcome of incomplete observation, not a special official endorsement of extraordinary origin.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

## What would have changed the assessment

A stronger case would not necessarily have required dramatic physical evidence. Several ordinary records could have made the report much more testable.

A second witness who independently described the same direction and shape would have reduced the possibility of a momentary individual visual error. A measured reconstruction from the exact road position could have established the object’s angular path against trees, road signs or customs buildings. A precise arm’s-length size comparison would have allowed an approximate angular diameter. Clear information about elevation, duration and the points where the object first and last appeared could then have supported basic speed-and-distance scenarios.

The gendarmerie report could also have mattered, especially if it contained a near-contemporaneous account taken before memory changed or the witness read other interpretations online. Traffic or customs records might have established whether another observer reported anything unusual. Even a negative search would have helped define the limits of corroboration.

None of those additions would automatically have produced an extraordinary conclusion. They might instead have supported a bird, a nearby object or an optical effect. Their value lies in making competing explanations testable. Because they are missing, the case has remained frozen at the level of a sincere but poorly constrained personal observation.

## The case’s place in Territoire de Belfort UFO history

Within the small published GEIPAN record for the Territoire de Belfort, the Grandvillars–Delle sighting attracts attention because it is the department’s category C case. It should not, however, be treated as the department’s strongest unexplained encounter in the sense normally implied by that phrase. The official database describes the phenomenon itself as a lack-of-reliable-information case, and an independent map based on GEIPAN’s open data lists Grandvillars as having no category D event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=title&amp;page=1&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas[N1019] de GRANDVILLARS (90) vers DELLE (90) 14.04.2013, 14/04/2013, C, Territoire de Belfort, Manque d&#x27;information...</span></span></span>

Its real importance is methodological. The witness provided a reasonably detailed and apparently candid narrative, including her own initial doubt. Yet a detailed story is not the same thing as a measured event. The decisive quantities—distance, angular size, speed, altitude and independent confirmation—were either estimated inconsistently or not recorded at all.

The black sphere therefore remains unsolved in the narrowest sense: nobody can now say with confidence what produced the perception. But the surviving evidence does not establish an unusual vehicle, advanced technology or even a clearly bounded aerial object. It supports only a brief observation of an ambiguous dark form beside the N1019, followed by an official conclusion that the information was insufficient. That makes the case unresolved, but weakly unresolved rather than strongly mysterious.
