## Introduction

The most important episode occurred around Authon-du-Perche in March 1980, when several gendarmes reported luminous objects and briefly gave the case unusual authority. Decades later, however, the French official investigation service GEIPAN concluded that the first phase was most probably Venus, while the remaining phases lacked enough reliable information for a firm judgement. That mixture—sincere witnesses, striking descriptions and weak measurement—is typical of Eure-et-Loir’s record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The first saucer stories reached Chartres in 1952

Eure-et-Loir’s modern UFO story began in the early 1950s, when reports of “flying saucers” were spreading through French and international newspapers. A local historical account based on coverage in *L’Écho Républicain* records several observations on 4 June 1952 around Chartres and Ymonville. Workers near Chartres Cathedral reportedly saw a white disc climb rapidly while rotating. Elsewhere, witnesses described a silvery, S-shaped trail, while a woman at Ymonville reported a pale yellow disc moving silently and leaving small flakes behind it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

These stories matter less as proof of extraordinary craft than as evidence of how quickly a new visual language took hold. “Disc”, “saucer” and “mysterious machine” became ready-made descriptions for lights, vapour trails and distant aerial objects. Even the surviving local account preserves disagreement at the time: one observer rejected an aircraft explanation, while another compared the trail with those left by wartime fighters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

The reporting also needs to be treated cautiously. The cases survive chiefly through retrospective summaries of newspaper coverage rather than complete witness interviews, weather records or original technical investigations. Eure-et-Loir’s digitised and catalogued local press holdings are extensive, covering Chartres, Dreux, Châteaudun, Nogent-le-Rotrou and surrounding districts, but locating an article is not the same as confirming the event it describes. Newspapers preserve what witnesses said; they do not necessarily establish what was in the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://presselocaleancienne.bnf.fr/html/villes-28-eure-et-loir" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: presselocaleancienne.bnf.fr">[Presse Ancienne]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">presselocaleancienne.bnf.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Presse Ancienne Villes, villages d&#x27;Eure-et-Loir &#124; Presse locale ancienne</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Presse Ancienne Villes, villages d&#x27;Eure-et-Loir &#124; Presse locale ancienne</span></span></span>

## The 1965 Dreux sighting gained authority from its witness

The department’s best-known early case involved Alexandre Ananoff, a respected writer and advocate of astronautics. On 19 August 1965, Ananoff and other witnesses near Dreux reportedly watched an intense light in a clear sky. Seen through binoculars, it appeared brighter than Venus, translucent and very fast. A second passage was reported later that evening, drawing national press and broadcast interest.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

The case became memorable because Ananoff was regarded as technically informed and anything but fanciful. Yet expertise in astronautics did not provide objective distance, altitude or speed. The air base at Évreux, which had reportedly registered two passages, suggested a weather balloon, rocket or satellite. Ananoff disputed those possibilities because the reported motion seemed too fast or irregular. The surviving account therefore leaves a genuine disagreement, not a confirmed exotic object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

Later retellings did not add radar plots, photographs or independently preserved tracking data. The case remains historically interesting because a prominent witness gave the story credibility, but its evidential strength is limited by the same problem found in many visual sightings: apparent speed cannot be measured reliably when the object’s distance is unknown.

## Authon-du-Perche in 1980: the department’s most instructive case

On 10 March 1980, an automobilist reportedly alerted gendarmes to a strange light between Les Étilleux and Nogent-le-Rotrou. At about 10.10 pm, a gendarme at Authon-du-Perche then saw a bright ball or disc with his wife. Other officers joined him, describing colour changes from orange to red and apparent movements punctuated by stops. Four gendarmes later drove towards the reported area and observed further red-orange lights, followed by what they described from a stationary position as a silent, low, oval luminous object with several distinct regions of light.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At first glance, this is the kind of account that seems difficult to dismiss. There were trained officers, multiple observers and a formal gendarmerie record. A private research group also interviewed witnesses and produced drawings. For years, the case was treated as unexplained.

GEIPAN’s later reassessment substantially weakened that interpretation. Investigators divided the evening into three separate phases rather than assuming that every witness had followed one continuous object. The first phase, seen from the gendarmerie, was judged very probably to have been Venus low on the horizon. Atmospheric turbulence, the visual illusion in which a stationary light appears to move, binocular defects and one observer’s astigmatism were all considered capable of producing the reported changes in movement, colour and shape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The other two phases were not positively explained, but neither were they retained as strong unexplained cases. GEIPAN found that the gendarmerie report combined the witnesses’ statements rather than preserving separate accounts, making it impossible to measure how closely their descriptions really agreed. Basic information such as exact directions, angular heights and apparent dimensions was missing. The private report also contained confusing or contradictory details gathered under conditions GEIPAN could not verify. The second and third phases were therefore placed in category C: not analysable because reliable data were insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This distinction is central to understanding Eure-et-Loir’s UFO history. “Not explained” is not the same as “evidence of an extraordinary craft”. In the [Authon case]({{ 'authon-case/' | relative_url }}), one phase received a conventional explanation and the rest became evidentially indeterminate. The later investigation weakened the original unified story while leaving open the narrow possibility that the travelling officers saw something not recoverable from the surviving record.

## Weak cases remain weak even when the description is dramatic

The Saussay report of 17 March 1986 is a good example. At about 4 am, one witness described a slowly moving luminous sphere that appeared to illuminate roughly 1,000 square metres of ground. The sighting lasted about 20 seconds. No corroborating testimony or further useful information was obtained, and GEIPAN classified it as category C because reliable data were lacking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1986-03-01090?field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=%2C436&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAUSSAY (28) 17.03.1986 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The description sounds impressive because it includes apparent ground illumination, a feature often treated as evidence that a light was nearby and physically powerful. Yet without angular measurements, a defined viewing direction, meteorological information, other witnesses or a trace at the site, even that detail cannot establish distance or size. Bright lights can appear to illuminate terrain, while memory may enlarge the estimated area after a brief nocturnal observation.

A later case at Rohaire shows how delay further reduces evidential value. A witness reported in 2014 that, two years earlier, he had watched an intense red-and-white light apparently climb, descend, change direction and divide. GEIPAN noted that the questionnaire was incomplete, the duration and speed were not supplied, and photographs mentioned by the witness were never submitted despite requests. The case was classified C, meaning that no responsible conclusion could be reached.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-11-08711?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=%2C28&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ROHAIRE (28) 29.11.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These cases are unresolved only in the limited sense that the source object cannot now be identified. They are not strong residual mysteries. Missing information is itself part of the result.

## Modern investigations often identify ordinary causes

More recent Eure-et-Loir cases show how photographs, astronomy software and environmental checks can turn a puzzling report into a conventional explanation.

In August 2012, two people travelling from Chartres towards Caen saw a bright yellow-orange light low on the horizon. The light seemed to travel with their car, and one witness photographed it. GEIPAN compared its colour, position, separation from the Sun, cloud conditions and visible streak with a parhelion, commonly called a sun dog. The case was classified A: explained without ambiguity. The apparent following motion was consistent with a distant atmospheric phenomenon remaining in roughly the same direction as the vehicle moved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-09252" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DE CHARTRES (28) VERS CAEN (14) 31.08.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Dreux in May 2018, a mobile-phone video showed a bright object moving rapidly across a storm-lit sky. GEIPAN found that the spot repeatedly appeared opposite the Sun across the centre of the image, the signature of an internal lens reflection. As the phone panned, the reflected spot seemed to cross the frame at unusual speed. The witness’s family disputed the interpretation and maintained that something had first been seen directly, but GEIPAN concluded that the recorded anomaly itself was a photographic artefact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2018-05-50594" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DREUX (28) 31.05.2018 TI1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Romilly-sur-Aigre in April 2019, two white, steady spheres crossed the sky together for about five seconds. Their straight movement with the wind, silent flight, fixed light and paired formation led investigators to conclude that they were very probably illuminated balloons.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-04-50749" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ROMILLY-SUR-AIGRE (28) 24.04.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Le Thieulin in March 2022, a witness reported a brilliant light making irregular jumps, first with the naked eye and later through binoculars. GEIPAN reconstructed the viewing directions and identified Sirius during the first observation and Arcturus during the second. Apparent darting by bright stars can result from atmospheric disturbance and involuntary eye movement, particularly when there is no nearby fixed reference point.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-03-51333?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=11&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=11%2C37&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">THIEULIN (LE) (28) 21.03.2022 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Together, these cases show that photographs do not automatically strengthen a UFO claim. A photograph can reveal a sun dog or lens reflection, but it can also introduce an anomaly that was never present in the sky.

## Did the department’s air bases create a military UFO connection?

Eure-et-Loir has a strong aviation history. Dreux-Louvilliers was a major United States Air Forces in Europe and NATO installation during the Cold War, built in the early 1950s and occupied until the American departure in 1967. The base supported a substantial military and civilian community and staged flying displays that made unfamiliar aircraft a visible part of local life.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://memoire.ciclic.fr/magazine/autour-des-archives/dreux-air-base-une-base-americaine-en-eure-et-loir" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: memoire.ciclic.fr">[Mémoire]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">memoire.ciclic.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mémoire&quot;Dreux Air Base&quot;: une base américaine en Eure-et-Loir &#124; Ciclic</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mémoire&quot;Dreux Air Base&quot;: une base américaine en Eure-et-Loir &#124; Ciclic</span></span></span>

Chartres-Champhol and Châteaudun also gave the department long-standing military aviation links. This background makes aircraft, exercises, navigation lights and distant airfield activity relevant possibilities when assessing local reports. It also helps explain why some witnesses described objects in technical or military terms.

However, the available evidence does not support a strong department-wide pattern of UFOs being tracked by local military radar or repeatedly approaching these bases. The 1965 Dreux story includes a reported response from Évreux air base, but no accessible primary radar record has emerged to establish an unidentified craft. The main Authon-du-Perche file involved gendarmes rather than military detection, and GEIPAN’s reassessment relied on witness geometry and astronomical reconstruction, not secret defence material.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

The military connection is therefore contextual rather than evidential. Eure-et-Loir had many opportunities for unusual aircraft to be seen, but proximity to an air base does not by itself make a light mysterious or imply military confirmation.

## What GEIPAN classifications actually mean

GEIPAN, a technical unit of the French space agency CNES, sorts reported unidentified aerospace phenomena into four broad categories. Category A is explained without ambiguity. Category B indicates that an explanation is considered very probable. Category C means that the report cannot be analysed properly because information is missing. Category D is reserved for observations that remain unexplained despite the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This system prevents two common errors. The first is treating every report sent to an official body as officially unexplained. The second is treating category C as equivalent to category D. In Eure-et-Loir, several apparently dramatic cases are category C because the files are too weak, not because investigators found strong evidence resistant to normal explanation.

## Why open farmland produces convincing sky mysteries

Much of Eure-et-Loir consists of broad agricultural landscapes with long, relatively unobstructed horizons. Such terrain can improve visibility, but it also makes distance difficult to judge. A bright planet near the horizon may seem close to a road or field. A distant light may appear to keep pace with a moving car. Atmospheric turbulence can make stars change colour or move. Isolated lights have few surrounding objects against which their speed and height can be measured.

Several recurring explanations in the department follow directly from these viewing conditions:

**Bright planets and stars.** Venus, Sirius and Arcturus can appear unusually intense and unstable, especially low in the sky.

**Atmospheric optics.** Ice crystals can create bright patches beside the Sun that resemble a luminous object travelling with the observer.

**Small airborne lights.** Illuminated balloons can move silently in groups and retain a steady glow unlike conventional aircraft lights.

**Optical artefacts.** Mobile-phone lenses produce reflections that move in ways unrelated to objects in the sky.

**Aircraft and aviation activity.** Military and civil flying have long been part of the department’s environment, although no specific aircraft explanation should be asserted without matching time, route and lighting data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-09252" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">DE CHARTRES (28) VERS CAEN (14) 31.08.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These explanations do not imply that witnesses were dishonest. GEIPAN itself treats human testimony as the basis of its investigations while recognising that perception, unfamiliar phenomena and incomplete recollection can add strangeness to an ordinary source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What remains genuinely uncertain

Eure-et-Loir does contain cases that cannot now be identified. The later phases of the Authon-du-Perche episode, the Saussay sphere and the Rohaire light remain without a demonstrated source. But their status should be described precisely: they are data-poor, not strong examples of a phenomenon defeating a comprehensive investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The older Chartres, Ymonville, Dreux and Beauce stories are similarly valuable as local history but uneven as evidence. They reveal the influence of the early saucer era, the authority granted to technically respected witnesses and the role of newspapers in turning fleeting observations into enduring narratives. The reported 1976 sighting near Louville-la-Chenard, for example, survives in local historical commentary but presently lacks the detailed, accessible case record needed for a reliable modern assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://alaindenizet.fr/soucoupes-volantes-en-eure-et-loir/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: alaindenizet.fr">[Alain Denizet]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">alaindenizet.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Alain Denizet Soucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Alain DenizetSoucoupes volantes en Eure-et-Loir - Alain Denizet...</span></span></span>

A stronger unresolved case would require more than sincerity or official occupation. It would need separately recorded witness statements, exact times and directions, angular measurements, original photographs or video, weather and astronomical checks, and preferably independent radar or physical evidence. None of the department’s best-known cases currently combines those elements.

## The fairest assessment of Eure-et-Loir’s UFO history

Eure-et-Loir is not a major centre of unexplained French UFO activity. Its importance lies in showing how local UFO history is actually built: a striking sighting enters the press, a credible witness gives it weight, an incomplete official record preserves part of the story, and later analysis either identifies a normal cause or discovers that essential data were never collected.

The Authon-du-Perche case is the clearest example. Multiple gendarmes did report unusual lights, so the event should not be reduced to invention. Yet the strongest later analysis separated the observations, identified Venus in the opening phase and judged the remaining descriptions too poorly documented to support a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/48513" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">AUTHON-DU-PERCHE (28) 10.03.1980 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Across the department, later reporting has generally weakened rather than strengthened extraordinary interpretations. Explained astronomical, atmospheric and photographic cases now form the clearest part of the official record. What remains unresolved consists mainly of short, isolated or retrospectively reported observations. The balanced conclusion is therefore neither that “nothing happened” nor that unexplained craft were established, but that ordinary phenomena, sincere misperception and weak documentation account for most of what can presently be assessed.
