## Introduction

The most instructive Puy-de-Dôme cases were eventually linked to Venus, aircraft, meteors, distant lightning, reflections, lanterns or probable balloons. Other reports remain unclassified in practical terms because too little reliable information survived. That distinction matters: an unresolved file is not necessarily evidence of an extraordinary object. In many local cases, it means that investigators lacked photographs, precise directions, corroborating witnesses or timely meteorological and aviation data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/projets/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

## What the official record actually shows

France’s official body for these reports is GEIPAN, a unit of the national space agency CNES. It collects testimony, consults the police, gendarmerie, aviation authorities, military air-operations services, meteorologists and technical specialists, and publishes anonymised case files. GEIPAN deliberately uses the broader term “unidentified aerial phenomenon” because witnesses may have seen a light, atmospheric effect or optical artefact rather than a physical flying object. Its remit is to explain reported strangeness, not to investigate extraterrestrial visitation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## Clermont-Ferrand, 1991: several witnesses, but still Venus

The early-morning sighting of 19 September 1991 is probably the department’s most useful case for understanding how witness credibility differs from identification. At about 5.20 am, a Clermont-Ferrand resident noticed a brilliant stationary point in the eastern or south-eastern sky. He watched it again after reaching home and contacted the gendarmerie at Cournon-d’Auvergne. A duty gendarme saw the light for several minutes, another caller independently reported it, and the Clermont-Ferrand police station also became involved. The principal witness continued watching for nearly an hour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete57.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This was therefore not a casual, unsupported tale. GEIPAN considered the case consistent and explicitly stated that the sincerity of the witnesses, including the police and gendarmerie personnel, was not in doubt. Yet their descriptions were also highly diagnostic: a fixed, star-like white light, very small in apparent size, visible for a long period in a clear sky and showing no detail through binoculars.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete57.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

When GEIPAN re-examined the old file in 2013, astronomical reconstruction placed Venus in the reported direction. At the time it was exceptionally bright, with a magnitude of about −4.4, and its position, colour and apparent behaviour closely matched the testimony. GEIPAN reclassified the case from an inadequately resolved category to category A: a definite misidentification of Venus. The conclusion did not accuse anyone of inventing the event. The witnesses accurately saw a bright phenomenon but interpreted a familiar planet as something unusual, probably under the combined effects of darkness, fatigue and surprise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete57.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This case matters because multiple witnesses do not automatically make an extraordinary explanation more likely. Several people sharing the same viewpoint can independently see the same real light and still make the same understandable mistake.

## The aviation connection was real, but not mysterious

One of the most striking regional reports occurred on 20 September 1956, when two military pilots flying near Brioude, south of Puy-de-Dôme, noticed a large aluminium-coloured spherical object. They climbed in an attempt to approach it but were unable to close the distance and abandoned the effort after roughly twenty minutes. Taken alone, the description could sound like a notable military encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1956-09-09134" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO MIL] BRIOUDE (43) 20.09.1956 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The crucial evidence came from Clermont-Ferrand. The director of the Puy-de-Dôme observatory confirmed that a large translucent balloon had been visible over the area throughout the day, drifting slowly at an altitude of approximately 20 kilometres. Its origin was not established, and neither the observatory nor the regional weather services claimed responsibility for releasing it. Nevertheless, GEIPAN judged the phenomenon to be a stratospheric balloon and classified the case B.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1956-09-09134" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO MIL] BRIOUDE (43) 20.09.1956 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The pilots’ inability to catch it was not considered evidence of extraordinary performance. The balloon remained many kilometres away, so changes in the aircraft’s position did little to alter its apparent bearing. A distant object against an almost featureless sky can also seem to accompany an aircraft because the observer lacks reliable visual cues for distance and relative motion. The mystery that remained was the balloon’s operator, not the physical nature of the object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1956-09-09134" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[AERO MIL] BRIOUDE (43) 20.09.1956 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A more recent aviation-linked case shows how much modern records can add. At Espinasse on 12 October 2023, a witness saw intense white lights moving rapidly across the sky and captured a limited video. GEIPAN used information from France’s national air-operations centre and identified the lights as four military aircraft. The file was classified A. This is an important contrast with older reports: access to flight and radar information can turn an impressive-looking light formation into an ordinary, verifiable movement of aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-10-51478" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANESPINASSE (63) 12.10.2023 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Billom and Sauxillanges: unresolved because the data are weak

Not every Puy-de-Dôme report has received a firm explanation. Near Billom in February 1976, a motorist described a brilliant red sphere moving rapidly from north to south at apparently low altitude. The observation lasted only a few seconds. The original police record contained uncertainty even about the exact date, while other people questioned in the area reported seeing nothing unusual. GEIPAN classified the case C because there was not enough dependable information to test possible explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-02-00289" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBILLOM (63) 17.02.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The label does not mean that investigators recognised an unexplainable craft. A brief red light could have several ordinary causes, including an aircraft, meteor, flare or distant light seen under misleading conditions. With no precise angular measurements, confirmed timing, photograph or independent corroboration, choosing between them would be speculation.

The same evidential problem affected a report made by two motorists travelling from Sauxillanges towards Collanges on 23 September 2012. They described several round luminous objects appearing and disappearing among clouds. Investigators considered metallic balloons reflecting sunlight: the cloudy weather, wind direction and apparently slow relative movement were compatible with that idea. But no balloon release or independent observation could be confirmed, so GEIPAN retained category C rather than treating the hypothesis as proven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-09-08372" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D996] DE SAUXILLANGES (63) VERS COLLANGES (63) 23.09.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These cases illustrate the most easily misunderstood part of UFO classification. “Unidentified” may describe the limits of the file rather than the properties of the phenomenon. The less precise the time, direction, duration and apparent size, the wider the range of possible conventional causes becomes.

## Repeated explanations across the department

Puy-de-Dôme’s geography gives witnesses broad views over ridges, plateaux and open countryside, but those views can also distort distance and movement. Lights seen against a dark or cloudy sky lack the reference points needed to judge their height, speed or size. The official cases repeatedly show the same small group of explanations.

**Astronomical objects.** Besides the 1991 Venus case, witnesses around Billom and Saint-Julien-de-Coppel reported a slowly moving, multicoloured, scintillating point in September 1997. It remained visible for about an hour and a half and was assessed as the probable course of a celestial object. Atmospheric turbulence near the horizon can make a bright star or planet flash different colours and appear unstable.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1997-09-01476" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBILLOM (63) 1997 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Reflections in glass.** On 1 January 2008, a motorist photographed the Puy de Dôme through a car window. A month later he noticed an orange luminous shape in the image. GEIPAN identified it as a reflection from an interior vehicle light, such as the roof lamp. The case demonstrates why a photograph can be less reliable than it seems: cameras faithfully record reflections and internal optical effects that the photographer did not notice at the time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-01-02215" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[A89] de CLERMONT-FERRAND (63) vers BRIVE-LA-GAILLARDE (19) 01.01.2008 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Lanterns and balloons.** A red light seen from the road between Riom and Pérignat-lès-Sarliève in November 2016 appeared to rise above the Gergovie plateau before vanishing. Its silent movement and compatibility with the measured wind led GEIPAN to classify it B as a probable paper lantern. The conclusion remained probabilistic because no release was directly traced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2016-11-09592" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Meteors.** At Chauriat in December 2019, a bright white-yellow point appeared and disappeared rapidly before dawn. GEIPAN classified it A as a meteor. Such events can appear surprisingly low, large or nearby, although they are generally many kilometres above the observer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-12-50877?field_date_value=2019-08-31&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;page=%2C63" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Distant thunderstorms.** At Lachaux, a series of horizon flashes recorded during 2023 was compared with lightning archives. GEIPAN found storms roughly 100 kilometres away during most of the observations. From high ground with an open northern view, distant lightning can illuminate ridges or clouds while the thunder remains inaudible. The case was classified A, although investigators openly recorded inconsistencies affecting two of the individual dates rather than hiding the residual uncertainty.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-12-51521" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Was there a distinct Puy-de-Dôme “flap”?

France experienced a famous nationwide wave of flying-saucer reports in the autumn of 1954, and the Auvergne region was not untouched. A report was associated with Gelles on 24 September, alongside sightings elsewhere in central and south-western France. However, accessible official material does not establish a well-documented, department-wide Puy-de-Dôme cluster comparable with the heavily publicised concentrations in several other parts of France.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vague_d%27observations_de_l%27automne_1954" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Vague d&#x27;observations de l&#x27;automne 1954</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Vague d&#x27;observations de l&#x27;automne 1954</span></span></span>

This absence should be handled cautiously. Newspaper archives, police records and later UFO catalogues do not always overlap, and many early press stories were too brief to reconstruct properly. Local reports may have circulated without entering the files later inherited by GEIPAN. Conversely, newspapers during national waves often repeated claims without follow-up, making an apparent cluster look stronger than the surviving evidence warrants.

The safer conclusion is that Puy-de-Dôme participated in France’s broader UFO culture but has no securely established local flap built around numerous high-quality, mutually reinforcing reports. The department’s better-preserved history becomes clearer from the 1970s onwards, when gendarmerie records and later GEIPAN reviews provided a more traceable documentary trail.

## What makes a local case persuasive

A credible witness is valuable, but credibility alone cannot determine what was seen. Puy-de-Dôme’s cases show that police officers, pilots and careful observers can report accurately while misjudging identity, distance or motion. The strongest investigations combine testimony with external checks rather than treating professional status as proof.

A locally reported phenomenon becomes more evidentially useful when it has:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* a precise time and viewing direction;
* several genuinely independent witnesses at separated locations;
* original photographs or video with metadata;
* radar, air-traffic or military-flight information;
* weather, lightning and astronomical records;
* consistent estimates of elevation, duration and apparent movement;
* prompt reporting before memories and timings begin to drift.

</div>

The Clermont-Ferrand Venus case had several [credible witnesses]({{ 'explained-cases-d41807/' | relative_url }}) but was resolved because its direction, duration and appearance could be reconstructed. The 1956 pilot report was explained because the observatory supplied an independent balloon observation. Espinasse was resolved through air-operations data. By contrast, Billom and Sauxillanges remained category C because the surviving information could not confirm or eliminate plausible ordinary causes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Notes%20d%27enquete57.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The balanced verdict

Puy-de-Dôme’s UFO history is interesting not because it supplies compelling evidence of exotic craft, but because it preserves examples of how unusual experiences are created, reported and tested. The department has sincere witnesses, police involvement, military and aviation connections, photographs, videos and unresolved reports. Yet the best-investigated cases generally became less mysterious as independent data accumulated.

The 1991 Clermont-Ferrand case is the clearest demonstration: several witnesses, including law-enforcement personnel, saw the same striking light, but later astronomical reconstruction identified Venus. The 1956 military encounter was reduced to a distant stratospheric balloon. Modern cases have been matched with aircraft, lightning, meteors and reflections. The remaining category C reports are weakly evidenced rather than strongly anomalous.

The fairest assessment is therefore that Puy-de-Dôme has a real and varied history of UFO reporting, but no surviving landmark incident whose evidence presently demands an extraordinary explanation. Its most valuable cases are those that reveal the gap between an honestly experienced mystery and a phenomenon that remains mysterious after careful investigation.

    var url = template.replace('{query}', encodeQuery(query || '')).replace('{query_plain}', String(query || ''));
    var joiner = url.indexOf('?') >= 0 ? '&' : '?';
    var params = [];
    if (cfg.mkrid) params.push('mkrid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.mkrid));
    if (cfg.campaign_id) params.push('campid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.campaign_id));
    params.push('mkevt=1');
    params.push('mkcid=1');
    params.push('toolid=' + encodeURIComponent(cfg.tool_id || '10001'));
    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
