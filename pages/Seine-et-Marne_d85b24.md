## Introduction

The wider record is instructive precisely because its outcomes differ. A brilliant “craft” seen from Meaux proved to be Venus; orange lights over Courtry were probably festive lanterns; green discs filmed at Mitry-Mory were judged likely to be projected on clouds. Seine-et-Marne also lay beneath the celebrated 5 November 1990 French “UFO wave”, now firmly identified as the atmospheric re-entry of a Soviet rocket stage. Together, these cases show how witness sincerity, aviation experience and even police involvement can strengthen a report without automatically identifying what was seen.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the departmental record actually contains

France’s official body for investigating unusual aerial reports is GEIPAN, a technical service of the national space agency CNES. It uses the broader term “unidentified aerospace phenomenon” because many reports involve lights, atmospheric effects or apparent shapes rather than demonstrably solid flying objects. GEIPAN classifies cases as A when securely explained, B when an explanation is highly probable, C when the information is inadequate, and D when the phenomenon remains unexplained after investigation. A D classification means “unexplained with the available evidence”, not “alien spacecraft”.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The published Seine-et-Marne files include all four kinds of outcome. They range from an explained astronomical observation at Meaux and probable lanterns at Courtry to information-poor reports from places such as Sivry-Courtry, Jablines, Gouvernes, Vaux-le-Pénil and Savigny-le-Temple. The database also contains the D-classified Ussy-sur-Marne and AF3532 cases. This uneven mixture argues against treating every departmental entry as evidence of the same underlying phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=136&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 1989 &#124; --/06/1989 &#124; B &#124; Seine-et-Marne &#124; Phénomène identifié non indiqué &#124; 22/03/2007 &#124; 1989-06-01SIVRY-COURTRY (77) 04.05.199...</span></span></span>

Nor does the public record demonstrate that Seine-et-Marne is a special national hotspot. The department is large, populous and crossed by busy air routes around the Paris region. It contains airfields and aviation facilities, but proximity to aviation can generate both valuable professional observations and more opportunities to mistake aircraft, balloons, lights or distant atmospheric effects. A statistical study of French reports found that population density and several environmental or industrial variables influenced their geographical distribution; it did not identify Seine-et-Marne as one of the principal unexplained residual clusters.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.00571" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: arxiv.org">[arXiv]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">arxiv.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on arxiv.org.</span></span></span>

## The 5 November 1990 “wave”: dramatic, widespread and explained

On the evening of 5 November 1990, witnesses across France reported an immense formation of lights, often interpreted as a dark triangular or elongated craft moving silently at low altitude. The event became one of the country’s best-known UFO episodes. Seine-et-Marne was part of that national pattern: GEIPAN’s archive includes a report from Lagny-sur-Marne among the many local files collected from across France.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The scale of the event initially appeared impressive. CNES security services received calls from numerous gendarmerie units, and reports continued for days. Some witnesses described structured craft rather than separate lights, while the brightness made the phenomenon seem much nearer than it really was. Even experienced observers found it strange.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The subsequent investigation, however, produced unusually strong identification evidence. NASA supplied orbital information for the re-entry of an object associated with a Soviet Proton launch, and CNES orbit calculations confirmed a path crossing France from the Bay of Biscay towards the east. GEIPAN concluded that observers had seen the disintegrating third stage of the rocket, travelling at high altitude as a long cloud of glowing debris. It classifies the event A: explained without ambiguity. Contemporary reporting likewise described how orbital information resolved the mystery within days.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This episode matters to Seine-et-Marne’s UFO history because it demonstrates how a real and spectacular aerial event can generate apparently incompatible descriptions. Separate fragments can look like lights attached to a single dark body. With no familiar distance reference at night, witnesses may place a high-altitude event just above houses or fields and infer impossible size or speed. GEIPAN’s later simulation work stressed the variability of such estimates and the possibility that intense light could remain visible through thin cloud.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The 1990 wave therefore should not be used as evidence that a giant craft crossed Seine-et-Marne. Its value is almost the reverse: it is a well-documented example of how sincere mass testimony can preserve the appearance of an extraordinary machine even when the physical source is independently known.

## AF3532 near Coulommiers: the strongest aviation case

The most important Seine-et-Marne aviation report occurred on 28 January 1994. An Air France Airbus A320 flying from Nice to London was above the Coulommiers area when a senior cabin crew member in the cockpit noticed something to the aircraft’s left. He initially thought it might be a weather balloon. The captain and co-pilot described a dark reddish-brown or brown disc-like phenomenon that appeared to change form before vanishing suddenly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The captain contacted air traffic control at Reims and was told that no known traffic was present in the relevant sector. More unusually, a military radar at Cinq-Mars-la-Pile recorded an unidentified track for about 50 seconds. According to GEIPAN’s summary, the track crossed the aircraft’s route, did not correspond to a filed flight plan and disappeared at approximately the same time as the crew’s visual phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These details make AF3532 more substantial than a typical single-witness light report:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the principal observers were professional aircrew;
* more than one person saw the phenomenon;
* the observation occurred in daylight;
* air traffic control found no matching declared aircraft;
* a separate radar system recorded an unidentified return nearby.

</div>

Yet the evidential limits are equally important. GEIPAN has remained cautious about assuming that the radar track and the visible phenomenon were the same thing. The apparent object was not photographed, its distance was unknown, and estimates of size derived from an unknown distance are inherently unstable. The official investigation states that the radar signature did not precisely reproduce the visual description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A weather balloon was an obvious possibility because the first observer himself suggested one. A later check with Météo-France found that a balloon had crossed the aircraft’s path, but about an hour earlier; by the reported observation time it should already have burst and been descending far to the south-east. That weakened the simplest balloon explanation without ruling out every form of balloon, airborne material or unusual optical effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN continues to classify AF3532 as D, describing it as an unexplained case of medium-to-strong consistency and marked strangeness. The file was updated in June 2026, but the classification remained unchanged. This persistence strengthens the statement that no satisfactory identification has yet been established. It does not strengthen claims about the phenomenon’s origin beyond that.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The most defensible assessment is therefore that AF3532 is Seine-et-Marne’s best-documented unresolved aviation case. Its multiple professional witnesses and associated radar information deserve serious attention. Its weaknesses—uncertain range, no image, delayed formal statements and an unproven visual-radar correlation—prevent a firmer conclusion.

## Ussy-sur-Marne: three gendarmes and a triangular form

Several months later, in the early hours of 24 June 1994, a gendarmerie patrol near Ussy-sur-Marne reported another of the department’s landmark incidents. The official record states that the officers were travelling on the D3E towards Changis-sur-Marne when they noticed a powerful light near the A4 motorway. As they approached, they described three yellow-white luminous points forming a triangle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The written police account says the lights appeared to belong to an unidentified flying object. The officers could not determine its altitude or dimensions. They drew a triangular underside with a luminous centre and a point directed towards the ground. The phenomenon reportedly moved slowly towards their vehicle, stopped above it, then departed westwards and finally accelerated until only a faint point remained on the horizon. The observation lasted about ten minutes, with no reported noise, smoke or smell.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This report is stronger than a retrospective civilian anecdote because it was made by an on-duty patrol, incorporated into a formal gendarmerie file and supported by a sketch, weather material and a location map. The officers were also mobile, allowing them to view the phenomenon from changing positions rather than from a single fixed point. GEIPAN lists Ussy-sur-Marne as a D case of medium-to-strong consistency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Even so, the file does not contain the kind of independent physical confirmation that would settle the matter. There is no photograph, video, preserved radar return or material trace. The three observers were members of one patrol and therefore not fully independent witnesses. Their impression of a solid triangular body may have been accurate, but it may also have arisen from the visual grouping of three lights against a dark sky. GEIPAN itself warns that observers commonly construct apparent contours around lights and make unconscious assumptions about distance, size and speed when the object is unfamiliar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Aircraft or helicopters are obvious alternatives, especially in the wider Paris aviation environment, but the reported silence, prolonged hovering impression and rapid departure make a straightforward identification difficult. Those features also depend heavily on distance estimates that the witnesses explicitly could not establish. A distant aircraft turning away can appear to stop or shrink rapidly; conversely, the officers’ changing position and prolonged observation reduce the force of a very simple “brief misidentification” explanation.

Ussy-sur-Marne therefore remains unresolved rather than confirmed. The report is credible in the limited sense that there is no good reason to doubt the officers saw something unusual. Credibility does not identify the source, and the available documents do not justify describing it as an alien vehicle, secret aircraft or demonstrated solid triangle.

## Why official witnesses do not guarantee identification

Seine-et-Marne’s files repeatedly show a distinction that is easy to miss: a trustworthy witness can make an honest and detailed report that is nevertheless mistaken about what caused the appearance. At Meaux on 19 December 1978, a resident watched a bright point for more than an hour, and gendarmes also saw it from the flat. Astronomical reconstruction placed Venus in the reported south-eastern direction and elevation. GEIPAN classified the case A. The officers confirmed the light’s presence; they did not thereby confirm an extraordinary craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Professional observers can offer useful advantages. Pilots understand ordinary aircraft movement, and gendarmes are trained to record details. But neither profession provides immunity from night-sky illusions, uncertain distance or an unfamiliar viewing geometry. GEIPAN explicitly notes that experienced aviation observers may still be surprised by stars, meteors or satellite re-entries, while aviation reports are also disproportionately represented among some of its most unusual unresolved files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The French reporting system is designed around this distinction. Gendarmes record the date, time, duration, direction, movement, appearance, dimensions and presence or absence of sound, then transmit relevant statements to GEIPAN. This creates a formal evidential trail without pre-judging the explanation. The gendarmerie itself emphasises that natural events, aircraft, drones, projectors and coloured lights can all produce reports initially perceived as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr/gendinfo/actualites/2022/comment-la-gendarmerie-prend-elle-en-compte-les-etrangetes-dans-le-ciel" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr">[Gendarmerie National]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">gendarmerie.interieur.gouv.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on gouv.fr.</span></span></span>

## The explained cases are essential, not incidental

The most useful counterweight to the dramatic 1994 incidents is the department’s collection of solved or probably solved cases. They reveal recurring mechanisms that can transform ordinary phenomena into striking reports.

At Courtry on 29 August 2009, about fifteen people at an outdoor gathering saw a sequence of red-orange lights move together, appear to stop and disappear one by one. One witness later supplied photographs and interpreted the extinctions as objects accelerating into space. GEIPAN found the colour, formation, drift and sequential disappearance characteristic of Thai lanterns. Local wind conditions were compatible with the observed motion, and the case was classified B: probable lanterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54205?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COURTRY (77) 29.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Courtry file is especially instructive because it had numerous witnesses and photographs—features often treated as automatic proof of an extraordinary event. In practice, the images helped support the conventional explanation. The apparent sudden departures were consistent with flames going out, demonstrating how disappearance can be mistaken for extreme acceleration.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/54205?field_agregation_index_value=orange&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">COURTRY (77) 29.08.2009 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Mitry-Mory on 25 November 2024, a resident filmed several green circular lights moving back and forth across an overcast sky. The eleven-second video gave investigators more objective material than a verbal description alone. GEIPAN found that the rounded patches, diffuse green colour, erratic automated movement and complete cloud cover were characteristic of a ground-based sky projector illuminating the cloud base. No specific projector was located publicly, so the case remained B rather than A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2019-08-31&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=10%2C31&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

At Crégy-lès-Meaux in November 2021, the database records an A-classified fireball, while a Chanteloup-en-Brie report from June 2022 was also classified A. Other Seine-et-Marne entries remain C because too little dependable information survived to test competing hypotheses. These outcomes matter because “unexplained” and “unanalysable” are not the same. A C case is not a stronger mystery than a D case; it is usually a weaker evidential file.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2021-06-26&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation_textuel&amp;page=3&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Do airfields, radar and the Paris aviation corridor form a pattern?

Seine-et-Marne has substantial aviation connections, including Melun-Villaroche aerodrome and air routes serving the Paris region. That makes aviation a natural part of the department’s UFO story. It provides trained witnesses, air traffic records and radar systems that can occasionally test a report, as in AF3532. It also fills the sky with aircraft whose landing lights, turns and changing aspect can appear unfamiliar from the ground.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melun_Villaroche_Aerodrome" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Melun Villaroche Aerodrome</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Melun Villaroche Aerodrome</span></span></span>

The surviving files do not support a claim that an airbase or airport was being repeatedly visited by the same unknown technology. AF3532 occurred over the Coulommiers area during a commercial flight; Ussy-sur-Marne was a ground observation near the A4; the 1990 event was a rocket re-entry spanning much of France; and later reports have been attributed to lanterns, projectors, planets and meteors. Their locations and descriptions do not establish a single departmental phenomenon.

Radar evidence also needs careful handling. A radar return may come from an aircraft, balloon, weather effect, anomalous propagation or technical artefact. It becomes persuasive only when time, position, movement and other characteristics correlate closely with a visual observation. In AF3532, the unexplained return raises the case above ordinary testimony, but GEIPAN’s investigators have not treated the correlation as proven.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## How the major cases compare

The departmental record is clearest when cases are separated by evidential quality rather than fame.

**Strongest unresolved case: AF3532.** It combines daylight observation by professional aircrew, multiple witnesses, air traffic enquiries and a nearby unidentified radar track. It remains unresolved, although the exact link between the visual and radar events is uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Strong ground-based unresolved case: Ussy-sur-Marne.** Three on-duty gendarmes produced a formal report, sketch and route reconstruction after a prolonged encounter. No independent instrument, image or physical trace confirmed the apparent triangular object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Most famous local participation in a national event: 5 November 1990.** Reports from Seine-et-Marne belonged to a spectacular mass sighting, but orbital data and trajectory reconstruction identify the source as a Soviet rocket-stage re-entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Best demonstrations of ordinary causes:** Meaux, Courtry and Mitry-Mory. Respectively, they show how Venus, lanterns and projected lights on cloud can create persistent, multiple-witness or filmed reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00580?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=26&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEAUX (77) 19.12.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**Weakest category:** reports classified C. These may sound unusual, but missing times, directions, photographs, independent statements or contemporary records prevent meaningful analysis. Their unresolved appearance reflects lack of evidence rather than strong evidence for something extraordinary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Mission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What later investigation strengthened—and weakened

Later work strengthened AF3532 by showing that the obvious weather-balloon candidate did not fit the recorded time and position well. The continuing D classification, most recently updated in 2026, confirms that GEIPAN has not found a satisfactory conventional identification. At the same time, no later evidence supplied an image, a more exact range or a definitive match between the radar track and the crew’s sighting. The case became harder to dismiss, but not easier to identify.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-01-01345" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The surviving Ussy-sur-Marne documentation strengthens confidence that the report was contemporary, formal and more detailed than later retellings sometimes suggest. The original police papers preserve the officers’ uncertainty about altitude and size, a detail that should temper dramatic reconstructions portraying a precisely measured craft directly above the patrol car. Later circulation of the case has not added independent witnesses or instrument data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/sites/default/files/2023-08/USSY-SUR-MARNE%20%2877%29%2024.06.1994%20%5BT-M%20PV%20T%2CD%2CC%20S%2CA%5D%201994307799-1544-94-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Later analysis decisively weakened extraordinary interpretations of 5 November 1990. Orbital identification, trajectory calculations and simulations explain why witnesses across a vast area reported the same direction, timing and train of lights. Disagreement from some witnesses—especially those convinced the display was nearby—does not outweigh the independent orbital evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Modern cases also show how improved evidence can make a report less mysterious. The Mitry-Mory video preserved colour, movement and cloud conditions sufficiently well for investigators to propose a specific lighting mechanism. This is an important corrective to the assumption that video necessarily makes a UFO claim stronger: useful footage strengthens whichever explanation fits it best.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-11-51599?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=2019-08-31&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=10%2C31&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MITRY-MORY (77) 25.11.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## A balanced verdict on Seine-et-Marne

Seine-et-Marne’s UFO history is neither empty nor evidence of a sustained extraterrestrial presence. It contains two genuinely notable unresolved incidents, both from 1994, surrounded by a larger and more typical record of astronomical mistakes, human-made lights, atmospheric events and reports too incomplete to judge.

AF3532 deserves its reputation because the witnesses, daylight setting and radar context make it unusually difficult to explain. Ussy-sur-Marne deserves attention because several gendarmes documented a prolonged triangular-light encounter in formal records. Neither case, however, provides recoverable physical material, decisive imagery or a chain of independent measurements capable of establishing what the phenomenon was.

The department’s most spectacular mass-sighting connection—the night of 5 November 1990—is also its clearest warning against judging a case by the number or confidence of witnesses. Thousands of people saw something real, but many interpreted a high-altitude debris train as a huge low-flying machine. The Meaux, Courtry and Mitry-Mory files repeat the same broader lesson on smaller scales.

The fairest conclusion is that Seine-et-Marne has a small core of unresolved evidence rather than a proven mystery with a single cause. Its cases are valuable because they show the whole range of UFO investigation: compelling testimony, official police records, aviation and radar data, perceptual uncertainty, later technical checks, convincing explanations and a residue that remains unidentified without becoming confirmed extraordinary technology.
