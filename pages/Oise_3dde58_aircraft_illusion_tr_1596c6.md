## Introduction

Oise’s most revealing triangular UFO report is not one that survived investigation, but one that showed how convincingly an ordinary aircraft can become a vast, hovering V-shaped object. Near Silly-le-Long shortly after midnight on 31 May 2015, two people travelling along the N2 saw white and red lights apparently fixed above the road. As they approached, they perceived a broad dark mass, seemingly motionless and suspended at low altitude. GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that investigates unusual aerial reports, later reconstructed the event using the witnesses’ route, an aircraft’s recorded position and a three-dimensional simulation. It concluded that the object was a light aircraft performing night circuits near Le Plessis-Belleville airfield.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-05-09194?page=%2C98" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSILLY-LE-LONG (60) 31.05.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Oise_3dde58_aircraft_illusion_tr_1596c6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Aircraft Illusions" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The case matters because the witnesses were not merely confusing one light with another. Several interacting effects—relative motion, poor depth cues, aircraft lighting, road geometry and uncertain size estimates—combined to create the impression of a giant triangular craft. It offers a practical model for understanding other apparently slow, silent or hovering shapes reported over Oise.

## The Silly-le-Long encounter

The witnesses were travelling north-east in a van between Le Plessis-Belleville and Nanteuil-le-Haudouin. At first they noticed two bright white lights and a red light, low ahead and slightly to the left. They were accustomed to seeing aircraft associated with the Paris region, but these lights appeared unusually low and, more importantly, did not seem to advance. The driver slowed as the van approached a bridge over the N2. Both witnesses then perceived a wide, dark form associated with the lights; the driver later described it as a very flattened V.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-05-09194?page=%2C98" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSILLY-LE-LONG (60) 31.05.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

After the van passed beneath the bridge, the passenger lost sight of the phenomenon because of the vehicle’s roof and lack of rear visibility. The driver continued looking sideways, backwards and through a mirror while still driving. He reported a dark, apparently solid structure with two large rectangular white areas at its rear. The experience lasted somewhere between roughly one minute and four minutes, depending on which witness estimate is used. The vehicle’s noise also made the absence of an audible engine difficult to interpret.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Several details strengthened the witnesses’ initial confidence. They knew the road, returned to the location later and recorded their accounts quickly. One drawing was made about an hour after the sighting, and the GEIPAN questionnaire was completed within roughly 36 hours. The two witnesses also tried not to compare their descriptions beforehand. These are useful precautions, but they do not remove perceptual error: prompt and sincere testimony can still preserve a mistaken interpretation formed during the event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The investigation found a Cirrus SR20 light aircraft conducting repeated touch-and-go manoeuvres at Le Plessis-Belleville. A touch-and-go is a training exercise in which an aircraft lands briefly and immediately takes off again, producing repeated low-altitude circuits around an airfield. The aircraft’s recorded route placed it in approximately the same viewing directions as the reported phenomenon during the critical period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN did not stop at saying that an aeroplane happened to be nearby. Investigators revisited the road with the witnesses, modelled the van’s movement and compared it with the aircraft’s GPS-derived track. They also examined weather, astronomy, road works, nearby industrial activity and the possibility of an exceptional road convoy. The fixed structure and convoy alternatives did not fit the location, lighting or later absence of any object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## How relative motion creates false stillness

The central mechanism was what GEIPAN calls the **pivot effect**. An observer and an aircraft can both be moving while the line of sight between them changes very little for a short period. From the observer’s viewpoint, the aircraft then remains near the same point against the landscape or sky, even though it is travelling normally.

A familiar daytime comparison is a distant vehicle on a curving road. It may seem almost stationary when its sideways movement across the viewer’s field of vision is minimal. At night, the effect becomes much stronger because the observer sees bright points without a clearly visible fuselage, distance marker or continuous background. The brain therefore has little direct information with which to distinguish a nearby hovering object from a more distant aircraft moving partly towards or away from the observer.

At Silly-le-Long, the geometry became especially persuasive near the bridge. The aircraft was travelling in the opposite general direction to the van, but its changing flight path and the van’s forward movement caused their connecting sightline to pivot around roughly the same area. The bridge provided a prominent reference point, making the lights seem fixed above or beside it. GEIPAN’s reconstruction found that this moment corresponded closely with the witnesses’ strongest impression that the object was stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-05-09194?page=%2C98" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSILLY-LE-LONG (60) 31.05.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is not simply the autokinetic effect, in which an isolated stationary light appears to wander against a featureless background. The Silly-le-Long aircraft really was moving, and so were the witnesses. Nevertheless, both mechanisms illustrate the same wider problem: night vision is poor at recovering motion when reliable reference points are absent or misleading. Aviation safety guidance warns that isolated lights in darkness can appear to move, remain fixed or occupy false relationships with the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/pilots/safety/pilotsafetybrochures/media/spatiald_visillus.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial D</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation Administration Spatial D</span></span></span>

The experience after the bridge was even less suitable for precise judgement. Only the driver continued observing, alternately through a side window and mirror while controlling the vehicle. The apparent object was then behind or beside him rather than directly ahead. Attention was divided among the road, the bridge, the mirror and a phenomenon already interpreted as extraordinary. Under these conditions, small changes in viewing angle can be missed, and brief flashes may be remembered as larger illuminated surfaces.

## Why lights become a triangular body

Aircraft lighting does not normally present observers with a neat diagram of an aeroplane. Its appearance depends on viewing angle, banking, glare, atmospheric clarity and which lights are directed towards the observer. Landing lights can dominate everything else when an aircraft faces roughly towards the viewer. Red and green navigation lights may appear, disappear or merge with brighter sources during turns. Anti-collision lights produce flashes that can be assigned to imagined corners or edges.

At Silly-le-Long, the witnesses initially interpreted the lights as aircraft-like. The strangeness emerged because their apparent stillness seemed to rule out an aeroplane. Once that conclusion was made, the darkness between the lights was perceived as part of a single massive body. This is a natural act of visual organisation: when lights maintain a stable arrangement, the brain tends to treat them as belonging to one object and fills in the space between them.

The triangular or V-shaped outline was therefore not necessarily a clear silhouette observed independently of the lights. One witness acknowledged that the front point had not been well seen and that drawings made the contours sharper than they appeared during the encounter. The passenger was less certain of a specific shape, remembering mainly a broad, flat mass and an impressive span.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also identified a substantial discrepancy between the real angular size of the aircraft and the reported size of the object. The SR20 would have subtended about 0.67 degrees at one reconstructed position, whereas witness estimates ranged from roughly 14 to 27 degrees. That is not a minor mismatch, and investigators explicitly treated it as a weakness in the aircraft hypothesis. They nevertheless judged that the estimates had been made without dependable ground references and under particularly poor observing conditions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Apparent size is inseparable from assumed distance. A small aircraft several kilometres away and a huge object a few hundred metres away can occupy the same angle in the sky. Unless distance is independently known—through stereoscopic vision at close range, radar, terrain occlusion or another reliable cue—the observer cannot derive physical dimensions merely by looking. In this case, the belief that the phenomenon was just above the bridge made the inferred width enormous. If the assumed distance was wrong, the calculated size was wrong with it.

Low objects near the horizon can also appear larger because the brain compares them with familiar landscape features. The GEIPAN report discussed the tendency for low-altitude angular size to be exaggerated and noted that the witnesses’ height estimates did not agree well with the later site measurements.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete123.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why Oise is prone to aviation misidentifications

The Silly-le-Long setting was unusually favourable to this kind of mistake. Le Plessis-Belleville is an active general-aviation airfield used by light aircraft, helicopters and flying clubs. It supports night visual flying with limitations, and a recent aviation-industry account reported more than 16,000 aircraft movements a year. Repeated circuits, turns and low approaches are therefore ordinary features of the local sky rather than exceptional events.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rodrome_du_Plessis-Belleville" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: Wikipedia">[Wikipedia]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">Wikipedia</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Aérodrome du Plessis-Belleville</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Aérodrome du Plessis-Belleville</span></span></span>

Oise also lies on the northern edge of the greater Paris aviation environment. Residents and road users may see distant airliners associated with Paris Charles de Gaulle alongside lower light aircraft operating from local aerodromes. Familiarity can help identification, but it can also create an expectation trap. A witness who knows how airliners “normally” appear may reject an aircraft because a smaller aeroplane is flying on a different route, at a different altitude or at an unfamiliar angle.

The department’s road network adds a second moving platform. A car can change direction and speed while an aircraft turns around an airfield circuit. Bridges, tree lines, embankments and descending sections of road repeatedly reveal and conceal the lights. Each interruption can make the encounter seem to have distinct phases: lights ahead, a dark body overhead and flashing panels behind. In reality, the observer may be receiving changing views of the same aircraft.

Dark rural or semi-rural stretches intensify the effect. Away from dense urban lighting, aircraft lamps are prominent but the airframe may remain invisible. A clear sky does not guarantee a good identification; it can provide excellent visibility of lights while offering almost no depth information. The result is the paradox seen at Silly-le-Long: an object can look exceptionally clear in some respects—bright lights, stable arrangement, striking apparent outline—while its distance, size and motion remain highly uncertain.

## What the investigation established—and what it did not

The Silly-le-Long conclusion was strong enough for GEIPAN to classify the case as category A, meaning identified after investigation. The major supporting points were the presence of a specific aircraft, its matching time and direction, the reconstructed pivot effect, the absence of a separate object noticed by the pilot and the fact that the witnesses never saw both the aircraft and the reported V simultaneously.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-05-09194?page=%2C98" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSILLY-LE-LONG (60) 31.05.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The explanation is not a perfect visual match. GEIPAN acknowledged differences in light colour and flashing behaviour, an angular separation that could reach 20 to 30 degrees in parts of the reconstruction, and the very large mismatch between the aircraft’s calculated apparent size and the witness estimates. The simulation also required assumptions about timing and vehicle speed, and its own documentation states that it did not reproduce every detail of the testimony.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-05-09194?page=%2C98" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANSILLY-LE-LONG (60) 31.05.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Those limitations do not leave two equally supported possibilities. They show why investigations must compare complete explanations rather than demand that every remembered feature match precisely. The alternative would require a huge illuminated object to have occupied the immediate vicinity of the flight path without being recorded independently, while a known aircraft followed a closely corresponding route and somehow went unnoticed. The aircraft hypothesis explains the timing, general position, lighting and crucial appearance of hovering with one documented source; the giant-craft hypothesis requires an additional unrecorded object.

The case therefore weakens, rather than supports, the idea that every triangular report describes a genuinely triangular vehicle. It does not prove that all such reports in Oise have the same cause. A stationary witness, multiple independent viewing sites, reliable photographs or corroborating radar could produce a very different evidential balance. Silly-le-Long instead demonstrates a specific high-risk combination: moving witnesses, a turning aircraft, bright night lighting, uncertain distance and a landscape feature that appears to pin the phenomenon in place.

For Oise’s UFO history, that is the lasting value of the encounter. Its most extraordinary qualities—the stillness, enormous size and V-shaped body—were not separate pieces of evidence pointing away from an aircraft. They were the connected products of the same viewing geometry and perceptual uncertainty.
