## Introduction

Only three cases in a November 2025 snapshot of the official GEIPAN database remained unresolved in Drôme: Chabeuil in 1978, Donzère in 1980 and Barret-de-Lioure in 2008. That makes them the central evidence base for the department’s enduring UFO claims—but not proof that extraordinary craft visited the area. GEIPAN’s category D means that a phenomenon remained unidentified after investigation, rather than that investigators established an exotic origin. Nationally, such cases form only a small minority of its published files.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/save_json_import_files/export_cas_pub_20251127093552.csv" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFichiers CSV CasGEIPAN continue à publier l&#x27;ensemble de ses archives sur son site public www.geipan.fr. é de différentes observatio...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Drome_06f265_strongest_unresolved_6ae8b0-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Unresolved Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

Each of the three has a feature that resists an easy explanation. Chabeuil involved an orange light apparently stopping and restarting; Donzère was reported as a silent, close-range object that followed a car; and Barret-de-Lioure was seen through an astronomical finder by experienced observers. Yet none produced a photograph, radar return, physical trace or instrument record capable of settling the matter. Their strength lies mainly in the detail and apparent sincerity of the testimony—not in independently measurable evidence.

## Chabeuil and the stop-start orange light

At around 7.30 pm on 28 February 1978, a 30-year-old woman driving with her ten-year-old son reported a very bright orange object moving rapidly across the sky from north to south. The observation reportedly lasted about three minutes. During that period, the phenomenon appeared to stabilise, then resumed a slower southward movement before disappearing instantaneously. Neither witness heard any sound.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-02-00491?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHABEUIL (26) 28.02.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The basic agreement between the two accounts gives Chabeuil more weight than a wholly solitary sighting. Both witnesses described a single orange or fire-coloured phenomenon, variable movement and complete silence. Their descriptions of its apparent size were also broadly similar: the mother compared it with a car, while her son said it looked as large as their Citroën Dyane. GEIPAN retained the case in category D, describing it as a strange or very strange phenomenon with medium-to-strong consistency.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/1871" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHABEUIL (26) 28.02.1978 T1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The reported pause is the most difficult detail to fit to a straightforward meteor. Meteors normally cross the visible sky continuously and briefly rather than stopping for an appreciable interval and then departing slowly. The three-minute duration also works against a conventional shooting star. The abrupt disappearance is less diagnostic, however: an aircraft turning away, a distant light becoming obscured or a bright object entering cloud can all seem to vanish suddenly.

There are important weaknesses. The witnesses were mother and child travelling together, not two independent observers whose accounts developed separately. The son was ten years old, and the surviving database entries provide little information about viewing angle, angular size, weather, cloud cover or identifiable stars. The adult witness’s estimated distance of 150 metres is especially uncertain because a lone light in a dark sky offers no reliable scale. Apparent distance and physical size cannot be calculated without knowing what the object was or having a fixed background reference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/1871" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHABEUIL (26) 28.02.1978 T1 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

No trace was found and no additional witness was identified, despite the observation occurring near an inhabited and road-served area. There is also no published photograph, flight record, radar detection or instrument reading associated with it. GEIPAN’s own summary therefore supports two conclusions at once: something unusual was sincerely reported, but the evidence was insufficient to determine what it was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-02-00491?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHABEUIL (26) 28.02.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A perceptual explanation cannot be excluded. GEIPAN notes that a bright point against a dark, poorly referenced sky may appear to move, stop or change direction through effects such as autokinesis, in which involuntary eye movements make a fixed light seem mobile. That possibility does not neatly explain every reported feature, particularly the initial rapid transit, but the limited positional data prevent a reliable reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComprendre un PhénomèneCette FAQ est dédiée aux personnes souhaitant signaler une observation de Phénomènes Aérospatiaux Non Identi...</span></span></span>

Chabeuil therefore remains a respectable unresolved light report, not a strongly evidenced close encounter. Its survival in category D reflects the lack of a satisfactory identification combined with a reasonably coherent account. It does not overcome the absence of objective corroboration.

## Donzère and the close-range road encounter

The Donzère case of 3 December 1980 is the most dramatic of Drôme’s three unresolved files. Shortly before 11 pm, a motorist travelling near Roussas reported seeing a large circular object roughly 150 metres ahead of him. He described it as fluorescent green, silent and stationary about ten metres above the road. When he stopped, the object allegedly moved slowly towards his vehicle. He drove on, passed beneath it and believed it followed him for about a kilometre before accelerating away in the opposite direction. The episode reportedly lasted three or four minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://carteovni.fr/cas/donzere-1980-1200833" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: carteovni.fr">[CarteOvni.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">carteovni.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">De couleur vert fluorescent il est…</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Observation OVNI à Donzere — décembre 1980 - CarteOvni.frLe 03 décembre 1980 vers 23h un automobiliste aperçoit à 150 m devant lui, un ob...</span></span></span>

This account carries greater apparent strangeness than Chabeuil because the witness described not merely a distant light but a structured, nearby object whose movements seemed related to his own. A large circular form hovering over a road, approaching a stopped vehicle and then following it is not an obvious match for a star, planet or ordinary meteor. GEIPAN consequently categorised the file as a strange-to-very-strange phenomenon of medium-to-strong consistency and left it unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B1%5D=14&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=15&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

There was also a possible second observation. Another man at Les Granges-Gontardes reported seeing a distant phenomenon or glow that evening. His database entry places the observation at about 9.30 pm, however, rather than close to the motorist’s reported 11 pm encounter, and describes the phenomenon as very far away. This makes it weak corroboration at best. It may indicate unusual lights were visible in the wider area, but it does not independently confirm a large object hovering over the road or pursuing a car.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/48888" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Donzère’s narrative detail is both its main strength and its main vulnerability. Close-range descriptions can appear evidentially powerful, but distance, altitude and dimensions judged at night are highly dependent on assumptions. A witness who believes a light is 150 metres away will interpret changes in brightness or bearing as local manoeuvres; the same visual information could look quite different if the source were much farther away. Without photographs, fixed angular measurements or a reconstructed line of sight, the claimed ten-metre altitude and large diameter cannot be independently checked.

The impression that a light is following a vehicle also has a familiar geometric explanation. A distant source can maintain roughly the same bearing as an observer moves, creating the sense that it is travelling alongside or behind the car. This “following” effect is often associated with the Moon, planets or distant lights. It does not automatically solve Donzère, because the witness also described passing beneath a circular object, but it shows why apparent pursuit is not by itself proof of responsive movement.

No known physical effect strengthens the account. The car was not reported as damaged or disabled, and the published summaries contain no radar trace, photograph, landing mark, vegetation change or measurable electromagnetic disturbance. Searches for further witnesses did not produce confirmation of the motorist’s detailed sequence. The official status therefore rests overwhelmingly on one person’s recollection of a frightening nocturnal event.

The case remains difficult to reduce to a single conventional object because the published description combines colour, silence, apparent hovering, proximity and rapid departure. Conversely, those features are available only through testimony and were not independently recorded. Donzère is consequently Drôme’s most striking unresolved story, but not necessarily its strongest evidence. Its high strangeness exceeds its evidential security.

## Barret-de-Lioure and the telescope witnesses

Barret-de-Lioure is the most methodically documented of the three cases. At about 9.25 pm on 8 July 2008, two amateur astronomers on the summit of the Montagne de Bergiès noticed a bright point that repeatedly appeared and disappeared. They located it using the 9×50 finder attached to an astronomical telescope and looked through the instrument in turn. Both described an oval, steel-grey object moving slowly southwards; one also perceived a pendulum-like motion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-07-02443" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The principal witness was an electronics technician who had practised astronomy for 14 years and belonged to an astronomical association. His questionnaire recorded a clear sky, a slight breeze and an observing altitude of 1,367 metres. He estimated the phenomenon’s naked-eye brightness at approximately magnitude –4 at maximum—comparable in apparent brightness to Venus—and gave an apparent diameter of about one degree through the finder. The observation lasted approximately five minutes, although the two witnesses did not see precisely the same beginning and end.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20astronomique-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These details give the case advantages absent from Chabeuil and Donzère. The observers were accustomed to looking at the sky; they used optical equipment; they supplied timing, direction and environmental information; and GEIPAN sent an investigator to the location. The investigator reported nothing abnormal at the site but accepted the principal witness’s good faith. GEIPAN described the case as unexplained, moderately strange and of good consistency, assigning an unusualness score of 0.62. The file was updated again on 16 June 2026 and remained in category D1.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2008-07-02443" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Experience, however, is not equivalent to instrumented proof. The observers used the small finder rather than making a photograph, video or recorded telescopic measurement. A finder gives magnification but does not automatically establish an object’s distance, real dimensions or speed. The stated one-degree apparent size was an estimate, and the witness described the motion as almost imperceptible. No camera was used.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20astronomique-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The optics also introduce uncertainty. A small bright source viewed through a finder can be affected by focus, atmospheric turbulence, glare and internal reflections. An out-of-focus point may appear as a disc or oval rather than a point. Slight movements of the observer or instrument may also produce apparent oscillation, particularly when the target lacks nearby visual references. These possibilities are not demonstrated explanations of the event, but the surviving record does not contain the controlled optical tests needed to exclude them.

Aircraft were present lower in the south-western sky, according to the questionnaire. The witness did not believe a known satellite or the International Space Station was passing at the time, but the wording was based on his knowledge rather than on a published retrospective orbital calculation. The repeated brightening and fading could be compatible with changing reflection angles from an aircraft, balloon or satellite, yet the reported oval form and slow southward movement prevented investigators from confidently adopting any of those explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Questionnaire%20astronomique-R.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Barret-de-Lioure is therefore arguably Drôme’s strongest unresolved observation in terms of witness competence and documentation. It is not the most spectacular account, but it was reported by two experienced sky watchers, examined on site and supported by a detailed questionnaire. Its decisive limitation is that the optical observation was never converted into durable evidence. The finder helped the witnesses see, but no image or measurement survives for others to analyse.

## Which case carries the most evidential weight?

The three cases are strong in different ways, so ranking them requires separating **strangeness** from **evidence quality**.


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Barret-de-Lioure has the best documentation.** Two experienced observers used an optical finder, supplied technical details and were followed up by a GEIPAN investigator. Its weakness is the absence of a recorded image or calibrated measurement.
* **Donzère has the highest reported strangeness.** A large silent object allegedly approached, overflew and followed a car. Its weakness is that nearly every important feature depends on one witness’s night-time estimates.
* **Chabeuil has the simplest shared account.** Two related witnesses agreed on a bright orange object with changing movement. Its weakness is the sparse environmental information and lack of independent corroboration.

</div>

None reaches the evidential level of a multi-sensor case. There was no radar confirmation, synchronised observation from separate locations, aviation report, physical trace or original photograph in any of the three files. Chabeuil and Donzère depend on unaided perception under night-time driving conditions; Barret-de-Lioure benefits from experienced observers and magnification but still lacks a permanent record.

This distinction matters because “unresolved” is sometimes misread as “confirmed extraordinary”. GEIPAN defines category D as an unidentified phenomenon remaining after investigation. It does not specify the phenomenon’s nature. Category C, by contrast, applies where inadequate information prevents effective analysis; category D indicates that investigators judged the evidence substantial enough to examine but could not find a satisfactory identification. Across GEIPAN’s published national database, category D cases accounted for about 3.1 per cent as of 25 June 2026.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why the cases still resist a firm conclusion

The same problem runs through all three files: the testimony contains enough unusual detail to frustrate a simple identification, but too little objective information to test the account decisively.

A photograph could have revealed whether Barret-de-Lioure’s oval shape belonged to the source or to the optical system. Accurate bearings taken at intervals might have allowed investigators to calculate a trajectory. Separate witnesses at Donzère could have established whether the apparent pursuit was real movement or a perspective effect. Contemporary weather, aircraft and radar records might have narrowed the options at Chabeuil. Those forms of evidence either were not obtained or do not appear in the published files.

Later reporting has not materially strengthened Chabeuil or Donzère. Their classifications have endured, but no new photograph, instrument record or decisive independent witness has emerged in the public material. Barret-de-Lioure received the most recent reassessment: its page was updated in June 2026 and still described the event as a D1 observation of good consistency. That continued status shows that GEIPAN has not adopted a conventional solution, but it does not add new physical evidence to the original sighting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-02-00491?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=14&amp;page=%2C10" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCHABEUIL (26) 28.02.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The most defensible reading is therefore modest. Drôme possesses three official cases that genuinely resisted the explanations available to investigators. Barret-de-Lioure is the best documented, Donzère the most extraordinary as narrated, and Chabeuil a comparatively clear shared light observation. All remain open questions, but each stops well short of demonstrating an unknown craft. Their lasting value is that they show precisely where UFO investigation often reaches its limit: coherent witnesses, intriguing behaviour and no decisive evidence capable of choosing between an exceptional event and an unusual misperception.
