## Introduction

[Allier]({{ 'why-alliers-strangest-sightings-changed/' | relative_url }})’s best-known unresolved sightings remain uncertain for a less dramatic reason than the word “unresolved” suggests: the evidence is too incomplete to support either a firm conventional explanation or an extraordinary one. The Yzeure report of an orange disc, the unexplained part of the Cosne-d’Allier incident and the unusual ground marks reported near Cérilly are all classified **C** by GEIPAN, the French space agency’s official investigation unit. In GEIPAN’s system, category C means that a phenomenon could not be identified because reliable data were missing; it is distinct from category D, in which useful evidence survives but the event still resists explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">information. Classification D...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Allier_716dd6_unresolved_allier_ca_153093-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Unresolved Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

That distinction is central to Allier’s UFO history. These cases are open files, but not strong evidence for exotic craft. Their descriptions can sound remarkable, yet none produced the measurements, recoverable material, independent observations or detection records needed to test what witnesses believed they saw.

## Yzeure’s orange disc report

At 7.42 am on 24 September 1979, a witness at Yzeure, beside Moulins, first noticed what appeared to be an aircraft’s vapour trail. According to the official summary, the witness then saw an orange, disc-shaped object apparently remain stationary for ten to fifteen seconds. It reportedly accelerated suddenly, became brilliant white and disappeared. No second witness was found, and GEIPAN classified the case C because the available information was insufficient.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-09-00662" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANYZEURE (03) 24.09.1979YZEURE (03) 24.09.1979. Date d&#x27;observation. 24/09/1979. Région. Auvergne. Département. Allier. Classification...</span></span></span>

The account contains several features that make it memorable: a definite shape, a colour change, a period of apparent hovering and a rapid departure. None of those features, however, was independently measured. A single observer looking at an object high in the sky usually has no secure reference for its distance or size. Without distance, apparent speed cannot be converted into physical speed; something nearby moving moderately can resemble something distant moving extraordinarily fast.

“Stationary” is similarly difficult to establish. An aircraft moving towards or away from an observer may show little sideways motion, while a bright point seen against an almost featureless sky can appear fixed. The transition from orange to white might reflect a changing viewing angle, atmospheric extinction near the horizon, sunlight striking a surface or simply a shift in perceived brightness. These are possible mechanisms rather than retrospective solutions: the record does not preserve enough directional, meteorological or aviation information to choose among them.

The report’s opening detail—a trail initially interpreted as coming from a jet—is potentially important, but it cannot now be evaluated properly. The available summary does not establish whether the trail and disc were physically connected, whether the “disc” was a compact bright section of the same phenomenon, or whether two unrelated objects were seen in succession. No photograph, radar report, aircraft identification or angular-position sequence is published with the summary. GEIPAN therefore records an unexplained observation through lack of information, not an object shown to have performed impossible manoeuvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1979-09-00662" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANYZEURE (03) 24.09.1979YZEURE (03) 24.09.1979. Date d&#x27;observation. 24/09/1979. Région. Auvergne. Département. Allier. Classification...</span></span></span>

Later reporting has not materially strengthened the case. A local retrospective repeats the official description, but does not introduce a second witness, contemporary photograph or newly recovered technical record. The account has remained stable as a story while remaining weak as a testable event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.lasemainedelallier.fr/ovni-dans-l-allier-les-precedents-cas-observes-et-analyses-dans-le-departement/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: lasemainedelallier.fr">[La Semaine de l&#x27;Allier]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">lasemainedelallier.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">21 janvier 1985.... Description: Dans la soirée du 21 janvier 1985 entre 23h 30 et 24h des témoins vont apercevoir des flashes de lumiè...</span></span></span>

## The two episodes at Cosne-d’Allier

The Cosne-d’Allier file is particularly easy to misread because it combines two separate incidents from the evening of 21 January 1985. One was solved during the gendarmerie investigation. The other remained unidentified because the enquiry found no corroborating information. Treating them as a single extended manifestation exaggerates the mystery and obscures the most useful part of the investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-01-01047" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCOSNE D&#x27;ALLIER (03) 21.01.1985 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Late that night, witnesses saw repeated white flashes close to the ground around the ruins of a house. Gendarmes returned to the location and reproduced the effect. Wind and damp conditions were causing a poor contact between an electric-fence wire and its support, producing short circuits and flashes. Witness statements described several horizontal bursts, their low height and the otherwise silent surroundings; the physical inspection supplied a direct cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B049%20%281985306965%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The earlier event, observed by a married couple while travelling by car between about 8.45 and 9.15 pm, was different. Their statements describe a yellow, triangular light that seemed to stretch into a rectangle, sometimes appeared blue and brighter, and later diminished until it resembled a star. At one point the couple felt that the light was following or overtaking them. GEIPAN’s summary says it then disappeared abruptly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-01-01047" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCOSNE D&#x27;ALLIER (03) 21.01.1985 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is a genuine two-witness report, which gives it more weight than Yzeure’s solitary observation. Yet the witnesses were together in the same moving vehicle and were viewing the same scene from almost the same position. Their accounts are mutually supportive, but they are not independent in the stronger evidential sense of observers at separated locations establishing a common trajectory.

The apparent pursuit is also difficult to interpret without bearings. A distant light can remain at roughly the same angle to a moving car and seem to accompany it, an effect familiar with the Moon, planets, towers and distant aircraft. Changes in trees, bends, gradients and the vehicle’s own movement can make a fixed or slowly moving light appear to approach, withdraw or cross the road. This does not prove that the Cosne light was astronomical or terrestrial. It shows why the witnesses’ impression of being followed cannot establish the object’s actual motion on its own.

The gendarmes made useful checks. Their report records that officers on duty in the area at around 9.30 pm had noticed nothing abnormal, that neighbouring brigades received no reports from other members of the public along the route, and that no further information capable of explaining the couple’s observation was discovered.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B049%20%281985306965%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Those negative findings cut both ways. They reduce the likelihood of a large, conspicuous phenomenon visible across a wide area, but they do not eliminate a local or direction-dependent light. They also leave no observational baseline from which to calculate altitude, distance, angular speed or route. The report contains estimates of colour and apparent form, but no photograph, timed sequence of bearings, physical effect on the car, radar confirmation or separately positioned witness.

The solved electric-fence flashes are therefore not evidence that every observation that evening had the same cause. Equally, the unresolved road sighting should not inherit mystery from the visually striking but completely explained flashes. The careful conclusion is narrower: one episode was reproduced and identified; a second could not be reconstructed well enough to identify. GEIPAN consequently retains the combined file as category C, explicitly describing the problem as a lack of reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1985-01-01047" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCOSNE D&#x27;ALLIER (03) 21.01.1985 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Cérilly’s lost ground marks

The Cérilly case of 18 October 1994 differs from Yzeure and Cosne-d’Allier because it did not begin with a light in the sky. At about 2 pm, an off-duty gendarme gathering mushrooms reportedly found a ground circle composed of stone fragments resembling clinker. Leaves touching the fragments had taken on what was described as a sulphurous green colour. As darkness approached, he left intending to return the following day, but could not relocate the site. GEIPAN classifies the report C for lack of reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-10-01373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCERILLY (03) 18.10.199422 Mar 2007 — CERILLY (03) 18.10.1994. Résumé. Découverte de cercles de cailloux au sol. Description. Le 18...</span></span></span>

The witness’s occupation makes the account interesting but does not turn the observation into physical evidence. The circle itself was never secured, mapped or photographed in the published record. No samples of the stone-like material or discoloured leaves were collected, so their composition could not be examined. There was no soil comparison inside and outside the ring, no test for heat or chemicals and no established boundary showing that the feature had been recently formed.

Most importantly, the location was lost. Investigators could not inspect the surrounding terrain for forestry work, old fire sites, dumped industrial material, paths, vehicle access or naturally occurring arrangements of stone. They could not determine whether the green colour represented staining, plant decay, mineral deposits, algae, reflected light or an inaccurate colour description. Nor could they check whether the ring had any association with an aerial phenomenon, because no craft or unusual light was reported in connection with its discovery.

Cérilly is therefore weaker than a conventional “trace case”, despite its physical-sounding description. A trace becomes useful evidence only when it is preserved, documented in place and compared with controls. Here, the reported material feature vanished from the investigation before any such work could begin. The case remains open because the claimed evidence could not be recovered, not because laboratory testing found an anomalous substance.

No later report located the circle or supplied a photograph, sample or second observation. The absence of follow-up evidence weakens attempts to treat it as a landing mark or other extraordinary residue. It remains an unusual personal discovery whose original cause is no longer recoverable from the surviving record.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1994-10-01373" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCERILLY (03) 18.10.199422 Mar 2007 — CERILLY (03) 18.10.1994. ## Missing traces, measurements and corroboration

The three cases fail at different points, but their evidence gaps form a consistent pattern. Yzeure lacks corroboration and spatial measurements. Cosne-d’Allier has two witnesses for the road episode but lacks independent viewpoints and objective tracking. Cérilly describes possible physical material, yet the site and material were lost before examination.

The missing elements are not optional refinements. They are what would allow an investigation to distinguish among competing explanations:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Direction and elevation:** Repeated compass bearings and angular heights can be compared with stars, planets, aircraft routes, satellites and the observer’s own movement.
* **Accurate timing:** A start and end time, preferably checked against a clock rather than estimated later, allows reconstruction of aviation, astronomical and atmospheric conditions.
* **Independent observers:** Witnesses at separate locations can establish whether a phenomenon was distant, localised or moving along a shared path.
* **Images with context:** A photograph is most useful when its time, location, lens, orientation and original file are available. An isolated bright shape without those details may add little.
* **Recoverable physical material:** Samples need secure collection, labelled locations, controls and a documented chain of handling before laboratory results can be interpreted.
* **Detection records:** Radar, radio, air-traffic or meteorological data can supply objective constraints, although their absence does not by itself prove that nothing was present.

</div>

GEIPAN’s classification method is designed to reflect this distinction between strangeness and consistency. “Strangeness” concerns how unusual a phenomenon remains after known possibilities have been considered. “Consistency” concerns the quantity, reliability and objectivity of the information available. A report may sound highly strange while scoring poorly on consistency, leaving investigators unable to classify the cause with confidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">information. Classification D...</span></span></span>

That is why category C should not be read as a shortlist of France’s strongest UFO evidence. CNES currently describes nearly one-third of GEIPAN’s cases as unidentified for lack of data, compared with only a small minority remaining unidentified after investigation. The numerical balance reinforces the point: incomplete documentation is common, while well-supported residual mysteries are much rarer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes.fr.</span></span></span>

## What “unresolved” means in Allier

The unresolved Allier files support three limited conclusions. First, the witnesses reported experiences they found genuinely unusual. Nothing in the official summaries requires an accusation of dishonesty. Second, an official record preserves a claim; it does not certify the witness’s interpretation of it. Third, the passage of time usually enlarges rather than repairs evidential gaps unless new documents or witnesses emerge.

Possible ordinary explanations remain available in broad terms for Yzeure and Cosne-d’Allier—aircraft, bright astronomical objects, reflections or viewing effects—but the surviving data are insufficient to select one responsibly. Naming a specific solution without the original geometry would merely replace an unsupported extraordinary claim with an unsupported conventional one. Cérilly is still less suited to retrospective identification because the feature’s position and material composition were never established.

The cases therefore occupy an important middle ground between “debunked” and “strongly unexplained”. They have not been demonstrated to involve exotic objects, but neither can every detail now be traced to a particular mundane source. Their enduring value lies in showing how rapidly an observation becomes scientifically untestable when bearings are not recorded, witnesses are not separated, photographs are absent or a suspected trace is left behind.

Within Allier’s broader record, this also prevents a misleading impression of a concentrated local mystery. The department contains reports that were later linked to ordinary causes as well as category C files whose causes remain unknown only because the record is incomplete. Yzeure, Cosne-d’Allier and Cérilly are notable not for proving that something extraordinary operated over Allier, but for demonstrating the practical difference between a compelling account and a durable body of evidence.
