## Introduction

Most UFO reports in Gard were not solved by discovering a single dramatic clue. They were solved by reconstructing ordinary details: the witness’s position, viewing direction, exact time, apparent motion, weather, astronomical objects and known traffic in the sky. In the official GEIPAN archive, Gard sightings have been attributed to Venus, stars, satellites, aircraft, sky lanterns and balloons. Other reports remain unclassified chiefly because investigators received too little reliable information to test any explanation properly.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=116&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=46%2C0&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">SAINT-AMBROIX (30) 18.01.2017 &#124; 18/01/2017 &#124; B &#124; Gard &#124; Avion &#124; 30/08/2017 &#124; 2017-01-18... That distinction is the key to understanding solved UFO cases in the department. An identified report does not mean that the witness was dishonest or careless: familiar objects can look genuinely strange when seen briefly, without distance cues or under unusual lighting. Equally, an officially unidentified report is not automatically stronger evidence for an extraordinary craft. In GEIPAN’s system, some cases remain unidentified simply because the evidence is too incomplete to support either identification or meaningful exclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestigation...</span></span></span>

## What GEIPAN’s classifications actually mean

GEIPAN is the unit of the French space agency CNES responsible for collecting, investigating and publishing reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena. Its work begins with testimony rather than with an assumption that a physical object was present. Witnesses are asked to complete a technical questionnaire covering matters such as location, time, duration, direction, angular size, movement and observing conditions. Photographs, videos, sketches or other records may supplement the account, but many Gard files depend largely on what one person remembered seeing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN7 Jul 2025 — GEIPAN, the French UAP research and information group created by CNES in 1977, collects, analyses and archives inf...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN also assesses the consistency of the evidence and the residual strangeness left after possible explanations have been examined. A case with a vivid description may still have low consistency if it comes from a lone observer, lacks photographs or has an uncertain direction and time. Conversely, a visually unimpressive light can be easy to identify when the timing and geometry match a known planet, aircraft or satellite.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/58788" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is why the word “unidentified” must be read carefully in Gard’s records. Category C often means “not enough dependable data”, not “ordinary explanations were ruled out”. GEIPAN can revisit C and D cases if new information becomes available, so classifications are investigative judgements rather than permanent declarations about the nature of an object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/faq-page" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">vestigation...</span></span></span>

## Nîmes cases solved by astronomy and satellites

Nîmes provides some of the clearest examples of how a striking light can become identifiable once its time and direction are checked against the sky.

On 6 September 2016, a Nîmes observation was classified A and identified as Venus. The planet is a recurrent source of UFO reports because it can be exceptionally bright, is often seen low in twilight or darkness and may appear to shimmer, change colour or move when viewed through disturbed air or against drifting cloud. With no obvious foreground reference, an observer may also find it difficult to judge whether the light is nearby or many millions of kilometres away. The decisive point is not merely that Venus was visible, but that its position matched the reported direction and time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmax%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%255Bmin%255D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=27&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">NIMES (30) 06.09.2016 &#124; 06/09/2016 &#124; A &#124; Gard &#124; Vénus &#124; 18/05/2020 &#124; 2016-09-06...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: September 6, 2016</span></span></span>

An older case from 5 January 1984 shows how an astronomical identification can emerge even from a more complicated account made during a journey from Nîmes towards Montpellier. One component of that report was eventually classified A as a confusion with Aldebaran, a bright star. GEIPAN’s published summary says that the star was observed in a temporarily favourable geographical configuration, meaning that the changing road direction and sightlines made a fixed celestial object appear more puzzling than it would have from a stationary viewpoint.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1984-01-01016" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The same journey also produced a separate component that remained category C. That contrast is instructive. Investigators could match one observation to a star because enough geometry survived in the record, while the other lacked sufficiently reliable information. A single episode can therefore contain both an identified sighting and an officially inconclusive one without contradiction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1984-01-01016" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A further Nîmes case, dated 15 August 2015, concerned an intensely bright point seen towards the south-east for about ten seconds before it disappeared. The witness himself suggested an Iridium satellite flare after consulting GEIPAN material. These flares occurred when sunlight reflected from the highly polished antenna surfaces of older Iridium communications satellites, producing a brief, brilliant point that could brighten and fade without crossing much of the visible sky. GEIPAN classified the sighting A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-08-09289?amp=&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=58&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The case matters because it demonstrates that investigation is not always a contest between an insistent witness and a sceptical authority. Witnesses may offer useful hypotheses, especially when they report the time precisely and understand what information is needed. The investigator’s role is then to test whether the proposed satellite, planet or star was actually in the correct place and could reproduce the reported appearance.

## Aircraft checks around Nîmes and rural Gard

Aircraft are another recurring explanation, particularly in a department containing busy civil and military aviation routes and the Nîmes-Garons airport area. At night, an approaching aircraft may seem almost stationary because it is travelling mainly towards the observer. Its landing lights can dominate while the navigation lights remain difficult to see, creating the appearance of one brilliant object hovering or moving unusually slowly.

A Nîmes report from 14 October 2015 was classified A as an aircraft. The archive also records a category B aircraft explanation at Saint-Ambroix on 18 January 2017, a category A airliner identification at Saint-Laurent-le-Minier on 8 July 2014, and a probable aircraft explanation at Montmirat on 19 August 1993. Solving an aircraft case normally requires more than noticing that aeroplanes exist nearby. Investigators must compare the reported direction and movement with plausible flight paths, consider whether the object’s apparent speed could have been distorted by distance, and examine the sequence of visible lights. A white light that later reveals red or green points, changes direction slowly or fades as it turns away is much more compatible with an aircraft than with a fixed astronomical object.

The difference between categories A and B is important here. A precise correspondence with a known flight or an unmistakable lighting pattern may justify category A. Where the behaviour resembles an aircraft but no definitive flight record or exact geometry is available, category B is more cautious. It records the most credible explanation without claiming proof beyond the surviving evidence.

## Lanterns and balloons that behaved like craft

Small illuminated or reflective objects can generate surprisingly elaborate descriptions because wind, perspective and changing illumination alter how they appear.

A Nîmes report from 17 December 2016 was classified B as a probable Thai-style sky lantern. Such lanterns carry a flame beneath a lightweight paper envelope. Seen from a distance, the flame may resemble an orange or reddish luminous object travelling silently. Gusts can make it rise, pause or change direction, while the flame’s extinction can cause the light to vanish suddenly. Because the launch itself is rarely witnessed, the observer sees only an unexplained light apparently manoeuvring in the night sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=122%2C21&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The observation, made on 8 August 2020 and updated by GEIPAN in October 2025, was classified B as a probable Mylar balloon. The published file records moderate-to-good consistency and low residual strangeness. Metallic balloons can reflect sunlight intensely, rotate, drift irregularly and change apparent shape as their surfaces flex. A distant balloon may therefore seem to flash, tumble or alter course even though it is simply being carried by the wind.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2020-08-51053?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B0%5D=12&amp;page=2%2C9" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UZES (30) 08.08.2020UZES (30) 08.08.2020. These cases illustrate why apparent movement must be separated from measured movement. Without a known distance, witnesses cannot calculate an object’s actual size, altitude or speed. A nearby balloon moving slowly can look like a large object far away; a lantern drifting with the wind can seem to hover; and a rotating reflective surface can create repeated flashes that resemble signalling lights.

## How investigators narrow an explanation

Gard’s solved cases reveal a practical sequence rather than a mysterious detection technology. Investigators work backwards from the observation and ask which known source best fits all the dependable details.

**First, they establish the viewing geometry.** The observer’s exact location, direction of sight and elevation are crucial. “In front of me” or “over the hills” is far less useful than a compass direction or a landmark that can be plotted. Astronomical identifications such as Venus or Aldebaran depend especially on this information.

**Second, they fix the time and duration.** An error of several minutes may weaken a satellite match, while an uncertain observation date can make meaningful aircraft or astronomy checks impossible. The ten-second duration in the August 2015 Nîmes case, for example, was compatible with a brief satellite flare rather than a planet visible for much longer.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-08-09289" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Third, they compare the description with known behaviour.** A constant light fixed in one direction points towards astronomy. A bright light that approaches, reveals multiple colours or changes as it turns may indicate an aircraft. A warm-coloured silent light drifting with the wind is compatible with a lantern. Irregular flashes and shape changes can suggest a rotating metallic balloon.

**Fourth, they examine independent material.** Depending on the case, this may include astronomical calculations, satellite trajectories, weather data, flight information, photographs, video or testimony from additional observers. GEIPAN’s methodology treats supporting material as a way to test testimony, not simply as decoration added to a witness statement.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/what-did-i-see/step-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANUnderstanding a PhenomenonGEIPAN only investigates phenomena reported by one or more direct witnesses of the observation. **Finally, they assess what remains unexplained.** An explanation does not need to reproduce every uncertain adjective in a witness’s account. Human estimates of size, distance and speed are particularly unreliable when an isolated light is viewed against a dark sky. Investigators therefore give greater weight to details that can be checked independently.

## Why some weak reports remain officially unidentified

Several Gard files are category C, including Nîmes observations from 11 August and 13 October 2015, one component of the 1984 Nîmes-to-Montpellier case, and a Pont-Saint-Esprit report from 12 July 2018. GEIPAN’s database describes these cases as lacking reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation_textuel&amp;page=26%2C11&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This does not mean investigators established that the objects were unlike aircraft, planets or satellites. More often, the weakness lies in the record itself:

* the viewing direction is approximate or missing;

The La Calmette report of 15 December 2023 demonstrates how GEIPAN expresses these limitations. The witness saw a phenomenon while driving on the N106 towards Nîmes, but the official case page notes that the testimony came from one person, lacked photographic or video evidence and did not establish the exact viewing direction. Those gaps reduced the consistency of the case and restricted how confidently investigators could compare the observation with possible sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2023-12-51503" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

A category C file is therefore better understood as an evidential dead end than as a surviving anomaly. There may well have been an ordinary explanation, but the information needed to identify it was not preserved. This is one reason later retellings can make a weak case sound stronger: the label “unidentified” survives, while the accompanying phrase “due to lack of data” is forgotten.

## What the solved cases change about Gard’s UFO history

Gard’s official record is most revealing as a collection of investigation outcomes rather than as a catalogue of extraordinary craft. The solved cases show recurring pathways from strangeness to identification: a fixed bright light becomes Venus or a star; a brief flash becomes a satellite reflection; a hovering light becomes an approaching aircraft; a drifting orange object becomes a lantern; and a flashing irregular shape becomes a metallic balloon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-08-09289" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

They also show that witness sincerity and mistaken identification can coexist. Nothing in an astronomical or aviation explanation requires the observer to have invented the event. The sighting may have been genuinely surprising, especially when viewed from a moving vehicle, through haze, near the horizon or without familiar visual references.

The strongest lesson is about evidence quality. Reports containing a precise time, reliable direction, short reporting delay and supporting material are often easier to solve, even when the phenomenon initially appears dramatic. Reports with uncertain geometry may remain unresolved despite sounding less unusual. Gard’s category C cases consequently should not be ranked above its category A or B cases merely because no final object name appears in the file.

Within the department’s wider UFO history, solved reports provide the necessary baseline for judging more controversial claims. They demonstrate how many seemingly unusual features can arise from perspective, atmospheric effects and incomplete observation. Any Gard case proposed as genuinely exceptional must therefore do more than remain unidentified: it must contain evidence strong enough to survive the same astronomy, satellite, balloon and aviation checks that resolved the department’s better-documented ordinary sightings.
