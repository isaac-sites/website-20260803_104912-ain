## Introduction

Casteljaloux in 1978 and Montayral in 2015 form the strongest unresolved strand in [Lot-et-Garonne]({{ 'which-lot-et-garonne-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }})’s official UFO record. Both remain classified as unexplained by GEIPAN, the French space agency’s investigation service, because the reported features did not fit any conventional explanation well enough. Yet neither case supplies strong evidence for an extraordinary aircraft. Each rests on one witness, with no photograph, video, radar confirmation or independently verified second observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Lot-et-Garonne_6df799_unresolved_casteljal_2b7e7e-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Unresolved Cases" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The cases are valuable precisely because they show what “unexplained” means in official practice. Casteljaloux is a dramatic but sparsely documented night-time account involving an orange luminous form, unusual movements and a frightened dog. Montayral is less spectacular but much better investigated: a stationary black form was reconstructed on site, compared with aircraft, balloons, astronomical objects and possible visual effects, then left unresolved because the leading balloon explanation remained too uncertain. Neither file confirms what was present; both record the limits of what investigators could establish.

## What was reported at Casteljaloux and Montayral?

The two observations occurred exactly 37 years apart, on 23 October, but otherwise bear little resemblance. There is no evidence that the shared date has any significance, and the files do not suggest a recurring phenomenon. Their connection is evidential rather than physical: they are isolated Lot-et-Garonne reports in which an unusual description survived official scrutiny without receiving a satisfactory identification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### Casteljaloux: an orange luminous object in 1978

At about 20:40 on 23 October 1978, a 60-year-old man in agricultural surroundings near Casteljaloux reported seeing a single luminous phenomenon for several minutes. GEIPAN’s summary describes it as round or disc-like, scintillating and emitting orange light. According to the witness, it changed speed and direction, made no sound and disappeared suddenly. He also said that a ball of fire emerged from it. His dog was reportedly frightened during the encounter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The catalogue data give an estimated distance of 200 metres and an apparent or inferred diameter of about one metre. Those figures should not be treated as measurements. With an unfamiliar light against the night sky, a lone observer normally has no reliable scale by which to determine an object’s true distance or size. GEIPAN itself stresses that witnesses can sincerely assign distance, speed and physical form to poorly referenced phenomena even when those properties cannot be established visually.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47732" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

What gives the Casteljaloux report its apparent strength is the combination of several unusual features: variable movement, silence, an orange glow, the reported ejection of a “fireball” and an animal reaction. What weakens it is that virtually all those features come from the same uncorroborated account. The gendarmerie obtained no other testimony supporting the sighting, despite its reported duration and conspicuous appearance. The public file contains no photograph, instrument reading, physical trace or detailed modern reconstruction.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The dog’s behaviour adds human interest but little diagnostic value. An animal may react to its owner, an unseen sound, another animal or an unrelated disturbance. Without a veterinary effect, recorded noise or independent witness, fear cannot demonstrate that the aerial phenomenon caused the reaction. Similarly, the phrase “ball of fire” is striking but too imprecise to distinguish between a separate light, a visual after-image, fragmentation, cloud effects or a mistaken reconstruction of a moving luminous source.

### Montayral: a black form beside an aerodrome

The Montayral observation occurred in daylight near Fumel aerodrome. At around 17:31 on 23 October 2015, a man who had stopped to walk his dog noticed what he described as a black, opaque spot surrounded by a black haze in an otherwise clear blue sky. He thought it was high and distant. The central form appeared roughly round, with a curved protrusion likened to the handle of an inverted teapot. It remained in the same apparent position until approximately 17:58. When the witness briefly looked towards his car, he could no longer find it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The description was unusually perceptual rather than mechanical. The witness compared the sight to missing pixels on a screen, a black mark added to a photograph, or a dark hole with distorted edges. He did not report lights, windows, exhaust, rotation, occupants or any direct interaction with the surroundings. No noise came from the phenomenon, although an unrelated engine sound was audible elsewhere.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

A later on-site reconstruction placed the line of sight at approximately 44 degrees north-east and seven degrees above the horizon. The apparent width was estimated at half the Moon’s diameter, about 0.25 degrees. Those angular estimates allowed investigators to model possibilities but not to calculate a unique distance: an object half a metre wide might have been roughly 100 metres away, while a 20-metre object could have been several kilometres distant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

That uncertainty is central to the case. The witness interpreted the blurred appearance as evidence that the form was near the far hills, but blur is not a dependable range finder. Atmospheric contrast, eyesight, focus, glare, motionless staring and the object’s own outline can all affect sharpness. The report therefore establishes an apparent dark form in a particular direction, not a confirmed large object suspended kilometres away.

## Why did investigators leave the cases unresolved?

GEIPAN does not use “unexplained” as a synonym for extraterrestrial. Its classification weighs residual strangeness against consistency: investigators ask how poorly the observation fits known phenomena and whether the available information is reliable enough to support that judgement. A case may remain unexplained when no proposed cause exceeds the service’s confidence threshold, even though one ordinary explanation appears more plausible than the alternatives.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">missions methodes et resultats</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPANSince 2008, a more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN. The difference between the two Lot-et-Garonne files is substantial. Casteljaloux retains a broad category D classification, but the surviving public material offers little insight into detailed hypothesis testing. Montayral received a field investigation, cognitive interview, geographical reconstruction, weather and astronomical checks, examination of air traffic data and review by GEIPAN’s expert panel. It was classified D1, meaning unexplained with moderate rather than extreme strangeness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-10-09361" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTAYRAL (47) 23.10.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

For Montayral, investigators considered several possibilities:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **An astronomical object** did not fit the black appearance or the way the phenomenon ceased to be visible.
* **A conventional aeroplane** could not readily remain in one apparent position for about 20 minutes.
* **A hovering helicopter** could remain stationary, but should probably have produced audible noise at the distance implied by the witness’s estimates. The available air-traffic information did not support one in the relevant position.
* **An eye condition or visual floater** did not fit well because such effects normally move with the eye or persist in other viewing directions.
* **A small balloon** matched silence and rounded form but was difficult to reconcile with the apparent distance and prolonged immobility.
* **A solar balloon** could appear black, but investigators considered its presence in that rural location insufficiently supported.
* **A hot-air balloon** gave the best overall fit. It could be large, distant, quiet and nearly motionless in weak wind, particularly during a suitable late-afternoon flying period.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The hot-air-balloon hypothesis failed to cross the threshold for a probable explanation. The reported protrusion was not positioned like a basket beneath an envelope, although investigators noted that the witness only discerned it after prolonged concentration. More importantly, the apparent disappearance occurred while the witness was looking away. A balloon might have descended behind terrain, but this required favourable assumptions about its size, height, descent rate, the witness’s angular estimates and the true length of the interruption. GEIPAN judged the hypothesis the strongest available but still well below a 50 per cent probability.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Investigators contacted balloon pilots in the department but received no replies. That left a practical line of enquiry incomplete, rather than providing evidence against a balloon. No operator came forward to confirm a flight, but neither was there a comprehensive negative check establishing that no balloon had been airborne.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2015-10-09361" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTAYRAL (47) 23.10.2015 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Casteljaloux is harder to reassess because the public record is far thinner. A bright orange object changing apparent direction might suggest an aircraft seen under unusual geometry, a lantern, a balloon carrying a light, an astronomical source affected by observer movement, or a genuinely transient luminous event. Yet the reported manoeuvres and emitted fireball cannot be tested without a precise trajectory, angular positions, timings, weather data and a clear account of whether the witness moved during the observation. The case remains unexplained, but the label reflects unresolved testimony more than a rich body of mutually supporting evidence.

## What evidence supports the reports?

The strongest evidence in both cases is the witnesses’ apparent willingness to provide a coherent account. Casteljaloux was reported to the gendarmerie, giving the case an official documentary origin rather than leaving it as folklore or a late anecdote. Montayral’s witness completed a detailed questionnaire and later participated in a cognitive interview and an on-site reconstruction. GEIPAN described him as precise and credible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Montayral also benefits from several checks that were broadly consistent with the account. Archived weather information supported clear conditions and good visibility, with light winds. The reconstructed positions of the Sun and Moon agreed with the witness’s recollection. Investigators found no obvious industrial smoke source in the viewing direction, and the available aviation data did not reveal an aircraft whose movement matched a stationary object lasting many minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

These points support the proposition that the witness had an unusual visual experience at the stated place and time. They do not establish that the experience involved a solid craft. Clear weather makes a distant object easier to see, but it also permits balloons, aircraft and atmospheric contrast effects to be visible over long ranges. The absence of an identified aeroplane excludes only some aviation explanations, not every untracked or non-aircraft object.

Casteljaloux’s account contains more dramatic reported behaviour, but less checkable support. The witness described several minutes of observation and a phenomenon that appeared close, mobile and bright. Had the estimated 200-metre range represented a measured fact, the case would be considerably stronger. In reality, no triangulation or physical reference verifies that distance. The details are therefore best read as the witness’s impression of proximity and motion, not demonstrated performance by an object.

## What prevents stronger conclusions?

The decisive weakness is independent corroboration. Neither sighting produced a second witness whose observations could be compared for direction, duration, shape or movement. Both men had dogs with them, but an animal cannot provide an independent descriptive account. The lack of additional reports is not proof that nothing unusual was present, particularly in rural areas, but it removes the possibility of triangulating distance or separating shared external features from individual perception.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

No image exists in either file. A photograph would not automatically solve the case, but even an ordinary phone image at Montayral could have preserved the object’s position relative to the horizon, tested whether the “haze” was optical or physical and allowed estimates of contrast and apparent size. At Casteljaloux, sequential photographs might have shown whether the light followed a continuous track, fragmented or merely appeared to change direction.

Radar evidence is also absent or limited. Montayral’s investigation used available air-traffic information to assess aircraft and helicopters, but there was no radar return corresponding to the reported object. That does not prove that the sky was empty: balloons and small or non-metallic objects may not produce useful returns, while not every radar system records every target. In Casteljaloux, the public record provides no comparable radar analysis.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The reported endings are especially weak. The Casteljaloux phenomenon “disappeared suddenly”, but the surviving summary does not provide enough detail to determine whether it faded, accelerated, passed behind an obstruction or was simply lost from view. At Montayral, the disappearance was not observed at all. The witness turned away and found the object gone afterwards. That gap allows an apparently impossible vanishing to be replaced by several ordinary possibilities, including descent behind terrain, gradual drift outside the searched area or failure to reacquire a small low-contrast target.

Elapsed time also introduces uncertainty. Casteljaloux was recorded through a late-1970s reporting process whose public summary is brief. Montayral was investigated more carefully, but the field interview began more than two months after the event. GEIPAN uses cognitive interviews to reduce leading questions and recover detail responsibly, yet no interview can recreate objective measurements that were never made at the time. The later reconstruction strengthens the documentation of what the witness remembered while leaving distance, physical scale and exact disappearance time unresolved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete492.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete492</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## Unexplained does not mean equally persuasive

Montayral is the stronger evidential case because the investigation tested concrete alternatives and documented why none was accepted. Its unresolved status is narrow: a distant balloon, especially a hot-air balloon, remained plausible but could not be demonstrated. The file sits close to the boundary between category B, meaning probably explained, and category D1. A confirmed balloon flight, a more reliable estimate of how long the witness looked away, or evidence that the line of sight crossed terrain suitable for a rapid descent could plausibly change the classification.

Casteljaloux is stranger in narrative terms but weaker as evidence. Its unusual movement and “fireball” report create a more memorable UFO story, yet the lack of detailed investigation material makes conventional hypotheses difficult either to prove or eliminate. The category D label should not disguise that limitation. An old case can remain unexplained because its features are extraordinary, because its documentation is inadequate for a decisive retrospective explanation, or through some mixture of both.

Later reporting has not materially strengthened either claim. No matching witness, image, operator record or physical evidence has emerged in the public files. Montayral’s page was updated in June 2026 but still retained its unexplained classification and its assessment of medium-to-strong consistency. Casteljaloux likewise remained category D after its 2021 update. Their endurance in the catalogue therefore reflects the absence of decisive new evidence in either direction, not accumulating confirmation of an unknown craft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/47733" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANCASTELJALOUX (47) 23.10.1978 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Within Lot-et-Garonne’s UFO history, the fairest conclusion is that both witnesses reported experiences that official investigators could not confidently identify. Montayral offers a comparatively disciplined unresolved investigation with a plausible but unconfirmed balloon explanation. Casteljaloux preserves a more dramatic claim whose evidential value is restricted by age, sparse documentation and the absence of corroboration. They deserve the term “unexplained”, but neither provides firm grounds for deciding what physical phenomenon—ordinary or extraordinary—was actually present.
