## Introduction

Isère’s official UFO files reveal a process of identification rather than a catalogue of unexplained craft. A witness reports something genuinely puzzling: silent orange lights, a bright point moving across the sky, a dark shape passing overhead or an object apparently behaving unlike an aircraft. Investigators then test the account against flight data, satellite predictions, weather, astronomy, video measurements, local events and the limits of human perception.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Isere_7db861_isere_geipan_case_pa_c885b7-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Case Files" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The result is often less dramatic but more informative. Some Isère reports have been identified as the International Space Station, sky lanterns or meteors. Others have been judged probably caused by drones or balloons because the available evidence fits those explanations better than any alternative. Older files frequently remain open not because they contain strong evidence of something extraordinary, but because too little dependable information survives to test the possibilities.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## How GEIPAN turns a sighting into a case

GEIPAN, the specialist unit of the French space agency CNES, does not begin by asking whether a witness has seen an alien vehicle. It begins with a narrower question: what known phenomenon best matches the reported time, direction, movement, appearance and circumstances?

Its classification system separates the strength of the evidence from the strangeness of the original experience. “Consistency” measures the quantity, reliability and objectivity of the available information. “Residual strangeness” measures how unusual the event still appears after investigators have compared it with conventional possibilities. A case can therefore sound extraordinary at first but become ordinary once better evidence is introduced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial when reading Isère’s archive. Category C does not mean that investigators found a well-documented anomaly. It means they lacked enough material to reach a reliable conclusion. Category D is the genuinely unresolved category, although even that label records the state of the evidence rather than confirming an extraordinary origin. GEIPAN can revisit C and D files when new information becomes available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

The national figures reinforce that point. As of 31 March 2026, GEIPAN reported that 66.5 per cent of its published cases were classified A or B, 30.3 per cent were category C because of insufficient information, and 3.2 per cent remained category D. The broad pattern in Isère fits this larger picture: most useful conclusions come either from identifying a conventional cause or recognising that the surviving evidence is too weak to support one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANClassification &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## The strongest explanations come from matching several clues

An explanation becomes persuasive when it accounts for several features at once. A mere resemblance is not enough. Investigators look for agreement between the proposed cause and the observation’s timing, path, speed, colour, sound, apparent size and environmental setting.

That process is visible in Isère’s better-documented modern files. They show different levels of certainty, from cases with a directly confirmed source to cases where one hypothesis simply fits the evidence much better than its rivals.

### Sky lanterns near Pierre-Châtel

On 6 March 2010, motorists travelling between Pierre-Châtel and La Motte-d’Aveillans saw about fifteen red-orange lights moving silently through the night sky. The observation lasted more than five minutes, and one witness recorded it on a mobile telephone. A group of evenly spaced, glowing objects moving without engine noise can easily appear organised or technological, particularly when distance and altitude are impossible to judge.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-03-02536?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=20%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D115] de PIERRE-CHATEL (38) vers MOTTE-D&#x27;AVEILLANS (LA) (38) 06.03.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The case became unusually strong because the investigation did not rely only on visual similarity. After local press coverage, a person came forward to say that thirteen sky lanterns had been released during a private celebration. A gendarmerie enquiry independently linked the lights to that event, about 2.5 kilometres from the witnesses. GEIPAN therefore classified the case A. The decisive evidence was not simply that the lights looked like lanterns; it was the match between their appearance, number, behaviour, location and an identified launch.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-03-02536?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=20%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D115] de PIERRE-CHATEL (38) vers MOTTE-D&#x27;AVEILLANS (LA) (38) 06.03.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This case also shows how publicity can help rather than distort an investigation. Newspaper coverage sometimes encourages exaggeration, but here it reached the people who knew the source of the lights.

### The International Space Station over Les Avenières

On 3 August 2014, a witness at Les Avenières watched a bright white point move silently from the south-west towards the north-east at roughly the apparent speed of an aircraft. The object produced no flashing navigation lights and no engine sound, making it seem unfamiliar.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08961" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAVENIERES (LES) (38) 03.08.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The timing and path closely resembled an International Space Station pass. Orbital information confirmed that the ISS was visible from the area between approximately 22:36 and 22:45, travelling in the same direction described by the witness. GEIPAN classified the report A because the predicted passage matched the observation and there was no separate sighting that would require a second object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2014-08-08961" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANAVENIERES (LES) (38) 03.08.2014 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The lesson is broader than this single file. Satellites are most conspicuous when the observer is in darkness but the spacecraft remains illuminated by the Sun. They can appear extremely bright, travel smoothly and vanish when they enter Earth’s shadow. Without checking orbital data, those features may look stranger than they are.

### A meteor over Saint-Égrève

GEIPAN’s Isère records also include a Saint-Égrève observation from 13 March 2014 classified A as a bright meteor. Such events often generate reports of fast lights, sudden flashes, changing colour or apparent fragmentation. Because a meteor can cross a large portion of the sky in seconds, witnesses may describe extreme speed or an object seeming much lower and closer than it really was.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_date_value=2020-11-18&amp;field_is_new_value=1&amp;order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=%2C2&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Meteor explanations become stronger when reports from different locations describe the same short-lived path at the same time. They are also supported by characteristic duration: an event lasting only a few seconds is more consistent with atmospheric entry than with a hovering aircraft or satellite. A vivid experience can therefore be entirely sincere while its estimated distance, size and trajectory are mistaken.

## Probable causes are not the same as proven identities

Category B files are especially useful because they show where official investigation stops short of certainty. Investigators may have a strong conventional hypothesis but no direct confirmation of the exact object involved. The conclusion is therefore “probably explained”, not “definitively solved”.

### A likely drone at Anthon

At Anthon on 22 September 2021, two residents heard a high-pitched whistling noise and then saw a dark mass cross their field of view in about one second. No photograph was taken, and only one formal witness account was available. These limitations reduced the consistency of the case: the observation was simply too brief to produce a detailed visual description.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-09-51256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANTHON (38) 22.09.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several clues nevertheless pointed towards a drone. The reported shape resembled some drone designs, the sound suggested a nearby powered object, and the sighting occurred near an area where model aircraft activity was permitted. GEIPAN classified the case B because a drone fitted the account well, although investigators could not identify a particular operator or machine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2021-09-51256" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANANTHON (38) 22.09.2021 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

This is a good example of disciplined uncertainty. The lack of a serial number, flight log or operator admission prevents an A classification. It does not make every explanation equally likely.

### Balloons over Échirolles

A more recent Échirolles case shows how video analysis can narrow the possibilities without producing absolute proof. At about 06:30 on 29 July 2024, two witnesses saw a silent white object moving slowly and steadily from south to north. It remained visible for roughly five minutes before vegetation blocked the view. One witness supplied a 44-second video.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-07-51591" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANECHIROLLES (38) 29.07.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Investigators considered both an aircraft and a cluster of balloons. An aircraft initially seemed possible because the object appeared elongated and followed a regular path. However, no matching aircraft appeared on the relevant radar information. The balloon hypothesis fitted more features: rounded elements were visible within the elongated form, the white surface could have reflected the newly risen Sun, and the slow straight movement matched objects being carried by the wind. Measurements from the video were compatible with a cluster roughly 80 centimetres to one metre long at a distance of around 40 to 70 metres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-07-51591" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANECHIROLLES (38) 29.07.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why photographs and videos do not settle every report

Images can strengthen a case, but they rarely interpret themselves. A small bright object may cover only a few pixels. Digital zoom can exaggerate blur, atmospheric movement and camera shake. Autofocus can make a point source expand into a disc or pulsating shape. Compression may create apparent edges or colours that were not visible to the eye.

The Échirolles video was useful because investigators could compare apparent movement, shape and scale with specific hypotheses. It did not contain a sharp close-up identifying an object directly. Its value came from measurement and context rather than visual spectacle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-07-51591" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANECHIROLLES (38) 29.07.2024 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The Pierre-Châtel recording likewise supported the description of multiple orange lights, but the most conclusive evidence came from outside the video: the identified lantern release and the gendarmerie enquiry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-03-02536?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=20%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D115] de PIERRE-CHATEL (38) vers MOTTE-D&#x27;AVEILLANS (LA) (38) 06.03.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## The witness can be credible and still be mistaken

Explained cases do not usually require fraud, fantasy or incompetence. Many familiar objects become difficult to recognise when seen without a clear horizon, at an unknown distance or under unusual lighting.

Several recurring problems appear in Isère’s files:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Apparent size is unreliable without distance.** A small nearby balloon can look like a large distant craft.
* **Silence does not prove exotic propulsion.** Satellites and balloons make no audible noise, while distant aircraft may be inaudible.
* **A steady course can look controlled.** Windborne lanterns or balloons may move smoothly for several minutes.
* **Brightness can conceal shape.** Sunlit spacecraft, reflective balloons and meteors can appear as featureless luminous objects.
* **Brief observations encourage reconstruction.** A one-second glimpse may leave the witness with an impression of shape and motion that cannot be checked in detail.

</div>

GEIPAN treats witness testimony as the starting point of an enquiry, but its methodology places more weight on information that can be checked independently. That may include radar records, orbital predictions, weather data, photographs, video geometry, several independent accounts or evidence of a local launch or event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

## The 5 November 1990 wave shows the value of a wider view

The most important explanation associated with Isère’s UFO history did not emerge from investigating Grenoble-area reports in isolation. Shortly after 19:00 on 5 November 1990, more than a thousand witnesses across France reported a large luminous formation crossing a clear sky. The breadth of the reports made the event seem exceptional, but it also provided the clue needed to explain it. GEIPAN later modelled the path as the atmospheric re-entry of space hardware, whose fragments produced a long procession of lights visible over a wide area.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/simulation-rentree-atmospherique-1990" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">simulation rentree atmospherique 1990</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANNouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5...10 Mar 2015 — Le 5 Novembre 1990, à partir de 19h, plus de mille témoins ré...</span></span></span>

Individual witnesses could reasonably interpret the display as a structured object. Points of light moving together may appear connected by an invisible body, particularly when the observer assumes they are nearby. Yet reports spread across France were more compatible with a high-altitude re-entry than with a single low-flying craft over each locality.

For Isère, the episode demonstrates why investigators compare local accounts with events beyond the department. A sighting over Grenoble may be explained by national satellite tracking, reports from distant regions or a known atmospheric event. Local testimony remains important, but its meaning can change when placed inside the larger geographical pattern.

## Why some older Isère files stay open

Older cases often have the opposite problem from the [1990 wave]({{ '1990-wave/' | relative_url }}): too little evidence rather than a large body of mutually supporting reports. A brief narrative may survive without an exact direction, reliable time, weather record, photograph, independent witness statement or original gendarmerie document. Investigators cannot reconstruct a satellite pass, aircraft route or astronomical alignment confidently when the basic coordinates of the observation are uncertain.

That is why category C must not be confused with category D. A C file records an evidence failure. It may contain an unusual story, but investigators cannot test it properly. GEIPAN’s methodology explicitly defines the category as unidentified because of missing data or information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/node/412" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANComment le GEIPAN classe-t-il les cas d’observations? &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Such cases remain part of Isère’s UFO history because people experienced and reported them. Their unresolved status, however, should not be treated as evidence that conventional explanations were eliminated. In many instances, those explanations were never testable in the first place.

## What would make an explanation stronger

The Isère files suggest a practical hierarchy of evidence. The strongest explained cases combine several independent forms of confirmation:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--step-flow" markdown="1">

1. **Precise timing and direction**, allowing comparison with aircraft, satellites, meteors and astronomical objects.
2. **More than one independent account**, preferably recorded before witnesses influence one another.
3. **Original photographs or video**, including unedited files and metadata.
4. **Environmental records**, such as wind direction, cloud cover and visibility.
5. **Radar, orbital or aviation data**, where relevant.
6. **A confirmed local source**, such as a lantern release, balloon launch, drone operator or public event.

</div>

The Pierre-Châtel case reached category A because a known lantern release closely matched the witnesses’ observation and was supported by a gendarmerie enquiry. The Les Avenières sighting reached A because the ISS prediction agreed with the time and trajectory. Échirolles remained B because the balloon explanation fitted the measurements well but the exact object was never recovered or independently identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-03-02536?field_agregation_index_value=c&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date&amp;page=20%2C0&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN[D115] de PIERRE-CHATEL (38) vers MOTTE-D&#x27;AVEILLANS (LA) (38) 06.03.2010 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why unresolved does not mean extraordinary

An unexplained case is a statement about investigation, not origin. It means that no satisfactory identification was reached with the information available. It does not mean that investigators proved the object was beyond known technology or natural science.

Isère’s archive makes this distinction particularly clear. Some reports that initially appeared highly unusual became straightforward once a launch, orbital pass, atmospheric entry or likely drone was considered. Other reports remain unclassified because their evidence is fragmentary. The most defensible reading is therefore neither that every sighting has been solved nor that every unsolved file points towards something exotic.

What the files actually reveal is a spectrum of confidence. At one end are directly identified lanterns and satellites. In the middle are probable balloons, drones and meteors supported by several matching clues. At the other end are reports that cannot be tested adequately. Understanding that spectrum is more useful than counting every A, B, C or D entry as though the letters represented competing beliefs.

The lasting value of Isère’s case files lies in showing how sincere observation, incomplete perception and external evidence interact. Their central lesson is not that witnesses see nothing. It is that people can see something real, describe it honestly and still need careful investigation to discover what it was.

    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
