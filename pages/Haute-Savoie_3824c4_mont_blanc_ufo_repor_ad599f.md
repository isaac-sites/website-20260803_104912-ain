## Introduction

The supposed Mont Blanc UFO wave of May 2013 did not survive close examination. Two episodes reported within little more than a fortnight created the impression that unusual craft might be crossing the massif: red lights near Domancy, outside Sallanches, followed by a bright white object photographed from Chamonix. The first was traced to sky lanterns released at a wedding. The second was identified in follow-up reporting as sunlight reflecting from an aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. <img src="{{ "/assets/images/Haute-Savoie_3824c4_mont_blanc_ufo_repor_ad599f-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Mont Blanc" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The episode matters within [Haute-Savoie]({{ 'why-haute-savoies-ufo-stories-rarely/' | relative_url }})’s UFO history because it shows how a “wave” can form through timing and publicity rather than through connected events. Both reports were initially puzzling, both were framed through the language of UFOs, and both concerned the visually dramatic Mont Blanc area. Yet they involved different objects, different conditions and separate conventional explanations. Later information weakened, rather than strengthened, the idea of an extraordinary regional phenomenon.

## The Sallanches report and the first press claims

The story began with an observation made at about 11.45 pm on Saturday 11 May 2013. Charles Inderschit, a resident of Domancy, said that he and his 12-year-old son saw numerous red luminous balls near a chapel on the road linking Sallanches and Le Fayet. He described the lights as silent and apparently suspended in the air before they moved vertically and disappeared. The observation lasted roughly one minute.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Des Ovni ont-ils survolé le Mont-Blanc?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Des Ovni ont-ils survolé le Mont-Blanc?</span></span></span>

The reported number was striking. Inderschit spoke of around 30 lights, while photographs supplied to the newspaper showed luminous marks against the night sky. He also claimed that his camera stopped working afterwards, which added an apparently mysterious detail to the account. However, there was no independent evidence that the malfunction had any connection with the lights. A broken camera after an observation may be memorable, but without technical examination it does not establish electromagnetic interference or any other unusual effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Inderschit reported the incident to the Sallanches gendarmerie. The officers told *Le Dauphiné Libéré* that it was the only report of its kind they had received and that the information was insufficient to alert GEIPAN, the French space agency unit that examines reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena. CNES likewise confirmed that it had not been notified. The mayor of Domancy had not heard of other observations when contacted by the newspaper.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Those details substantially limit the strength of the original case. Two related witnesses saw the event together, but there was no cluster of independent observers reporting from different positions, no radar or aviation evidence, and no official investigation capable of reconstructing the lights’ direction, altitude or distance. The witnesses’ impression that the objects were stationary or moving vertically was necessarily based on viewing small lights against a dark sky with few reliable distance markers.

### The wedding-lantern explanation

The mystery was resolved on 18 May, only hours after the initial newspaper coverage appeared. A newly married man contacted the Sallanches newsroom and said that his wedding had taken place at the Tour Carrée in Domancy on 11 May, in the same area and at the relevant time. He and his guests had released approximately 15 sky lanterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/18/domancy-les-ovnis-etaient-des-lanternes-thailandaises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

Sky lanterns are small paper balloons lifted by heated air from a flame. Seen at night, they can resemble orange or red balls travelling silently in formation. Their motion depends on local air currents: individual lanterns may drift apart, appear almost stationary from one viewing angle, rise suddenly or vanish as their flames go out. That behaviour closely matches the central features of the Domancy account without requiring an exotic object.

There is a discrepancy between the estimated numbers: the witnesses counted about 30 lights, whereas the wedding party reportedly launched around 15 lanterns. This does not overturn the identification. Estimates made during a brief night-time observation can be unreliable, especially when lights move, disappear behind terrain or cloud, and reappear. Camera movement or long exposure can also make individual lights seem elongated or multiply represented. More importantly, the wedding account supplied a specific source at the correct place, date and time.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Des Ovni ont-ils survolé le Mont-Blanc?</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. Des Ovni ont-ils survolé le Mont-Blanc?</span></span></span>

The explanation was not merely a generic suggestion that the lights “might have been lanterns”. It came from someone claiming direct responsibility for releasing them. That makes the Domancy case considerably stronger as an identification than a retrospective guess based only on visual resemblance.

## The Chamonix photograph identified as an aircraft

A second report emerged on Monday 27 May, roughly two weeks after the Domancy sighting. At precisely 12.29 pm, according to the timestamp on his mobile-phone photograph, a Chamonix meteorologist noticed a white object crossing the sky above the Mont Blanc massif. He observed it between the Aiguilles de Chamonix and the Dôme du Goûter.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/28/un-etrange-machin-dans-le-ciel-de-chamonix" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

The witness said that the object moved slightly faster than an airliner and made no distinctive sound. He estimated its altitude at between 6,000 and 7,000 metres, below the cirrus clouds he had been monitoring. The entire observation lasted only eight to ten seconds. One photograph showed a small bright point, but the object occupied too few pixels to reveal a reliable shape or structure. No second witness was identified in the press report.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/28/un-etrange-machin-dans-le-ciel-de-chamonix" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

The witness’s profession made the report interesting, but expertise does not remove the limitations created by distance and brevity. Estimating the speed and altitude of an unidentified point requires knowing its size and range. Without that information, a relatively large distant aircraft can appear similar to a smaller, nearer object. Sound is equally unreliable in mountainous surroundings: distance, wind and the delay between light and sound can make an aircraft seem silent during a short observation.

Later on 29 May, *Le Dauphiné Libéré* published an identification offered by Jacques Fabry, who studied aircraft condensation effects. He argued that the bright point was sunlight reflected from an aircraft’s fuselage, with the glare enhanced by moisture or condensation particles associated with the aircraft. The newspaper consequently reported that the “strange object” was an aeroplane.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/29/le-machin-n-etait-qu-un-avion" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréOVNI/HAUTE-SAVOIE. The published follow-up did not provide a flight number, radar plot or independently reproduced optical analysis. The identification should therefore be described as persuasive rather than demonstrated beyond all doubt. Nevertheless, an aircraft is a much better fit than an unknown craft: the object followed a rapid crossing path at an estimated cruising altitude, appeared in daylight, was represented photographically only as a bright point and was seen once for less than ten seconds.

The photograph itself does not constitute strong evidence for anything more unusual. A picture can confirm that the camera recorded a bright object, but it does not automatically confirm the witness’s estimates of its size, speed or distance. When an object is only a few pixels wide, enlargement adds blur rather than hidden detail. The image is therefore consistent with an aircraft glint but too poor to establish a distinctive unidentified vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/28/un-etrange-machin-dans-le-ciel-de-chamonix" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

## Why the alleged UFO wave weakened under scrutiny

The two reports looked connected mainly because they occurred near Mont Blanc during the same month and were covered using similar UFO language. Their actual characteristics were very different. The Domancy event took place at night and involved a group of red lights drifting for about a minute. The Chamonix event occurred in daylight and involved one white object crossing the sky for eight to ten seconds. One was linked directly to a wedding lantern release; the other was interpreted as reflected sunlight from an aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/18/domancy-les-ovnis-etaient-des-lanternes-thailandaises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

Several factors helped create the short-lived appearance of a cluster:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* **Geographical labelling:** Reports from Domancy, Sallanches and Chamonix could all be presented as events “over Mont Blanc”, even though the witnesses were observing different parts of a large mountain region.
* **Media momentum:** The Chamonix article explicitly referred back to the earlier lantern case, encouraging readers to interpret the second observation as part of a sequence.
* **A memorable setting:** An unidentified point above famous peaks sounds more remarkable than the same observation described near an ordinary town or transport corridor.
* **Sparse visual references:** Mountain skylines provide a clear background but rarely establish an airborne object’s distance. A light may seem close to a summit while actually being many kilometres in front of or beyond it.
* **Loss of context in photographs:** Mobile-phone images often record only a bright dot. Without original metadata, a sequence of frames, known landmarks and aviation data, later viewers cannot reliably reconstruct its path.

</div>

The episode also demonstrates why witness credibility and case strength are separate questions. There is no reason to assume that the Domancy family or the Chamonix meteorologist fabricated their observations. Each may have described honestly what appeared puzzling at the time. What weakened the cases was not necessarily the witnesses’ sincerity, but the arrival of better contextual information and the absence of evidence supporting extraordinary performance.

Neither case generated the features expected of a robust regional UFO incident: multiple independent viewing locations, consistent descriptions, radar confirmation, physical traces or an official case file containing sufficiently detailed measurements. In Domancy, the gendarmerie had received only one family’s report and GEIPAN was not alerted. In Chamonix, the press described a single brief observation and a low-detail photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/17/des-ovni-ont-ils-survole-le-mont-blanc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Le Dauphiné LibéréHAUTE-SAVOIE. ## The most defensible assessment

The May 2013 Mont Blanc reports do not support the claim that unidentified craft crossed Haute-Savoie in a coordinated wave. The first incident is convincingly explained by sky lanterns released from a nearby wedding at the relevant time. The second is most plausibly an aircraft seen under lighting conditions that produced an unusually bright reflection, although the published identification lacked the detailed flight records that would make it conclusive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.ledauphine.com/haute-savoie/2013/05/18/domancy-les-ovnis-etaient-des-lanternes-thailandaises" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ledauphine.com">[Le Dauphiné Libéré]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ledauphine.com</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on ledauphine.com.</span></span></span>

Accordingly, the Domancy event belongs in the explained category, while the Chamonix photograph is best regarded as a weakly documented observation with a credible conventional explanation. It would be misleading to preserve either merely as an enduring “Mont Blanc mystery” while omitting the rapid corrections published at the time.

Within Haute-Savoie’s wider UFO history, the lasting lesson is how quickly separate reports can be assembled into a flap. The dramatic location and close dates gave the incidents a shared identity, but the evidential connection disappeared once each was examined individually. What first resembled repeated unknown activity over the massif reduced to two familiar causes: celebratory lights carried by the wind and an aircraft briefly catching the sun.
