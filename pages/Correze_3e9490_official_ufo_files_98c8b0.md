## Introduction

Corrèze’s official UFO files do not prove that an extraordinary craft visited the department. They show something more useful: how reports that initially sound remarkable can end up in very different evidential categories. In the public GEIPAN archive, Corrèze cases include identified aircraft, the International Space Station, a satellite flare and other probable explanations, alongside reports left unresolved because the available information was too weak. The archive page reviewed for this article lists no Corrèze case in the strongest unexplained category, D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=29%2C5&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

That distinction matters because “unidentified” can mean two almost opposite things. A well-documented phenomenon may remain unexplained despite investigation, but a case may also remain unidentified because investigators lack precise directions, independent statements, original photographs or reliable timing. The Corrèze record is dominated by the second problem. From the sparse 1977 report at Laroche-près-Feyt to the videos recorded near Favars in 2019, the recurring lesson is that an intriguing account is not necessarily a strong case.

## How GEIPAN’s A-to-D system changes the meaning of “unexplained”

GEIPAN is the French national unit that collects, analyses and publishes reports of unidentified aerospace phenomena. It operates within the French space agency, CNES, and can receive information from private witnesses, the gendarmerie, police, civil aviation and other public bodies. Its investigation may involve checking aircraft movements, astronomical positions, satellites, weather conditions and the geometry of the observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

Its familiar classifications are best read as assessments of both explanation and evidence quality:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A:** the reported phenomenon has been identified.
* **B:** an ordinary explanation is considered probable, although not demonstrated with complete certainty.
* **C:** the information is insufficiently reliable or complete to reach a sound conclusion.
* **D:** the phenomenon remains unexplained after investigation despite evidence of sufficient quality and quantity.

This means C is not a halfway version of D. A C case is generally an evidential failure: investigators cannot establish what happened because the record is too incomplete. A D case is potentially more interesting because the unresolved status survives a meaningful investigation.

CNES’s current national figures illustrate the difference. It reports that 24.6 per cent of archived phenomena are clearly identified, 39.7 per cent probably identified and 32.4 per cent unresolved for lack of data. Only 3.3 per cent remain unidentified after investigation. In other words, most officially unresolved reports belong to the low-information category, not the small group of comparatively robust mysteries.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

The categories are nevertheless expert judgements rather than mechanical verdicts. A study presented at a GEIPAN workshop examined the consistency of historical GEPAN assessments and found that experts agreed on the same four-part classification in about 65 per cent of multiply assessed reports. Agreement improved when A and B were combined, but the results still showed that judgement calls about information quality, witness reliability and strangeness can affect classification.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/03_ROSPARS_full.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANqualitative expert evaluationJuly 16, 2014 — by JP ROSPARS · 2014 · Cited by 1 — A classification will be considered as reliable if...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: July 16, 2014</span></span></span>

For Corrèze, the practical consequence is clear: the letter attached to a file must be read alongside the investigation notes. A classification tells readers where the case finished, but the reasoning explains why.

Several conclusions follow immediately. First, the archive is not a catalogue containing only mysterious events. Identified and probably identified reports are retained because they show what witnesses originally found puzzling and how that puzzle was resolved. Secondly, Corrèze’s public case list contains more ordinary explanations than enduring mysteries. Thirdly, its C cases should not be counted as evidence equivalent to a D case.

The list is also shaped by reporting behaviour. It records cases submitted or preserved through official channels, not every unusual light ever seen in the department. A rise or gap in entries can reflect public awareness, changes in reporting systems, archive survival and GEIPAN publication practices as much as any change in the sky.

## Laroche-près-Feyt: an official file without enough evidence

The Laroche-près-Feyt report dates from 11 May 1977 and is classified C. GEIPAN’s searchable index describes it simply as a case lacking reliable information. That wording is the central fact: the official archive confirms that a report existed, but it does not convert the report into a substantiated extraordinary event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=8&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

This older file illustrates the limitations of historical official material. A gendarmerie statement can preserve a witness’s account more securely than a later retelling, but official provenance does not automatically supply missing measurements. Investigators still need dependable information about direction, elevation, duration, movement, weather, celestial objects, aircraft activity and the witness’s position. When these details are absent or too uncertain, competing explanations cannot be tested properly.

The surviving classification therefore answers one question but leaves another open. It establishes that GEIPAN could not identify the reported phenomenon from the available record. It does not establish that investigators encountered a well-documented object displaying capabilities inconsistent with aircraft, astronomy or atmospheric effects.

This is why Laroche-près-Feyt should be described as unresolved through insufficiency, not as a confirmed anomaly. Its value within Corrèze’s UFO history lies largely in showing how far an official file can fall short of evidential completeness.

## Favars: many observers and video, but still a weak case

The Favars report of 29 November 2019 is the most revealing Corrèze example because it appears, at first glance, to contain several features that ought to strengthen a case. A witness noticed an unusually bright, scintillating light shortly before 10 pm. Nine other people reportedly came outside to see it. The observation continued for roughly 80 minutes, and video recordings were eventually supplied to investigators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The descriptions included apparent appearances and disappearances, separation into parts and movement away from the main light. Those details give the account its strangeness. Yet GEIPAN classified it C rather than D, assigning moderate consistency to the evidence and stating that the case could not be identified because reliable data were lacking.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The investigation shows why the apparent strengths were less decisive than they sound.

**Ten people did not produce ten detailed statements.** GEIPAN received one main testimony. Another person later provided a brief email and videos, but the investigating team was unable to reconstruct independent accounts from the full group. Multiple alleged observers can improve a case only when their recollections are separately recorded and can be compared.

**The videos did not establish the reported motion.** They showed a bright light with an appearance compatible with an out-of-focus star recorded in darkness. The movement or splitting described by the main witness was not confirmed by the recovered secondary witness or by the footage.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**The observation’s long duration favoured an astronomical possibility.** A light remaining in broadly the same area for about 80 minutes is more compatible with a star or planet than with a brief meteor, fast aircraft passage or rapidly travelling object. Scintillation is also especially noticeable when starlight passes through a long, turbulent path near the horizon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

**The investigators could not match the account confidently to a particular star.** Sirius lay near the stated bearing but had not yet risen at the beginning of the reported observation. Procyon was also considered, but its position did not match the direction and elevation supplied by the witness. The estimated angular height was itself imprecise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Favars therefore occupies an important middle ground. The astronomical hypothesis is plausible, but the available information is too contradictory or imprecise to demonstrate it. Equally, the failure to identify a particular star does not make an extraordinary craft the leading explanation. GEIPAN’s C classification records this stalemate accurately: there is enough material to suggest a conventional cause, but not enough dependable geometry to close the case.

## Why witnesses, duration and video may still leave doubt

The Corrèze files expose three common misunderstandings about UFO evidence.

### A group of witnesses is not automatically independent evidence

A crowd can strengthen a report, but only if investigators know what each person observed before discussion influenced their memories. In Favars, the group reportedly watched together, yet only one full testimony entered the evidential record. The claim “ten witnesses saw it” is therefore stronger socially than it is analytically.

People observing together also share the same viewing conditions and can reinforce one another’s interpretation. One person saying that a light has split or moved may guide what others subsequently notice. This does not imply dishonesty; it is a normal feature of group perception and memory.

### A long observation can make misidentification more likely

Duration sounds like a mark of reliability because the witness has more time to look. In practice, it can point towards a stationary or slowly changing astronomical object. Stars near the horizon can flicker, change colour and seem to move because of atmospheric turbulence, involuntary eye movement and the absence of fixed visual references.

A long duration is therefore useful evidence, but not necessarily evidence for an unusual vehicle. In Favars, GEIPAN treated the approximately 80-minute observation as one reason to favour an astronomical explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### A video is only as good as what it records

Phone footage can establish that a light was present, but a small bright point against a black sky usually contains little information about distance, size or structure. Autofocus, digital zoom, camera movement and image stabilisation can create pulsing shapes or apparent motion. Without landscape references, accurate time stamps and a known viewing direction, the recording may add less than viewers expect.

The Favars videos did not corroborate the most unusual reported behaviour. Their value was therefore limited: they supported the presence of a luminous point but did not demonstrate an object dividing into parts or performing exceptional manoeuvres.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2019-11-50866" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFAVARS (19) 29.11.2019 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Ordinary explanations are part of the official record

The identified Corrèze reports are not peripheral to the story. They reveal the mechanisms most likely to produce convincing local UFO reports.

At Ussac in June 2010, GEIPAN classified the sighting B and attributed it to the International Space Station. The ISS can appear as a bright, steady light crossing the sky without the flashing navigation lights associated with an aircraft. To a witness unfamiliar with satellite passes, its brightness and smooth movement can seem highly unusual.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2010-06-02588" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANUSSAC (19) 25.06.2010 &#124; GEIPAN## USSAC (19) 25.06.2010...</span></span></span>

At Meyrignac-l’Église in March 2011, the official conclusion was a probable Iridium flare: sunlight reflecting from one of the highly reflective antennas fitted to the original Iridium communications satellites. Such flashes could brighten rapidly and then fade, creating the impression of an object switching on, approaching or accelerating away.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation_textuel&amp;page=40%2C0&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">MEYRIGNAC-L&#x27;EGLISE (19) 23.03.2011 &#124; 23/03/2011 &#124; B &#124; Corrèze &#124; Flash iridium &#124; 12/03/2012 &#124; 2011-03-23... These explanations demonstrate why checking time and direction matters. Satellites can be tested against orbital predictions; aircraft against routes, lighting patterns and movement; astronomical objects against sky position; and distant light projections against cloud conditions and the location of ground-based sources. A witness’s sincere impression remains important, but the explanation depends on reconstructing the physical scene.

## What Corrèze’s C cases do and do not prove

Corrèze has at least three C-class entries in the reviewed list: Laroche-près-Feyt, Saint-Privas and Favars. It would be misleading to combine them into a count of “three unexplained UFOs” without qualification. Their shared classification means GEIPAN considered the reliable information inadequate, not that each case resisted a strong investigation.

C cases may remain open for very different reasons:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* the account was reported long after the event;
* only one detailed witness statement exists;
* direction or elevation is approximate;
* the date or time is uncertain;
* photographs lack useful reference points;
* alleged additional witnesses cannot be interviewed;
* the description conflicts with the available images;
* possible aircraft, stars or satellites cannot be checked retrospectively.

</div>

These deficiencies affect what can reasonably be inferred. An investigator may be unable to prove a satellite explanation because the time is uncertain by half an hour, while also being unable to exclude it. The correct conclusion is then that the case cannot be resolved—not that all conventional explanations have failed.

The absence of a listed Corrèze D case is therefore more informative than the raw number of unidentified reports. Within the public results examined here, no local report is presented as both sufficiently well documented and genuinely unexplained after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=29%2C5&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANRecherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

That finding may change if GEIPAN revises classifications, publishes additional archived material or receives a new report. It is nevertheless the position shown by the current public case list: Corrèze has unresolved files, but they are unresolved chiefly because the evidence does not support a firm conclusion.

## What the archive proves about Corrèze

The official files prove that people in Corrèze have repeatedly reported lights or objects they could not identify at the time. They also prove that some reports entered a structured national process rather than surviving only through press stories or UFO literature. That process produced specific ordinary explanations in several cases and documented the limits of the evidence in others.

They do not prove that the department has experienced visits by unknown technology. Nor do they show an official cover-up: GEIPAN publishes identified, probably identified and insufficient-data cases alongside the much smaller national class of robust unresolved reports. Its archive is designed to expose the reasoning and surviving documents, not simply to collect claims.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

The most defensible reading of the Corrèze dataset is therefore cautious but not dismissive. Witnesses sometimes reported genuinely puzzling experiences. In several cases, later checks made ordinary explanations more likely. In others, too little dependable information survived to decide. Favars is the clearest example: numerous observers and video made the report appear strong, yet the absence of independent testimony, precise angular measurements and corroboration of the alleged motion kept it in class C.

Corrèze’s official UFO record is ultimately a lesson in evidential thresholds. “Unidentified” describes the result of an investigation, not the nature of the object. Until a case combines unusual behaviour with accurate timing, independent observations, useful imagery and checks against ordinary sources, an unresolved file remains a record of uncertainty rather than proof of something extraordinary.

    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
