## Introduction

Most officially recorded UFO reports from [Cantal]({{ 'cantals-ufo-story-beyond-the-mystery/' | relative_url }}) do not lead to an enduring mystery. Outside the disputed 1967 [Cussac]({{ 'cussac/' | relative_url }}) encounter, the department’s small GEIPAN record is largely made up of sightings that were either traced to ordinary objects or could not be assessed because the original information was too poor. Recent examples have involved a bright star, a weather balloon and powerful event lighting projected on to cloud. Older files often contain little more than a brief statement or gendarmerie record.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Cantal_202658_cantal_explained_sig_973248-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Other Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

This matters because a witness can give an honest and detailed account while still misidentifying what was seen. Distance removes scale, darkness removes landmarks, atmospheric turbulence makes stars appear mobile and multicoloured, and lights on cloud can look like self-contained objects. Cantal’s record therefore illustrates a less dramatic but more useful side of UFO investigation: the mystery often fades when position, weather, timing and local activity are reconstructed.

## What the official records contain

GEIPAN, the unidentified aerospace phenomena service of France’s national space agency CNES, does not treat every unexplained report as evidence of an extraordinary craft. It compares the account with astronomical, meteorological, aviation and human-made explanations, then assesses both the remaining strangeness and the “consistency” of the evidence — meaning the quantity and reliability of the information available.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLa méthodologie de classification au GEIPAN26 Feb 2021 — La classification du GEIPAN (A/B/C/D) s&#x27;appuie sur l&#x27;évaluation de deux no...</span></span></span>

## Stars, balloons and projected lights

### Arcturus: a moving, colour-changing “object”

The Saint-Urcize case of 20 September 2024 shows how an ordinary star can produce a surprisingly elaborate report. At about 10.23 pm, a traveller staying in a hired camper van noticed a bright point towards the north-west. It appeared to shimmer, change between yellow-white and red and move abruptly. He filmed it twice and took several photographs, giving GEIPAN more material than is available in many UFO cases.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51582" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The additional evidence did not confirm an object manoeuvring over the landscape. A reconstruction based on the witness’s position, a daylight reference photograph and astronomy software placed the bright star Arcturus close to the reported direction. Arcturus was low in the sky, at an elevation of roughly nine degrees, where its light would have travelled through a long and turbulent path in the atmosphere. GEIPAN concluded that this turbulence accounted for the strong scintillation and apparent colour changes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51582" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The apparent movement had two likely sources. First, a highly zoomed handheld telephone magnifies tiny movements of the camera. With no hill, tree or building visible in the recording, the viewer cannot easily separate camera shake from movement of the light. Second, a stationary point seen against a dark, featureless background can seem to wander through small involuntary eye movements, an effect often called autokinesis. GEIPAN classified the case A: an observation of Arcturus.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2024-09-51582" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The case is valuable precisely because the witnesses supplied photographs and video. Recording the light strengthened the investigation but weakened the exotic interpretation. It showed that images alone are not decisive; investigators still need orientation, camera behaviour and fixed reference points.

### Mauriac: a weather balloon mistaken for something stranger

A Mauriac observation dated 4 January 1990 is listed by GEIPAN as a category A weather-balloon case. The surviving online summary is sparse, so it does not support a detailed reconstruction of what the witness originally described. It nevertheless provides a second recurring Cantal explanation: a high-altitude balloon appearing bright, slow-moving or nearly stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-01-01193" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Weather balloons are particularly effective UFO lookalikes because their apparent behaviour depends on distance and illumination. A balloon can reflect sunlight strongly even when the observer’s surroundings are becoming dark. At great range, its envelope may appear only as a brilliant point, while its instrument package and suspension line remain invisible. Its movement can also be difficult to judge without a fixed foreground reference. GEIPAN notes that balloons may seem star-like, oscillate, drift slowly or appear temporarily motionless.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Cantal’s upland landscape can make those uncertainties more noticeable. A light observed beyond a ridge offers few clues to distance, height or actual size. Something small and nearby, large and distant, or extremely high can produce a similar angular appearance. The Mauriac classification does not imply carelessness by the witness; it shows how little visual information the unaided eye receives from a remote illuminated object.

### Pierrefort: lights on cloud, not objects among the clouds

The most instructive modern case occurred near Pierrefort on 21 August 2022. Four people watching from the terrace of a country house saw several white oval forms moving in regular pairs across a cloudy night sky. The lights followed repeated, slightly rising paths and remained visible for about five minutes. One witness supplied testimony and a video.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Their appearance suggested separate luminous objects. The investigation instead linked them to powerful moving event lights, sometimes called sky tracers, directed on to the underside of cloud. A celebration was taking place at Paulhac, approximately 11 kilometres from the observation point, and the entertainment company confirmed that it had equipment capable of projecting strong moving beams into the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Cloud was central to the illusion. The projected beam became conspicuous where it struck the cloud layer, creating a bright oval patch, while the section of beam travelling through relatively clear air remained difficult or impossible to see. To a distant observer, the bright patch could therefore appear detached from any source on the ground. Repeated movements generated by programmed equipment then resembled coordinated objects following the same route.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

There was a minor timing uncertainty: the company indicated that the lights were formally used later than the reported sighting. GEIPAN judged that an earlier equipment test was plausible, given the close geographical match, confirmed presence of the projectors, suitable cloud and highly characteristic repeating motion. The case was classified A rather than left open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This is stronger than simply suggesting “perhaps a searchlight”. The explanation links the shapes, movement, weather, distance and a verified local event. It also explains an apparently puzzling absence: the witnesses did not see beams connecting the lights to the ground because those beams were viewed from far away through clean air and became visible mainly at the cloud base.

## Why some reports cannot be solved

Not every Cantal report has enough information for such a reconstruction. Several older files remain category C, but their status reflects evidential weakness rather than a finding that ordinary explanations failed.

A report attributed to Pierrefort in spring 1960 was not submitted until May 2008, almost half a century after the event. Two former schoolchildren recalled seeing a luminous phenomenon one morning at about 7.30 am and watching it disappear towards the horizon after several minutes. The date was uncertain even to the season, and no further detail was supplied. GEIPAN considered meaningful investigation effectively impossible.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1960-04-02014" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPIERREFORT (15) --.--.1960Geipan. The time of day and slow disappearance leave numerous possibilities — a bright astronomical object, an aircraft, a balloon or sunlight reflecting from a distant object — but none can be tested reliably without a secure date, direction, angular position or contemporary record. Assigning a confident explanation now would be as unjustified as declaring the case extraordinary.

A second Pierrefort-area file, from 3 December 1978, concerns three people who reported a strong early-morning light illuminating pasture, together with flashing red and blue lights. Gendarmes found no object or trace at the scene. Yet the published record lacks the positional, meteorological and timing details required to compare the report properly with aircraft, vehicles, distant lighting or other sources. GEIPAN therefore classified it C for insufficient reliable information.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1978-12-00573" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPIERREFORT (15) 03.12.1978PIERREFORT (15) 03.12.1978; Date d&#x27;observation. 03/12/1978; Région. GEIPAN noted that some features resembled a bright meteor, but the single submitted account was too brief to confirm that hypothesis. Requests for further information produced no usable addition, so the case remained C.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-08377" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMONTSALVY (15) 27.08.201227 Aug 2012 — Date d&#x27;observation. 27/08/2012; Région. These files illustrate why “not identified” must be read carefully. An investigation can end without an answer for two very different reasons:

1. **The evidence is detailed, reliable and still resists ordinary explanations.**
2. **The evidence is too incomplete to test any explanation properly.**

Cantal’s non-Cussac category C cases fall principally into the second group. They should not be converted retrospectively into evidence of unexplained craft merely because the original object was never named.

## What makes ordinary objects look extraordinary

Across the resolved Cantal cases, the same perceptual problems recur.

**Missing distance cues distort size and speed.** A point of light contains no built-in measure of how far away it is. A witness who assumes it is nearby will infer a smaller object moving quickly; one who assumes it is distant may imagine something enormous. Neither estimate is secure without triangulation or a known landscape reference.

**Low stars behave unlike the familiar stars overhead.** Near the horizon, starlight passes through more atmosphere. Turbulence bends and breaks up the light, producing rapid changes in brightness and colour. A bright star such as Arcturus, Sirius or Capella may therefore look like a flashing artificial source. GEIPAN specifically warns that low bright stars are regularly reported because their scintillation appears unusually active.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Handheld zoom creates false motion.** Digital enlargement does not add stable detail. It enlarges hand tremor, focus changes, compression artefacts and optical distortions. When the frame contains only black sky, the resulting video can make a stationary star appear to dart, pulse or alter shape.

**Cloud can act as a projection screen.** A moving spotlight may produce a crisp luminous patch on cloud while leaving no obvious visible connection to its source. Repeated programmed sweeps create regular formations and coordinated motion, as in the 2022 Pierrefort case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

**Balloons combine brightness with deceptive movement.** Reflective balloons may be visible by day or around twilight and can seem stationary, oscillate or change speed as winds vary at different heights. Small rotations can also produce flashes that suggest powered or controlled behaviour.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/qu-ai-je-vu/etape-1" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These mechanisms do not mean that every witness report is trivial. They mean that the witness’s description records an experience, not necessarily the physical nature of its cause. Investigation begins by taking the experience seriously and then separating appearance from object.

## Unexplained versus insufficiently documented

Cantal’s wider official record is best understood as a contrast between a few well-supported identifications and several weak historical files. The Saint-Urcize and Pierrefort investigations had useful photographs, video, geographic reconstruction, weather information or confirmation of local activity. Those additions allowed investigators to reduce uncertainty and reach ordinary explanations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2022-08-51419" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The category C cases lack that foundation. They may sound unusual in a short summary, but the absence of key data prevents investigators from calculating direction, checking celestial positions, comparing wind drift, identifying flights or testing whether a light source could have been operating nearby. Their unresolved wording is therefore an administrative statement about evidence, not a scientific endorsement of an anomalous object.

This distinction also explains why later reporting has weakened rather than strengthened most of Cantal’s minor UFO claims. Modern phones provide more recordings, while mapping software, astronomical programs, weather archives and direct checks with event organisers make ordinary causes easier to test. In the strongest recent Cantal files, those tools have identified the supposed object rather than confirming it.

Beyond Cussac, the department’s published record consequently offers no clear pattern of repeated, well-documented unexplained craft. What it offers instead is a compact demonstration of how stars, balloons and projected lights acquire extraordinary features when observed briefly, at night or without reliable points of reference — and how a case that remains unnamed is not necessarily a case that resisted explanation.
