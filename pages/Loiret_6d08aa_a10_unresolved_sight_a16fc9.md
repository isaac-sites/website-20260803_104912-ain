## Introduction

The A10 sighting of 23 January 1982 remains the strongest officially documented unexplained aerial case associated with [Loiret]({{ 'which-loiret-ufo-cases-still-resist/' | relative_url }}). Two motorway gendarmes travelling south towards Orléans watched an indistinct pale form in the sky and successive areas of countryside apparently illuminated beside the road. Their observation lasted about 20 minutes and unfolded over nearly 30 kilometres, continuing from the Eure-et-Loir side of the departmental boundary into Loiret.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Loiret_6d08aa_a10_unresolved_sight_a16fc9-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for A10 Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The case matters not because the witnesses proved that they saw an extraordinary craft, but because an unusually detailed contemporary file survived. Investigators reconstructed the route, photographed locations, checked weather and astronomical conditions, contacted the nearby Orléans-Bricy air base and examined the possibility of a helicopter carrying a searchlight. That explanation could reproduce parts of the account, but it required equipment, altitude and operating circumstances that did not fit comfortably together. GEIPAN therefore classified the report as unexplained while explicitly acknowledging that the missing measurements and the passage of time prevent a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## What the gendarmes reported along the motorway

The encounter began at about 4.35 am after the patrol had turned around near the limit of its motorway sector and was heading south on the A10. The driver first noticed a poorly defined pale mass ahead, high in the windscreen and approximately aligned with the motorway. Unsure of what he was seeing, he asked his passenger, who confirmed that he could also see an unusual form. The passenger described it as roughly cone-like, with its narrower end pointing upwards, although neither man could estimate its altitude or give it a sharp outline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Near the Allaines toll area, the driver then saw a bright patch illuminating trees close to the road. His first thought was practical rather than exotic: a vehicle might have overturned in a ditch. When the patrol reached the location, there was no accident and the illumination had gone. Farther south, another area of ground appeared lit several hundred metres east of the motorway. The driver described this illumination as pale green, while the passenger remembered a grey-white or light-grey glow.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The most unusual detail concerned electricity pylons and farm buildings. The driver said that part of a high-voltage pylon became sharply visible, including its metal crosspieces and cables, while adjacent parts remained dark. He believed the illuminated section was oblique, suggesting light arriving from above. Later, hangars, trees and further stretches of ground appeared in the lit area. Yet neither witness saw a conventional beam linking the pale aerial form to the landscape.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Their descriptions were broadly compatible but not identical. The driver thought the pale mass and ground illumination moved with the patrol. When he stopped near kilometre point 69, he said the phenomenon also appeared to stop; after the car restarted, it seemed to move again. The passenger offered a more cautious interpretation. He suspected that the luminous area might have remained stationary while the changing viewpoint from the moving car caused different fields, pylons and buildings to pass through it. This disagreement is important because it leaves open whether the apparent pursuit was a real movement or a perspective effect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

At one stage the men pulled over, stopped the engine and got out. The driver directed a powerful torch towards the pale mass but saw nothing further. Neither man heard an unusual noise. The observation was intermittent rather than continuous, however: they were travelling in a vehicle, their attention was divided, and the aerial form was lost from view during parts of the route. The later reconstruction divided the incident into ten phases extending from around kilometre point 61 to the Saran-Ormes area, a journey of roughly 29 to 32 kilometres depending on which end point is used.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## Why the record is stronger than an ordinary sighting

Many historical UFO reports depend on a single retrospective story. The A10 case is different because the witnesses gave formal statements within days, while the landscape and their memories were still comparatively fresh. Gendarmes returned to the route, matched observations to kilometre points and produced photographs, maps and a sketch of the illuminated pylon. The file also incorporated weather information, astronomical data, motorway records, electricity-line details and a contemporary local newspaper article.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The witnesses also had some practical advantages. They were accustomed to motorway patrol work, road accidents and night-time visual conditions. The driver did not begin with an extraterrestrial interpretation: he initially treated the first ground illumination as a possible traffic emergency. The passenger stated that he had little particular interest in UFOs beyond ordinary media coverage. These points do not guarantee accurate perception, but they reduce the likelihood that the account was consciously constructed to match a preferred UFO narrative.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Their evidence nevertheless has clear limits:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--metric" markdown="1">

* There were no photographs, films, instrument readings or independent radar data.
* Neither witness measured angles, altitude, apparent size or the width of the illuminated areas.
* The two men disagreed over the colour of the light and whether the phenomenon itself moved.
* Almost all of the account was formed while they were travelling at night in a moving car.
* No toll worker, motorway employee or resident along the route supplied a confirmed matching observation.

</div>

These omissions matter because a detailed verbal account can still preserve a mistaken interpretation. A reconstruction can show where witnesses believed events occurred, but without angular measurements it cannot securely determine the position, height or true movement of the source. GEIPAN judged the file’s consistency to be good, especially because it involved two witnesses and a prompt official inquiry, while also criticising the lack of angular data and original imagery.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

A woman from Ingré had separately written to the local press about an unusual observation made six days earlier. The original investigators included her letter in the dossier, but she was not formally interviewed and her account remained imprecise. The later review therefore declined to connect it to the motorway event. The surviving evidence supports one well-recorded encounter, not a demonstrated regional wave.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## How astronomy was tested and rejected

An astronomical source was worth considering because distant bright objects can appear to accompany a moving vehicle. This familiar perspective effect, sometimes called the “following light” illusion, occurs because a planet or star is so far away that its direction changes very little as the observer travels. The passenger’s impression that the aerial form and illuminated zone may have been stationary made this possibility relevant.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The sky check did not produce a convincing match. The Moon was not above the horizon, although Jupiter, Saturn and Mars were present. GEIPAN considered it unlikely that a planet, normally point-like to the unaided eye, would be perceived as an extended pale mass through substantial cloud. More decisively, an astronomical object could not account for the reported illumination of nearby trees, hangars and selected sections of pylons. The review assigned the planetary explanation a very low indicative probability of 10 per cent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

One cautious alternative is that the witnesses combined two unrelated perceptions: a planet or diffuse light in the sky and an independent source illuminating the ground. That cannot be ruled out from the surviving material. It is nevertheless less economical than a single airborne light source, and no separate ground-based activity was identified along the route.

## Why the helicopter theory came closest

A helicopter carrying a steerable searchlight is the most natural conventional explanation because it could generate both main parts of the report. Seen through or behind cloud, the aircraft might appear only as a diffuse pale shape, with its navigation lights obscured. Its searchlight could illuminate fields, buildings and pylons while the helicopter travelled approximately parallel to the motorway. A beam viewed from the side is not necessarily visible unless particles or moisture scatter enough light towards the observer. Rotor noise can also be weakened by distance, wind direction, the moving patrol car and intervening atmospheric layers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The weather offered partial support for such a scenario. Records indicated broken or extensive stratocumulus at approximately 1,200 and 1,500 metres, rather than a completely clear sky. An aircraft behind the lower layer could therefore appear as an indistinct luminous patch, while its searchlight reached the ground through gaps. GEIPAN stressed that silence and the absence of a visible beam were not, by themselves, decisive objections.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The theory also explains why the phenomenon appeared to travel with the patrol. A helicopter following the same north-south corridor could remain within a fairly constant viewing direction for many kilometres. Alternatively, a more distant helicopter circling or conducting a search might produce successive illuminated zones that seemed linked to the pale mass.

For these reasons, the helicopter hypothesis was not dismissed casually. GEIPAN considered it substantially more promising than astronomy and gave it an indicative reliability of 30 per cent. That figure did not mean there was a 30 per cent statistical probability in the scientific sense; it was an investigator’s structured estimate of how well the proposal fitted the available features.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

## Where the helicopter explanation breaks down

The theory becomes strained when the reported illumination is compared with the cloud height and the capabilities of searchlights. If the helicopter was in or above cloud at around 1,200 to 1,500 metres, its lamp had to illuminate recognisable structures on the ground across a long slant distance. GEIPAN used the later Spectrolab SX-16 Nightsun as a benchmark: a very powerful airborne searchlight producing roughly 30 to 40 million candlepower, with a useful target-identification range of about 1.6 kilometres. Manufacturer specifications broadly support those performance figures.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The difficulty is historical as well as optical. The review found no evidence that French civil or military helicopters in 1982 carried a system capable of producing the required effect from the inferred altitude. It noted that comparable equipment was fitted to French public-service and military helicopters much later. The small, sharply defined section of pylon described by the driver was also awkward: from cloud level, a normal searchlight should have produced a wider footprint unless it was exceptionally tightly focused. GEIPAN compared the necessary concentration to a laser-like beam not known to have been available as airborne equipment in that setting.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The colour and quality of the illumination create another problem. The driver compared it to “black light”, apparently not as a technical description of ultraviolet radiation but as an attempt to convey an eerie, subdued illumination in which details were visible without the surroundings appearing brightly floodlit. A searchlight strong enough to reach the ground from above the clouds should normally have produced a brilliant white-lit scene. The passenger’s grey-white recollection is less difficult for the helicopter theory, but the witnesses’ conflicting colour descriptions leave the optical character uncertain.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Operational evidence was inconclusive rather than wholly negative. The nearby Orléans-Bricy air base reported that no aircraft or helicopter was flying from the base and that its radar station had been shut down since the previous evening. That excludes neither an aircraft from another base nor an unrecorded civilian flight. However, no corresponding helicopter operation was established, and the original file does not show an exhaustive nationwide search of possible operators.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The supposed mission is difficult to reconstruct as well. A helicopter performing a ground search would normally need to operate below cloud or deliberately direct its lamp through gaps. A helicopter merely returning from a mission with its lamp left on would not easily explain why the beam appeared to dwell on a pylon long enough for the driver to see structural details. The hypothesis can be saved only by adding several assumptions: an unidentified operator, unusually capable equipment, favourable openings in the cloud and deliberate or coincidental targeting of successive objects beside the road.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

None of these objections proves that no helicopter was present. Cloud-base estimates can be imperfect, witness distances can be badly wrong at night, and the apparently narrow pylon illumination may have been misremembered or misinterpreted. The problem is cumulative: the explanation works in general outline but does not fit the recorded particulars without significant adjustment.

## Evidence that weakens the case

The absence of corroboration is the most serious weakness. The phenomenon was reportedly visible across a large area for about 20 minutes, yet motorway staff working overnight noticed nothing, and enquiries among people living beside the route found no additional witnesses. A helicopter searchlight powerful enough to illuminate buildings and high-voltage structures might reasonably be expected to attract attention.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

That absence should not be overstated. The event occurred before dawn in a sparsely populated agricultural landscape. Most residents would have been indoors or asleep, and toll workers were not necessarily watching the eastern sky. The motorway itself was almost empty; the driver recalled only two vehicles passing while the patrol was stopped. Lack of witnesses therefore lowers confidence but does not contradict the report outright.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The geometry is also less secure than the vivid narrative suggests. Neither man could estimate the height of the aerial form. Distances to the ground illumination were rough guesses made in darkness. The driver believed the phenomenon stopped when the patrol stopped, whereas the passenger thought the scene might have been stationary all along. This difference leaves room for a “following” illusion, changing sightlines through gaps in trees or cloud, and the merging of more than one light source into a single event.

There is also no physical residue. Although the witnesses described environmental illumination, they reported no damage, heat, electrical interference or lasting alteration to the pylons, buildings or fields. In evidential terms, the case consists entirely of testimony supported by a prompt route reconstruction—not physical evidence of an object.

## What the later review added

The surviving gendarmerie papers preserved the event, but the decisive classification came from a much later GEIPAN re-examination. The reviewers used modern mapping to divide the journey into phases, checked the witnesses’ directions against the road and reassessed the weather, sky and possible aircraft explanations. They also identified a geographical error in later reporting: the final light was sometimes described as being over the woods of Ormes, although Ormes lay west of the witnesses while the phenomenon was consistently reported to their left, towards the east.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The later analysis strengthened the case in one sense. It demonstrated that the report was not merely an untested mystery placed automatically in an unexplained category. Astronomy was compared with the geometry; cloud levels were considered; helicopter lighting performance was examined; and the strongest conventional hypothesis was retained until its weaknesses became too substantial.

At the same time, the re-examination exposed why the file cannot support a stronger claim. Under current GEIPAN practice, a case of this kind would normally prompt an on-site reconstruction with the witnesses and a structured cognitive interview designed to recover detail while reducing suggestive questioning. Decades after the event, that procedure was no longer possible. GEIPAN stated that the case lay close to the boundary between genuinely unexplained and insufficiently exploitable because the original investigation lacked the measurements required for a modern assessment.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

Later reporting therefore neither solved the case nor transformed it into evidence of an exotic vehicle. It strengthened the conclusion that the obvious explanations do not cleanly fit, while weakening any attempt to draw precise claims about the phenomenon’s shape, height, technology or origin.

## Why category D does not mean extraterrestrial

GEIPAN’s category D meant that the phenomenon remained unexplained after investigation. It did not mean that an unknown craft was established, still less that an extraterrestrial origin was considered probable. In the A10 report, the classification reflects the combination of relatively good testimony, high reported strangeness and the failure of the tested hypotheses—not positive evidence for a particular extraordinary explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The distinction is especially important because this historical case was retained under the older A–D system rather than the later D1/D2 categories. GEIPAN explained that modern unexplained classifications normally require stronger validation, often including a field investigation. Because no contemporary GEIPAN team had interviewed the men or conducted its own on-site reconstruction, the reviewers deliberately avoided giving the case the stronger status implied by the newer framework.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete22.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Microsoft Word</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Microsoft Word - CR AC JPA MPD.docx...</span></span></span>

The fairest reading is therefore narrow. Two gendarmes probably did encounter an unusual and memorable pattern of aerial and ground illumination. Their core account was recorded promptly and investigated more thoroughly than most local sightings. A helicopter with a searchlight remains the best conventional model, but no known flight was identified and the required lighting performance does not comfortably match the reconstructed cloud height or the narrow illumination described. Astronomy explains the apparent following motion but not the landscape lighting.

What remains unresolved is the source of the light effects. The evidence does not establish a structured craft, remarkable manoeuvres, advanced technology or non-human involvement. The A10 sighting earns its place in Loiret’s UFO history because it sits in the difficult middle ground between a weak anecdote and a solved misidentification: a credible official report whose best explanation remains plausible in principle, yet incomplete in the details that matter most.
