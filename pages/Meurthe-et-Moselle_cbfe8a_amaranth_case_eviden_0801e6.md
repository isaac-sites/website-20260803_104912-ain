## Introduction

The Amaranth case is the best-known UFO incident in [Meurthe-et-Moselle]({{ 'meurthe-et-moselle/' | relative_url }}), but it is not a case in which physical evidence proved that an extraordinary craft hovered over a garden. On 21 October 1982, a biologist living near Nancy reported watching a silent, oval object descend into his small enclosed garden, remain close to the ground for about 20 minutes and then rise rapidly out of sight. Wilted amaranth plants were found nearby, prompting a detailed investigation by GEPAN, the French space agency unit then responsible for unidentified aerospace phenomena.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Meurthe-et-Moselle_cbfe8a_amaranth_case_eviden_0801e6-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Amaranth Case" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The inquiry was unusually thorough for a single-witness report. Gendarmes collected plants, investigators reconstructed the viewing geometry, military radar records were checked and laboratory scientists examined the vegetation. Yet the central difficulty never disappeared: the object was seen by only one person, was not photographed and left no independently verified trace. The plants were genuinely dried out, but their condition could not be securely linked to the reported object. The case therefore remains unresolved in the limited sense that no explanation has been established—not verified evidence of an exotic vehicle.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## The witness account and garden reconstruction

At about 12.35 pm, the witness said he was outside his home when he noticed something shining as it approached from the south-east. He initially thought it was an aeroplane. According to his statement to the gendarmerie later that day, the object descended towards him and stopped roughly one metre above the lawn. He described an oval form about 1.5 metres across and 80 centimetres thick, with a polished metallic lower half and a blue-green upper section. It produced no sound, heat, cold, smoke or other sensation that he could detect.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The most striking claim was proximity. The witness said he first watched from around 1.5 metres away and then approached to approximately half a metre. During the observation he went upstairs to fetch a loaded camera, returned and attempted to take a picture, but the camera failed—a defect he said it had displayed before. After about 20 minutes, the object supposedly rose vertically and rapidly until it vanished. He reported no indentation, scorching or flattened grass, although he said the grass blades briefly stood upright as the object departed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The report reached the authorities quickly. The witness and his wife went to the local gendarmerie at about 6 pm, less than six hours after the event. A police telex was sent the following morning, and GEPAN was informed on 23 October. Investigators travelled from Toulouse and examined the property on 29 October, taking measurements, photographs and further plant samples. This relatively prompt response reduced some of the problems that affect cases investigated months or years later.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The site itself mattered. The garden was only about 10.5 square metres, surrounded by walls, fencing, buildings and vegetation. Investigators reconstructed the witness’s movements and the alleged position and path of the object within this confined space. They found that his successive accounts were broadly consistent and that the reported geometry could be fitted into the garden. GEPAN’s psychological and narrative assessment found no clear contradiction or obvious sign that he was inventing the story.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That finding is often misunderstood. Consistency supports the possibility that the witness was sincere, but sincerity is not the same as accuracy. A lone observer can honestly misjudge an object’s distance, dimensions, movement or method of support. Because there was no second witness, investigators could not compare independent descriptions or triangulate the object’s position. Even a detailed reconstruction therefore remained dependent on the witness’s own estimates.

The witness’s occupation also influenced how the case was received. The attending gendarme regarded his scientific employment, social position, request for discretion and restrained presentation as reasons to take him seriously. Those were reasonable grounds for conducting an inquiry, but later critics argued that GEPAN placed too much confidence in the idea of a “qualified” witness. Scientific training in biology does not make a person immune to visual misinterpretation, especially when viewing an unexpected object without a reliable distance reference.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## What the wilted plants did and did not prove

The plants turned an unusual sighting into a potentially testable case. When the witness returned to the garden later on 21 October, he noticed that the upper parts of several amaranths near the reported hovering position appeared dried and folded. Gendarmes visited the next morning and observed a visible contrast between damaged and apparently normal plants. The affected leaves were curled and dehydrated, while flower clusters had changed colour and bent over their stems.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Samples were collected in several stages. The gendarmerie took material on 22 and 27 October; GEPAN gathered another series on 29 October. Some specimens were sealed quickly, while others remained in open plastic bags before being repackaged days later. The specialist laboratory ultimately concentrated on two samples gathered on 22 October: one wilted plant and one comparison plant regarded as normal. The researchers did not control the original sampling and warned that the delay and storage conditions limited interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The simplest measurable difference was water content. The comparison sample contained about 80 per cent water, while the wilted specimen contained about 40 per cent. That confirmed an obvious fact: the damaged plant really had lost substantial moisture. The laboratory also detected variations in sugars and amino acids, particularly in the flowers. Some differences were real, but several could be related to plant maturity, normal nitrogen transport, bacterial activity during storage or other forms of stress.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The researchers did not find a distinctive biochemical signature that identified a source of injury. They noted that slightly elevated compounds in the wilted material could reflect damage from parasites, physical injury or excessive fertiliser. Differences in the flowers might also have arisen because their seeds were more mature than those of the comparison plant. Most importantly, refrigeration followed by later freezing did not stop continuing enzymatic changes, so the samples no longer preserved a reliable snapshot of the plants at the time of the reported event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Weather offered a plausible ordinary cause. The laboratory discussion noted that temperatures had fallen below freezing before the sighting and that parts of the flower bed, positioned beside a fence, might have been affected unevenly by cold and damp conditions. The scientists explicitly stated that the drying could therefore have been climatic. Their final judgement was that plant biochemistry, given the condition of the samples, could not explain why some plants looked different from others.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

This distinction is crucial. The investigation established that some plants were wilted. It did not establish:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--comparison" markdown="1">

* exactly when the damage began;
* that the affected and comparison plants had been in identical condition beforehand;
* that frost, disease, fertiliser, maturity or local exposure could be excluded;
* that the alleged object was positioned close enough to cause the damage;
* or that a single physical mechanism connected the sighting, the grass movement and the wilted flowers.

</div>

The plant evidence was therefore an observation requiring explanation, not a forensic trace identifying an extraordinary craft.

GEPAN also explored the witness’s statement that grass blades rose vertically during the departure. A laboratory consultant showed that an intense electrical field could make grass move through a corona-discharge effect. He estimated that a field of at least 30 kilovolts per metre would be required for grass about 15 centimetres high. Because the witness reported no sensation while standing close to the object, the report suggested that any such field could only have appeared briefly during departure. It further proposed that a stronger field near the amaranths might conceivably have damaged them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

That experiment demonstrated physical possibility, not historical causation. No electric field was measured in the garden, no electrical damage was independently identified and the grass movement itself came solely from the witness’s account. The report carefully acknowledged that it was impossible to affirm that an electrical effect caused the wilting. Later retellings that present the laboratory test as a reproduction of the UFO’s physical influence go beyond what the official document supports.

## Official checks found no confirming aircraft or radar evidence

GEPAN checked the sky, weather records and local air traffic. The reported direction of approach was broadly compatible with the witness’s description of the Sun’s position, which showed that the reconstruction was at least internally coherent. Investigators also contacted civil and military aviation authorities because the Nancy area was served by an airport with both functions.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Military radar recordings covering approximately 12.33 to 12.55 pm showed no notable anomaly in the relevant sector. Five intermittent returns appeared between about 12.40 and 12.55, but they were too irregular to indicate genuine movement and were described as commonplace radar noise. No known aircraft was identified as the object, but neither was there radar confirmation of a low-flying craft entering and leaving the garden.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The absence of radar detection does not prove that nothing physical was present. A small, low or weakly reflective object could escape surveillance designed for aircraft. Equally, the lack of a radar track removes one possible source of independent support. The case rests on testimony plus ambiguous plant damage, not on a coordinated visual-radar event.

There was also no usable photograph of the object. The camera failure was reported as a pre-existing mechanical problem rather than an unexplained electromagnetic effect. Photographs taken later recorded the garden and plants, but not the phenomenon itself. They help reconstruct the environment; they cannot establish what occupied it on 21 October.

## Later sceptical challenges weakened the physical case

Sceptical reviews have concentrated on the gap between the reality of the wilted flowers and the unproven assumption that the reported object caused them. A 2009 critique of GEPAN’s historical work argued that autumn frost offered a natural explanation and that the witness’s professional status may have discouraged sufficiently critical examination of perceptual error. It also questioned the sampling and preservation procedures used in prominent French trace cases, including Amaranth.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://skepticalinquirer.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/29/2009/01/p47.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: skepticalinquirer.org">[Skeptical Inquirer]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">skepticalinquirer.org</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Skeptical Inquirer</span></span></span>

A later French analysis proposed a more specific alternative: a two-coloured metallic party balloon resting on tall plants in the enclosed garden. The author argued that such an object might appear oval, metallic and blue-green, remain almost motionless while supported by vegetation and then rise as the air warmed or a breeze dislodged it. Previously overlooked gendarmerie photographs appeared to show dense vegetation in the relevant part of the garden, potentially providing a support that was less obvious in GEPAN’s later photographs and diagrams.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fichier-pdf.fr/2018/11/13/amarante-ovni-bilan-final-2108/amarante-ovni-bilan-final-2108.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fichier-pdf.fr">[Fichier PDF]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fichier-pdf.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fichier PDF</span></span></span>

The balloon proposal addresses several otherwise puzzling features: silence, apparent weightlessness, reflective surfaces, a confined sheltered location and eventual upward movement. It also offers a reason why the witness might have been unable to see how the object was supported. However, it remains a reconstruction rather than a demonstrated solution. No balloon was recovered, the original photographs are limited in quality, and the proposed size, exact resting point and departure mechanism cannot now be tested.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.fichier-pdf.fr/2018/11/13/amarante-ovni-bilan-final-2108/amarante-ovni-bilan-final-2108.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: fichier-pdf.fr">[Fichier PDF]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">fichier-pdf.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Fichier PDF</span></span></span>

Nor does the balloon hypothesis automatically explain the wilted plants. It could separate the case into two ordinary events—a misidentified lightweight object and unrelated seasonal plant stress—but that is an economical interpretation, not direct proof. The passage of decades, incomplete photographic material and the absence of the original object make a decisive retrospective test unlikely.

More speculative explanations, such as hallucination or a complex perceptual episode without any external object, have also been raised. These account for the lack of physical confirmation but require a prolonged and highly structured experience in an apparently alert witness. GEPAN found no evident psychological reason to reject his testimony, although its report also acknowledged that a sufficiently large perceptual mistake could not be ruled out.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

## Why the case remains unresolved rather than verified

The strongest aspect of the Amaranth case is not any single piece of extraordinary evidence. It is the combination of a prompt, detailed statement, a cooperative witness, a measured site reconstruction and genuine plant damage. These factors justified serious investigation and explain why the incident became central to the UFO history of Meurthe-et-Moselle.

Its weaknesses are more decisive when judging what the case proves. There was one observer, no image of the object, no confirmed radar return, no recovered material and no laboratory finding that tied the vegetation to a particular energy source. Sampling and storage compromised the biochemical work, while frost, plant maturity, disease, injury and uneven local conditions were not excluded. Later photographs made a lightweight balloon explanation more plausible, but not conclusive.

GEPAN’s own final assessment was notably restrained. Investigators found no reasonable basis for simply dismissing the witness, yet they emphasised that the absence of another observer prevented independent confirmation. They stated that the plant analyses produced no tangible conclusion and that even apparently unusual physiological changes would remain difficult to interpret without controlled comparative research. They also warned against assuming that separate French trace cases represented the same recurring phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/note_tech_17.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The most defensible judgement is therefore that something unusual was reported in a Nancy-area garden, and that some nearby plants were visibly wilted, but the connection between those two facts was never established. The case is unresolved because the available evidence cannot securely identify what the witness saw. It is not verified because none of that evidence demonstrates that a physically extraordinary object hovered there.
