## Introduction

Several of the most dramatic UFO reports from [Vosges]({{ 'why-vosges-ufo-reports-rarely-stay/' | relative_url }}) were eventually traced to ordinary lights seen under unfamiliar conditions. A triangular “craft” near Dompaire matched an airliner repeatedly circling Épinal–Mirecourt airport. A glowing oval that seemed to chase a car between Remiremont and Dinozé was a nightclub projector sweeping the cloud base. Groups of silent red or orange lights at Plombières-les-Bains and Bussang were identified as sky lanterns released during local festivities.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Vosges_53546a_vosges_explained_sig_2100de-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for Explained Sightings" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

These cases matter because the witnesses were not necessarily careless or dishonest. Each ordinary source produced features commonly treated as signs of something extraordinary: silence, sudden changes of direction, hovering, formation flight and apparent pursuit. GEIPAN, the investigation unit of France’s national space agency CNES, solved the stronger cases by reconstructing routes, checking aviation or event records, comparing wind direction and timing, and returning to the observation sites. The results show how a sincere and vivid sighting can become convincing before the observer has enough information to judge distance, scale or movement.

## The Dompaire triangle was an aircraft circuit

On the night of 30–31 March 2007, four young people travelling around Dompaire, Ubexy and Bouxurulles repeatedly saw red and white lights in a dark, stormy sky. The lights appeared to form a metallic triangle that moved backwards and forwards at changing speeds. At one point, the witnesses believed it passed only 15 to 20 metres above their vehicle. Their observations continued intermittently from about 8 pm until 2 am and left them deeply impressed.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007Elles forment un triangle de couleur métallique qui se déplace en va et vient et à allure variable avant de...</span></span></span>

Taken on its own, the testimony sounds like a close encounter with a large triangular machine. The investigation became much less mysterious once aircraft movements were checked. A Swiss Hello AG MD-90, a later version of the DC-9 airliner, had been using Épinal–Mirecourt airport for crew training. It made repeated “touch-and-go” circuits, in which an aircraft lands and immediately takes off again rather than stopping. GEIPAN recorded 30 such approaches during the afternoon, followed by further circuits between 7.47 pm and 9.16 pm and a final departure towards Zurich at 9.45 pm.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007Elles forment un triangle de couleur métallique qui se déplace en va et vient et à allure variable avant de...</span></span></span>

That repetitive flight pattern explains why the lights appeared, disappeared and returned from different directions. An aircraft flying a circuit alternately approaches the observer, turns across the field of view and heads away. Its landing lights can become intensely bright when directed towards the witness, then fade rapidly during a turn. Red, green and white navigation or anti-collision lights can also be perceived as the corners of a single dark object when the aircraft body is invisible against cloud or night sky.

The witnesses’ impression that the triangle passed extremely low is less reliable than it first appears. At night, observers usually lack the surface detail, shadows and familiar background objects needed to estimate distance. A large aircraft farther away can therefore look like a smaller object nearby. Its noise may arrive late, be masked inside a vehicle or be reduced by wind, terrain and the direction in which the aircraft is travelling.

The sightings reported after the training aircraft had left were also examined. GEIPAN noted that the group had sometimes mistaken street lamps or vehicle lights for the phenomenon while moving between locations. A freight aircraft landed at Épinal at 1.58 am, offering another conventional source for the final airborne light. The agency classified the Dompaire case as category A, meaning fully identified, with the main observations attributed to the MD-90’s training circuits.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007Elles forment un triangle de couleur métallique qui se déplace en va et vient et à allure variable avant de...</span></span></span>

The case is especially instructive because the witnesses’ description was not simply “a plane”. They perceived an object making unusual movements and approaching dangerously closely. The solution came not from dismissing that experience but from matching it with an aircraft whose real flight path naturally produced repeated, changing views.

## Cloud projections can seem to follow a vehicle

A different mechanism produced an equally unsettling encounter on 6 July 2007. At about 1 am, a couple driving from Remiremont towards Dinozé saw a pale blue-white, vapour-like oval in the cloudy sky. They described an inclined ring with smaller luminous circles around its outer edge. The light moved silently and appeared to follow their car for more than 20 kilometres. They continued to see it after reaching home, and the intermittent observation lasted roughly 35 minutes.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-07-08259" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDE REMIREMONT (88) VERS DINOZE (88) 06.07.2007Le 6 juillet 2007 aux environs d&#x27;une heure du matin, un automobiliste et sa femme von...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN concluded that the display came from a multi-beam sky projector operated by a nightclub in Épinal. Such equipment sends narrow beams upwards and moves them through programmed patterns. When the beams strike low cloud, the observer does not see the complete column of light. Instead, the illuminated patch on the cloud base can resemble a solid oval, ring or group of discs floating in the sky.

The identification rested on several converging details. The shape resembled known sky-projector patterns; the nightclub possessed the appropriate equipment at the time; and the projector operated on Thursday nights as well as Fridays and Saturdays. The sighting occurred in the early hours of a Friday. Investigators also found that the phenomenon’s visibility and behaviour were consistent with the projected light reaching the cloud layer. A later site reconstruction reproduced the relevant viewing conditions closely enough for GEIPAN to classify the case as category A.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-07-08259" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDE REMIREMONT (88) VERS DINOZE (88) 06.07.2007Le 6 juillet 2007 aux environs d&#x27;une heure du matin, un automobiliste et sa femme von...</span></span></span>

The apparent pursuit is a familiar visual effect. A distant light has very little parallax: as the observer moves, it does not shift noticeably against its background. It can therefore remain in roughly the same direction from a moving car, much as the Moon seems to follow a traveller. In the Remiremont case, the projected pattern could also sweep or reappear as the nightclub equipment moved its beams and as gaps or variations in the cloud changed what was visible.

Road bends further complicated the impression. When a vehicle turns and a distant light remains visible through successive stretches of road, the observer may interpret the repeated reappearance as deliberate tracking. Fear then changes what receives attention: every return of the light becomes evidence of pursuit, while moments when buildings, trees or hills block it can be remembered simply as temporary disappearances.

The quality of the witnesses’ recollections actually helped solve the case. Notes made near the time, a drawing and a careful reconstruction gave investigators enough precise information to test the projector hypothesis. This is an important contrast with poorly documented sightings: detailed testimony does not guarantee an extraordinary answer, but it makes ordinary explanations easier to check rather than merely speculate about.

A related but less conclusively documented report occurred at Fresse-sur-Moselle on 1 September 2012. A yellow circle repeatedly appeared and disappeared in cloud on a Saturday evening. GEIPAN considered a lighting display the probable explanation, although gendarmes did not locate a nearby establishment operating one. Because powerful beams can illuminate clouds from more than ten kilometres away, the agency classified the low-strangeness case as category B, meaning probably rather than perfectly identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/en/node/55701?order=field_classification_des_cas&amp;page=55&amp;sort=asc&amp;undefined=" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANFRESSE-SUR-MOSELLE (88) 01.09.2012La description du phénomène (cercle jaune apparaissant ou disparaissant dans les nuages) fait pen...</span></span></span>

The distinction is useful. Remiremont–Dinozé was solved through equipment records and reconstruction. Fresse-sur-Moselle had the characteristic appearance of a projected light but lacked a confirmed source. An explanation can therefore be highly plausible without being demonstrated to the same standard.

## Moving cars create false speed and direction

The Dompaire and Remiremont cases expose a wider problem in roadside UFO reports: the observer is moving while trying to judge an object whose distance is unknown. That makes apparent motion a mixture of several things—the source’s real movement, the car’s movement, bends in the road, intervening terrain and shifts in the observer’s viewing angle.

Three effects are especially important in Vosges, where winding roads, wooded slopes and broken horizons frequently interrupt the view.

**Unknown distance changes apparent speed.** A nearby object crossing the same angle of sky as a distant one must be moving much more slowly in real terms. Without a reliable distance estimate, witnesses cannot convert angular movement into physical speed. A light that appears to dart across the sky may simply be farther away or viewed during a sharp turn.

**Lights can seem joined into one object.** When several points maintain roughly the same arrangement, the mind naturally interprets them as lamps attached to a dark structure. This is one reason aircraft lights can become a triangle. The apparently solid area between them may be an inference rather than something directly visible.

**Occlusion resembles sudden acceleration.** A light disappearing behind trees, hills, roofs or cloud may appear to switch off or shoot away. Its later reappearance can then be interpreted as an abrupt change of direction. In mountainous or wooded terrain, repeated concealment can make a steady source look manoeuvrable.

These mechanisms do not mean every roadside report has the same cause. They explain why statements such as “it followed us”, “it hovered” or “it suddenly accelerated” require reconstruction before they can be treated as measurements. In the solved Vosges files, the witness experience was real, but the extraordinary motion was often created by geometry.

## Sky lanterns produced silent formations

At Plombières-les-Bains on 8 September 2013, two people saw several brilliant red lights arriving one after another at 4.19 am. The lights moved silently in the same direction, and one witness recorded video that was later posted online. A sequence of red lights travelling together without engine noise could easily be interpreted as a formation of controlled objects.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08560" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPLOMBIERES-LES-BAINS (88) 08.09.2013Ce cas d&#x27;observation a fait l&#x27;objet d&#x27;une enquête de terrain par des enquêteurs mandatés par le...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN commissioned a field investigation rather than relying solely on the visual resemblance to lanterns. Investigators compared the video, witness accounts, measurements, observation time and wind direction. They eventually located the release point and established that sky lanterns had been launched during a private celebration. The case was classified A, fully identified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2013-09-08560" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANPLOMBIERES-LES-BAINS (88) 08.09.2013Ce cas d&#x27;observation a fait l&#x27;objet d&#x27;une enquête de terrain par des enquêteurs mandatés par le...</span></span></span>

The investigation also revealed a common obstacle: the witnesses had already considered and rejected lanterns after consulting material online. That did not mean their testimony was insincere. It showed that a witness’s elimination of an explanation is only as strong as the comparison being used. Online examples may show lanterns at close range, in calm conditions or through a good camera, whereas distant lanterns can look like intense, steady points with no visible flame or envelope.

Sky lanterns have several properties that make them persuasive UFO stimuli:

* they glow red, orange or yellow rather than displaying standard aircraft navigation lights;
* they are silent and can remain visible for several minutes;
* several released in succession may follow broadly similar paths;
* weak or variable winds can make them appear stationary, then begin moving;
* individual lanterns can brighten, dim or vanish abruptly when their flames fail;
* differences in distance can make one light look much larger than the others.

</div>

## Repetition is often a clue, not a mystery

Repeated appearances are sometimes taken to imply surveillance or purposeful returns. In the solved Vosges cases, repetition usually pointed towards the operating cycle of the ordinary source.

At Dompaire, the aircraft was deliberately flying circuit after circuit. At Remiremont, the projector ran through recurring movements. At Plombières, several lanterns had been released successively. The reports therefore became more intelligible when investigators asked what local activity would naturally repeat at the recorded interval.

Timing also supplied information that appearance alone could not. Weekend nights increased the likelihood of parties, entertainment lighting and lantern releases. A sighting near an airport during recorded training activity made an aircraft explanation testable. Cloud cover strengthened the projector hypothesis because the cloud base supplied the screen on which the light became visible.

This does not justify assuming that every weekend light is a lantern or every triangle an aircraft. It does show why investigation must connect the sky description with what was happening on the ground. A witness may accurately report the colour and sequence of lights while misunderstanding the mechanism producing them.

## What made the explanations convincing?

The strongest explanations in the Vosges record did more than name an object that vaguely resembled the report. They linked several independent features of the sighting to a documented source.

For the Dompaire triangle, the decisive match was between the witnesses’ route, the repeated lights and the recorded flight circuits of a particular airliner. For Remiremont–Dinozé, investigators connected the shape and behaviour with a known projector, confirmed that suitable equipment existed locally and reconstructed the observation. At Plombières-les-Bains, the timing, wind, video and release location all converged on sky lanterns.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2007-03-01763" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANDOMPAIRE (88) 30.03.2007Elles forment un triangle de couleur métallique qui se déplace en va et vient et à allure variable avant de...</span></span></span>

That approach is more reliable than deciding from a description alone. “Orange light” does not automatically mean a lantern, and “triangle” does not automatically mean an aircraft. The identification becomes persuasive when it explains not only the object’s appearance but also its direction, duration, recurrence and relationship to local records.

GEIPAN’s classifications reflect the degree of identification rather than the emotional impact of the event. Category A denotes a perfectly identified phenomenon; category B a probably identified one; category C a case left unidentified because of insufficient information; and category D a phenomenon that remains unidentified after investigation. In the agency’s published statistics dated 25 June 2026, categories A and B together account for about two-thirds of classified cases nationally, while category D represents just over three per cent.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/stats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANStatisticsB Probably identified phenomenon … the GEIPAN uses &quot;dynamic&quot; statistics. The explained Vosges cases therefore fit a broader official pattern: most investigated reports do not require an unknown technology, but identification often depends on information the witness could not have possessed at the time.

## What these cases change about Vosges UFO history

The department’s explained sightings are not merely minor entries to remove from a catalogue. They show why some of the most memorable features of UFO testimony are not, by themselves, strong evidence of an extraordinary craft.

Silence can result from distance, wind, vehicle noise or a source with no engine. A triangle can be three aircraft lights mentally joined into one object. Apparent pursuit can arise from a distant source remaining at a nearly fixed bearing. Hovering may reflect a lantern moving slowly towards or away from the observer. Sudden disappearance can be caused by a turn, cloud, terrain or an extinguished flame.

The investigations also caution against treating witness credibility and correct interpretation as the same question. The Dompaire group, the Remiremont couple and the Plombières witnesses appear to have described experiences that genuinely puzzled or frightened them. Their good faith was compatible with misidentification. Indeed, the detail they preserved was often what allowed investigators to solve the reports.

Within the wider UFO history of Vosges, these cases provide a necessary comparison with reports that remain unresolved. They demonstrate the standard an explanation should meet: a specific source, compatible timing and geometry, and preferably corroborating records or reconstruction. Where those elements are absent, an ordinary cause may still be possible, but it should not be presented as proven. Where they converge, as they did at Dompaire, Remiremont and Plombières-les-Bains, seemingly extraordinary movements can be understood as the ordinary behaviour of aircraft, projected light and drifting lanterns seen from an unfamiliar viewpoint.
