## Introduction

The Longuenesse reports remain the strongest officially unresolved UFO case in [Pas-de-Calais]({{ 'which-pas-de-calais-ufo-cases-still/' | relative_url }}), but “unresolved” needs careful handling. During several nights in September and October 1990, a local witness and police patrols reported stationary or slowly moving lights near Longuenesse and Saint-Omer. The principal witness described an oval form edged with flashing red and green lights; officers independently recorded bright coloured points and, on one occasion, four lights travelling in a V formation. France’s official UFO service still lists the case as category D: unidentified after investigation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-09-01214" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[cnes-geipan.fr]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990Observation durant plusieurs jours et durant plusieurs heures à partir de 21h30 de formes ovoides avec d...</span></span></span>

That classification makes Longuenesse unusual, not proven extraordinary. The surviving file contains repeated observations, police testimony and references to photographs, giving it more substance than a vague one-off story. Yet it offers no published radar return, calibrated image, physical trace or reliable measurement of distance and altitude. Longuenesse therefore matters chiefly because it shows how an official investigation can reach a genuine “we do not know” without endorsing spacecraft, secret technology or any other specific explanation.

## The Repeated Longuenesse Observations

The case began on the evening of 8 September 1990. A 52-year-old notary’s clerk reported seeing what appeared to be an oval object surrounded by eight or nine flashing red and green lights. It could remain stationary, he said, but could also move at roughly the apparent speed of an aircraft. No sound was heard. Similar phenomena were reported on 9 and 11 September and again around the middle of October, usually appearing from about 9.30 pm and sometimes remaining visible for hours. On one night, the witness said it was still present when he awoke at approximately 4 am.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-longuenesse-1990-pv-n755-1990307161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl fr geipan longuenesse 1990 pv n755 1990307161</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyGEIPAN Case 1990-09-01214, LONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990, GEIPAN / CNES · 2007 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

Those repetitions are the first reason the file carries more weight than many UFO reports. A fleeting light can easily be a meteor, aircraft reflection or momentary perceptual error. A phenomenon apparently returning on separate nights provides investigators with opportunities to compare directions, timings and behaviour. It can also create complications, however: repeated appearances may indicate a recurring astronomical object, scheduled aircraft traffic, ground-based lighting or several unrelated stimuli being interpreted as one continuing mystery.

The police involvement is the case’s most important evidential feature. After being called to the area, officers reported seeing through binoculars a bright, fixed point north of a local car dealership, above the treetops, emitting blue-green and red flashes. They also saw other white lights moving across the sky. A separate patrol near a canal bridge reported four luminous points arranged in a V and moving from east to west. Police forensic personnel were reportedly called and took photographs.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-longuenesse-1990-pv-n755-1990307161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl fr geipan longuenesse 1990 pv n755 1990307161</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyGEIPAN Case 1990-09-01214, LONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990, GEIPAN / CNES · 2007 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

This does not mean that every witness saw the same physical object. The records contain several descriptions: one oval form with perimeter lights, a bright stationary point, moving white lights and a four-light V formation. These could have been different aspects of one phenomenon, but they could equally have been several ordinary objects seen in the same period. The official file preserves the observations; it does not demonstrate that they all belonged to a single craft.

The principal witness added two striking details during a formal interview on 22 October. Through binoculars, he believed he could see intermittent moving lines inside the oval form. He also reported disruption to two television channels, accompanied by an audible hum. That apparent electrical effect sounds impressive, but its value is limited because neighbours did not report the same interference and no technical examination established a connection with the lights.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-longuenesse-1990-pv-n755-1990307161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl fr geipan longuenesse 1990 pv n755 1990307161</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyGEIPAN Case 1990-09-01214, LONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990, GEIPAN / CNES · 2007 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

## Why the File Was Not Easily Explained

The 1990 police paperwork recorded at least two ordinary suggestions: reflections from lasers used by a local nightclub and the Soviet space station Mir. Neither was established as an adequate explanation, and the case remained open.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-longuenesse-1990-pv-n755-1990307161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl fr geipan longuenesse 1990 pv n755 1990307161</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyGEIPAN Case 1990-09-01214, LONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990, GEIPAN / CNES · 2007 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

A laser hypothesis might account for coloured light projected onto cloud or haze, especially if the apparent source remained fixed or moved slowly. Pas-de-Calais contains other official cases in which nightclub or display lasers were successfully identified. For example, GEIPAN classifies a 1988 Hulluch report as probably caused by a laser and a 1993 Le Portel case as definitively laser-related. That local precedent makes the suggestion reasonable, but Longuenesse included reports of apparently structured lights, long duration and movements across different directions. The available file does not show that investigators identified a specific venue, beam direction or weather layer capable of reproducing the full sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=44&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Mir was also an understandable candidate because an orbiting spacecraft can appear as a bright moving point. It is far less suitable for an object said to remain in roughly the same area for hours, return throughout a night or display persistent red and green lights. A low-Earth-orbiting spacecraft crosses a local sky in minutes rather than hovering until early morning. The Mir suggestion therefore appears in the police record as speculation, not a demonstrated match.

Aircraft remain a plausible partial explanation. Red and green navigation lights are standard features of aeroplanes, while a head-on aircraft can appear almost stationary before turning. Distance and speed are exceptionally difficult to judge at night, particularly when the observer has no known object size or reliable background reference. GEIPAN itself uses aircraft cases to demonstrate how perspective can create the impression of a hovering object, a silent triangle or an oddly shaped luminous body.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Aircraft do not neatly resolve everything in the Longuenesse account, however. The alleged hours-long duration, repeated stationary appearance and reports of multiple formations would require either recurring traffic, misassociation of several aircraft, or an additional fixed light such as a star or distant ground source. No published reconstruction identifies particular flights or radar tracks. The aircraft hypothesis is therefore credible but incomplete on the evidence presently available.

Astronomical scintillation is another possibility for the fixed coloured point. Bright stars low in the sky can appear to flash red, green and blue because atmospheric turbulence refracts their light. Binoculars may enlarge the blurred pattern without revealing a solid object. Yet an astronomical explanation would need the recorded compass direction, elevation and exact observation times to be checked against the sky. The public summary does not present such a calculation.

## What GEIPAN Categories Actually Mean

GEIPAN is a technical unit of France’s national space agency, CNES. It collects witness reports, obtains information through the police, gendarmerie, civil aviation and other bodies, investigates possible explanations, anonymises the resulting files and publishes its conclusions. It does not describe itself as a service searching for extraterrestrial visitors. Its preferred term covers unidentified aerospace phenomena because witnesses do not always observe a clearly defined object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Its categories separate three questions that are frequently confused: Was the phenomenon identified? Was the proposed identification merely probable? Was there enough evidence to conduct a meaningful analysis?


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **Category A** means the phenomenon was identified with high confidence.
* **Category B** means a known explanation is considered probable, although not completely proved.
* **Category C** means the report cannot be resolved because reliable information is missing.
* **Category D** means the phenomenon remains unidentified after investigation. The distinction between C and D is particularly important. A category C case may sound spectacular, but its mystery can be little more than missing data. Category D is more selective: investigators consider the information sufficiently consistent to examine, yet no known hypothesis satisfactorily accounts for the reported strangeness. GEIPAN evaluates both the residual unusualness of a report and its “consistency”, meaning the quantity, coherence and reliability of the evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMission &amp; Geipan &#124; GEIPAN</span></span></span>

Its entry was updated on 28 November 2024 without being moved into an identified or under-documented category. This confirms that, as of that update, the official status remained unresolved. It does not show that a new investigation proved the witness’s interpretation or strengthened an exotic explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=44&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Unresolved, Unclassifiable and Successfully Explained Cases

Longuenesse becomes clearer when set beside other Pas-de-Calais entries. The department’s official files include reports in every major category, and the differences are often more informative than the apparent drama of the original story.

Berck’s reported giant triangular object from October 2010 is category C, not D. However extraordinary the description may sound, GEIPAN considers the surviving information insufficiently reliable for a firm analysis. Similar C classifications apply to reports from Richebourg, Arras, Calais and several older departmental cases. These are unknown because the evidence is inadequate, not because an investigation established an unusually resistant phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=44&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Other reports were resolved through conventional identification. A 1986 case covering Vendin-le-Vieil and Hénin-Beaumont was attributed to the Moon. An Arras sighting in September 1987 was probably an atmospheric re-entry. A July 1990 departmental report was classified as a probable aircraft, while the later Marck aviation case was linked to an airliner. GEIPAN identified a Thai lantern at Vendin-lès-Béthune, a bird at Tatinghem and lasers at Hulluch and Le Portel.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field=&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=44&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

These examples show why vividness is a poor guide to evidential importance. The Moon, an aircraft seen at an unusual angle or a laser striking cloud can generate a far stranger account than a genuinely unresolved but visually modest point of light. Conversely, repeated police observations do not automatically establish an unknown vehicle; they simply make it harder to dismiss the record as a casual mistake or unsupported anecdote.

Official national figures reinforce this pattern. CNES currently summarises GEIPAN’s holdings as approximately 24.6 per cent clearly identified, 39.7 per cent probably identified, 32.4 per cent unidentified for lack of data and 3.3 per cent unidentified after investigation. The small D share is not evidence that those cases are extraterrestrial. It means only that the service has not found a satisfactory recognised explanation from the available evidence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes.fr/en/projects/geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes.fr">[CNES]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">GEIPAN &#124; CNES</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPAN &#124; CNES...</span></span></span>

## What the Longuenesse Status Really Tells Us

The best case for taking Longuenesse seriously is straightforward. The reports recurred over several nights; the principal witness supplied a formal statement; more than one police patrol recorded lights; binoculars were used; and forensic police reportedly attempted photography. The case entered the national official system and has survived later database updating as category D. It is therefore more substantial than an anonymous internet story or a newspaper anecdote detached from its original documentation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://ufotransparency.com/files/intl-fr-geipan-longuenesse-1990-pv-n755-1990307161" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: ufotransparency.com">[UFO Transparency]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">ufotransparency.com</span><span class="citation-popover-title">intl fr geipan longuenesse 1990 pv n755 1990307161</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">UFO TransparencyGEIPAN Case 1990-09-01214, LONGUENESSE (62) 08.09.1990, GEIPAN / CNES · 2007 · UFO Transparency...</span></span></span>

The doubts are equally substantial. No published photograph provides measurable detail. No radar, flight-log or meteorological reconstruction accompanies the public case summary. Witnesses could not establish objective distance, size or altitude. Several descriptions may concern different stimuli. The television interference was isolated and unverified. The police reports prove that officers saw lights, but not that the lights formed a structured vehicle or displayed capabilities beyond those of aircraft, astronomical sources or projected illumination.

Later reporting has therefore preserved rather than dramatically strengthened the claim. The online release made the original police record accessible and the 2024 update retained the D classification, but no decisive new sensor evidence appears to have emerged. Longuenesse remains officially unidentified because the collected observations are too coherent to dismiss yet too poorly instrumented to solve.

Within the UFO history of Pas-de-Calais, that is precisely why the case matters. It sits between two less satisfying extremes: stories that are mysterious only because their documentation is weak, and reports that become ordinary once the relevant aircraft, Moon, laser, bird or balloon is identified. Longuenesse is a defensible official unknown—but an unknown cause is not the same thing as evidence of an extraordinary craft.

    if (reference) params.push('customid=' + encodeURIComponent(reference).slice(0, 256));
    return url + joiner + params.join('&');
  }
  function domainLabel(marketId) {
    var cfg = markets[marketId] || markets[defaultMarket] || {};
    return cfg.domain_label || 'eBay';
  }
  function flagClass(marketId) {
    return 'fr-ebay-market-flag fr-ebay-market-flag--' + String(marketId || '').toLowerCase().replace(/_/g, '-').replace(/[^a-z0-9-]+/g, '');
  }
  function closeMenu(section) {
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var picker = section.querySelector('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
    if (menu) menu.hidden = true;
    if (trigger) trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', 'false');
    if (picker) picker.classList.remove('fr-ebay-market-picker--open');
  }
  function availableMarkets(section) {
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      return Array.prototype.slice.call(select.options)
        .map(function (option) { return option.value; })
        .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
    }
    return Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]'))
      .map(function (button) { return button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'); })
      .filter(function (marketId) { return !!markets[marketId]; });
  }
  function applyMarket(section, marketId, persist) {
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    if (available.indexOf(marketId)< 0) marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-localized-link]')).forEach(function (link) {
      var query = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-query') || '';
      var reference = link.getAttribute('data-ebay-reference') || '';
      link.setAttribute('href', buildUrl(query, reference, marketId));
    });
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select && available.indexOf(marketId) >= 0) select.value = marketId;
    var selectedText = marketId;
    if (select && select.selectedIndex >= 0) selectedText = select.options[select.selectedIndex].text;
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-selected-market-flag], [data-ebay-trigger-market-flag]')).forEach(function (flag) {
      flag.className = flagClass(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-trigger-market-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = selectedText;
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-domain-label]')).forEach(function (label) {
      label.textContent = domainLabel(marketId);
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.setAttribute('aria-selected', button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option') === marketId ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      var active = button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button') === marketId;
      button.setAttribute('aria-pressed', active ? 'true' : 'false');
    });
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-selected-market', marketId);
    if (persist) {
      try { if (window.localStorage) localStorage.setItem('phoenix-ebay-market', marketId); } catch (err) {}
    }
    closeMenu(section);
  }
  sections.forEach(function (section) {
    if (section.getAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init') === '1') return;
    section.setAttribute('data-ebay-localized-init', '1');
    var available = availableMarkets(section);
    var marketId = defaultMarket;
    if (window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation && window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket) {
      marketId = window.PhoenixAffiliateLocation.inferMarket(available, {
        storageKey: 'phoenix-ebay-market',
        defaultMarket: defaultMarket
      });
    } else if (available.indexOf(defaultMarket)< 0) {
      marketId = available[0] || defaultMarket;
    }
    var select = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-select]');
    if (select) {
      select.addEventListener('change', function () {
        applyMarket(section, select.value, true);
      });
    }
    var trigger = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-trigger]');
    var menu = section.querySelector('[data-ebay-market-menu]');
    if (trigger && menu) {
      trigger.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
        event.stopPropagation();
        var expanded = trigger.getAttribute('aria-expanded') === 'true';
        menu.hidden = expanded;
        trigger.setAttribute('aria-expanded', expanded ? 'false' : 'true');
        var picker = trigger.closest('.fr-ebay-market-picker');
        if (picker) picker.classList.toggle('fr-ebay-market-picker--open', !expanded);
      });
      Array.prototype.slice.call(menu.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-option]')).forEach(function (button) {
        button.addEventListener('click', function (event) {
          event.stopPropagation();
          applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-option'), true);
        });
      });
      document.addEventListener('click', function () { closeMenu(section); });
    }
    Array.prototype.slice.call(section.querySelectorAll('[data-ebay-market-button]')).forEach(function (button) {
      button.addEventListener('click', function () {
        applyMarket(section, button.getAttribute('data-ebay-market-button'), true);
      });
    });
    applyMarket(section, marketId, false);
  });
})();
