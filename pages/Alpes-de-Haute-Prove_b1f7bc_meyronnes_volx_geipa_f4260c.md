## Introduction

Beyond the famous [Valensole]({{ 'valensole/' | relative_url }}) case, [Alpes-de-Haute-Provence]({{ 'alpes-de-haute-provence/' | relative_url }}) has a quieter collection of reports that remain unresolved for very different reasons. At Meyronnes in December 1976, several military witnesses reported slow, silent objects over a mountain valley, but the surviving file lacks enough reliable measurements to test ordinary explanations. At Volx in August 2012, a lone observer gave a detailed account of a dark, fast-moving object, prompting a much fuller investigation; GEIPAN still lists the phenomenon as unidentified.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-08284" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANVOLX (04) 08.08.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The contrast matters. Meyronnes is officially classified **C**, meaning that insufficient dependable information prevents a sound conclusion. Together, the cases show why “unresolved” can mean either an intriguing observation that resisted proposed explanations or simply a file too incomplete to decide.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">D1/D2), D1 et D2 étant des subdivisions de D. ## The 1976 Meyronnes military sightings

The Meyronnes file concerns events on two consecutive nights, 8 and 9 December 1976, in the Ubayette valley near the former commune of Meyronnes. At about 11 pm, military personnel reported seeing several round objects moving slowly and silently from the south-east towards the north-west. GEIPAN’s summary says that the observations lasted only a few seconds, although the original gendarmerie dossier contains several witness statements and supporting diagrams.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-12-00367?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C357&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMEYRONNES (04) 08.12.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The surviving police file is more substantial than a simple anecdote. It was assembled as a formal gendarmerie procedure, included a synthesis, witness interviews, photographs and sketches, and was circulated to civil and military authorities. One diagram attempted to record angular separations, an estimated elevation of about 20 degrees and a possible distance of several kilometres. Such estimates can help reconstruct an event, but in this case they were approximate rather than instrument-derived.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0134%20%281976309131%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

The witnesses’ military status gives the report some historical interest. Service personnel working in a mountain environment could reasonably be expected to notice aircraft, flares or unusual activity. Yet occupation alone does not guarantee accurate estimates of distance, size or speed, particularly at night, when an isolated light may offer no reliable scale. The file does not contain radar confirmation, photographs of the objects, recovered material or measured flight data.

An unusual secondary detail entered the case from a dog-handling unit roughly ten kilometres away. According to the official summary, its ten dogs howled abnormally on the same two nights. Another witness described an earlier incident, about six weeks before, in which a red light rose near the foot of the ridges and disappeared behind a summit. These details suggest that investigators looked for a wider pattern, but neither establishes that the animals, the earlier light and the military observations had the same cause.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-12-00367?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C357&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMEYRONNES (04) 08.12.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The authorities did not simply file the statements and forget them. Surveillance was reportedly maintained from 12 to 30 December, but no further phenomenon was seen. That negative result weakens any suggestion of persistent or routinely repeated activity in the valley, although it cannot explain what the witnesses had already reported.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-12-00367?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C357&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMEYRONNES (04) 08.12.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

### Why Meyronnes is not an “unexplained craft” case

GEIPAN classifies Meyronnes as **C**, with the description “lack of reliable information”. That distinction is crucial. The file records an unidentified observation, but it does not supply enough dependable information for investigators to show that familiar causes were systematically eliminated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/1976-12-00367?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=%2C357&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANMEYRONNES (04) 08.12.1976 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several ordinary possibilities therefore remain open:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* distant aircraft or helicopters seen without an obvious sound;
* military illumination or training activity for which no complete record survives;
* stars or planets appearing to move because the witnesses or their vehicle were moving;
* atmospheric distortion or lights partly obscured by mountain ridges;
* inaccurate recollection of duration, number or trajectory when statements were formalised.

</div>

None of these explanations is demonstrated in the published file. Equally, the file does not demonstrate controlled manoeuvres, extraordinary acceleration or a physical object with known dimensions. The most responsible conclusion is the official one: the incident is undecidable because its evidence is too weak, not because an exotic interpretation has prevailed.

## The unexplained 2012 Volx object

The Volx report is different in both form and investigative depth. At about 11 pm on 8 August 2012, a witness was lying on a terrace watching the stars when a dark object crossed the sky from north-east to south-west. He described it as resembling a weaver’s shuttle: elongated, brown-green, with diffuse edges and a dim, dark-red circular area in its centre. It appeared to obscure or distort the star field behind it, travelled in a straight line and produced no audible noise. The entire observation lasted approximately five seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-08284" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANVOLX (04) 08.08.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The witness contacted GEIPAN later that month and subsequently made a statement at the Manosque gendarmerie. Because the account was unusual but contained uncertainties, a local investigator visited the site with him in January 2015. The scene was reconstructed twice to refine the duration, viewing angles and trajectory. Investigators estimated that the phenomenon entered view at about 30 degrees elevation in the north-east, passed close to the zenith and disappeared behind vegetation at about 27 degrees elevation in the south-west.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

This fieldwork is the strongest feature of the case. It moved the inquiry beyond the witness’s initial written description and tested whether his account remained reasonably consistent at the location. GEIPAN found one understandable discrepancy in his original drawing of the direction of travel, but did not regard it as undermining the whole testimony. The witness had also made a sketch and noted the time shortly after the sighting, reducing—but not eliminating—the risk of later memory expansion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Investigators checked several conventional categories. No relevant bright planet was above the horizon, and no particular aircraft, satellite or other recorded aerial traffic was identified for the stated time. Meteorological data indicated a clear sky, good visibility and a westerly wind. A Météo-France specialist reportedly recognised no known weather phenomenon matching the account.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The object’s apparent movement also seemed inconsistent with something passively carried by the wind. GEIPAN calculated a range of hypothetical sizes and speeds based on different assumed altitudes. At very low altitude, an animal might fit parts of the speed-and-size relationship, but not the reported shape and colour. At typical helicopter or aircraft altitudes, the absence of engine noise became difficult to reconcile with the object passing almost overhead in a quiet setting. At greater assumed altitudes, the calculated dimensions or velocity became implausibly large.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

These calculations illustrate the case’s interest, but they should not be mistaken for direct measurements. The object’s true distance was unknown. Its apparent size was reconstructed years later using a hand-held comparison, and the five-second duration was based on witness recall and reenactment. Small errors in viewing angle, apparent size or duration could produce large changes in calculated speed and dimensions.

### The alternatives were not equally convincing

The 2015 report assigned very low weight to an aircraft, satellite, wind-borne object or animal. It considered a helicopter somewhat more plausible but still difficult because of the missing sound. The investigator gave the greatest weight to an unidentified rare natural phenomenon, while explicitly acknowledging that no such phenomenon had actually been established. Hallucination, reflected light and deliberate deception were judged possible but weak.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

Some passages in this part of the report are notably speculative. The suggestion that an atmospheric disturbance might have followed local north-south features such as the Durance, railway, canal or power lines was not backed by a demonstrated physical mechanism. Likewise, discussion of unknown propulsion technologies added no testable evidence. These ideas reveal how few satisfactory conventional matches were found, but they do not strengthen the observation itself.

GEIPAN’s formal conclusion was narrower: the reported object did not resemble a known object or phenomenon when its appearance, path, apparent speed and silence were considered together. The original investigation classified it **D1**, describing the testimony as detailed and of good consistency, while stressing that it came from one distant observer and had no photograph.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

The change in label therefore need not indicate that new physical evidence was discovered; it may reflect presentation or classification policy.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/fr/cas/2012-08-08284" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANVOLX (04) 08.08.2012 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What the classifications actually tell us

GEIPAN assesses cases through two linked ideas. **Strangeness** measures how far the reported phenomenon remains from recognised explanations after investigation. **Consistency** reflects the quantity and reliability of the available information. A case needs both residual strangeness and adequate evidential consistency to enter category D.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">D1/D2), D1 et D2 étant des subdivisions de D. The principal labels are best read as investigative outcomes rather than claims about alien technology:

* **A:** explained with high confidence.
* **B:** probably explained.
* **C:** not classifiable because dependable information is insufficient.
* **D:** still unidentified after investigation, sometimes subdivided as D1 or D2 in older or more detailed records.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/actualites/methodologie-classification-geipan" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">methodologie classification geipan</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">D1/D2), D1 et D2 étant des subdivisions de D. Category C is often misunderstood as a weaker form of “unexplained”. In practice, it sits partly outside the main scale because investigators cannot properly measure the remaining strangeness. Meyronnes is a good example: multiple witnesses and official paperwork sound impressive, but the key observational data are too uncertain to discriminate reliably between an unusual object and an ordinary light seen under difficult conditions. GEIPAN itself notes that C cases should, strictly speaking, be treated separately from the A, B and D sequence.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/glossaire" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANLe glossaire du GeipanCLASSIFICATION DES CAS... Volx better fits what readers ordinarily mean by an unresolved case. The witness supplied a prompt report, a gendarmerie statement, a sketch and a detailed site reconstruction. Investigators checked astronomy, weather, aircraft activity and possible object performance. Even so, the evidential ceiling remains low: one observer, five seconds, no image, no independent witness and no instrument record. Its D classification means that a satisfactory identification was not found, not that every natural or human explanation has been disproved.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete374.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete374</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANModéle de document par défaut CNES version 1.5 mars 1999...</span></span></span>

## What the department’s smaller cases add

The wider Alpes-de-Haute-Provence record contains many category C reports from places including Sisteron, Les Mées, Ongles, Riez, Entre­vaux, Pierrevert and Noyers-sur-Jabron. Their presence does not establish a geographical “hotspot”. It mainly shows how frequently brief nocturnal observations reach investigators without the photographs, precise angles, corroboration or timely technical checks needed for a firm conclusion.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B13%5D=13&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B14%5D=14&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B15%5D=15&amp;field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B16%5D=16&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_date_value=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=1&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=57.70414723434193&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=19.642587534013032&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=49.921875&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=-9.843750000000002&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=1&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=asc&amp;video=on" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Meyronnes and Volx therefore contribute different lessons to the department’s UFO history. Meyronnes shows that several apparently credible witnesses and a formal military-police file can still leave too little usable evidence. Volx shows that even a careful modern investigation may end without identification when the original event was fleeting and witnessed by only one person.

Neither case rivals Valensole in cultural influence, alleged physical traces or historical fame. Their value is more methodological. They expose the broad territory hidden by the word “unresolved”: at one end, reports that cannot be assessed properly; at the other, unusual observations that remain resistant to identification but are still unsupported by decisive independent evidence.
