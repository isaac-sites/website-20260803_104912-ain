## Introduction

Some of [Haute-Corse]({{ 'what-haute-corses-ufo-files-actually/' | relative_url }})’s most convincing UFO reports appear to have been ordinary aircraft seen under unusually deceptive conditions. The clearest examples are the five divers who watched a bright, apparently stationary object offshore near the Golo river in August 2011, and the witnesses who saw five silent lights changing formation over Bastia in August 2013. GEIPAN, the French space agency’s unit for investigating unidentified aerospace phenomena, concluded that the first case was probably a succession of airliners and identified the second as a delayed Air Corsica Airbus approaching Bastia-Poretta Airport.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

These conclusions matter because neither sighting initially sounded much like a conventional aeroplane. The reported objects seemed silent, oddly shaped and either stationary or moving as a coordinated formation. Yet the geography of Haute-Corse’s eastern coast places observers beside busy approach routes over the sea, where aircraft may be seen nearly head-on, with powerful lights masking their bodies and little sideways movement to reveal their speed.

## The divers’ sighting off the Golo

On the morning of 20 August 2011, five amateur divers surfaced from a dive near Punta d’Arco, roughly 5.4 kilometres off the eastern Corsican coast and close to the mouth of the Golo. At about 10am, while removing their equipment aboard an inflatable boat, they noticed a luminous phenomenon that appeared motionless in the sky. It remained visible for several minutes, disappeared, then seemed to return in a different form with a brilliant light beside it.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The principal witness described an elongated, metallic-grey object with indistinct edges, rather like a rugby ball, although another diver reportedly perceived something more triangular. A very bright white light appeared on or near it. The observers heard no engine noise and believed the object was stationary. Those features made the report memorable: several witnesses were present, the sky was clear, and the sighting lasted far longer than a fleeting meteor or momentary reflection.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

There were nevertheless serious evidential limitations. Only one of the five divers was interviewed directly by the investigator. A second witness agreed to complete a questionnaire but never returned it, while the other three wished to remain anonymous. The observation itself was not filmed. A white point visible in video recorded before the dive might have been related, but GEIPAN could not establish that it was the same object later seen after the divers resurfaced.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The timing of the report also weakened the investigation. GEIPAN received the questionnaire more than a month after the event, too late to request the relevant radar record. Field enquiries did not begin until the following April, almost eight months after the sighting. By then investigators had to reconstruct the case through witness memory, airport activity records and visits to nearby locations rather than through complete contemporaneous data.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

### A crowded hour over the eastern coast

The aviation evidence strongly favoured an aircraft explanation. Bastia-Poretta’s traffic records showed five departures and six arrivals between 10am and 11am that Saturday. The day was classified as exceptionally busy because it fell during the August holiday season, and air-traffic staff reported that many additional flights crossed above Corsica on international routes. The airport lay less than ten kilometres from the Golo area and almost directly along the relevant line of sight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Nothing unusual was reported by Bastia-Poretta air traffic control, the air-transport gendarmerie, the local gendarmeries at Borgo and Cervione, or Solenzara air base. People working near the coast also recalled no unusual event, despite beaches and holiday accommodation being busy at the time. There was no corresponding local press report. The absence of independent confirmation does not prove that the divers saw an aeroplane, but it sits uneasily with the very large apparent object later described by the principal witness.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

GEIPAN considered helicopters and maritime patrol aircraft as alternatives. Helicopters regularly crossed between Corsica and Italy or operated locally for rescue, tourism and photography. A military maritime aircraft might also fly circuits offshore. Both possibilities were judged less satisfactory because a nearby helicopter or low-flying patrol aircraft ought to have produced more obvious noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The preferred explanation was therefore not one mysterious aircraft performing a sequence of abrupt changes, but several unrelated airliners appearing successively in roughly the same part of the sky. One may have produced the white point seen before the dive; another, approaching Bastia nearly head-on with lights illuminated, may have created the main post-dive observation. GEIPAN classified the case as category B: probably identified as aircraft rather than proven beyond all doubt.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

That distinction is important. The investigators did not match every phase to a named flight, because the delayed report had cost them the radar evidence needed for a precise reconstruction. Their conclusion rested instead on the dense traffic, the airport alignment, the lack of anomalous aviation reports and the known visual effects of approaching aircraft. The case was weakened substantially, but not solved with the documentary precision achieved in the later Bastia incident.

## Five lights over Bastia

At about 11.50pm on 5 August 2013, four people were on a roof terrace in central Bastia overlooking the sea. One noticed five white points travelling from north to south. They initially formed a V, point first, and appeared to move together. As the others joined him, the lights seemed to change position and trace geometric figures, described as resembling a figure of eight around an imaginary axis. The entire observation lasted approximately 30 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

Again, the report contained the elements that often make aircraft misidentifications sound extraordinary. The lights were described as star-like and equal in brightness. No fuselage was visible. They appeared to belong to one large object, made no audible noise and seemed to descend before disappearing. The main witness remained convinced that he had not seen an aeroplane. GEIPAN nevertheless considered him sincere, calm and genuinely interested in finding an explanation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

Investigators found a highly specific match. Air Corsica flight AT514 from Paris, operated by an Airbus A320, should have landed at Bastia at 11pm but was severely delayed. Airport documentation recorded its landing at exactly 12.02am, only minutes after the sighting. The aircraft approached in clear conditions, and the observed north-to-south movement offshore was consistent with a normal route towards Bastia-Poretta.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

Aircraft arriving from Paris commonly crossed the Cap Corse area and passed offshore beside Bastia before turning towards the airport. GEIPAN’s reconstruction showed two possible approach patterns, one running relatively close to the city and another making a wider loop over the sea. Both would have placed the aircraft within the witnesses’ broad viewing sector before landing south of Bastia.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

The five visible points were interpreted as a combination of position lights and powerful forward-facing or wing-mounted lights. As the A320 turned and descended, the apparent spacing of those lights would change. Lights that first formed a V could compress, overlap or seem to rotate around one another even though they were fixed to a rigid airframe. The possible switching-on of additional landing lights could also have altered the apparent pattern during the brief observation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

This was a much stronger identification than in the divers’ case. The time, direction, location and observed descent all coincided with the only expected late-night arrival. Air-traffic control was waiting for that delayed flight, and neither controllers nor the air-transport gendarmerie recorded any second or anomalous object. GEIPAN therefore classified the report as category A: identified as AT514 during its approach to Bastia-Poretta.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

## Why an aeroplane can appear stationary and silent

The central illusion in the Golo case was apparent immobility. An aircraft travelling almost directly towards an observer shows little movement across the background sky. Although it is closing the distance rapidly, its bearing may change so slowly that it seems to hover. Only when it turns, passes to one side or grows noticeably larger does its forward motion become obvious.

This effect is particularly persuasive over open water. The observer has few nearby reference points against which to judge movement, distance or scale. A bright light above a featureless horizon provides no reliable indication of whether its source is a small nearby object, a large distant aircraft or something between the two. The divers’ estimated size was therefore based mainly on apparent angular size and memory rather than a known distance. GEIPAN explicitly considered the reported size to have been overestimated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Silence is not equally mysterious in every geometry. Jet noise is strongest behind and around an aircraft rather than directly in front of it. If an approaching aircraft is still several kilometres away, wind, sea noise and the direction of engine exhaust can make it difficult to hear. GEIPAN specifically noted that aircraft seen head-on offshore could remain inaudible because their engines and exhaust were directed away from the observers.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The Federal Aviation Administration recommends the use of landing lights around airports and below 10,000 feet to make aircraft more conspicuous. It also notes that aeroplanes may carry several different exterior systems, including position lights, strobes, rotating beacons and landing lights. To a distant observer, those bright sources may be visible while the much dimmer body and wings remain lost against the sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/air_traffic/publications/media/AIM-Chg-2-dtd-3-21-24.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationAIM Change 2 dtd 3/21/24March 22, 2024 — 21 Mar 2024 — the anti−collision lights should be turned when tax...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: March 22, 2024</span></span></span>

At night, the problem becomes more severe because there are fewer visual cues. Aviation guidance recognises that isolated lights in darkness are difficult to judge accurately and may appear to move even when stationary, an illusion known as autokinesis. The Bastia witnesses were looking from a rooftop towards a dark sea and night sky, conditions in which five lights could easily be perceived as a structured object rather than as lamps attached to an aircraft.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/sites/faa.gov/files/21_phak_glossary.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Federal Aviation Administration PHAK Glossary</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationPHAK GlossaryJuly 10, 2023 — Autokinesis. ## Glare can hide the aircraft that produces it

The Golo sighting occurred in bright sunshine with the Sun close to the observers’ viewing direction. The temperature at Bastia was already about 29.8°C, visibility was good and the air was nearly calm. GEIPAN proposed that sunlight reflected from an aircraft body, combined with forward lights and atmospheric shimmer, could have produced the brilliant white appearance and indistinct outline.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

Glare does more than make an object brighter. It can erase structural detail. A distant fuselage may become a luminous patch whose edges fluctuate because of haze or heat distortion. Separate reflections can appear to detach and merge as the aircraft’s angle changes. This offers a conventional route from a normal airframe to the divers’ reports of a metallic oval, a triangular form and an isolated light apparently rejoining the main object.

At night, concentrated landing lights can create the reverse problem: the body disappears while the lamps remain. Landing lights project a narrow, intense beam ahead of the aircraft, making them especially conspicuous to people positioned near its approach direction. The observer may therefore see several brilliant points without seeing wings, tail or fuselage clearly enough to recognise their source.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.faa.gov/regulations_policies/rulemaking/committees/documents/media/2019-11-07%20ACS%20WG%20Interim%20Recommendation%20Report%20-%20Powered-Lift%20Handbook.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: faa.gov">[Federal Aviation Administration]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">faa.gov</span><span class="citation-popover-title">2019 11 07 ACS WG Interim Recommendation Report Powered Lift Handbook</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Federal Aviation AdministrationAviation Rulemaking Advisory Committee7 Nov 2019 — Landing lights usually cast a beam that is narrow and c...</span></span></span>

A turn then seems to change the object’s geometry. Lights fixed along an A320’s wings and fuselage do not maintain the same apparent spacing when viewed from different angles. A symmetrical pattern seen nearly head-on may broaden as the aircraft banks, while some lamps become hidden and others intensify. Over a 30-second interval, this perspective change can look like lights rearranging themselves independently.

## Delayed reporting changed the strength of the two cases

The difference between the 2011 and 2013 conclusions was largely a difference in recoverable evidence. In the divers’ case, the first report arrived more than a month late, preventing a radar request, and the field investigation did not begin until the following year. Investigators could demonstrate that the setting was rich in air traffic, but they could not assign each appearance to a particular flight.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The Bastia lights were reported within days, although the full questionnaire and local investigation followed later. Crucially, airport staff retained a flight ticket documenting AT514’s exceptional delay and exact landing time. More detailed tracking information had already been discarded because the civil aviation service kept it for only two months, but enough remained to establish a close match.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete407.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete407</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete407</span></span></span>

Both files also depended heavily on one principal account despite the presence of several observers. In 2011, only one diver completed the full process; in 2013, only one of the four rooftop witnesses formally testified. Multiple people seeing something at the time is useful, but it does not automatically provide independent evidence if their separate descriptions, timings and viewing positions are never recorded.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The practical lesson is that unusual observations near Bastia should be reported promptly and precisely. Exact time, direction, elevation, duration and location can be compared with flight movements before detailed records expire. Several witnesses should write their accounts separately before discussing the event extensively. Photographs or video should include buildings, the horizon or other fixed references rather than an isolated point of light.

## What these cases change in Haute-Corse’s UFO history

The two investigations do not show that every strange light around Bastia is an aircraft. They do show that aircraft can reproduce many features often treated as evidence against an aviation explanation: apparent hovering, silence, changing shape, coordinated lights and abrupt disappearance. In both cases, those features arose in locations and directions closely connected to Bastia-Poretta’s traffic.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/sites/default/files/Compte%20rendu%20enquete326.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Compte rendu enquete326</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Compte rendu enquete326</span></span></span>

The 2011 report remains less securely resolved because no named flights were reconstructed and some witness information was never collected. Its category B classification is therefore appropriately cautious: successive aircraft were judged probable, not conclusively demonstrated. The 2013 report is much less ambiguous because a specific Airbus was in the right place at the right time and followed the apparent path of the lights.

Together, the cases weaken the idea that silence or unusual light geometry alone can distinguish an extraordinary craft from an approaching airliner. Around Bastia and the eastern coastal plain, the first questions should instead be geometric: Was the object near an airport approach? Was it moving almost directly towards the observer? Could glare be hiding its outline? Did its “changing formation” begin when it turned?

That is why aircraft misidentification occupies an unusually important place in Haute-Corse’s UFO record. The department’s coastal geography does not merely place conventional aircraft in view; it often presents them in the precise orientation most likely to conceal their speed, structure and sound. The resulting sightings can be sincere, detailed and genuinely baffling without requiring anything more exotic than an aeroplane seen from the wrong angle.
