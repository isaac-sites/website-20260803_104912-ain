## Introduction

On the evening of 5 November 1990, people across Vendée watched a broad formation of lights move silently through the dark sky. Some described a triangle, delta, diamond or platform; others thought they saw a huge solid body carrying coloured lamps, searchlights and glowing exhaust trails. The scale of the reports made the episode one of the most memorable supposed UFO events in the department’s history.



<img src="{{ "/assets/images/Vendee_da066b_vendee_1990_mass_sig_c13586-overview.webp" | relative_url }}" alt="Overview image for 1990 Sighting" loading="eager" decoding="sync" fetchpriority="high">

The evidence now strongly indicates that Vendée did not witness a giant craft. The lights formed part of an exceptional atmospheric re-entry: the third stage of a Soviet Proton rocket breaking apart high above France. Its timing, direction, orbital identification and reconstructed path all match the main body of reports. What makes the case important is therefore not an enduring mystery, but the unusually clear demonstration that real space debris can produce a convincing impression of a vast, low-flying and silent machine.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## What witnesses reported across Vendée

The display appeared shortly after 7pm, when many people were still outdoors, travelling home or looking across open rural horizons. GEIPAN’s national archive lists Vendée reports from places including Barbâtre, Saint-Gervais, La Barre-de-Monts and La Chapelle-Achard. These locations spread the event from the coastal and marsh areas of north-west Vendée into the department’s interior, fitting a phenomenon seen across a wide section of sky rather than a local object hovering over one commune.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Statements preserved in the gendarmerie files show both a strong common core and striking variation. Witnesses repeatedly mentioned:

* numerous white, orange, red or blue lights moving together;
* a general south-west to north-east passage;
* glowing trails or luminous “powder” behind the formation;
* an apparent duration of several tens of seconds;
* an impression of low altitude and great size;
* little or no audible noise.

</div>

One farmer reported at about 6.58pm that an “immense delta” with rounded wings crossed from south-west to north-east. Points of light appeared to define its outline, while two broad luminous trails marked its passage. He thought it was flying slowly at low altitude, although he observed it for only 15 to 20 seconds.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0952%20%281990307284%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Another witness described many white lights arranged symmetrically around what seemed to be a bulky trapezoidal object. A powerful white beam appeared to shine backwards, and a pale trail remained visible after the lights had disappeared. The witness estimated the object at only 30 to 50 metres above the ground and roughly the volume of a house, but also emphasised that it was the complete silence that made the sight especially disturbing.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0952%20%281990307284%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Other statements in the same collection produced still larger estimates. One observer imagined an object between 100 and 300 metres across; a father and son compared a rectangular or diamond-shaped platform to a rugby pitch. Their descriptions differed over the exact outline, colours and apparent movement, yet converged on the impression of an enormous illuminated structure passing without engine noise.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0952%20%281990307284%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

These accounts should not be dismissed as fabricated simply because their size and altitude estimates were wrong. They were recorded promptly, often by gendarmes, and many witnesses clearly struggled to describe something genuinely unfamiliar. Their testimony is strong evidence that an extraordinary luminous display occurred. It is much weaker evidence for the witnesses’ interpretation of that display as a single solid vehicle.

## How investigators identified the rocket stage

The event was never merely assigned a convenient explanation after the fact. French investigators established a specific orbiting object whose re-entry matched the night, time and route of the sightings.

Within an hour, CNES security services were receiving calls from gendarmerie brigades reporting a luminous phenomenon crossing a large part of France. SEPRA, the CNES service then responsible for unusual aerospace reports and atmospheric re-entries, checked with civilian and military aviation authorities, meteorological services and NASA. On 8 November, NASA supplied the identification of object 20925, also catalogued as 1990-094C and associated with the Gorizont 21 mission. CNES orbital specialists then confirmed that its path crossed France from the Bay of Biscay towards Alsace. The identification was released to press agencies on 9 November, and contemporary national reporting described the object as fragments of a Soviet rocket disintegrating during re-entry.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The object was the third stage of a Proton launcher used for a Soviet communications-satellite mission two days earlier. A knowledgeable observer had already advised SEPRA on 6 November that re-entry object 1990-094C was the likely cause. The calculated course placed it at about 103 kilometres above the Bay of Biscay and around 83 kilometres high as it left eastern France.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s later reconstruction describes the visible phenomenon as a cloud of fragments descending through roughly 100 to 50 kilometres altitude. Its speed fell from about seven to two kilometres per second as atmospheric drag broke up and heated the stage. Different pieces glowed with different intensities and produced separate trails, while the complete train stretched over tens of kilometres. From the ground, this created not a compact point like an ordinary meteor, but a slow-looking procession of lights occupying a large angular area of sky.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Several features reinforce the identification:

**Timing.** The principal reports cluster at about 7pm, matching the calculated re-entry window.

**Direction.** Vendée witnesses generally saw the phenomenon moving from the south-west or west towards the north-east or east, consistent with a track entering France from the Bay of Biscay.

**Geographical sequence.** Reports continued across France and beyond Vendée, which is difficult to reconcile with a local aircraft or craft flying at the low altitudes claimed by witnesses.

**Appearance.** Multiple glowing fragments, long trails and changes in brightness are expected when a large rocket stage breaks into pieces during atmospheric entry.

**Silence.** At tens of kilometres altitude, no nearby engine noise would accompany the lights. Any delayed sonic effects would not necessarily be connected by witnesses with the already distant display.

GEIPAN classifies the episode as category A, meaning an observation explained without ambiguity. Its current case page identifies the phenomenon specifically as the atmospheric re-entry of a Proton third stage, rather than leaving it as a probable or unresolved interpretation.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/cas/1990-11-01225" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

## Why the formation looked like a solid craft

The hardest part of the case is not explaining the luminous fragments. It is explaining why so many people believed that those fragments were fixed to a dark, structured machine.

At night, a light in an otherwise empty sky provides almost no dependable distance information. Unless observers recognise the object and already know its approximate size, they cannot calculate how far away it is from vision alone. A brilliant fragment 80 kilometres away can therefore be interpreted as a smaller lamp a few hundred metres away. Once the assumed distance becomes too small, the apparent dimensions of the entire formation become enormous: house-sized, pitch-sized or even larger. GEIPAN notes that witnesses to bright fireballs commonly place them below 1,000 metres even when they are actually tens of kilometres high.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

A second effect concerns shape. Human vision naturally groups lights that move in the same direction and at the same speed. Rather than perceiving unrelated points, the observer tends to construct an implied surface joining them. Three lights may become the corners of a triangle; several rows may become windows on a rectangle; a brighter fragment may become a headlamp or searchlight. GEIPAN argues that this contour-building effect accounts for the dark triangles, trapezoids and platforms described on 5 November.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The surviving testimony illustrates the process unusually well. Witnesses agreed that lights travelled together, but they did not agree on the supposed body carrying them. The same broad display became a delta, diamond, trapezoid, rectangle or irregular platform. Even observers standing together could disagree about its geometry. Such differences are more compatible with people mentally joining a changing group of fragments than with several clear views of one sharply defined airframe.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B0952%20%281990307284%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Open source on cnes-geipan.fr.</span></span></span>

Apparent slowness added to the illusion. A distant formation can move at several kilometres per second yet drift gradually through an observer’s field of view. Because the debris path was broadly across the witnesses’ line of sight and remained far away, its angular movement resembled that of a large aircraft rather than the brief streak normally associated with a meteor. GEIPAN also notes that the speed could appear different from different viewing positions: slow, rapid or briefly almost stationary.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The persistent trails supplied further “mechanical” detail. Fragmenting metal created bright wakes, while separated pieces could resemble exhaust ports or lamps attached to a tail. A witness who saw one brilliant fragment flare might interpret it as a searchlight switching on or an object turning. These descriptions preserve real visual features, but translate them into the familiar language of aircraft.

## Why some witnesses rejected the explanation

The rocket-stage identification did not persuade everyone. Some witnesses remained certain that they had watched a solid craft at close range, sometimes beneath clouds or just above trees. Others argued that a re-entry might explain part of the evening but not reports involving abrupt manoeuvres, beams apparently reaching the ground or objects said to follow different courses.

Those objections deserve separation into two questions. First, was a Proton stage unquestionably re-entering over France at the relevant time? The orbital identification, timing and track make the answer yes. Second, did every statement collected that evening describe that one phenomenon accurately? Almost certainly not. A mass event can gather mistaken times, unrelated lights, memory changes and extreme descriptions around its central cause. Establishing the re-entry does not require treating every sentence in hundreds of reports as exact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

GEIPAN’s position is that the outlying accounts sit within the normal spread of errors produced by an unusual and poorly understood visual event. Its investigators point to the broad statistical distribution of reports: most descriptions fit the re-entry closely, while a smaller number contain exceptional movements, distances or forms. Concentrating only on those extremes can make the evening seem to contain several independent mysteries, but it ignores the much larger body of mutually consistent observations.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

There are genuine limitations. The complete archive is extensive, and GEIPAN has acknowledged that not every stored document has been subjected to a new, case-by-case modern re-investigation. The public collection nevertheless contains nearly 180 gendarmerie reports, alongside questionnaires, press material and later analysis. That leaves room to debate individual testimony, but not a comparable evidential gap around the principal event.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/53" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Nouvelle simulation de la rentrée atmosphérique du 5 novembre 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

The delay of several days before a firm public explanation also helped the UFO interpretation take hold. Newspapers and broadcasters initially reported an unexplained national spectacle, while airports, military installations and gendarmerie units were themselves receiving calls and seeking answers. By the time CNES announced the orbital match, witnesses had already exchanged descriptions and encountered dramatic accounts of enormous triangles. The rocket explanation therefore arrived after the craft narrative had acquired emotional and cultural force.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/sites/default/files/PV%20n%C2%B01731%20%281990307298%29.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: cnes-geipan.fr">[GEIPAN]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">cnes-geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">GEIPANBORDEREAU D &#x27; ENVOIJune 16, 2006 — Le 05/11/1990 à 19 heures … le 3&#x27; étage d&#x27;une fusée Proton qui de- vait mettre sur orbite, de té...</span><span class="citation-popover-meta">Published: June 16, 2006</span></span></span>

## What the case means for Vendée’s UFO history

The 5 November sighting is Vendée’s strongest mass-witness case, but it is not one of the department’s strongest unresolved cases. Its value lies in the combination of unusually vivid testimony and unusually strong identification.

It shows why the number or sincerity of witnesses cannot, by itself, establish what an object was. Hundreds or thousands of people can observe the same external event and accurately report its main direction, brightness and duration, while collectively making similar errors about distance, altitude and structure. Mass testimony can confirm that something happened without confirming that the witnesses correctly understood it.

The episode also demonstrates why official records matter. Viewed as isolated stories, Vendée accounts of a silent triangular platform appear difficult to explain. Placed beside reports from the rest of France, orbital data and the reconstructed Bay of Biscay-to-Alsace track, they become local views of one continent-scale re-entry. The geographical pattern supplies information that no single witness could possess.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/fr/node/51509" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">RENTREE ATMOSPHERIQUE 5 NOVEMBRE 1990 &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Later investigation has therefore weakened the claim that a giant unknown craft crossed Vendée, while strengthening confidence that witnesses saw a rare and spectacular aerospace event. Their experience was real; the inferred vehicle was not. That distinction makes 5 November 1990 a landmark in the department’s UFO history: not evidence of extraordinary technology, but a particularly instructive example of how extraordinary appearances arise from known objects under exceptional viewing conditions.
