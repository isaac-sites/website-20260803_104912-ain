## Introduction

[Haute-Corse]({{ 'what-haute-corses-ufo-files-actually/' | relative_url }}) has officially unresolved UFO reports, but the crucial point is that none of the department’s published GEIPAN cases is currently classified as a strong, investigated unknown. The records that remain open are category C cases: reports that could not be identified because the surviving information was too incomplete, imprecise or unreliable to support a firm conclusion. That is very different from a category D case, in which a sufficiently detailed investigation leaves a genuinely unexplained phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

## What GEIPAN’s classifications mean

GEIPAN is the unit within France’s national space agency, CNES, that collects, investigates, anonymises and publishes reports of unidentified aerial or aerospace phenomena. Its classification system assesses two related factors: how strange an observation remains after ordinary explanations have been tested, and how consistent the available evidence is. Consistency depends on the quantity and reliability of information, including witness detail, corroboration, photographs, recordings and the coherence of separate accounts.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ClassificationA more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN. The categories are easy to misread:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* **A:** the phenomenon has been identified with little or no ambiguity.
* **B:** an ordinary explanation is considered probable, though not completely proved.
* **C:** the case cannot be assessed properly because reliable information is missing.
* **D:** the phenomenon remains unexplained despite evidence substantial enough to investigate.

</div>

GEIPAN further distinguishes D1 cases, which retain moderate consistency, from D2 cases supported by stronger evidence such as independent witnesses, recordings or physical effects. Category C is therefore not a weaker form of D. It is a different evidential condition: investigators do not have enough dependable material to decide whether the event was ordinary or genuinely anomalous.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ClassificationA more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN. This matters particularly in Haute-Corse because its publicly listed historical cases divide between probable explanations and insufficient-data files. Favalello in March 1980 was classified B, with Venus judged the likely source. Santo-Pietro-di-Venaco in January 2003 was also B, attributed to a meteoroid. By contrast, Vezzani, Borgo, Vescovato and San-Martino-di-Lota were placed in category C. The archive does not list any of these four as a robust unexplained D case.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

## The 1980–83 cluster

The concentration of reports between 1980 and 1983 is the most noticeable pattern in Haute-Corse’s GEIPAN record. It includes Favalello and Vezzani in 1980, Borgo in 1981 and Vescovato in 1983. However, the cases were separated by months or years, described different phenomena and ended with different assessments. They do not form a clearly connected wave of sightings of one recurring object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

Favalello provides an important control case within the cluster. Its classification as probably Venus shows that a report could remain striking to the witness while still being matched to a familiar astronomical source. Bright planets can appear unusually large, coloured or mobile when viewed close to the horizon, particularly when haze, cloud or intervening terrain removes ordinary distance cues. GEIPAN’s conclusion does not prove every detail of the recollection was wrong; it means Venus was considered the explanation most capable of accounting for the observation as a whole.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/recherche/cas/tab?field_classification_des_cas_target_id%5B12%5D=12&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_phenomene_textuel&amp;page=%2C5&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPANFAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B, Haute-Corse, Vénus, 21/04/2008, 1980-03-03, voir. ### Vezzani’s close orange light

At Vezzani on 27 April 1980, the published testimony describes an orange, fiery-looking spherical object seen at about 00:30. The witness estimated it to be roughly the apparent size of a football, stationary, silent and only five or six metres away. Those details make the account one of the most immediately striking in the department’s archive.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/3106" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VEZZANI (2B) 27.04.1980 T2VEZZANI (2B) 1980. Date d&#x27;observation. 27/04/1980. Région. Corse. Département. Haute-Corse. Classificatio...</span></span></span>

They also expose its central weakness. Distance and physical size are exceptionally difficult to estimate for an isolated light at night. A witness who sees no visible structure, shadow, surface detail or interaction with nearby objects may interpret an unknown angular size as a nearby object when it is actually a distant light. The published entry supplies a police document and two testimonies, but GEIPAN still classified the event C rather than D, indicating that the available material was not consistent enough to establish the geometry or exclude ordinary sources.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/3106" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">VEZZANI (2B) 27.04.1980 T2VEZZANI (2B) 1980. Date d&#x27;observation. 27/04/1980. Région. Corse. Département. Haute-Corse. Classificatio...</span></span></span>

The responsible conclusion is not that the witness saw nothing. It is that the reported distance of a few metres cannot be treated as independently established. Without a precise line of sight, duration, environmental reference point or corroborating physical effect, the most extraordinary part of the account is also the least testable.

### Borgo’s low-flying luminous object

The Borgo report of 3 August 1981 is the richest of Haute-Corse’s category C cases. At about 22:55, a 70-year-old witness reported watching an object for roughly 40 seconds at an estimated distance of less than 400 metres. It appeared to move slowly from east to west, close to the ground, with a rounded front, a bright halo and a large multicoloured flame. The glow reportedly illuminated the surrounding landscape. The witness feared it might strike the mountain but heard no impact.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/fr/temoignage/3648" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BORGO (2B) 03.08.19813 Aug 1981 — BORGO (2B) 03.08.1981. Date d&#x27;observation. 03/08/1981. Région. Corse. Département. Haute-Corse. C...</span></span></span>

Several features invite conventional possibilities: a distant aircraft seen at an unusual angle, a helicopter, a meteor-like event misjudged as slow, or an object whose apparent descent was created by the mountain skyline. None can be demonstrated from the public summary. GEIPAN records that no additional information was obtained during the inquiry that would permit an explanation. That is why the case stayed C. The obstacle was not necessarily a shortage of possible explanations, but the absence of data capable of choosing between them.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/en/node/49043?field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=title&amp;page=18&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">BORGO (2B) 03.08.1981BORGO (2B) 03.08.1981. Borgo is therefore the clearest example of the difference between narrative vividness and evidential strength. The account contains motion, colour, apparent low altitude and environmental illumination, yet it rests on a single observation without a photograph, radar record, confirmed flight correlation or independent witness. A detailed story can still be a weakly constrained case.

### Vescovato’s thin public file

Vescovato, dated 8 May 1983, is also classified C for lack of reliable information. The public search record confirms the date, department and classification, but offers little accessible detail with which to reconstruct the event. This makes it less useful for historical analysis than Borgo or Vezzani. There is too little public evidence to compare the witness’s description against aircraft, astronomical objects, atmospheric effects or other local reports.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_is_new_value=All&amp;field_is_revisited_value=All&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_latitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_longitude_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_date_d_observation&amp;page=31&amp;sort=asc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-title">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de cas &#124; GEIPAN...</span></span></span>

Its position within the 1980–83 sequence should not be exaggerated. The date alone does not establish a continuing local phenomenon. For a genuine cluster, investigators would look for repeated descriptions, overlapping times, compatible flight paths, several observation sites or a shared environmental trigger. The published Haute-Corse files do not provide that pattern.

## Later reports did not strengthen the cluster

The next listed Haute-Corse case after the early 1980s was Santo-Pietro-di-Venaco on 11 January 2003. GEIPAN classified it B and identified a meteoroid as the probable explanation. That result weakens any simple claim that later reports continued an unexplained departmental pattern. It instead shows the archive applying a familiar transient astronomical explanation when the timing and reported behaviour permitted one.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

San-Martino-di-Lota on 30 August 2009 returned to category C, but for an especially basic reason: the date or location was imprecise. Without a dependable time and observation point, investigators cannot reconstruct the sky, consult meaningful aviation data, establish where the witness was facing or compare the report with astronomical positions. The file remains unresolved administratively, but its uncertainty provides little support for an unusual object.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

A case may remain open because the foundational coordinates of the event are uncertain, not because ordinary explanations were tested and failed.

## Why missing details leave cases unresolved

Night-time aerial observations are highly dependent on geometry. A bright point provides few clues to its real distance, size or speed. Mountains can hide the horizon, make a distant light appear close to the ground and create the impression that an object is descending towards a ridge. Coastal and airport traffic can also appear stationary when moving towards an observer, while changes in brightness may be interpreted as hovering, acceleration or disappearance.

To discriminate between those possibilities, an investigation needs more than a memorable description. The most useful details include:


<div class="content-enhancement content-enhancement--insight-grid" markdown="1">

* an exact observation time and position;
* a measured viewing direction and elevation;
* stable landmarks showing the object’s path;
* independent witnesses at different locations;
* photographs or video retaining original metadata;
* weather, astronomical and aircraft records;
* prompt reporting before memory and timing become less precise.

</div>

GEIPAN explicitly treats the quantity and reliability of such information as part of a case’s consistency. It also notes that older reports were often built mainly from gendarmerie statements, without the digital tools, standardised questionnaires and rapid access to flight or astronomical data available in later investigations. Reassessment can change classifications when new evidence appears, but decades-old category C cases are particularly difficult to resolve because missing measurements cannot usually be recreated.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://geipan.fr/index.php/en/node/58787" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">ClassificationA more detailed classification (A/B/C/D1/D2) has been used by the GEIPAN. ## Which Haute-Corse cases remain unexplained?

In ordinary language, Vezzani, Borgo, Vescovato and San-Martino-di-Lota remain unexplained because no definite cause has been assigned. In GEIPAN’s more precise language, however, they are unassessable category C cases rather than unexplained category D cases. The official archive’s strongest conclusion is therefore about the limits of the evidence, not the existence of an extraordinary phenomenon.<span class="citation-link-wrap"><a class="citation-inline-link" href="https://www.geipan.fr/en/recherche/cas/tab?field_agregation_index_value=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmax%5D=&amp;field_date_d_observation_value%5Bmin%5D=&amp;field_departement_target_id=&amp;field_document_existe_ou_pas_value=All&amp;field_phenomene_target_id=&amp;field_type_de_cas_target_id=All&amp;order=field_departement_textuel&amp;page=110&amp;select-category-export=nothing&amp;sort=desc" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer nofollow" aria-label="View source: geipan.fr">[Geipan]</a><span class="citation-popover" role="note"><span class="citation-popover-source">geipan.fr</span><span class="citation-popover-snippet">Recherche de casCase list; SANTO-PIETRO-DI-VENACO (2B) 11.01.2003, 11/01/2003, B; FAVALELLO (2B) 03.03.1980, 03/03/1980, B; VEZZ...</span></span></span>

Among them, Borgo is the most substantial narrative and Vezzani the most striking apparent close encounter. Neither has the corroboration needed to establish its reported distance, motion or physical nature. Vescovato is too thinly documented in the accessible record to carry much analytical weight, while San-Martino-di-Lota demonstrates how uncertain dates and locations can make even basic checking impossible.

For Haute-Corse’s UFO history, the official files are valuable precisely because they resist a simple choice between belief and dismissal. They preserve reports that witnesses found unusual, show where probable explanations were available, and mark cases that could not be responsibly decided. What they do not provide is a well-supported Haute-Corse case in which strong evidence survived systematic investigation with no plausible conventional explanation.
